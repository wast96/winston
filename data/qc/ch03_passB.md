## Chapter Three: The Killing of Zhao Tieqiao

### 8. The Traitor Shows His Loathsome Face

"What's that you say? The traitor is Zhao Tieqiao?" When Wang Yaqiao took the call Wu Hongtai had put through from Shanghai to his secret lodging in Hefei, he could not keep the shock down.

"Yes — yes! It was Zhao Tieqiao's doing!" Wu Hongtai's tone left no room for doubt.

"You're sure there's no mistake? How could he have betrayed Wang Leping?" Wang Yaqiao had no deep acquaintance with Zhao Tieqiao, but he had in fact met the man once in Shanghai not long before. It was Wang Leping himself who had brought this great scholar, Western suit, leather shoes, fresh home from study in America, to be introduced at his residence. In all fairness, the polished, deeply read Zhao Tieqiao had left Wang Yaqiao no bad impression. Wang Yaqiao, who had spent his life among knives and killing, nursed a genuine fondness — out of what corner of his heart, who can say — for men of real learning, and toward a brilliant graduate of the finance department of the University of California like Zhao Tieqiao he had been full of respect from the first. He had even felt a touch of envy that Wang Leping should have found such a man for a secretary. But now Wu Hongtai in Shanghai had fixed his sights on Dr. Zhao Tieqiao, the man Wang Leping had trusted so completely in life, and that Wang Yaqiao found very hard to swallow.

"Ninth Master, it was this whelp and no one else — there is no mistake! If I'm wrong, I'll stake my own head on it!" Wu Hongtai's flat certainty over the wire left the half-believing Wang Yaqiao unable to settle his mind. He could not take Zhao Tieqiao for the inside traitor on Wu Hongtai's say-so alone, yet neither could he wave the man's judgment away. To be safe, Wang Yaqiao ordered Xuan Jimin, who had gone to Shanghai with Wu Hongtai, to hurry back to Hefei that very night. He wanted the truth of the matter confirmed from Xuan Jimin's own mouth — for in Wang Yaqiao's eyes, the man most worth trusting was still Xuan Jimin, who had killed Zhang Qiubai for him in Nanjing.

"Ninth Master, you shouldn't be so quick to doubt Wu Hongtai. His report stands on solid ground — even I have to admire the unerring eye the man has for a traitor. He's the scholar-killer of our Hatchet Gang, after all!" Back at Wang Yaqiao's secret lodging in Hefei, Xuan Jimin reported how he and Wu Hongtai had gone scouting in Shanghai. Not long after they arrived, it seemed, they heard that Dai Li too had come up from Nanjing and was throwing a cocktail party of a novel sort at the Great World. Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai were just then working their channels to get to the bottom of Wang Leping's assassination, and hearing of the affair, they went to the party together.

They reached the Great World only to discover that this grand affair of Dai Li's was being thrown for one man, someone called Zhao Tieqiao, and that those crowding in to share the occasion included not only the leading figures of Shanghai's military and government worlds but a host of commercial magnates besides, along with flower-bright film actresses, Peking opera players, and young misses of tender years. So warm a scene took Wu Hongtai and Xuan Jimin by surprise. And they noticed, entering at Dai Li's side, a tall, elegantly built young man of easy grace, a cluster of vivid young beauties pressing around the pair.

"What, gentlemen — you don't even know Zhao Tieqiao? Why, he's the luckiest man in all Shanghai today!" Seated beside Wu Hongtai and Xuan Jimin was a gorgeous woman of the social rounds. From her seat she had long been sending envious glances toward Zhao Tieqiao, who had come in arm in arm with Dai Li; and when she caught Wu Hongtai quietly asking who the newcomer might be, she rattled off his particulars as though counting the treasures of her own house: "Him? He's a man of real power now — took the purse strings right out of Li Guojie's hands, and none too politely either. Just look at Li Guojie's face — did you ever see such a picture of ruined luck!"

Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai found their eyes drawn to Zhao Tieqiao. The young man was imposing indeed, an uncommon hauteur in every lift of his hand and turn of his step. From time to time he and Dai Li murmured together; then they moved side by side to the flower-banked seats of honor, and applause and shouts of acclaim burst from the room: "Boss Dai, tonight no one goes home sober!" "Mr. Dai, they say tonight's feast was laid on by you personally — a scene to make a man jealous!" "And after the wine, will there be dancing?"

Dai Li rose with a proud air, saluted the company with perfect courtesy, and spared a special smile for that garden of women lovely as the nation's fairest flowers. "My thanks to you all," he said. "Only, I regret to say, tonight's party and the ball to follow are not mine to host. The true host is here among us — the newly installed director-general of the China Merchants Steam Navigation Company in Shanghai, Mr. Zhao Tieqiao!"

"Well! So it's Director-General Zhao who gives the feast!" "Look at him — what dash the man has. One glance tells you he's built for great things." "Just so — they say he used to serve under Wang Leping. Now he's brought Wang Leping down, plucked fortune out of calamity, and got the fattest post on the whole Shanghai Bund into the bargain." "Exactly — the word is it took Chairman Chiang's own gracious approval before Zhao Tieqiao could take the first chair at the China Merchants company." From the first, Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai had felt that Dai Li's appearing in person at a businessman's banquet was itself a thing worth noting. Now, listening to these ladies, who had the inner history of Zhao Tieqiao's rise at their fingertips, chorusing his praises, they felt more strongly still that Zhao Tieqiao was no ordinary arrival.

"So this Zhao fellow once worked under Wang Leping?" Wu Hongtai, passing himself off as a visitor from out of town, made a point of asking the women beside him about Zhao Tieqiao.

A young matron fond of airing her knowledge said: "Naturally. Zhao Tieqiao was only a student newly home from abroad — but he could see that at Wang Leping's side he'd never rise in a lifetime, so he made up his mind to find himself a better door, didn't he?"

Another woman put in: "And now they say that since he took office Zhao Tieqiao has gathered every scrap of power to himself and left the company's board chairman, Li Guojie, a king without a court. Look — the Li fellow has started down his road to Maicheng!"

Xuan Jimin looked where the woman pointed, over the bobbing heads, and there indeed, beside Dai Li and the strutting Zhao Tieqiao, sat a pale-faced businessman in wooden silence. Head lowered, sighing, he did nothing but smoke in listless boredom, not daring to raise his eyes against the overbearing gaze of Zhao Tieqiao. And just as Li Guojie sat in this extremity of awkwardness, as if on a cushion of needles, Zhao Tieqiao rose to give the toast. Lifting his glass, he declaimed in rising and falling cadences: "Mr. Dai was too modest just now. In truth, tonight's banquet and tonight's ball are both in Mr. Dai's honor. Why such extravagance? Because Mr. Dai is the benefactor who saw my worth. Had he not shown me the road, I would be groping down a pitch-black lane to this day. Only now do I understand, and understand deeply: for a man who means to rise and make his name, nothing matters more than a guide to point the way — and Mr. Dai has been the finest teacher and truest friend of my life. I am proud to know Mr. Dai! Come, friends — ladies, gentlemen — lift your glasses with me and drink deep; let no one leave here sober! Let every guest join me: three brimming cups for Mr. Dai, who came to Shanghai in person for my inaugural feast!"

The room answered in a chorus. And as Xuan Jimin stood listening to Zhao Tieqiao's toast, he felt Wu Hongtai quietly tugging at his lapel. They slipped out of the banquet hall onto the terrace beyond, where the whole of Shanghai lay blazing with lamps, and far off the Huangpu River glimmered with faint, ghostly patches of light under the black hood of the night.

"Brother Jimin, it's the old saying to the letter — wear out iron shoes hunting and find nothing, then have it drop into your hands without lifting a finger." Wu Hongtai's voice was bright with excitement. "The man the Ninth Master sent us to find — isn't he standing right in front of us?"

Xuan Jimin gave a start. "You mean the traitor who carried our secrets to Chiang Kai-shek is the host of tonight's party?"

Wu Hongtai nodded with entire conviction. "Not a hair's-breadth off. As I see it, the murder of Mr. Wang Leping was certainly this Zhao's mischief working in the dark. Perhaps you don't know — when Zhao Tieqiao first came home from abroad, at the very moment he could find no post anywhere, it was Wang Leping who gave him his secretaryship. And now Wang Leping dies by an assassin's bullet, and this Zhao Tieqiao shows not one grain of grief — instead he shoots up overnight into fame and fortune, director-general of the China Merchants company no less! Think for a moment: that directorship is a plum of the first water. In its day, plenty of big men fought Li Guojie for that fat post. How should it ever fall to a little Zhao Tieqiao with no roots in the political world?"

Xuan Jimin felt the force of it. "True. Unless Zhao Tieqiao had rendered some great service, Chiang Kai-shek would never have handed him a post of such weight as director-general of the Shanghai China Merchants company. And yet — a young man just back from study abroad, without an inch of political footing: what service could he possibly have laid before Chiang Kai-shek? It passes understanding!"

"Zhao Tieqiao's rise plainly runs through Dai Li." Back in the banquet hall, Wu Hongtai had watched every intimacy between Dai Li and Zhao Tieqiao with perfect clarity. He reasoned it out: "What's in front of us now can't be untangled from Dai Li's earlier trip to Hefei to call on the Ninth Master. When Dai Li was still one of our Hatchet Gang, was he ever half so grand? What made him so, if not going over to Chiang Kai-shek? So I ask myself — might Zhao Tieqiao's promotion and sudden glory have something to do with Wang Leping's murder, and with General Fang Zhenwu's seizure in Nanjing?"

Xuan Jimin started violently. "You're saying the man who betrayed the Ninth Master's and Bo Wenwei's secret plan against Chiang — who carried it to Chiang Kai-shek's ear — was Zhao Tieqiao?"

Wu Hongtai weighed his words. "As I see it, he is the only man who could have played the traitor's part." But Xuan Jimin was unpersuaded. "And yet the Ninth Master's covert turning against Chiang with Bo Wenwei and Fang Zhenwu — Zhao Tieqiao could not possibly have known of it. The man never so much as brushed the hem of the Ninth Master's circle. How could he inform on him to Chiang?"

Wu Hongtai had his answer ready. "Brother Jimin, how can you forget? Wang Leping was the Ninth Master's sworn elder brother, bound by the full eight bows. More than that: before his death, Wang Leping held the liaison with Shi Yousan and Fang Zhenwu. With Zhao Tieqiao sitting at Wang Leping's elbow as his secretary, is it really impossible that he came into the secrets of the move against Chiang?"

"Yes — yes, of course!" Hearing Wu Hongtai lay it out point by point, Xuan Jimin sighed as the light broke on him. "Your reading is exactly right. It was this Zhao who sold his friend in the dark to buy his own rise, and left Wang Leping dead and wronged under the enemy's hidden gun. Now he has killed his master, pocketed the sweet post Chiang Kai-shek tossed him — and so of course he feasts his benefactor, Dai Yunong. Which means that before Dai Li ever came to Hefei to see the Ninth Master, he already had the whole plan of our Hatchet Gang's operation from Zhao Tieqiao! Seen by that light — what a venomous piece of work this Zhao Tieqiao is!"

Wang Yaqiao heard Xuan Jimin's report out and still stood between belief and doubt. "Whether Zhao Tieqiao truly is the spy behind Wang Leping's murder — that, from you and Wu Hongtai, is so far only inference; there is no hard proof in any of it. If we mean to avenge Wang Leping, deduction that snatches at wind and shadow will not serve. What I want is firm evidence that Zhao Tieqiao and Dai Li are locked together and have sold our intelligence. If he truly is the inside traitor who brought Wang Leping down — hmph — then this Mr. Zhao's good days are very nearly done!"

The next day Xuan Jimin went down to Shanghai again, to keep hunting with Wu Hongtai and the others for hard proof of Zhao Tieqiao's sale of himself to Chiang. By then Wang Yaqiao had mended his health in Hefei. In mid-June, with the heat on the Shanghai Bund at its fiercest, Wang Yaqiao — his wife Yaying beside him, Wang Ganting, Niu Anru and others in attendance — boarded a small boat on a black and rainy night and slipped down the rolling Yangtze straight for Shanghai, unseen by man or ghost.

Once in Shanghai, Wang Yaqiao kept deep indoors and out of sight, watching which way the wind blew. He lodged, as of old, in the shabby courtyards of the Anhui Guild Hall where the Hatchet Gang had first made its name. It was here that he meant to finish Zhao Tieqiao's life — in payment for his beloved friend Wang Leping, dead so miserably before the muzzle of Dai Li's gun.

"Ninth Master — we have it! Solid proof of Zhao Tieqiao's dealings with the enemy, in our hands at last!" On a night of thin, sifting rain, Wu Hongtai and Xuan Jimin came quietly to Wang Yaqiao's bedchamber. Wang Yaying, knowing the two would come only on grave business, hurried the attendants away, set Wang Yaqiao and the two men to their council in the inner room, and stood guard herself outside the door. Within, by a lamp-flame no bigger than a bean, Wu Hongtai made his report: "Li Guojie told me with his own mouth — it was Dai Li who pulled Zhao Tieqiao into the water. It was by selling his benefactor Wang Leping that he came by that scalding-hot seat of his, director-general of the China Merchants company."

"Hongtai — slowly now. Give me the whole of it, root and branch: how did Zhao Tieqiao sell his master for his own advancement?" Wang Yaqiao, seeing the vengeance he had waited on through so many days finally show its shape, felt the avenger's blood come up hot. Everything now stood ready; only the east wind was wanting. He would have Wu Hongtai and Xuan Jimin lay before him, exactly as it happened, all that two months of watching Zhao Tieqiao had brought in.

It had gone like this. After Xuan Jimin's second journey to Shanghai, they sent out several Hatchet Gang men each day, dressed as cobblers and rickshaw pullers, to dog Zhao Tieqiao's steps in secret and catch him in the act. To reach proof of Zhao's collusion with Dai Li in the selling of Wang Leping, Wu Hongtai and Xuan Jimin contrived ways of getting close to the China Merchants staff. But ordinary clerks could hardly know the truth of Zhao Tieqiao's affairs. It was then they observed that Zhao, since taking office — with Dai Li and Chiang Kai-shek at his back for mountain and patron — did not so much as glance at Chairman Li Guojie. Wu Hongtai and Xuan Jimin sensed their opening, for Li Guojie was an Anhui man too; and so, under cover of standing him dinners, Wu Hongtai drew near to Li once, twice, and again. At first Li Guojie would not open his heart to Wu Hongtai, only letting off flashes of grievance over the wine. Then one day Wu Hongtai carried Li off to drink at the Disiya restaurant and plied him hard, cup after cup, and Li Guojie, the fire long banked in his chest, did what men do: he spoke truth in his cups, and cursed Zhao Tieqiao roundly. "Damn him — a cur that bites with his master's teeth! If he weren't the apple of Chiang Kai-shek's eye, I'd buy men of the knife myself and have that dog's head off his shoulders. Otherwise, mark me, this China Merchants company will end as his private estate!"

Wu Hongtai pressed the advantage. "A little Zhao Tieqiao — how did he ever hook himself onto Chiang Kai-shek?"

Li Guojie said: "At the start, Chiang Kai-shek had no idea on earth who Zhao Tieqiao was. Then, by some path or other, Zhao got to know Dai Li. It was Zhao who gathered into his hands what he had picked up at Wang Leping's side — the secret of the Hatchet Gang's rising in league with Fang Zhenwu, Peng Jianguo and Shi Yousan. Dai Li happened just then to be in Shanghai collecting intelligence on Wang Yaqiao, so Zhao Tieqiao poured out to him every secret he had wormed from Wang Leping. When the mutiny had been broken, Chiang Kai-shek, privately hugging himself for escaping with his life, ordered Zhao Tieqiao commended. Of course Zhao's design was never merely the removal of a Wang Yaqiao or a Fang Zhenwu; what he wanted was real power to grasp in Shanghai. So he went further, and secretly passed to Dai Li the time and place of Wang Leping's part in the rising and of his coming to Shanghai — and delivered his own patron to the assassin's gun. Damn the man! This creature who sold his master for his own rise now comes to snatch my rice bowl too. And tell me this: if his bond with Chiang Kai-shek grows any deeper, will he stop at my money and my chair? One day, who knows, he may borrow Dai Li and Chiang Kai-shek to take my head off as well!"

"Can such things be?" Wu Hongtai played the astonished listener, sighing over his wine.

"True as heaven itself!" Li Guojie smote his chest. "I'm not a man to have sand rubbed in my eyes either. Let him dare lift a hand against me one day, and I'll show him no courtesy!"

With Wu Hongtai's and Xuan Jimin's report before him, Wang Yaqiao's last doubt that Zhao Tieqiao had played the spy and traitor was gone. That same night, in the Shanghai Anhui Guild Hall, he convened a secret council to work through, point by point, how Zhao Tieqiao was to be removed in Shanghai. But the meeting had barely opened when a man burst in from outside with urgent word: "Ninth Master — a telephone call from Li Guojie. He's asking whether Wu Hongtai is here!"

Wang Yaqiao turned to Wu Hongtai in open surprise. The other ranking Hatchet Gang men at the council — Xuan Jimin, Wang Ganting, Niu Anru, Liu Decai — all turned eyes clouded with doubt on Wu Hongtai. Wu himself had no notion why Li Guojie should suddenly be ringing the Anhui Guild Hall.

Wang Yaqiao nodded to him. "Take his call. Let's hear what fresh intelligence this Li Guojie has to offer."

### 9. A Steamship for a Man's Head

Wu Hongtai went down to take the call and came hurrying back with his face tight, saying urgently to Wang Yaqiao: "Ninth Master, heaven only knows what game Li Guojie thinks he's playing. He telephones me — and what he wants, if you please, is to invite you, Ninth Master Wang, to a banquet."

"Li Guojie asks me to dinner?" Wang Yaqiao felt a silent start. True, he had known this grandson of Li Hongzhang's direct line since his Hefei days; but in all the years since, the two had scarcely crossed paths. For a man of Li Guojie's money and standing to play host struck Wang Yaqiao as strange out of all reckoning. And remembering the plan they had been discussing that very hour — the killing of Zhao Tieqiao — Wang grew warier still: "What is this Li after? Can our plan against Zhao have sprung a leak already?"

Seeing suspicion rise in Wang Yaqiao again, Wu Hongtai gave a low chuckle. "The Ninth Master worries too much. You think Li Guojie means to plead for the Zhao Tieqiao we hate? Never in this life. By everything I know, Li Guojie and Zhao Tieqiao may both draw pay at the China Merchants company, but the two of them are enemies to the bone by now. Li Guojie hates Zhao Tieqiao to death — how could he come to beg mercy for him?"

"True, quite true." Xuan Jimin watched Wang Yaqiao sitting there pulling squeaking mouthfuls from his water pipe, silent this long while, and knew he was chewing again on the lesson of the last failed move against Chiang — knew, too, that a shadow of mistrust had crept over even the devoted brothers at his side. So he spoke up in Wu Hongtai's support: "What Hongtai says holds together. In the old days, every rein of the China Merchants company ran through Li Guojie's one pair of hands. Since Zhao Tieqiao took office, his footing is nothing like it was. Zhao doesn't spare Li Guojie a glance; that naked grab for power set Li Guojie's blood boiling long ago. I'll stand surety for it: whatever he wants, it is not to plead for Zhao Tieqiao."

"Then why does he ask me to drink with him?" Wang Yaqiao could make nothing of it.

Wang Ganting said: "Ninth Master, Brothers Xuan and Wu have been scouting Shanghai for days on end; what they say can't be lightly set aside." Liu Decai and Niu Anru agreed that for Li Guojie to feast Wang Yaqiao at just this moment was odd on its face — yet they too held firm that Li could not possibly be coming to intercede for Zhao Tieqiao. As for why Li Guojie should lay a banquet at all, the riddle defeated every man in the room.

"Ninth Master — if there's wine on offer, I say we go and drink it." It was Wu Hongtai, sunk in thought until now, who finally spoke. "To my mind, rubbing shoulders with Li Guojie may even serve our strike at Zhao. On the matter of standing against Zhao Tieqiao, at the very least, Li Guojie and we have come to the same place without exchanging a word."

Wang Yaqiao said: "You mean Li Guojie wants to borrow our hands to kill his enemy for him?"

Wu Hongtai glanced at Xuan Jimin, and both men nodded with conviction: "Very possible." They were still upstairs turning over why Li Guojie should suddenly feast Wang Yaqiao when the brother keeping the gate came up again, a great red card borne on his palm: an invitation, sent round by Li Guojie's man. Wang Yaqiao took it and read the small, neat brush characters:

*To the esteemed: at twelve noon tomorrow, in a private room at the Great Fortune restaurant, a humble table has been laid. The honor of Elder Brother Jiuguang's presence is respectfully begged. — Li Guojie, a fellow son of Anhui*

Wang Yaqiao turned Li Guojie's card over and over, then let his gaze settle on Wu Hongtai and Xuan Jimin. "His feud with Zhao Tieqiao — can it truly have come to the point of paying for a killing?"

Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai answered as one: "His grudge is no lighter than ours. Every dollar of Li Guojie's is draining into Zhao Tieqiao's pockets as we speak!"

"Good, then." Wang Yaqiao's mind was made up; his palm came down suddenly on the table. "If Li Guojie truly means to use the hand of Wang Jiuguang — why, nothing could suit me better. And since it's our Hatchet Gang he wants to use, then let him bleed for the privilege!"

At that, the whole company broke into knowing laughter.

At the stroke of noon next day, Wang Yaqiao — in jacket and trousers of white spun silk, with Wu Hongtai, Xuan Jimin, Liu Decai and several more Hatchet Gang brothers close about him — came straight from the Anhui Guild Hall to the Great Fortune restaurant on Nanjing Road. Before the private room on the second floor a beaming gentleman already stood in wait, in a long gown of gray silk, a paper fan stirring in his hand, the picture of modest courtesy. At the sight of Wang Yaqiao arriving with killers at his back, he hurried forward, all smiles and ceremony: "Please — Ninth Master, this way, please! There's not an Anhui man making his living on Shanghai soil today who doesn't stand a little taller in the Ninth Master's light!"

Wang Yaqiao spared him no answering courtesy, only led Wu Hongtai, Xuan Jimin, Niu Anru, Liu Decai and the rest straight into the private room, which was wide and full of light. Soon the serving girls filed in with dish upon dish of delicate Suzhou cooking, and Wang Yaqiao saw it was all of the finest: chicken steamed close with chestnuts, smoked fish touched with vinegar, creamed river shrimp, fragrant duck in its own juices — and a pot of aged nü'erhong, which, once opened, filled the room with a heady sweetness.

"Come, come — Ninth Master! Drain this cup, and let it stand for a lifetime's regard!" Li Guojie waved the serving girls out and offered the first cup with great solemnity. "Guojie has long meant to set out wine in your honor, but the chance of our meeting never offered itself. Today, at long last, the Ninth Master condescends to grace my table — so let me lift the first cup in respect."

But Wang Yaqiao's hand came up and covered the cup. "Worthy brother Guojie — at table there are three wines I do not drink. An enemy's wine I do not drink. A benefactor's wine I do not drink. And wine whose bottom I cannot see — that I do not drink either. Since you honor me with this table today, you are either repaying a debt or asking a favor. First let me understand plainly what you want of me; then I'll drain the cup dry."

Li Guojie pressed the wine on him again and again; Wang Yaqiao refused and went on refusing. At last Li Guojie could only let out a long sigh, and a mask of misery settled over his face. "The Ninth Master knows I come of a house of officials; taking up trade in Shanghai was itself a thing done for want of any other road. In Chiang Kai-shek's officialdom there was never going to be a place for our house of Li. Trade I could have borne — but Chiang Kai-shek must come hunting trouble for me even there. As matters stand, I can barely draw breath in the China Merchants company!"

Wang Yaqiao traded a knowing flicker of the eyes with Wu Hongtai and Xuan Jimin: exactly as foreseen. But he put on a puzzled face. "Worthy brother, what talk is this? You are chairman of the China Merchants board by open right and proper title — the first chair, fair and square. In that company your word ought to be law. What's this, then, out of a clear sky, about not being able to go on?"

Li Guojie's face crumpled like a mourner's. "Once, it's true, I lived handsomely enough. But from the day Chiang Kai-shek and Dai Li sent Zhao Tieqiao in as director-general, my days turned bitter! Damn him — what sort of thing is this Zhao Tieqiao? On the surface, honest and steady, got up in a scholar's manner. Live alongside him a while and you learn the treachery and the tyranny of the man. He has walled off against me every road in that company that money travels by. Tell me yourself, Ninth Master — how is a man to live in the China Merchants company now?"

By now Wang Yaqiao had the whole shape of Li Guojie's banquet clear in his mind, and was laughing up his sleeve. Who would have guessed that he and Li Guojie would arrive, without a word passed between them, at the same thought — the killing of Zhao Tieqiao? Still he kept talking around it, left and right, never touching the point, waiting for Li Guojie to come out with it himself. "Zhao Tieqiao? What sort of man is he? We've never met," Wang said. "And I'll not believe an outsider dares set himself against us Anhui men. Besides, brother Guojie — with money in your hands, what's to fear from one soft-handed scholar?"

Li Guojie leaned in close on the instant. "Ninth Master, you have it exactly. No venom, no true man. Since this Zhao Tieqiao has thrown away humanity, then I, Li, will throw away scruple. Driven to the last wall, I turned it over and over — and all at once I thought of the one man who could pull me out of this sea of bitterness. And that man, Ninth Master Wang, is you!"

"Me?! And how am I to save you?" Wang Yaqiao went on playing his riddle-game with Li Guojie. "I hold no office and command no power — a bare-pocketed nobody. How should I shake the director-general of a China Merchants company?"

Li Guojie, watching Wang Yaqiao spin his mysteries, said: "Ninth Master — enough of keeping me dangling! There's not a soul making his way on the Shanghai Bund who doesn't know the Ninth Master's name for standing by his own. The Hatchet Gang under your hand are cold-faced killers to a man, and their business is justice for men of spirit ground down by bullies. Here I stand, bullied in Shanghai by Zhao Tieqiao — will you truly fold your hands and look on?"

"What's this — you want me to remove Zhao Tieqiao?" Wang Yaqiao made his eyes go wide with astonishment, studying Li Guojie's small white face, now filmed with sweat.

Li Guojie answered with great gravity: "That is my meaning exactly, Ninth Master. No one on earth can save me now but you. And set your mind at ease — the thing once done, I, Li, will not be found wanting in gratitude." With that he opened his leather case, slid out a thick paper packet, and pressed it quietly into Wang Yaqiao's pocket. "A small token only. Eight thousand — call it luck-money to open the account. And once it's done..."

"Very well. Since my worthy brother Guojie is being trampled on in Shanghai, how could I, Wang Jiuguang, sit by and watch and never lift a hand?" Wang Yaqiao riffled through the notes, counting — and handed them back to Li Guojie. "But tell me, brother: for a piece of business of this size, is this truly all you'll part with — small change that might cover the tea?"

"No, no, Ninth Master — this trifle was never meant to stand as the fee. It was only tea-money for you and the brothers." Li Guojie suddenly set his teeth and took the plunge. "Let us do it this way. The Ninth Master is a man who has seen the world, and what sum would be fitting I honestly cannot say. Only — do the thing first. Finish this Zhao, and when it's done, I promise the Ninth Master a surprise!"

Wang Yaqiao had Niu Anru gather in the money, took a swallow of wine, and said: "Let's hear it, then. What surprise, exactly?"

"Ninth Master, it stands like this: the price of work of this kind — of course I know it. But every note the China Merchants company owns passes through Zhao Tieqiao's hands now; how could I lay such a sum before the Ninth Master at one stroke?" Li Guojie was a sharp article of the trading world. He could see that Wang Yaqiao had taken the killing of Zhao Tieqiao upon himself and balked only at the thinness of the deposit — hence the delay in striking hands on it. To clinch the business, and to hurry Wang Yaqiao's hand, he said: "So let it be this. To repay the Ninth Master's kindness, I, Li Guojie, will gladly give up a great ship — the *Jiang'an*, our own company freighter. Now surely that counts as a gift worth the name?"

"The freighter *Jiang'an*?" For a moment Wang Yaqiao was privately staggered: he had not dreamed that to kill one enemy, Li Guojie would part with a great seagoing vessel by way of payment. Then his heart warmed to it. A ship, plainly, was worth more to him than another few thousand of Li Guojie's dollars; if the Hatchet Gang one day owned a great ship, the run from the Huangpu up to Hefei would gain a tool that answered the hand like a good blade. Still, Wang Yaqiao kept one more wit about him. "Brother Guojie," he said, "in truth, a company ship for Zhao Tieqiao's head is a fair bargain from where you sit — with him gone, you'll be free to rake in silver with both arms. Only mind this: the *Jiang'an*, when all is said, is public property. You make me a present of her — will that breed trouble for you down the road?"

"It will not. When has Li Guojie ever unsaid his own word?"

"Good — then one word each, and it's settled." Wang Yaqiao was an old hand of the rivers and lakes, and he closed on the spot. "But officials trust to papers and private men to seals. Since my brother Guojie means to make so great a gift, an empty mouth is thin surety — that won't quite do, will it?"

Li Guojie struck himself on the chest. "Rest easy, Ninth Master. I'll send a man round with the deed at once — will that answer?"

Wang Yaqiao gave his word then and there. "Done. A gentleman's word once out, four horses cannot run it down. The moment your deed touches my hand, we'll lay Zhao Tieqiao's head on the ground for you — no more and no less!"

And so, then and there in the Great Fortune restaurant, the Hatchet Gang captains and Li Guojie settled their design, and the cups went up all around and rang together. Amid the passing of wine and the touching of glasses, the great enterprise of killing Zhao Tieqiao was fixed.

### 10. Out of Nowhere: An Assassination in Broad Daylight

Far down the pitch-black road, a pair of headlamps came alight.

It was the dead of night, June 30, 1930. The French Concession, hushed in the small hours of midsummer, lay unusually still and serene. Little Western houses, their lamps burning, hid in silence among stand after stand of black-shadowed plane trees. Yet the road was not quite empty: now and again a pair of night-walking lovers passed, and here and there people whom the sultry heat had cheated of sleep sat out taking the cool. Not one of them could have dreamed that on this quiet summer night, at a corner where the French Concession police drifted past at all hours, a burst of ear-splitting gunfire was about to break.

The men lying up beforehand in a pool of plane-tree shadow at the Gordon Road corner in the French Concession each gripped a short pistol, black hoods drawn over their heads: three killers Wang Yaqiao had picked with care — Niu Anru, Wang Ganting and Xia Shao'en. All three, Niu Anru at their head, had been through the Nanjing attempt on Chen Diaoyuan, and knew the assassin's trade at first hand. A full month earlier, once Wang Yaqiao and Li Guojie had settled the great design against Zhao at the Great Fortune restaurant, Wang had sent out picked men in two working parties. One, formed of Wu Hongtai, Liu Deshan and others, was the scouting party, set to watch Zhao Tieqiao's movements in secret. The other was the killing party, which drew up several schemes of attack.

To hurry Zhao Tieqiao out of the world, Wang Yaqiao made over to Wu Hongtai a "Naona" sedan. With it, Wu and his men could run the roads between Gordon Road and the Shanghai China Merchants offices at will, keeping their quiet watch on Zhao: when he went in to the office, when he came home, when he went out to his banquets and his balls — every step and pause of the man's day was in their ledger. And out of all that watching, day and night, came one finding: Zhao's evenings ran thick with engagements, dinner treading on dinner till he could scarcely keep pace. Only when this nightly rhythm was firmly in hand did Wang Yaqiao commit the second party — Wang Ganting, Niu Anru and their fellow killers — to make the attempt in the hottest deep nights of late June.

That evening, word came to Niu Anru from Wu Hongtai: Zhao Tieqiao was off again to the Great World, for an evening of Peking opera singing. Niu resolved to ambush Zhao's car at the Gordon Road corner in the depth of the night, asked leave, and had Wang Yaqiao's nod. Wu Hongtai's men had long since taken the measure of Zhao's night travels, and knew besides that his house stood barely a li past the Gordon Road corner; so they reckoned with confidence that at that corner Zhao's car must slacken speed. In that instant they would close from the front and both flanks at once and shoot the car's front window to pieces; the driver would surely die on the spot or run for his life, and then Zhao Tieqiao would be a turtle in a jar. One wild volley, and the traitor's life would end where he sat.

Now the clock's hands stood at twelve. And just as Niu Anru and Wang Ganting had reckoned, a Buick sedan came on through the sealed dark. Its lamps flashed; sure enough, its speed fell away sharply as it swung into the turn at the corner. In that instant Wang Ganting flung up a hand to Niu Anru and Xia Shao'en in the shadow of the plane trees, and the three of them burst out together, rushing the slowing car from three sides. Wang Ganting, foremost — swifter in the doing than in the telling — threw up his pistol and fired, one sharp crack at the car's front window. But against all his reckoning, the tension of the moment pulled the shot wide: the front glass did not shatter, and the bullet's only work was to jolt awake Zhao Tieqiao, half asleep on the rear seat. He started up, looked out, saw hooded figures rushing from the roadside all at once, and knew that killers of the worst kind had found him. He shrieked: "Don't stop the car!" The stunned driver, black shapes hurling themselves madly at his machine, stamped down on the pedal and the car leapt forward, tearing away like a thing possessed into the deep lanes of the French Concession.

"Crack — crack — crack." Almost in the same breath, Niu Anru and Xia Shao'en opened up on the flying car, and the shots ran together into one long rattle. It was this sudden fire that roused the French Concession police on their night rounds; whistles went up at once, and the French officers and Chinese constables on night duty came running for Gordon Road from every side. Niu Anru, Wang Ganting and Xia Shao'en would have pressed the chase, but with the whistles knitting into one all around them there was nothing for it but the withdrawal plan: abandon the French Concession and run for the British. There a car stood waiting — Liu Decai had charge of the pickup. The three tumbled in, the driver raced the motor, and the car flew off down a black alley. By the time the French Concession police came up, the assassins had vanished without a trace.

"Steady — if once won't do it, we go a second time. However it falls, this time we take the Zhao fellow's life, and nothing less will settle it!" After that first testing of the blade on June 30, rumor of every color ran the length of the Shanghai Bund. Some said this was Huang Jinrong's work against Zhao Tieqiao — the time-honored method of Shanghai's three great bosses, a misfortune few new-risen favorites escaped. But Huang Jinrong publicly denied in the press that the affair had anything to do with him, on the grounds that Zhao Tieqiao, on taking up the China Merchants directorship, had already been to his house to pay his respects. At that, opinion swung round and called it a joint stroke by Du Yuesheng and Zhang Xiaolin against Zhao Tieqiao. Du Yuesheng did not stoop to answer the rumor, while Zhang Xiaolin told the newspapermen to their faces: "Boss Du is a friend of Zhao Tieqiao's. We're all friends here — where would such a farce come from? And besides: if we truly meant to move against Director Zhao, we'd hardly be such fools as to raise our dust inside the French Concession!"

While the rumors multiplied, the French Concession police threw up new checkpoints around Gordon Road: past eight at night, no Chinese of doubtful appearance was to set foot in the Concession at all. As for Zhao Tieqiao, after that brush with death he never again dared an evening engagement. He was home from the company almost before the light failed, and after that would not stir a step past his door. New private bodyguards thickened around him; by night, inside his gate and out, it was all but a post every ten paces and a sentry every five. Never mind Wang Yaqiao's no longer daring to keep killers in the streets around Gordon Road — had assassins come, there was no longer any door by which the killing could begin.

And so, for the time being, the assassination hung suspended.

Wang Yaqiao met again and again in secret with Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai over plans of action. Li Guojie, his deposit already paid, was on the telephone to Wang almost daily, pressing him: "Ninth Master, didn't you tell me nothing could miscarry? How is it the first stroke ends with the hen flown and the eggs smashed? And bear this in mind — my *Jiang'an* has been tied up at the Wusong wharf this long while already!"

Wang Yaqiao, for his part, burned like a man on coals. In all his years of work on the Shanghai Bund he had never struck anything so thorny. Day and night he turned over how to root out Zhao Tieqiao quickly. He went himself, by hired rickshaw, wheeling through the several corners near the French Concession that led toward the Zhao house; and when he saw the Concession grown into a forest of checkpoints, police thick as hemp stalks, he knew that to go on with assassination there was hope thrown away. He shifted his eye to the thronged heart of the city. But in the bars, the dance halls, the pleasure haunts where Zhao Tieqiao had once liked to show himself, no shadow of the man now fell. Gun-startled on that midsummer night, Zhao was the man once bitten by a snake who fears the well-rope for three years. In all those quarters, scarcely an opening was to be found. And just when Wang Yaqiao stood at a dead loss, one day his car happened to pass the great gate of the China Merchants company. On a sudden he ordered the car slowed, then braked well short of the gate under the roadside shade, and through the window Wang Yaqiao sat quietly studying the comings and goings at the entrance. In that moment a bold notion sprang up whole in his mind: the China Merchants company itself was the perfect ground for the killing!

"The reason I fix on the ground inside and outside the China Merchants gate is simple: it is the one place where Zhao Tieqiao's guard is thinnest. And our stroke can no longer fall by night. After dark the man is all nerves — he won't so much as put his head out of doors; whatever ground we prepare against him by night, the labor is thrown away." Wang Yaqiao had called yet another council under the lamp. Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai at first spoke their worry over a killing by daylight, and their opposition too; but when Wang Yaqiao had laid out the reasoning of it, every man came over, heart and mind. Wang said: "All of Zhao Tieqiao's defenses are massed against the night. That a man might come for him in broad day has not yet entered his head. And the China Merchants building is a place where callers pass in and out at will — even the gatekeepers barely trouble the visitors. That is what hands us our best chance of shooting him down. This is what is called striking where no one expects you, hitting where no guard is set. So our best moment to fall on him is morning, when he arrives for work — or midday, when he leaves."

And so the new plan of attack was fixed.

July 24, and Shanghai lay under another day of annihilating heat.

In the early morning, Wang Ganting, Niu Anru, Xia Shao'en, and a junior killer added to the party for the occasion all got themselves up as seasoned merchants come to talk business with the China Merchants company. One after another they strolled in at the gate at their ease, and the old man keeping it hardly put a question to them. Inside, Niu Anru and the others found the corridors chill and deserted, with scarcely a clerk in sight — which was exactly as the killers would have wished it. But from half past seven, the start of the working day, when they walked in at the gate, no shadow of Zhao Tieqiao appeared. By the pattern Wu Hongtai's scouts had fixed, Zhao should long since have reached the company; this morning, for no reason anyone knew, there was no sign of him. Had Zhao Tieqiao sensed something at last? Or had this play of Wang Yaqiao's staging sprung some flaw of its own? If Zhao failed to come in this morning, then to go on with the daylight plan hereafter would be a far worse gamble — for men seen passing in and out of the China Merchants company time after time could hardly escape notice.

Niu Anru was waiting in the company's reception hall with his heart on a slow fire when suddenly a figure flicked past the doorway — Xia Shao'en, posted to watch the gate, throwing him the signal: Zhao had appeared!

Niu Anru shot to his feet and was out of the ground-floor hall at a run. There he saw a familiar Buick sedan already through the gate, braking smoothly to rest below the terrace at the front of the building. The same car that had slipped past their muzzles a month before — today, come what might, the Zhao Tieqiao inside it would not get away from under their eyes again. Niu Anru had barely cleared the glass doors when a man stepped proudly down from the car: tall of figure, a short jacket of pale gray above black Western trousers, and in his hand, as a badge of his standing, a gentleman's cane. The very Zhao Tieqiao they had waited on so long! Niu Anru and the others went taut as wire. Behind Zhao, they saw, followed only a secretary in spectacles — by day, it seemed, the bodyguards did not attend him. The secretary carried Zhao's leather case under one arm; the two men walked one behind the other toward the glass doors, and Zhao Tieqiao had just mounted the steps before the building when Niu Anru heard the crack of a shot — Xia Shao'en had fired first. In the courtyard of the China Merchants company the report rang out with startling clearness! And yet the courtyard did not stir at the sound. For Zhao Tieqiao, the bullet in him, neither cried out nor went down: he stood pointing at his bleeding chest, looking to left and right. His mouth opened and worked in pain, but no sound would come.

"Crack! Crack!" Niu Anru fired twice into Zhao Tieqiao, aimed and sure. Xia Shao'en and the other killer fired in the same instant, and Zhao Tieqiao, struck again and again, his chest one sheet of blood, could hold up no longer: he pitched forward with a dead thud onto the steps before the door.

"Murder! Someone's shot the Director-General!" The secretary, the leather case still jammed under his arm, fled headlong into the empty hall, screaming up into the building. Only then did the clerks sitting over tea and small talk in their offices take alarm; and by the time they came pouring out of the building white-faced, they found Zhao Tieqiao already stretched stiff in a pool of blood, past all moving.

The Shanghai Bund went up in an uproar. Every paper, under banner headlines in heavy black type, rushed out the stupefying news: the new star of the commercial world shot dead by nameless gunmen. And all the while, Wang Yaqiao was busy with another matter — how to take over the freighter *Jiang'an*, riding on the river off Wusong.

"Jimin — Hongtai — now it's your turn on the stage!" One day in early August, when the storm over the killing of Zhao had blown itself quiet, Wang Yaqiao called Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai into the Anhui Guild Hall. "We have killed Zhao Tieqiao — and Li Guojie no longer breathes one word about the *Jiang'an*. As I read it, unless we go and take her ourselves, the Li fellow will never hand her over with a bow. Go now — get the *Jiang'an* into my hands, and we'll talk after!" At that, Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai both cried out: "We hold the deed Li Guojie wrote at the start — let him try to welsh on that!" And with the words still hot, they gathered up a handful of strapping men and stormed off, all wind and fire, for the Wusong wharf.

Wang Yaqiao kept to the guild hall, drawing on his water pipe, stretched on the smoking couch trading idle talk with his wife Yaying, waiting only for Wu Hongtai and Xuan Jimin to bring home the good news. But in the midst of their ease there came a rush of hurried footsteps beyond the door, and a petty chief burst in at a run, shouting: "Ninth Master — it's bad! There's fighting broken out on the river!"

Wang Yaqiao and Wang Yaying scrambled up from the couch, neither with the least idea what could have broken loose at the riverside.
