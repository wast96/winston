## Chapter Two: The First Rehearsal for the Murder of Chiang Kai-shek

### 5. The Bad Blood Between Wang Yaqiao and Chiang Kai-shek Went Back a Long Way

March 1928. Hefei.

Toward evening, a rickshaw came down a nearly empty road toward the Tongmenghui dormitory compound at the heart of the city. In it sat a gentleman in a felt hat and a long gown of gray cloth. Fresh from the railway station, the passenger had deliberately pulled his brim down low, so that no one on the street would see the long, horse-like face beneath it. He was Dai Chunfeng.

He rode the flying rickshaw alone with his thoughts. Before his eyes hung, and would not fade, the image of a man of middling height with a squared-off face and a drooping mustache on his lip: the man who was giving Chiang Kai-shek such headaches, Wang Yaqiao.

"Take my card in to the Ninth Master, please — tell him a man named Dai Chunfeng begs to see him!" Dai Chunfeng was born in the town of Xiakou in Jiangshan county, Zhejiang, and whenever he thought back to his teens, scraping along on the Shanghai Bund, he remembered with gratitude the man who had first seen something in him: Wang Yaqiao, chief of the Hatchet Gang. In those days he had run away from the Jiangshan countryside and served first as an orderly under the Zhejiang warlord Zhou Fengqi; the rigor of army life did not suit him, and he fled alone to Shanghai to drift through the ten-li foreign strip. Dai Chunfeng still remembered it clearly. It was spring, and he had come to a dead end in Shanghai, when one day down Nanjing Road came a column of men carrying great gleaming hatchets, and the people beside him sighed with admiration: "Take a look, boy — there goes a real hero, the Ninth Master Wang. He came to Shanghai from Anhui to try his fortune too, and look what he's made of himself here. The Hatchet Gang — look at the style of them!" "Look at Wang Yaqiao. There's a true man. He fought his way up with one hatchet, and now, on the whole Shanghai Bund, even Huang Jinrong and Du Yuesheng give him ground!"

"Wang Yaqiao?!" Dai Chunfeng heard the name and started inwardly.

"That's the one. You've lived as a man for nothing, boy — in Shanghai and you don't know the great name of the Ninth Master Wang?" Dai Chunfeng stood at the roadside, and someone, seeing him rooted there staring after Wang Yaqiao striding grandly past, jeered at him: "You useless little biesan! Go on — clear off! A man who doesn't even know the Ninth Master — do you count as a man at all?"

In truth, how could Dai Chunfeng not have known Wang Yaqiao? Years before, when he was begging along the streets and a motorcar knocked him senseless and left him sprawled across the road, it was Wang Yaqiao who had saved his life! Now the swagger and power of the Hatchet Gang shook him to the core, and a light went on in the head of a young man at the end of his road. He turned it over for several days and at last resolved to hang every hope of getting ahead on that Anhui man. So one day, screwing up his courage, Dai Chunfeng presented himself at the Anhui Guildhall. He had no visiting card, so he had sat up through the night writing a letter to recommend himself, and at the guildhall gate he respectfully handed it to the Hatchet Gang disciple keeping the door. Then he waited outside, carefully deferential, certain that a chief as famous as Wang Yaqiao would never take notice of a derelict like him. But hardly had the account of his life gone in when word came out: "Boy — the Ninth Master asks you in!" Dai Chunfeng could scarcely believe his ears. "The Ninth Master... will see me?" The man watched him stand there frozen and said, "What are you gawking at? Or must the Ninth Master come out and invite you himself?"

Only then did Dai Chunfeng wake as if from a dream. He hurried after the man around a screen wall to the main hall, and stepping softly inside he discovered several men crowded round a table of mahjong, deep in the clattering war of the bamboo grove. Dai Chunfeng stood carefully to one side and dared not open his mouth. He had waited some two hours before a head lifted at the Eight Immortals table and someone asked: "Hey — young fellow. They say your people came from Anhui too?"

Dai Chunfeng stole a look. The man questioning him was the very Wang Yaqiao he had seen two days before at the corner of Nanjing Road — the drooping mustache on his lip was unmistakable. He bowed low in a fluster and said humbly, "Ninth Master, yes, yes — my forebears did indeed once make their living on Anhui ground, so the Ninth Master and I are countrymen after all. That is why I dared come to throw myself on the Ninth Master, in hopes of finding a place in Shanghai to plant my feet and make my life!"

Wang Yaqiao saw that for all the coarseness of the man's bearing there was inside the coarseness a guarded, capable something that ordinary men did not have, and he looked at him with new eyes. "If you're an Anhui man, why does your letter call you a native of Jiangshan in Zhejiang? Isn't that a contradiction? You wouldn't be an impostor working a swindle, would you?"

Dai Chunfeng half rose, bowing, an apologetic smile on his face. "The Ninth Master misunderstands me. Down on my luck I may be, but I would hardly misreport my ancestral home just to scrounge a bowl of rice, surely? I have come to the Ninth Master not for office and not for fortune, but out of admiration for the Ninth Master's character and conduct — above all that lofty way of yours, upright in your dealings and open-handed with your money, which I, Dai Chunfeng, revere. So I came expressly to pay my respects. Let the Ninth Master give me a bowl of rice to eat, and I ask nothing more."

"Good — good, good!" Wang Yaqiao heard speech out of the common run, saw the burning light in the man's eyes, and knew in his heart that the day would come when this one stood no lower than himself. He rose quickly and came to stand before Dai Chunfeng. "Dai Chunfeng, in the Shanghai of today officials and bandits run wild together, and among men of every color the fish are jumbled in with the dragons. That at such a moment you should come to me, of all men, shows an upright character. Very well — stay under my roof. And hear me well: so long as I, Wang Yaqiao, am here, you need never fear for your bowl of rice, Dai Chunfeng."

From that day on, Dai Chunfeng was a minor disciple of the Hatchet Gang in Wang Yaqiao's Anhui Guildhall. In time Wang came to prize him beyond the others, for Dai Chunfeng had not only a gift for pleasing people but also the cool sharp-wittedness no gang figure can do without. What startled Wang Yaqiao most was that this disciple of no account had nerve out of all proportion to his station. Even in their weakness for women the two men were almost exactly alike. Kindred spirits know each other: in the end Wang Yaqiao not only raised Dai Chunfeng to a position of trust but smeared blood in oath with him, and they exchanged cards and knocked heads as sworn brothers. It was more than Dai Chunfeng had dared dream the day he first came to the Anhui Guildhall to beg a place.

"Yunong, I am sending you to Hefei to carry one message to this Wang Yaqiao you swore brotherhood with in Shanghai: from now on he is to live like an honest man, and there is to be no more setting himself up as an equal against me, Chiang Chung-cheng." Dai Chunfeng saw the rickshaw closing on the provincial Tongmenghui dormitory and felt a sudden tightness in his chest. He was, after all, a very different man now. Once he had served under Wang Yaqiao in Shanghai; today he was one of Chiang Kai-shek's secret agents. He had come from Nanjing to Hefei not to talk over old times with the sworn elder brother of his youth, but on a special mission from Chiang Kai-shek: to sound a warning bell in Wang Yaqiao's ear. No one on earth knew the chief of the Hatchet Gang better than Dai Chunfeng — and in his heart he had no confidence at all that the persuasion Chiang had charged him with could be brought off. Just then the puller said, "Here you are, sir — the Tongmenghui dormitory you were looking for!"

At that hour, upstairs in the provincial Tongmenghui building, a short, compact figure stood silent before an oval floor-length window. This was the man Dai Chunfeng was so anxious to find: Wang Yaqiao, at the window with his own thoughts. After the attempt on Chen Diaoyuan in Nanjing miscarried, Wang Yaqiao had slipped quietly back to Hefei, where he and the men of the Hatchet Gang went on plotting a second strike at Chen Diaoyuan's life.

"Ninth Master, Chen Diaoyuan is this moment begging Chiang Kai-shek for a transfer out of Anhui. We didn't kill him in Nanjing, but we frightened him out of his wits. He tells everyone he meets that Anhui is no place for the likes of him to linger — better to hold office somewhere else than be assassinated by the Hatchet Gang." On the eve of Wang Yaqiao's second attempt, a mysterious figure suddenly appeared one day at his residence: a tall man of imposing bearing, a pair of tiger's eyes under two thick brows. Wang looked, and it was Wang Leping, second-ranking elder from the founding of the Hatchet Gang in Shanghai. Close behind Wang Leping followed a fine-featured man: Yu Likui, a soldier, and one of Wang Yaqiao's closest friends. When the two of them reported that Chen Diaoyuan was to be transferred out of Anhui, Wang Yaqiao was overjoyed. "Ha! Didn't Chen Diaoyuan sell himself to Chiang Kai-shek precisely to get the chairmanship of Anhui? Not two years in office, and now he wheels his horse for the hills? Has he forgotten what a feng-shui treasure-land Anhui makes for squeezing the people's money?"

Wang Leping's friendship with Wang Yaqiao went back to their early Shanghai years; this time he and Yu Likui had gone specially to Nanjing to steal the intelligence and carried it to Hefei for the man planning the second attempt. "Ninth Brother, it's like this," he said. "You didn't kill Chen Diaoyuan last time, but you shot his crony Zhang Qiubai dead on the spot. Chen Diaoyuan has learned that the Ninth Brother is not a man to provoke. He knows that if he stays on in Anhui he can go on fleecing the people and lining his own purse — but with you here, sooner or later there will be a second attempt, and a third. Chen Diaoyuan holds his skin precious. Would he dare sit here waiting for the Hatchet Gang's guns?"

Wang Yaqiao laughed. "Then Chen Diaoyuan is no fool. If he truly leaves Anhui, he picks his life up and carries it away with him. Otherwise, my brothers could hardly promise not to put a bullet through his skull some day!"

Yu Likui pressed him: "Ninth Brother, Chen Diaoyuan's evils in Anhui are past counting. Now is the perfect moment to strike. Is he to do his wickedness, slap the dust off his backside, and run for his life?"

Wang Yaqiao waved a level hand. "Likui, there you are wrong. From the day our Hatchet Gang was founded in Shanghai, my rule — Wang Jiuguang's rule — has been to kill the wicked for the relief of the many, never to butcher a man who has looked into himself and woken up. Chen Diaoyuan has taken his fright; he knows now that evil comes to a bad end; he has handed Chiang Kai-shek his resignation. Let him go. So long as he works no more evil on Anhui ground, we simply leave him be. When I moved against him in the first place, it was never for any private grudge of mine — it was to shoot Chen Diaoyuan dead as a lesson set in front of Chiang Kai-shek. All of you mark this: the target of our Hatchet Gang, first and last, is Chiang Kai-shek, that detestable autocrat and traitor to the people!"

And indeed, the day after Wang Leping and Yu Likui brought their news, a single transfer order from Chiang Kai-shek packed Chen Diaoyuan off to the Northern Expedition's First Group Army, to command its 37th Army. Before long Fang Zhenwu arrived in Hefei to take up the provincial chairmanship. Fang Zhenwu had gained a province, but he was no man of Chiang Kai-shek's inner circle, and so frictions bred daily and chafed on every side. Wang Yaqiao saw in this an opening: the moment to raise an anti-Chiang movement on Anhui soil.

"Ninth Brother, toppling Chiang — of course every heart is with it. But I keep doubting whether this united front of yours can succeed." Soon after Chen Diaoyuan left Anhui, Wang Yaqiao called an emergency council of the Hatchet Gang. When Wang Leping and Yu Likui heard him lay out his plan to link Fang Zhenwu, Shi Yousan and Bo Wenwei against Chiang, both were fired by it. Wang Leping above all — a veteran of the Revolutionary Alliance with an old score of his own against Chiang Kai-shek — backed Wang Yaqiao's anti-Chiang front to the hilt. Yet he still harbored doubts about whether the likes of Shi Yousan would truly turn their coats. "Shi Yousan is the one who worries me," he said, and the worry was plain. "He has nursed his grudges against Chiang for years, true enough. But everyone knows the man's word and his deed live apart — breaking faith is a habit with him. Will he really rise against Chiang at our side?"

"Shi Yousan is treacherous by nature, granted — but he keeps a large army quartered in Jiangsu, and that is a force nobody can afford to ignore." Wang Yaqiao had made a careful study of every warlord's strength across the Jiangnan country, and it told when he talked. "General Bo Wenwei is the commander-in-chief of our anti-Chiang alliance," he said to Wang Leping and Yu Likui. "His policy is to link every baron who can be linked into one great front against Chiang — only so can the Hatchet Gang's design of many years be realized. Fang Zhenwu has now agreed to lend us an arm in the anti-Chiang camp: there is our condition of success. And Fang Zhenwu's condition for joining us is that Shi Yousan's troops must take part — only then will he dare to rise. Without that, Chiang Kai-shek cannot be brought down."

Wang Leping thought this exactly right. "A fine plan. Only — who is to play the persuader with Shi Yousan?"

"I go myself, naturally. I don't believe Shi Yousan will refuse me face." Wang Yaqiao plainly had the whole recruitment of anti-Chiang forces settled in his chest already. He turned his eyes on Yu Likui, who had said nothing for a long while. "What worries me now is the Independent Fourth Brigade at Changzhou. Likui — could you go in my stead and talk them round?"

Yu Likui had known Wang Yaqiao's kindness since his early years and admired him from crown to sole. Handed the charge of turning the Fourth Brigade at Changzhou, he slapped his chest. "Ninth Brother, if you trust me, let me go to Changzhou myself. Everyone knows Brigade Commander Peng Jianguo has been my close friend for years — and I know he hates Chiang Kai-shek to the marrow of his bones. Send me to Changzhou and the banners will open in victory!"

"Splendid!" For years Wang Yaqiao had carried one wish in his heart: to bring Chiang Kai-shek down. Now, under his generalship, that wish was turning into a strategy ready to be set in motion. "When the hour comes, three roads rise as one: Fang Zhenwu in the north, Peng Jianguo's Changzhou brigade in the south, Shi Yousan in the west — and in the center, our Hatchet Gang to answer from behind. That makes the formation Three Kingdoms on One Side. Let Chiang Kai-shek escape destruction then if he can!"

Wang Yaqiao was standing at the window over these grand designs when a disciple came up from below and reported: "Ninth Master, a caller from Nanjing. He says he must see the Ninth Master face to face."

"Oh? A caller from Nanjing?" Puzzled, Wang Yaqiao took the card the disciple handed up and found on it a stranger's name: Dai Li! He started inwardly. Of all moments — just as he was drawing his ring around Chiang Kai-shek, here was Dai Chunfeng, who had once bowed to him as elder brother in Shanghai, suddenly arrived in Hefei. "Dai Li — and who is Dai Li?" he said, half to himself. "Can it really be Dai Chunfeng, from the old days? And if it is, what business brings him here from Nanjing? Has the Chiang fellow scented something?" Suspicion stirred in him, and his first thought was to refuse the caller. Then he thought again, and waved a hand at the disciple. "Send him up!"

### 6. Dai Li and Wang Yaqiao

The caller from Nanjing, the moment he stood before Wang Yaqiao, snatched off his felt hat and bent in a deep bow. "Ninth Brother! Don't say you no longer know your own little brother?"

"Dai Chunfeng? So it really is you!" Wang Yaqiao looked the caller carefully up and down, then asked, "And when did you change your name?"

In Wang Yaqiao's presence Dai Chunfeng was forever the soul of deference, guarded in every movement — as painstakingly careful as on the day he had first prostrated himself at the Hatchet Gang's door in Shanghai. He knew that in the years when he was scrounging his meals in Shanghai it was this Wang Yaqiao who had taken him in, and that his gradual rise there afterward owed not a little to Wang Yaqiao too: when Dai Chunfeng came to know Dai Jitao and the brothers Chen Lifu and Chen Guofu in Shanghai, it was Wang Yaqiao who stood as middleman. And of course the reason Dai Chunfeng finally left Shanghai was that in Wang Yaqiao's Hatchet Gang he could never get the standing he thought his due — so in 1925 he abruptly went far off to Guangdong. What set it off was a newspaper he saw in the street one day, carrying a report of Chiang Kai-shek presiding over the opening ceremony at the Whampoa Academy. Dai Chunfeng also saw, printed in the paper, a photograph of Chiang Kai-shek and Dai Jitao together. And so one day he slipped quietly into Wang Yaqiao's private quarters at the Anhui Guildhall, cupped his hands and bowed: "Ninth Brother, I am thinking of going to Guangzhou to look around."

"You want to go to Guangzhou?" Wang Yaqiao could not fathom, then, why Dai Chunfeng — a man who had sworn brotherhood and exchanged cards with him — should suddenly want to leave. But he put nothing in his way. "Chunfeng," he said, "it seems the water in my pond runs too shallow to keep a fish your size. So be it — you want to go, I won't stop you. But tell your Ninth Brother the truth: what exactly are you after in Guangdong? Not going to throw in with Chiang Kai-shek, surely?"

Dai Chunfeng knew that Wang Yaqiao's opposition to Chiang Kai-shek went to the bone, and he hurried to cover himself. "Ninth Brother, you wrong my heart. Chiang Kai-shek and I have never been carts running on the same road — why on earth would I go over to him? I only want to enter the military academy. Perhaps the Ninth Brother knows how it is: you and I are not alike, Ninth Brother. In Shanghai and Anhui you broke open your ground long ago — and what am I? You think well of me, but I know that in these parts around Jiangning a man like me will never rise. So if I am ever to lift my head, the soldier's road is the best one — and that is why I mean to sit for the Whampoa Academy. And of course, if your little brother ever truly makes his way in the army, he will never forget the Ninth Brother who first saw worth in him!"

"Go, then." Wang Yaqiao was an open-handed, big-hearted man. In his heart he was against Dai Chunfeng's going to Guangdong, and he knew perfectly well that the Whampoa military academy Dai Chunfeng was bound for lay inside Chiang Kai-shek's own sphere. But he swallowed the words that would have held him back, and said, against his own heart: "With your talent and learning, worthy brother, the army may indeed be the fitter field for you. Only take one word of advice. Go to the academy, become an officer — I support that a hundred times over. What I fear is that you may one day end up a henchman of Chiang Kai-shek's. If that day comes, there is nothing left for you and me but to part forever!"

"Set your heart at rest, Ninth Brother!" Dai Chunfeng had never imagined Wang Yaqiao would see straight through to his purpose on the spot. In truth, only he himself knew why he was going to Guangdong. It was less that he meant to attach himself to Dai Jitao, the Nationalist elder he had met in Shanghai, than that he was going to offer himself to his fellow-provincial from Zhejiang, Chiang Kai-shek. Sworn to Wang Yaqiao through life and death he might be, but Dai Chunfeng had already seen that a Hatchet Gang forever hacking and killing was fated never to come to much, while Chiang Kai-shek held an army in his fist, and great things would surely grow from that. So he had made up his mind to go south and beg audience of Chiang Kai-shek on the strength of shared native place — exactly as he had once come to Shanghai and begged audience of Wang Yaqiao on the strength of an Anhui ancestry. Knowing full well that Wang Yaqiao was Chiang Kai-shek's sworn enemy, he could only speak against his heart: "It was by your generous hand, Ninth Brother, that I, Dai Chunfeng, came to look anything like a man at all. If I sit for the academy now, it is for the sake of my future — a man does what he must!"

"Good, then — go!" Moved by such apparent sincerity, Wang Yaqiao gave him a farewell banquet, and before long the two men parted in tears. Now the years had flicked past like a shadow, and Wang Yaqiao had never imagined Dai Chunfeng would turn into Dai Li. What he did know was this: after leaving him in 1925, Dai Chunfeng had indeed become a cadet of the sixth Whampoa class, in the cavalry section. Later, the dash the man showed in the ranks — one who dared kill and dared charge — drew Chiang Kai-shek's notice, and Dai Li followed Chiang on the famous Northern Expedition. But Dai Chunfeng did not keep the promise he had made to Wang Yaqiao before leaving Shanghai for Guangdong, to hold himself far from Chiang Kai-shek. Instead, pleading that Jiangshan in Zhejiang lies a stone's throw from Chiang's own Fenghua, he won Chiang Kai-shek's trust quickly on the eastern route of the northern march. Later still, Dai Chunfeng received four characters written in Chiang Kai-shek's own brush: Steadfast Through Every Hardship.

What startled Wang Yaqiao most of all was that from the time Dai Chunfeng followed Chiang north, this kowtowed younger brother who had once sworn before him that soldiering would be the honor of his life took up instead that loathsome wartime trade: the secret agent's. From things Dai Jitao let fall later, Wang Yaqiao learned that along the Northern Expedition's roads Dai Chunfeng, on Chiang Kai-shek's secret assignment, had gone again and again into the five northern provinces — to Tianjin, Beiping, Taiyuan, Zhengzhou and Xi'an — spying out the military movements of the Beiyang warlords for the Chiang house. From that time on, Dai Chunfeng was Chiang Kai-shek's secret agent. Thinking of all this, Wang Yaqiao could not help a wariness hardening in him toward the man, and he said: "Aren't you doing handsomely for yourself under the Chiang fellow? What brings you running to Hefei? Or am I, Wang Jiuguang, to play Zhou Weilong for you this time?"

"Ah — no, no, Ninth Brother, you misunderstand me!" Dai Li had not expected that Wang Yaqiao's tongue would be what it always was: one thrust straight to the vein, and no face spared. He knew exactly what the words meant — the old story of his turning Zhou Weilong at Wuhan. That was after the Nationalists fell into the straits of the Nanjing-Wuhan split, when Chiang Kai-shek sent him to Wuhan to pry out the military secrets of Tang Shengzhi's army. But Tang Shengzhi had long since marked his movements, and just as Dai Li, with the intelligence stolen and in hand, made ready to slip quietly out of Wuhan, Tang Shengzhi gave the sudden order to draw in the net. So Dai Li became a prisoner in Tang Shengzhi's jail. He lost his liberty, and the intelligence he had won fell into Tang Shengzhi's hands along with him. By rights Dai Li should have died there at Tang Shengzhi's hands, but his life was not fated to end: he learned that the prison holding him stood under the watch of a military-police company commander named Zhou Weilong — and Zhou Weilong had been his classmate at Whampoa. Already sentenced to death by Tang Shengzhi, Dai Li begged an urgent interview with Zhou Weilong on the night before the execution, and his talk — the gist being that if Zhou would follow him over to Chiang Kai-shek, a major general's rank was waiting — actually moved his old schoolmate's heart. That was how Dai Li, under Zhou Weilong's escort, fled back to Nanjing with him. Now, hearing Wang Yaqiao touch him up with the Zhou Weilong story, Dai Li understood that his relations with Chiang Kai-shek lay before Wang Yaqiao like an open palm, and he hurried to protest: "Ninth Brother, I am not a man who forgets old feeling. In the early Shanghai days I, Dai Yunong, had no one to lean on and nothing to my name. If the Ninth Brother had not stood on honor then and taken an unknown outlander under his wing, where would Dai Yunong be today?"

Wang Yaqiao watched Dai Li shrink back into the old meekness and timidity, and only then motioned him to a seat and had tea and refreshments brought. "You know that Zhou Weilong and I are not cut from the same cloth — so waste no schemes on me. Chunfeng, if you have truly stopped at Hefei in passing, to look in on me, then we are brothers still. But if you have come as the Chiang fellow's persuader, don't blame me when old feeling counts for nothing."

"Of course, of course." Dai Li had known before ever setting out that the task Chiang Kai-shek had given him was a hard bone to gnaw: Wang Yaqiao was not a man to be won over with honeyed talk. Now, with his mouth sealed shut at the first meeting, he could only swallow down the persuasions he had prepared, keep up a hollow joviality, and fence. "The truth is, Ninth Brother, the bitterness in my heart is more than you can know. Yes, I serve under Mr. Chiang now. But those years with the Northern Expedition, sent running from place to place sniffing after military intelligence — that was a drudge's errand and nothing more. A peddler's lone round, and never what I wanted. Where in that is 'doing handsomely'?"

No one knew his Dai Li better than Wang Yaqiao. Watching the man look left and right and talk of everything else, he knew his thrust had found the sore spot, and he gave a cold heh-heh of a laugh. "Spare me that talk. If you are so out of favor with the Chiang fellow, then what, pray, is this 'Ten-Man Team' of yours? Plenty of people inside the Nationalist Party understand by now what you are to Chiang. That being so, why stand in front of me spinning ghost-talk that touches nothing?"

Dai Li sat frozen, a long moment without words. He had not expected Wang Yaqiao to be as sharp as ever — sharp enough to know even the secret that, on Chiang Kai-shek's confidential order, he had organized ten of the old Whampoa friends, Zhou Weilong and Zhang Yanyuan among them, into a clandestine special-services group inside the Party. Since the other man saw his identity and his errand so clearly, to keep working the stratagem of deceiving heaven to cross the sea was plainly unwise; so he cupped his hands gravely toward Wang Yaqiao. "Ninth Brother, since the talk has come this far, I'll tell no lies before a true man. The plain truth is: this trip to Hefei — yes, Mr. Chiang sent me."

"Oh? And here I thought you had come to look in on me." Wang Yaqiao gave one contemptuous snort of cold laughter. "Good. Frank at last. Dai Chunfeng, let's open the skylight and say our words in the light. I am Wang Jiuguang — walking, I change no name; sitting, I change no surname. I keep to Hefei; old Chiang sits tight in Nanjing; well water need not trouble river water. What business has he, sending you here?"

Wang Yaqiao's tone stayed cold and cutting, and Dai Li thought: better to lay every card face up, then, and see how this Wang Yaqiao answers. "Ninth Brother, you know yourself that I am now foreman of Mr. Chiang's 'Ten-Man Team,' and so certain secrets touching the National Government pass through my hands. Since this spring, among the military and political men of every stripe around Jiangning, an undercurrent has been gathering that bodes very ill for Mr. Chiang. Certain people in particular are brewing a terrible anti-Chiang conspiracy — several columns of men are to merge and are preparing to rise in arms. And so, before Mr. Chiang takes measures to crush it, he has sent your little brother specially to Hefei, to bring the Ninth Brother word! I was, after all, the brother who knocked heads with you in the old days, a man who received the Ninth Brother's great kindness — it was unthinkable that I should know of this and not tell you!"

Dai Li's words fell on Wang Yaqiao like thunder out of a clear sky. They meant that Chiang Kai-shek already knew he was this very moment plotting a rising with Fang Zhenwu, chairman of Anhui, and Shi Yousan, whose army stood garrison in Jiangsu — knew even, down to the last fold, that he had secretly sent his trusted friends Wang Leping and Yu Likui to set the anti-Chiang work going in the Fourth Brigade at Changzhou. For a moment Wang Yaqiao sat stunned. Dai Li's information was simply too exact: plainly, someone had leaked the anti-Chiang plans he and Bo Wenwei and the rest were weaving. Even so, he could not let one flicker of fear show in front of Dai Li. He forced his calm, tipped back his head and laughed. "Well, well, Dai Chunfeng — so you have made something of yourself after all: running to my door to play Chiang Kai-shek's envoy of intimidation! Come then. You say someone is secretly stitching several forces together. Meaning what? I, Wang Jiuguang, am a plain commoner in the crowd. Am I supposed to possess the power to set the armies of every road in motion?"

Dai Li had assumed that one light touch of his words would bring the truth out of Wang Yaqiao, whereupon he would apply the threats and the menace, and so accomplish Chiang Kai-shek's purpose of striking first. He had never imagined that Wang Yaqiao, far from being drawn, would counterattack from behind his own wall — and now it was Dai Li standing in the awkward place. He saw that further urging was useless, and in a few short sentences beat his hasty retreat. "The Ninth Brother reads too much into this. What I had was hearsay and no more. I meant it kindly — I took those rumors for true and came ahead to report, only to counsel the Ninth Brother to walk carefully. With Mr. Chiang, after all, my word still counts for something. If the Ninth Brother really had been tricked aboard some thief's boat, to rein in the horse at the cliff's edge now would still be the mark of a superior man. Should Mr. Chiang truly discover someone scheming against him, he will not smooth things over lightly, as he used to do. This time he will throw open the gates of killing — and what good is repentance then, when it comes too late? But as it now appears the Ninth Brother has not one thread tying him to those military schemers stirring in the shadows, your little brother's mind is at ease!"

That night, giving nothing away, Wang Yaqiao laid on a small private banquet to wash the dust of the road from Dai Li, and the two talked over the old feeling of their eight-bows brotherhood. At first light next morning, Dai Li took a hurried leave of Wang Yaqiao and boarded the express back to Nanjing, to report to Chiang Kai-shek how his attempt to bring Wang Yaqiao over had failed.

After Dai Li was gone, the more Wang Yaqiao thought, the colder his fear grew. It came to him suddenly that the Dai Li who had called the night before was nothing so much as Jiang Gan stealing the letters, straight out of the Romance of the Three Kingdoms. They had once been friends sworn through life and death on the Shanghai Bund, and he had saved Dai Yunong from his destitution — but the Dai Chunfeng of today had shed not only the old name but the old heart with it, and gone over to his enemy, Chiang Kai-shek. The meaning that had leaked through Dai's words was all too clear: somewhere in the work he and Bo Wenwei, Wang Leping and Yu Likui had been weaving in secret — the plan for Fang Zhenwu, Shi Yousan and the Changzhou Fourth Brigade to rise in mutiny against Chiang — a terrible traitor had appeared. Nothing else could explain how Dai Li could say to his face that "several military forces are stirring." Which link in a conspiracy so tightly sealed had given way, Wang Yaqiao could not yet judge. But long years of maneuvering among the political camps along the Nanjing-Shanghai line told him this much: someone had secretly gone over to Chiang Kai-shek, or been bought by Dai Li's "Ten-Man Team."

"If someone has secretly surrendered and informed, then the Chiang fellow is bound to strike first for the upper hand — and someone is going to suffer for it!" When Bo Wenwei received a visibly shaken Wang Yaqiao at his residence and heard what Dai Li had come to Hefei the night before to say, his own mind pulled tight. Fang Zhenwu was new in his post: the will to move against Chiang was in him, but of concrete preparation there was as yet none. Shi Yousan had agreed, under Wang Yaqiao's repeated persuasions, to rise with the rest — but he was a general who swallowed his word as a habit, not a man to lean one's weight on. As for the Changzhou Fourth Brigade that Wang Leping and Yu Likui were working, its commander Peng Jianguo was still in the midst of his preparations. If Chiang Kai-shek got wind of their "Three Kingdoms on One Side" plan now and answered with the quick knife that cuts the tangled hemp, the consequences would not bear thinking of. Bo Wenwei's fist came down on the table, decision made: "The essential thing now is to move — raise the troops at once. That is the only sound course."

"Nothing better!" The same resolve had come to Wang Yaqiao unbidden; the two men had met on it without exchanging a word of planning.

They decided on the spot to drive at once to General Fang Zhenwu's quarters in the city of Hefei and settle with him, urgently, a plan for moving against Chiang ahead of time. But — utterly beyond Wang Yaqiao's and Bo Wenwei's imagining — that very morning at dawn, Fang Zhenwu had left for Nanjing.

"What? Why would General Fang suddenly go to Nanjing?" At the Fang residence, Bo Wenwei and Wang Yaqiao were told that in the night Fang Zhenwu had received a confidential telegram over Chiang Kai-shek's own hand, summoning him posthaste to the capital for an important military conference; at first light he had set out traveling light, only a secretary and a few guards with him. Wang Yaqiao went white and cried out: "It's gone wrong — all wrong! This journey bodes far more ill than good for General Fang. It is Chiang Kai-shek's trap, nothing surer. We must send men after him this instant and bring him back!"

Bo Wenwei only heaved a long sigh. "Jiuguang — too late, too late. By this hour General Fang is long since in Nanjing. Had you the Flying Legs themselves, I fear you could never overtake him now."

On the road back to the provincial Tongmenghui headquarters, Wang Yaqiao and Bo Wenwei suddenly saw a guard step out ahead to flag down the car, and the two of them shouted for the driver to stop. The guard's face was dark as he came up to report: "Word just arrived from Nanjing, confirmed — General Fang Zhenwu has this moment been arrested by Chiang Kai-shek and thrown into prison!"

"What did you say?" At the news that Fang Zhenwu had lost his liberty in Nanjing, Wang Yaqiao's face went white with fury; a mouthful of fresh blood came up, and he pitched to the ground...

### 7. Who Was the Traitor?

When Wang Yaqiao came back to himself, he found he was lying at home.

"Jiuguang — you're awake at last!" Keeping watch at the edge of the bed was a woman of flower-like beauty: Wang Yaying, the beloved confidante of Wang Yaqiao's heart. Wang Yaying's original name was Cong Yunyu, and she was Hubei-born. From girlhood she had yearned toward revolution, and in the Wuchang rising of 1911 she served as a detachment commander in the most celebrated women's organization of the day, the Northern Expedition Women's Restoration Army. Wang Yaqiao happened to be in Wuhan just then, and in the thick of one fierce engagement he saved Cong Yunyu's life. Having lost her heart to him at first sight on the battlefield, Cong Yunyu followed at Wang Yaqiao's side from that day on. In 1914 she came with him from Hubei to Hefei in Anhui, and before long they were husband and wife.

To fuse her own life wholly with that of Wang Yaqiao, that man of great wisdom and great daring, Cong Yunyu had willingly taken her husband's surname and changed her name to Wang Yaying. Now, seeing him spit blood and collapse in one burst of fury at the wreck of the great anti-Chiang rising he had set in motion, she could not help weeping aloud: "Jiuguang, whatever falls from heaven, a man must stand and bear it. If you mean to do the great deed that will astonish all under heaven, then you must have the daring that great deeds demand! How else is a great thing ever done?"

Wang Yaqiao heard Wang Yaying out and gripped her hand hard. "Yaying, there is much in what you say. Only my heart will not rest. Why did this good work of ours have to die in a traitor's hands?"

"And who is the traitor?"

"Whoever the creature is who sold himself to Chiang Kai-shek, I don't yet know him. But sooner or later I will. And once I have my hands on him, his head will be the price he pays for betraying the revolution!"

And while Wang Yaqiao was still racking his brains over Fang Zhenwu's peril in Nanjing, a still heavier blow came suddenly up from Shanghai to Anhui: his bosom friend Wang Leping — elder of the Revolutionary Alliance and fellow plotter of this anti-Chiang mutiny — had been assassinated by agents sent out by Dai Li!

"Heaven! Brother Leping — why should this doom have fallen on you?" Late on the night of February 17, at his house on Avenue Joffre in Shanghai, Wang Leping had been shot dead in a burst of wild fire by agents planted in advance near the Xiafei lanes. When the news reached Wang Yaqiao he rose up in a storm of weeping; grief for his lost friend drove the fire into his heart, another mouthful of fresh blood came up, and he fell senseless on the bed once more.

When he woke, he found watching at his bedside not only his wife Wang Yaying but two mainstays of the Hatchet Gang come specially from Shanghai to bring him the news: Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai. Seeing Wang Yaqiao surface at last from his faint, they all pressed forward with a cry: "Ninth Master, you must hold on! If the sky itself falls, the Hatchet Gang cannot be without its Ninth Master!"

Wang Yaying looked at his haggard face and the hatred blazing out of his eyes, and knew that under this blow his spirit had been driven to the extremity of pain. She hurried to soothe him: "The world's affairs are past foretelling; life is chance and change. Jiuguang, since Chiang Kai-shek's allotted span is not yet run out, let us simply draw the lesson from this defeat. So long as you live, the Hatchet Gang exists. And so long as the Hatchet Gang is not dead and not extinguished, the revolution has hope. Our hand has slipped this once — but the day of rising again will come."

"Yes. I, Wang Jiuguang, am no little man pasted out of paper. And besides, we still have the Fourth Brigade at Changzhou. If Yu Likui and Peng Jianguo can only bring the troops over to us, one thread of hope is left. The Fourth Brigade fights hard. If I go myself to work on Peng Jianguo, and then we march on Nanjing, we can still catch Chiang Kai-shek with his guard down. Why despair of bringing Fang Zhenwu out?"

But when Wang Yaqiao had finished, the people around him, for some reason, made no sound at all. Even Wang Yaying was avoiding his eyes. He could make nothing of it.

"Yaying — what is the matter with all of you? Why the long faces? Has one blow left us unable to rise for good?" Unable to understand why his wife shrank from him, Wang Yaqiao fixed her with a glare and roared out the question. But Wang Yaying made no answer, only stole a timid glance at him and would not let out what she was longing to say.

"Jimin! Hongtai! What has come over you two? Has Chiang Kai-shek killed Wang Leping and struck the whole lot of us dumb?" Wang Yaqiao did not yet know how grave things had already become outside — still less that Chiang Kai-shek was massing troops from every quarter and closing in on the Hatchet Gang's home nest, Hefei in Anhui. Seeing even Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai, men whose faith in the fight against Chiang had never once wavered, now hanging their heads in dejection, he could not hold back: "Why won't you speak? Have you all turned soft-boned cowards? I, Wang Jiuguang, have no love for that breed. I am a man who would rather live standing than die kneeling."

Yet for all Wang Yaqiao's rising temper, Wang Yaying, Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai kept their heads bowed and said not a word.

In that unbearable silence Wang Yaqiao felt a shapeless threat pressing in, and understanding broke on him suddenly. "Yaying — none of you dares look me in the eye. There is something on your minds. Don't tell me Changzhou has gone wrong as well?"

He had struck the very point, and Wang Yaying knew that hiding it longer would serve nothing. She sighed. "Jiuguang, we never meant to tell you what has happened outside — you cannot take another shock to the spirit. General Bo Wenwei telephoned just now to say that on no account must what happened at Changzhou be told you now; everyone feared the rage would break your health, with blow falling upon blow. But you would keep pressing and pressing — ah, as things stand there is nothing for it but the truth. Jiuguang, Changzhou is hopeless too!..."

"What did you say?" Wang Yaqiao saw the tears start down his wife's face and knew the worst had come. His skull rang with a sudden roar, the room swam, and he nearly pitched over on the bed again. This time, though, he brought up no blood. He held himself grimly upright while his face turned from red to white and the cold sweat came pattering off his forehead. From his wife's half-swallowed words he had already sensed how grave the situation was. In fact the rising on which he had staked such hopes — Yu Likui at Changzhou, moving Brigade Commander Peng Jianguo to revolt — had died in the womb as well. He roared out in fury: "Is Peng Jianguo not to be trusted either?"

"No — no, it isn't that Commander Peng failed us. A traitor appeared! Before Yu Likui and Commander Peng could rise, Chiang Kai-shek sent his troops in!" Since Wang Yaying had broken the thing open, Xuan Jimin went on and laid before the anguished Wang Yaqiao, item by item and just as it happened, all the news he and Wu Hongtai had carried from Changzhou.

It had gone like this. Yu Likui, carrying the orders of Wang Yaqiao and Bo Wenwei, had long since slipped secretly into Changzhou, and with him had gone crack killers of the Hatchet Gang — Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai among them. Once they arrived, the work of winning the brigade over ran smoothly indeed. Peng Jianguo, a commander with a patriot's heart, heard his old friend Yu Likui convey the great design against Chiang that Wang Yaqiao and Bo Wenwei shared, and embraced it warmly. "Chiang the Baldhead should have fallen long ago," he said. "The moment Mr. Sun died of his illness he betrayed the revolution in the open and butchered revolutionaries — the public fury of the nation was roused long since. We are soldiers; it falls to us to rid the country of its traitors. If Wang Jiuguang and General Bo Wenwei trust me, then I, Peng Jianguo, am willing to act jointly with Fang Zhenwu. My one fear is that this Shi Yousan cannot be leaned on."

Yu Likui said: "The Ninth Master is going in person to win over Shi Yousan's army — trust him to hold ten parts in ten of the odds. Besides, Shi Yousan has swallowed Chiang Kai-shek's cold shoulder and his elbowing for years. Now is the great hour for toppling Chiang — why would he stay out of it?"

And then, while Yu Likui and Peng Jianguo sat in Changzhou laying their plans for the rising, a traitor suddenly appeared within. On the secret report, Chiang Kai-shek rushed heavy forces from Nanjing against Changzhou. Peng Jianguo and Yu Likui, their preparations incomplete, had to throw themselves into battle at a moment's notice; outnumbered and overwhelmed, Peng Jianguo's troops lasted only a few passages of arms, their casualties terrible, and most of what remained surrendered to Chiang Kai-shek. So the Changzhou anti-Chiang mutiny died in the womb. Peng Jianguo cut his way out of the encirclement at the head of his remnant and fled along the railway toward the Shanghai-Hangzhou line, while Yu Likui was captured in the sudden onslaught by the troops Chiang had sent, carried secretly to Nanjing by special car, and locked away in Tangshan Prison — where he and General Fang Zhenwu, arrested before him, became fellow prisoners.

Wu Hongtai had more to report: "Shi Yousan's army was crushed by Chiang Kai-shek at the same time, and broke apart — and there too the cause was a traitor's information. So all three roads of the anti-Chiang force have crumbled to nothing!"

Wang Yaqiao sat rigid and still on the sickbed. The report of Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai cut him deeply, but this time he did not cry out or faint, and no blood came. He buried the whole weight of his pain and hatred deep out of sight, and said to them all in anguish: "This defeat means Chiang Kai-shek's life-thread was not fated to snap — and that we read the field too brightly, all of us. The first thing we must do now is find out beyond doubt: who is the traitor?!"

Wang Yaying looked at Wang Yaqiao — eyes glaring wide, both fists clenched, hating past all soothing — and for a moment had no answer to give. "Jiuguang, a traitor there certainly is. But since he sold us from the shadows, he is certain to be a man skilled at hiding. The word 'traitor' is not written on anyone's face. How are we to tell you who he is?"

Xuan Jimin could see that Wang Yaqiao ached to seize the traitor this instant and put him under ten thousand knives to ease the hatred in his heart; but hard proof there was none to offer. "Madame is right. We know only that inside the anti-Chiang forces someone was secretly passing intelligence to Chiang. Who did it, for the moment, cannot be traced."

"Wu Hongtai — why don't you speak? Do you know who it is that has been pulling my stage down around me?"

Wu Hongtai shook his head. "Ninth Master, I don't know who did it either."

Wang Yaqiao brooded aloud: "This time, in Hefei and in Wuhu, I sat many times in secret council with General Bo Wenwei over the move against Chiang. The men who knew the inside of it can be counted on the fingers — none but the senior military men party to the thing. General Fang Zhenwu; Mr. Wang Leping; and, of course, Shi Yousan and Peng Jianguo. Beyond them, only the few men closest to my heart — you two included — no more than seven or eight in all who knew what was afoot. If there is a traitor, then, he can only come from inside that circle. And every man of it is a friend I trusted past doubting. So tell me — why would such a man carry my great design to my enemy, Chiang Kai-shek?"

At these words Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai both felt the air tighten, for they too stood inside the circle of Wang Yaqiao's suspicion. The two looked at each other, awkward and wordless, finding nothing to say. Seeing it, Wang Yaying put in quickly: "Jimin, Hongtai — what the Ninth Master says is only the truth of it. The men who knew the mutiny's secrets were indeed all his trusted intimates. And yet the thing has happened precisely among those men. If we do not press this quickly until the water falls and the stones show, then a spy left on at the Ninth Master's side is a bomb that may go off at any hour — is it not so?"

Xuan Jimin answered, open-faced: "Ninth Master, we are men who have gone through death at your back. Never could we do a thing so against heaven and conscience. For us to sell the Ninth Master would be to sell ourselves. The attempt on Chen Diaoyuan — we all had our part in it. Even if we wished to sell the Ninth Master, Chiang Kai-shek would never trust us — for our hands, when all is said, carry Zhang Qiubai's blood!"

Wu Hongtai swore his own faith in turn: "Ninth Master, Brother Jimin has it exactly. With a traitor inside, no protesting of ours can lift the doubt. To my mind, what matters most now is this — send me and Brother Jimin to Shanghai!"

Wang Yaqiao understood their protestations, but the doubts still lay coiled and heavy in him. From the day Dai Li crossed the river from Nanjing to play the persuader, to the day Fang Zhenwu was seized by Chiang in the capital, he had never stopped wondering whether among his own people there lurked a spy planted by Chiang Kai-shek. Nothing less could have brought the great anti-Chiang design he had shaped so long with Bo Wenwei and Fang Zhenwu to ruin on the very lip of success. Now, seeing the set resolve in both men's faces, he struck the table sharply. "Brothers, you take my words too heavily. I, Wang Jiuguang, am not so small a man as to doubt brothers sworn through life and death. Only — this thing happened too strangely. Who was it that stole the very core of our secret? Let me once find him, and I will tear him into ten thousand pieces. This man did not only wreck my design against Chiang; he brought General Fang Zhenwu and Brother Yu Likui to ruin — and, what stabs the heart worst of all, my truest friend Wang Leping died miserably under the guns of the Chiang gang! If I cannot find the spy who informed on us, how am I, Wang Jiuguang, to hold up my head among living men?"

"Ninth Master, that is exactly why I have asked for this duty — to go to Shanghai myself!" Wu Hongtai heard the tears in Wang Yaqiao's voice and the depth they rose from, and he stepped forward a pace, cupped his hands gravely, and swore: "I am certain of it — the traitor is not in Hefei. He is in Shanghai!"

Wang Yaqiao looked at Wu Hongtai, the man of many stratagems, and said: "What makes you see that he is in Shanghai?"

Wu Hongtai said: "It was in Shanghai that Mr. Wang Leping met his miserable death. That night, someone knew in advance that Wang Leping would be returning to his house near the Xiafei lanes off Avenue Joffre — so the agents were planted beforehand along the one road his car had to take. The moment Wang Leping stepped down from the car, the guns spoke. And they fired out of the dark. That tells us the man who reported Wang Leping's movements to Chiang must himself be in Shanghai. How could any of us in Anhui have foreseen that on that particular night Wang Leping would go home to the Avenue Joffre residence?"

At Wu Hongtai's analysis Wang Yaqiao's eyes suddenly kindled, and his hand came down crack on the table. "Sound reasoning! Hongtai, I always knew you carried an uncommon head on your shoulders. If you can find the man in Shanghai who sold Mr. Wang Leping, then he is the traitor who informed to Chiang — and you will stand the Hatchet Gang's man of merit."

Two days later, Wu Hongtai and Xuan Jimin, got up as merchants and taking a few junior disciples with them, left Hefei in secret and slipped — with neither spirits nor ghosts the wiser — into a Shanghai thick with the smell of blood. They found on arriving that most of the friends once connected with the Hatchet Gang now kept their distance, each afraid that contact with the two of them would draw the notice of Chiang Kai-shek's watchers. So instead of putting up at the old Anhui Guildhall, Wu Hongtai and Xuan Jimin rented an inconspicuous little courtyard near Jing'an Temple, lying hidden there through the days and going out to work only after dark. Wu Hongtai was sure the man who had sold Wang Leping was in Shanghai — but on that enormous Bund, in that boundless sea of faces, where does one go to find a single traitor who moves like a ghost? Yet Wu Hongtai and Xuan Jimin proved to have real craft in them. On only their ninth day in Shanghai they marked out a suspicious man — and concluded on the spot that this was the shameless traitor who had washed Wang Yaqiao's whole anti-Chiang design down the river. His name was Zhao Tieqiao!
