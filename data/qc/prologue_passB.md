## Prologue: Chiang Kai-shek · Dai Li · Wang Yaqiao

Nanjing. In the setting sun, Chiang Kai-shek's official residence on Huangpu Road.

A small military jeep came quietly up a lane roofed over in green shade, swung nimbly around before the residence's main gate — slipping past the rifle-bearing sentries out front — and drove in through the side door on the left into that heavily guarded compound. It rolled through the lush green of the back garden, then slowly along a deserted path of gray brick into the deep hush of the front court. Before long the mysterious little jeep braked beneath a small gray building.

The man who stepped down from the jeep wore a felt hat, and the dark glasses on his nose hid a pair of deep, shadowed eyes. He was the master spy Dai Li. He had come rushing over the moment the call arrived from the Generalissimo's aides' office, and as he walked into the deep quiet of the residence, a tightness rose in him at once.

Dai Li stole into the corridor of the small building. Inside all was still, hardly a trace of anyone. Holding his breath, he climbed the spiral staircase with its scarlet carpet, and only at the turn onto the second floor did he discover a secretary waiting there. The secretary came forward to meet him and said in a low voice, "Mr. Dai, the Generalissimo is waiting for you in the inner room. There is a matter of the utmost urgency he wants you to handle!"

Dai Li's heart hammered. This was the first time in his life he had come alone to see Chiang Kai-shek. When he stepped into the vast office, he suddenly saw, beneath the Blue Sky and White Sun flag on the far side, a man in a long gown of gray cloth sitting bolt upright, studying him fixedly — cold, and saying not a word.

The strain showed at once on Dai Li's face. "Principal — you sent for me…?"

Chiang Kai-shek still said nothing. He merely raised a hand and beckoned to Dai Li, who stood stiffly in attendance, too nervous to come closer. Dai Li at once stepped forward and bowed again to the motionless, upright figure. "Principal, whatever the task, your student will carry it out at any cost!…"

"Good. Good, good. Dai Yunong — come here." Stealing a sidelong glance, Dai Li saw that this Chiang Kai-shek was a very different man from the Principal he had once seen at the Whampoa Military Academy in Guangzhou. Chiang was now the supreme leader of the Nationalist Party, and his bearing had grown more forbidding, more gravely composed. "Yunong." The eyes fixed on Dai Li softened. Watching covertly, Dai Li saw the grim-faced Chiang suddenly take a paper envelope from a drawer and give it a shake, and a stack of black-and-white photographs slid into view. Chiang picked one up and pushed it across to him. "Do you know this man?"

Dai Li hardly dared breathe. He took the black-and-white photograph carefully from Chiang's hand. It showed the blurred back of a man! He shook his head without a word.

"Mm. Look at these, then." Chiang pushed the whole stack over to him. Dai Li's heart beat harder still. He hurriedly took the thick sheaf of photographs and studied them one by one. All of them, he saw, had been snapped in secret by someone hidden in the shadows. Nearly every one showed the same man — some from the front, some in profile, but most of them from behind.

At first Dai Li could not make out who it was these stolen, none-too-successful shots had caught. Then, suddenly, astonishment lit his eyes, for he saw clearly that one of the photographs showed the man's face full on — and the contours of that face were deeply familiar. The man seemed to be holding forth in a speech at some assembly, the rostrum of a great meeting behind him. There, sitting stiffly correct, were senior Nationalist officials, all of them familiar faces: Chiang Kai-shek among them, along with other Nationalist grandees — Li Zongren, Feng Yuxiang, Yan Xishan.

"Who is he?" The longer Dai Li looked, the more familiar the man in the felt hat and black gown appeared. It was above all the small mustache on his lip and the black-framed spectacles on his nose that suddenly called someone to mind, and he could not keep the name from bursting out: "Principal — can this be the man who made such a name for himself in Shanghai in those years, the master of the Hatchet Gang… Wang Yaqiao?"

"Damn his mother — that is the man!" Chiang Kai-shek, seething, shot Dai Li a look. "In the old days, when you were down and out in Shanghai — did you know him?"

"I… I had heard Wang Yaqiao's name." Under Chiang's stern gaze, Dai Li could not keep from shivering. He not only knew Wang Yaqiao; in those years the two of them had been on far from ordinary terms. But Dai Li, who had followed Chiang through years of campaigning north and south, knew well how dangerous Chiang could be. If the man had summoned him to the Huangpu Road residence over this Wang Yaqiao in the photographs, there had to be some extraordinary reason behind it. Dai Li hastily swallowed what he had meant to say, and answered, hesitating, "In those days I was just scraping along in Shanghai, poor and desperate. I never had occasion to come near Wang Yaqiao…" "Is the Hatchet Gang formidable?" Chiang asked next.

"Formidable! The Hatchet Gang is one of the fiercest outfits in Shanghai, second only to the Green and Red Gangs. And Wang Yaqiao himself is more capable still. They say even the three great tycoons of the Shanghai Bund — Huang Jinrong, Du Yuesheng, and Zhang Xiaolin — dare not cross him! But why does the Principal suddenly speak of this man?"

"This fellow Wang Yaqiao has become a man who must be removed." Only now did Chiang, through gritted teeth, spell out his intent. "As far back as the Northern Expedition there were those who spoke to me of Wang Yaqiao — told me he was one of those ruthless heroes that troubled times breed, with vast forces at his back, and urged me to be rid of him early. But I didn't take the man seriously then. I thought the Hatchet Gang was nothing but a pack of Anhui riffraff — why waste a thought on them? Since the National Government was formed at Nanjing, though, this person has gone further and further beyond all bounds. Damn his mother — he actually dared, to my face, to curse me, Chiang, for no true disciple of Sun Yat-sen! Is that to be borne? If I go on tolerating his antics, then one day I, Chiang, may well have to yield my place to this Wang Yaqiao!"

"What! Can such a thing be — has this Wang Yaqiao eaten a bear's heart and a tiger's gall, that he dares attack the Principal to his face?" At this, Dai Li drew in a breath of cold air. And suddenly, in one of those snatched photographs, he saw it: Wang Yaqiao speaking at the founding assembly of the National Government, and behind him Chiang Kai-shek, glaring at him in fury. Only now did Dai Li grasp why he had been summoned in such haste. "Then the Principal's meaning is…"

"Yunong, your Ten-Man Team — every one of them I brought from Wuhan to Nanjing," Chiang said. "Until now your task has been nothing more than gathering intelligence. But things are different now. We have established a government in Nanjing, and the Ten-Man Team under your command should exist to protect the National Government. Do you follow my meaning?"

"I follow, I follow." From Chiang's words Dai Li understood that Wang Yaqiao had already gone onto Chiang's blacklist. And yet — he himself could not have said why — he asked a question whose answer he already knew: "Does the Principal mean to send us to gather intelligence on Wang Yaqiao's recent activities?"

"No — not gather intelligence. I want you to find out at once where he is living in Nanjing." Chiang raised his hand savagely and made the gesture of taking off a head. "Understood?"

Dai Li understood Chiang's intent now: he wanted Wang Yaqiao assassinated, and quickly. He knew that after attending the founding assembly of the National Government, Wang Yaqiao had not yet returned to Shanghai. At this secret order to kill, something wavered suddenly in Dai Li. But before Chiang Kai-shek, with all that power gathered in his hands, he dared not show the slightest hesitation. He quickly put away the photographs of Wang Yaqiao, snapped his heels together, straightened his back, and said loudly: "Set your mind at rest, Principal. So long as Wang Yaqiao is still in Nanjing, we will bring you his head at once!"

At that, a faint crease of a smile finally showed on Chiang Kai-shek's face.

Dai Li bowed to him once more, then moved toward the door.

"Yunong." Chiang beckoned again and called Dai Li back. "Remember — the operation must be watertight, and the work done briskly. Above all, give those delegates who came to Nanjing for the assembly no pretext whatever to cry assassination. Do you follow my meaning?"

"Principal, I understand!" Dai Li gave his most reverent assent once more to the Chiang Kai-shek seated beneath the flag of the Blue Sky, White Sun, and Wholly Red Earth, then quietly withdrew.

The night was black as ink. The ancient city of Jinling blazed with lights.

On the dark reach of the Yangtze lay the mirrored clusters of scattered lamplight. Dai Li kept watch alone in the deep little courtyard at No. 54 Ji'e Lane. He switched on the desk lamp and examined, one by one, the photographs Chiang Kai-shek had handed him. Plainly, they were all covert shots taken by agents of Wang Yaqiao's movements in Nanjing. Before his eyes rose a scene of great ceremony: the hall of the founding assembly of the Nanjing National Government. Just as Chiang Kai-shek and the Nationalist dignitaries on the rostrum were applauding warmly, there suddenly came, down a strip of red carpet, a middle-aged man in a black gown. It was the Anhui man Wang Yaqiao, attending the assembly as a delegate of the workers.

"Delegates, gentlemen — today I, Wang Yaqiao, have a few words to speak from the heart!" The moment Wang Yaqiao mounted the rostrum and began his speech, the hall fell silent. Chiang Kai-shek looked at this Anhui man before him — short of stature, rough and countrified in his speech — and felt a stir of displeasure. Then Chiang's face grew uglier still, for Wang Yaqiao began openly saying things that glanced at Chiang and struck at him.

Wang Yaqiao's voice rang out: "Every delegate here knows that the Father of the Nation, Mr. Sun Yat-sen, left us his last testament: alliance with Russia, alliance with the Communists, support for the workers and peasants — the Three Great Policies that we Nationalists are bound to obey. And yet, what cuts us to the heart is this: with Mr. Sun's bones not yet cold, the very men who once called themselves students in Mr. Sun's presence have now raised the butcher's knife and opened wholesale slaughter on our workers in Shanghai! Conduct that so betrays Mr. Sun's testament — how can it not chill the hearts of us who are Mr. Sun's true believers?…"

Applause broke out here and there in the hall; it was plain that Wang Yaqiao's speech had struck an answering chord in some of those present. On the rostrum Chiang Kai-shek sat as though on a mat of needles, his eyes locked fast on Wang Yaqiao, his face iron-gray.

Wang Yaqiao's speech mounted higher and higher: "Mr. Sun has passed from us — but we, his true believers, are still here. Within the Nationalist Party there are still great numbers of true revolutionaries, and they will never consent to see anyone trample Mr. Sun Yat-sen's Three Great Policies underfoot! And so, as the delegate of the workers, I solemnly appeal here today to the delegates of every circle at this assembly: hold fast forever to Mr. Sun's Three Great Policies! Defend the Three Principles of the People to the death! Down with reactionary warlordism! Long live the revolution!…"

At some point, no one knew when, Chiang Kai-shek had quietly left his seat.

When his thoughts reached this point, Dai Li felt a jolt of alarm, and his face changed on the instant. He drew a Browning pistol carefully from his coat pocket and racked it open under the lamp; inside gleamed the bright shine of cartridges.

And just as he was clenching his teeth, steeling himself for the killing, a familiar voice sounded suddenly in his ear: "Dai Chunfeng, you may go to Guangdong, or you may seek another road and rise as high as you please. But you must never forget — no matter when, no matter what — that you were once a follower of my Hatchet Gang!"

"Rest easy, elder brother. Even if the day comes when I, Dai Chunfeng, hold office, I will not forget that you saved my life."

"I, Wang, confer a kindness and look for no repayment. All I ask is that you never forget your roots. You were born of suffering people, remember that!"

"Rest easy. I will remember elder brother's words."

At the memory, the hand holding the Browning gave a sudden involuntary shudder. Cold sweat seeped out on Dai Li's forehead…

Midnight, and Nanjing lay in utter silence.

Suddenly, several police cars packed with plainclothes agents burst out of the secretive compound at No. 45 Ji'e Lane, tore through the pitch-black night into the deserted streets, and raced straight for Hongwu Street. In an instant their dazzling headlamps slit the midnight darkness, and the piercing scream of the cars startled awake citizens long since gone to bed. In the first car sat the "Ten-Man Team" with Dai Li at its head, every man gripping a pistol, every face set for killing.

But Dai Li's mind was elsewhere. Another picture stood before him: the summer of 1916, in the ten-mile foreign sprawl of Shanghai, a little guttersnipe begging his way from street to street, whose name was Dai Chunfeng. A light rain was falling that day, and as Dai Chunfeng worked the streets for food he was struck head-on by a motorcar speeding toward him, and thrown flat on the road. He had not eaten a full meal in days; sallow and wasted, knocked to the ground, his face suddenly streaming blood, he fainted dead away.

Just then a rickshaw came along from the other side of the road, and in it sat a gentleman in a mandarin jacket over a long gown. This was the Anhui man Wang Yaqiao, who had already made something of a name for himself on the Shanghai Bund. When he saw a beggar knocked senseless by a car, and the many passersby standing around him sighing, he leapt down at once and waved over another rickshaw. "Come, lend a hand! We were all born poor and suffering alike — how can we stand by and watch a young man lose his life like this?"

The rickshaw man, once he had Wang Yaqiao's money, gathered the gravely injured Dai Chunfeng into the rickshaw and rushed him to a mission hospital. In that hospital Dai Chunfeng lay unconscious two full days and nights before he was brought around. And that young man called Dai Chunfeng was none other than the Dai Li who had now become the leader of Chiang Kai-shek's "Ten-Man Team"!

In all the years afterward, Dai Li never let the memory of that terrible accident fade. He remembered that after he came to, he had asked the doctor at his bedside, "Who was it who saved my life?" The doctor told Dai Chunfeng: "You were born lucky. If you hadn't happened on Ninth Master Wang of Anhui, that little life of yours would likely have ended then and there. It was Ninth Master Wang whose good heart hired the rickshaw for you, and the Ninth Master again who paid for your medicine once you were brought in. Remember it well, boy — Ninth Master Wang is the benefactor who saved your life!"

"Ninth Master Wang?" Dai Chunfeng did not yet know who his benefactor was.

The doctor said, "What — you knock about the Shanghai Bund and you don't even know Ninth Master Wang? He is the chief of the Hatchet Gang — Wang Yaqiao!"

"Wang Yaqiao?!" Dai Chunfeng was thunderstruck. Nobody though he was on the Bund, living by begging street to street, he had long since heard the great name of Wang Yaqiao many times over. Now, learning that the man who had saved him at the brink of death was the very master of the Hatchet Gang, feared by all who set eyes on him, Dai Chunfeng's despairing eyes suddenly caught light. Wild with joy, he murmured, "Heaven is helping me! I must go find Ninth Master Wang!…"

From that day on, Dai Chunfeng attached himself to Wang Yaqiao's door — first with a thousand thanks for the saving of his life, and then, of his own glad will, as a small disciple under Wang Yaqiao's banner. But the world turns as it pleases, and a few years later Dai Li could never have dreamed, even in sleep, that he would take orders from Chiang Kai-shek and go to Hongwu Street to arrest Wang Yaqiao, the Anhui delegate who had dared attack Chiang before the whole assembly.

"We're here — just ahead is No. 51 Hongwu Street. Wang Yaqiao and his bodyguards are lodged there!" As Dai Li sat in the police car lost in the past, He Zhongyan of the "Ten-Man Team," beside him, suddenly clapped him on the shoulder and pointed in warning into the depths of the pitch-black little street, signaling him to stop the cars at once.

But Dai Li paid no attention. The police car drove on, wailing, straight into Hongwu Street.

He Zhongyan warned him again: "Yunong, if we keep charging ahead we are bound to alarm the people inside — and then Wang Yaqiao's bodyguards will be nothing to trifle with!"

There was nothing for it; Dai Li ordered the cars halted. And in that moment he suddenly spotted a bodyguard keeping the gate of No. 51 Hongwu Street. Dai Li, who had just been fretting over how to escape his dilemma, had a flash of inspiration. Before the men of the "Ten-Man Team" around him could make a move, he sprang from the car ahead of them all, rushed a few steps forward, suddenly drew the Browning from his waist, leveled it at the man keeping the gate, and — catching him utterly off guard — fired. The shot cracked out, splitting the stillness of midnight. As the gatekeeper went down under Dai Li's bullet, a clamor of panicked voices rose inside the courtyard of No. 51 Hongwu Street. Moments later gunfire burst out like beans popping in a pan. The bodyguards, startled from their sleep by the sudden shot, charged into the street together, firing madly at the encircling "Ten-Man Team," and Dai Li and his men fired desperately back. In an instant a storm of lead swept Hongwu Street.

And under the cover of that gunfire, Wang Yaqiao went over the back wall and was gone.

The next day the Nanjing papers all carried the National Government's warrant of arrest, ordering a hunt across the Jiangnan provinces for the fugitive chief of the Hatchet Gang, Wang Yaqiao. And precisely because Chiang Kai-shek had ordered him hunted, the once little-noticed Anhui man Wang Yaqiao became, overnight, one of the most talked-of figures of the Republican era. But how did Wang Yaqiao rise in the first place? And why should he have thrown Chiang Kai-shek into such alarm and fury? All of it must be told from the beginning — from his attempt to assassinate the Anhui grandee Chen Diaoyuan.
