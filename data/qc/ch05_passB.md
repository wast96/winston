## Chapter Five: The Great Assassination on Lushan

### 15. Assassinate Chiang Kai-shek — Blessing, or Disaster?

Having used the affair of the Jiang'an to outwit the three big bosses of Shanghai, Wang Yaqiao knew perfectly well that the three of them had not truly dropped their guard against him, and he knew too that Du Yuesheng was hunting at every hour for a chance to pay him back. So he decided to withdraw into private life in Shanghai and live as a gentleman of leisure, keeping his face out of public view. He bought a small Western-style house at No. 24 Rue Pottier in the French Concession, kept close day after day, went out as little as possible, and stayed always alert to a knife in the dark from the three bosses. In this, beyond any doubt, lay Wang Yaqiao's shrewdness.

But the tree may wish for stillness; the wind does not stop. After the Spring Festival of 1931, an unlikely visitor walked into that quiet little courtyard on Rue Pottier, and simply by arriving, this man threw Wang Yaqiao's newly settled life back into turmoil. From that day on he was drawn once more into an assassination more perilous than any before.

The visitor's name was Li Shaochuan. He had been a senior figure in the Nationalist Party, and in the early days, when Wang Yaqiao first came down from Anhui to make his way on the Shanghai waterfront and found the going hard, it was Li Shaochuan who looked after him and pulled him up. One New Year, when Wang could not even scrape together the holiday pay for the brothers under him, it was the open-handed Li Shaochuan who put out a hand and got him through the year's end. Later, when Wang was locked in his contest with the three big bosses over the Jiang'an, it was this same Li Shaochuan who worked the channels between them until at last Du Yuesheng, Huang Jinrong and the rest had no choice but to concede defeat.

Now it was March, and Shanghai lay under a lid of dark cloud. Spring had come, yet the cold bit hard, and the sky looked ready to break into heavy rain at any moment. It was at just this moment that Li Shaochuan arrived, unannounced and uninvited, and gave Wang Yaqiao, hidden away in the French Concession, a private start — Li Shaochuan was not a man who crossed his threshold lightly. Wang did not dare be casual about it. He hurriedly told the maid to bring tea, then sent the servants out and said to his guest, "A man doesn't climb the steps of the Hall of the Three Treasures with nothing to ask. Is there something today you want me to take care of?"

Li Shaochuan and Wang Yaqiao were close, and he never talked in circles. He glanced left and right, then suddenly dropped his voice. "Brother Yaqiao, do you know a man called Hu Hanmin?"

"Hu Hanmin? Of course I know him. Back when I moved in Mr. Sun Yat-sen's circle in Guangzhou I knew Mr. Hu well. Mr. Sun was Grand President then and Mr. Hu was secretary-general of the presidential office. How could I not know him?" Privately Wang Yaqiao thought it strange; he could not for the life of him guess why Li Shaochuan had opened by raising Hu Hanmin, a man with little enough to do with either of them.

Li Shaochuan sighed. "Brother Jiuguang, do you know that Mr. Hu is in trouble now?"

"Who doesn't know that? The papers carried the news not long ago that Chiang Kai-shek has put Mr. Hu under house arrest. Is there a man in the country who isn't disgusted by Chiang's outrages?" Seeing that Li Shaochuan would not leave the subject of Hu Hanmin, Wang felt himself tighten. He might be the head of a Shanghai gang, but he had always kept his lines open to the men of the Nationalist officialdom. He knew, naturally, that one night a month earlier Chiang Kai-shek had used the pretext of a dinner invitation at Tangshan outside Nanjing to lure Hu Hanmin — then president of the Legislative Yuan of the Nanjing government — out to Tangshan, and had then, without warning, given the order to shut him up there. The country was in an uproar. Political factions of every stripe denounced Chiang's gangster methods, and the anger ran through government and public alike. Wang Yaqiao, a gang chief who had been squeezed by Chiang Kai-shek for years, naturally felt every sympathy for Hu Hanmin's plight; but it had never occurred to him that one day he might actually do something for the man. In those days he was worried that a tall tree catches the wind, and had already resolved to furl his banners and still his drums.

Seeing that Wang Yaqiao still could not guess why he had come, Li Shaochuan let him further into the secret. "Brother Jiuguang, do you know that ever since Chiang locked Hu Hanmin up, Sun Ke has been stirring the anti-Chiang Cantonese officials to resign their posts in the Nanjing government in a fury and go off to Guangzhou?"

"That... I believe the papers had that too." Wang Yaqiao still could not see what his friend was driving at. He knew Chiang Kai-shek was nowhere near as ascendant as he had been two years earlier, when he was elected chairman of the National Government at Nanjing; Chiang had gone from the man everyone rallied to, to the man every official hated. As Chiang's name grew filthier by the day, Sun Yat-sen's son Sun Ke, seeing Chiang imprison Hu Hanmin, one of his father's chief ministers in his lifetime, had in his anger stirred up a whole flock of government dignitaries to fly to Guangzhou, where they formed the southwestern opposition. Among these Nationalist civil and military grandees were not only the famous republican general Li Liejun, but Tang Shengzhi and Tang Shaoyi as well. Their parting of ways left Chiang in a very bad way indeed. Wang Yaqiao looked at his old friend in perplexity. "I don't follow. What has any of this to do with us? I, Wang Jiuguang, am a commoner after all. When two factions of officials go at each other, all we can do is sit on the mountain and watch the tigers fight."

"Does my brother not hate Chiang Kai-shek, then?" Seeing Wang still dodging the point and talking around it, Li Shaochuan went straight at it and let out the true purpose of his visit. "As I recall, Chiang wanted to send Dai Li to assassinate you long ago. Which shows that Chiang will not only harm one Hu Hanmin who dares oppose his dictatorship; sooner or later he will have no room for the likes of you gang chiefs either. Chiang is a born king of murderers. Didn't you say as much to me yourself, long ago? And if so, why not stand up now and rid the people of this scourge?"

"You want me to stand up and rescue Hu Hanmin?" Hearing this, Wang Yaqiao felt a jolt. He had had a strong sense of justice from boyhood; how could he not hate Chiang Kai-shek, who used the power in his hands to crush the revolutionary forces wholesale? But to be asked to step out single-handed and assassinate Chiang Kai-shek — that came at him from nowhere. He shook his head again and again. "It won't do, Brother Shaochuan, it won't do. I take your meaning kindly, of course, and my hatred for Chiang is hardly the growth of a single day. But going for Chiang right now is nothing like Zhao Tieqiao. Who doesn't know that Chiang not only has the Ten-Man Team out gathering intelligence for him, but has set up a new Attendants' Office besides? There are dozens of sharp bodyguards in it, most of them marksmen who could put an arrow through a willow leaf at a hundred paces. Our Hatchet Gang are only rough men good for a brawl. How would we dare go making trouble in Nanjing? Besides, Hu Hanmin's business has little enough to do with either of us. Why should Brother Shaochuan want his younger brother to run such a risk?"

"No, this risk you have no choice but to run. Because I too am acting on another man's trust; I have come here to beg your help with all my strength." Seeing that Wang Yaqiao would not agree, Li Shaochuan understood that unless he told him the whole truth, Wang would never take the field. So he laid out in detail the bitter standoff between the two camps inside the Nationalist Party since Hu Hanmin's imprisonment at Tangshan. "Now that Sun Ke and the others are far off in Guangdong," he pleaded, "they are very close to setting up a National Government of their own and leaving Chiang suspended in mid-air. And Hu Hanmin is the spiritual leader of Sun Ke and the southwestern opposition. With two forces fighting this fiercely, it may well drive Chiang Kai-shek to break the cauldrons and sink the boats. I have come because Lin Huanting, Hu Hanmin's in-law, has had it on solid information that Chiang may put Hu Hanmin secretly to death any day now. You may know that Lin Huanting has been a close friend of mine for years. He came to my door himself to beg my help. How could I look on and let a man die?"

Only now did Wang Yaqiao understand why his friend Li Shaochuan had come so suddenly, and what he truly meant by asking him to strike at Chiang Kai-shek. He thought of all the evil Chiang had done him in the past, and of Li Shaochuan's years of friendship, and something turned over in him. "Chiang Kai-shek is a criminal of the ages, a man ten thousand fingers point at. By rights, for me, Wang Jiuguang, to kill him would be nothing but reasonable. But my brother must understand that killing Chiang is no small matter. If it is bungled, we may fail to beat the tiger and be swallowed by the tiger instead. And Chiang Kai-shek is no longer the little biesan scraping a living on the Shanghai waterfront. He knows now that he has too many enemies, which is why he keeps so many bodyguards about him who are good with a gun. They say a hundred men and more go with him the moment he steps out of doors; he has simply become a modern emperor. Even if my Hatchet Gang men truly wanted to act, I'm afraid we would be no match for him."

From Wang Yaqiao's words Li Shaochuan understood that though he had hated Chiang for years, he judged the hour for striking not yet come. Knowing that as a friend he could not force him, he said, "Brother Jiuguang, I too am acting on a trust; that is the only reason I came to you to smooth this through. Hu Hanmin's in-law believes that the first hero under heaven is Wang Yaqiao, and that you alone can save Hu Hanmin. But you have your misgivings, and that too is only reasonable. I won't press you to act. All the same I still believe that Wang Yaqiao will rid the nation of a scourge, because you are, after all, a Chinese with blood in his veins."

"Very well, Brother Shaochuan." With the thing put to him in those terms, Wang Yaqiao knew he could put it off no further. He thought for a long while and at last said, "This is a matter of great consequence. Allow your younger brother a few days to consider it seriously, and then decide. Will that do?" Li Shaochuan was overjoyed that he had agreed at all, and hurriedly took his leave. "Nothing could be better. Now that is how a hero talks. Only, whether you do it or not, I beg you to keep it absolutely secret."

After Li Shaochuan had gone, Wang Yaqiao sat alone drinking and thinking. Looking at the photograph on the wall opposite — the great Hatchet Gang assembly at Jing'an Temple — he found himself running back over all the perils he had come through since founding the gang. The one that made his flesh crawl above all was the autumn of 1928, when he attended the inaugural meeting of the National Government at Nanjing and Chiang Kai-shek sent Dai Li in secret to kill him. He knew that if Dai Li had not fired a warning shot in time as he led the Ten-Man Team down on his lodgings in Hongwu Street, he might well have died a wretched death that night at the Ten-Man Team's hands. And hating Chiang Kai-shek, he could not help thinking of Dai Li; and Dai Li left a desolate ache in him.

"Elder brother, you saved my life. In this life we could not be born on the same day at the same hour, but I would gladly die on the same day at the same hour with you." What rose before Wang Yaqiao's eyes was the scene in 1924 at the Anhui Guild Hall in Shanghai, when he and Dai Li smeared their lips with blood and swore brotherhood. It was not long after he had pulled Dai Chunfeng out from under a car — this fellow from Zhejiang who had come to Shanghai and lived by begging — and one day the man had simply turned up at his door. By then Dai Chunfeng had shed his beggar's rags and put on the new Western suit Wang had given him, and he looked strikingly handsome and martial. Wang Yaqiao looked up and thought at first he had the wrong man. Washed clean of blood and filth, Dai Chunfeng turned out to have a fine, commanding face, with a certain boldness between the brows. Wang Yaqiao looked him up and down and said at once, "Dai Chunfeng, from your face — you may be living under another man's roof now, but your day will come. If you don't think it beneath you, why not simply move into my guild hall and stay?"

"Elder brother, thank you for saving my life. If the day ever comes when your good words prove true and I really rise in the world, I will never forget what happened here today." Seeing how generously Wang Yaqiao treated him, Dai Chunfeng dropped to his knees on the spot and knocked his head on the floor.

From then on Dai Chunfeng looked on Wang Yaqiao as his benefactor and elder brother. He took Wang's word in everything he did, and soon enough made a name for himself along the ten-li foreign strip. Wang Yaqiao found that although Dai Chunfeng came from the bottom, he was a quick, resourceful man with both nerve and cunning, and he used him everywhere. Sometimes he even took him along to banquets full of men of standing, so that from that time Dai Chunfeng, through Wang's connections, came to know Dai Jitao, Hu Zongnan and others who would later be great men of the Nationalist Party. It was during this Shanghai period too that, after Dai Chunfeng had asked him repeatedly, Wang Yaqiao — who loved making friends — agreed to swear the eight-bow brotherhood with him. But then, quite against Wang Yaqiao's expectations, in 1925 Dai Chunfeng suddenly received a letter from Dai Jitao, who had gone to Guangzhou. It said: "Chiang Kai-shek is building his own strength here, and the Whampoa Military Academy has been founded on Mr. Sun Yat-sen's instruction. Why not come here and train, and give yourself a chance to make something of yourself..."

"Elder brother, if the gangs have less of a future in them than a soldier's career, then I might as well go to Guangzhou and join Dai Jitao?" It was around the autumn of that year that Dai Chunfeng finally told Wang Yaqiao the secret in his heart: he wanted to go to Guangdong and enter the Whampoa Academy. Wang Yaqiao knew that joining Dai Jitao was only a pretext, and that in truth he was going over to Chiang Kai-shek. Though Wang despised Chiang from the bottom of his heart, he did not want to use an elder brother's authority to force Dai Chunfeng out of a military career merely because they had kowtowed to each other. He said to him without hesitation, "We may be brothers, but I never force any man. Every man has his own ambition; go to Guangdong by all means. Only, when you are riding high one day, don't forget the bond we swore. If you can hold friendship high, then I, Wang Jiuguang, will not have known you in vain."

At this Dai Chunfeng dropped to his knees with a thud, knocked his head on the floor three times before Wang Yaqiao, and swore through his tears, "Elder brother, believe me — Dai Chunfeng is no ingrate. If ever my day comes, I will never forget that you saved my life."

After they parted, Wang Yaqiao asked after Dai Chunfeng's soldiering in the far south from time to time. But the news that reached him disappointed him again and again. Dai Chunfeng had indeed entered the artillery course at Whampoa on Dai Jitao's introduction; what Wang had not expected was that during the Northern Expedition he attached himself to Chiang Kai-shek. By the time the expeditionary army reached Wuhan, Dai Chunfeng had even changed his name — to Dai Li. They said Chiang had chosen the new name for him personally. From then on Dai Li was chief of the Ten-Man Team, the secret-service squad that gathered intelligence and assassinated the party's internal opposition, exclusively for Chiang Kai-shek.

"Elder brother, do you still remember me? I am that same Dai Chunfeng who nearly became a ghost under a car in Shanghai!" Around January 1927, after Dai Li had followed Chiang Kai-shek — by then risen to commander-in-chief of the National Revolutionary Army — from Hubei to Nanchang, he was sent on Chiang's orders to Shanghai to make contact with all the forces there. And so he came once more to the Anhui Guild Hall where he and Wang Yaqiao had exchanged pledge cards and sworn brotherhood. The Dai Li who now appeared before Wang Yaqiao was a dashing officer of the expeditionary army, uniform pressed sharp, bayonet slung at his belt. That Dai Li had not yet dared forget the debt to the man who first saw his worth; on meeting he still observed the manners of a disciple. Over the wine they talked with feeling, like brothers long parted, and Dai Li gave the Hatchet Gang chief a lively account of what had happened to him since he left Shanghai, dwelling especially on how he had come to know Chiang Kai-shek on the northern march. Of course Dai Li had not forgotten his real errand, which was to contact political forces of every stripe in Shanghai on Chiang's behalf, and he began praising Chiang to Wang Yaqiao — how Chiang loved talent, how he valued political men of every faction. At last he came out with what he had really wanted to say. "Elder brother," he said gravely, "with your talent and ability you can raise wind and rain on the Shanghai waterfront, but in the end you are only a gang chief. One hand alone cannot clap. You may have the ambitions of a great bird, but you will never make anything grand of it in the end. To my humble mind, elder brother, you would do better to come to Nanchang with me and go over to Mr. Chiang. I promise you that once you are under Mr. Chiang's command your prospects will be boundless. If you play it well, you might even end up a general, or a minister. Wouldn't that be a hundred times better than running a little Hatchet Gang in Shanghai? So my advice, elder brother, is that you..."

Wang Yaqiao thought little of this, but he did not reproach Dai Li to his face for going wherever the milk was. He only shook his head slightly. "Chunfeng, do you think that in the world as it stands, scraping up some post in the Nationalist Party is the highest honor a man can have? It is not. I still believe what I told you when you went off to Guangdong: every man has his own ambition, and no man should be forced. If you have an itch to be an official, go and beg an office from old Chiang. As for me, Wang Jiuguang, from the day I was born I was never the stuff officials are made of. Least of all under that fellow Chiang. He and I have never been carts running on the same road. You had better stop canvassing for him."

Dai Li knew Wang Yaqiao's character well enough to know that high office and fat pay would never move him. Two days of talk got him nowhere, and he had to withdraw with his tail between his legs. As they parted Dai Li again clasped his hands and struck his chest and swore over and over, "Elder brother, our roads may run apart from now on, but Dai Chunfeng is a man who does not forget old kindness. The day you have any use for me, I will spill my brains on the ground before I refuse."

Later Chiang Kai-shek moved his forces from Nanchang to Nanjing and, by every trick of politics, made himself chairman of the National Government and chairman of the Nationalist Party's Military Affairs Commission at once. And merely because Wang Yaqiao said a few words on the platform at the celebration meeting in support of Sun Yat-sen's Three People's Principles, Chiang set out to have him killed; had Dai Li not helped him in secret, he might really have died that night under the Ten-Man Team's guns.

Now, running through all Chiang Kai-shek's outrages since Sun Yat-sen's death and Chiang's rise, Wang Yaqiao was full of feeling. And just now his friend Li Shaochuan had come to his house on behalf of Hu Hanmin's in-law Lin Huanting and begged him bitterly for help, lighting new grievances on top of old. Kill this tyrant, this scourge of the people, and rid the nation of a plague! But when he thought of the attendants around Chiang with their German guns, he wavered again. He was still unable to make up his mind when a woman came quietly in. He looked up: it was his own graceful, quick-witted wife, Wang Yaying.

"Yaying, you have come at exactly the right moment!" Wang Yaqiao's eyes lit up.
