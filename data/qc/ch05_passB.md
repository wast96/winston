## Chapter Five: The Great Assassination on Lushan

### 15. Assassinate Chiang Kai-shek — Blessing, or Disaster?

Having used the affair of the Jiang'an to outwit the three big bosses of Shanghai, Wang Yaqiao knew perfectly well that the three of them had not truly dropped their guard against him, and he knew too that Du Yuesheng was hunting at every hour for a chance to pay him back. So he decided to withdraw into private life in Shanghai and live as a gentleman of leisure, keeping his face out of public view. He bought a small Western-style house at No. 24 Rue Pottier in the French Concession, kept close day after day, went out as little as possible, and stayed always alert to a knife in the dark from the three bosses. In this, beyond any doubt, lay Wang Yaqiao's shrewdness.

But the tree may wish for stillness; the wind does not stop. After the Spring Festival of 1931, an unlikely visitor walked into that quiet little courtyard on Rue Pottier, and simply by arriving, this man threw Wang Yaqiao's newly settled life back into turmoil. From that day on he was drawn once more into an assassination more perilous than any before.

The visitor's name was Li Shaochuan. He had been a senior figure in the Nationalist Party, and in the early days, when Wang Yaqiao first came down from Anhui to make his way on the Shanghai waterfront and found the going hard, it was Li Shaochuan who looked after him and pulled him up. One New Year, when Wang could not even scrape together the holiday pay for the brothers under him, it was the open-handed Li Shaochuan who put out a hand and got him through the year's end. Later, when Wang was locked in his contest with the three big bosses over the Jiang'an, it was this same Li Shaochuan who worked the channels between them until at last Du Yuesheng, Huang Jinrong and the rest had no choice but to concede defeat.

Now it was March, and Shanghai lay under a lid of dark cloud. Spring had come, yet the cold bit hard, and the sky looked ready to break into heavy rain at any moment. It was at just this moment that Li Shaochuan arrived, unannounced and uninvited, and gave Wang Yaqiao, hidden away in the French Concession, a private start — Li Shaochuan was not a man who crossed his threshold lightly. Wang did not dare be casual about it. He hurriedly told the maid to bring tea, then sent the servants out and said to his guest, "A man doesn't climb the steps of the Hall of the Three Treasures with nothing to ask. Is there something today you want me to take care of?"

Li Shaochuan and Wang Yaqiao were close, and he never talked in circles. He glanced left and right, then suddenly dropped his voice. "Brother Yaqiao, do you know a man called Hu Hanmin?"

"Hu Hanmin? Of course I know him. Back when I moved in Mr. Sun Yat-sen's circle in Guangzhou I knew Mr. Hu well. Mr. Sun was Grand President then and Mr. Hu was secretary-general of the presidential office. How could I not know him?" Privately Wang Yaqiao thought it strange; he could not for the life of him guess why Li Shaochuan had opened by raising Hu Hanmin, a man with little enough to do with either of them.

Li Shaochuan sighed. "Brother Jiuguang, do you know that Mr. Hu is in trouble now?"

"Who doesn't know that? The papers carried the news not long ago that Chiang Kai-shek has put Mr. Hu under house arrest. Is there a man in the country who isn't disgusted by Chiang's outrages?" Seeing that Li Shaochuan would not leave the subject of Hu Hanmin, Wang felt himself tighten. He might be the head of a Shanghai gang, but he had always kept his lines open to the men of the Nationalist officialdom. He knew, naturally, that one night a month earlier Chiang Kai-shek had used the pretext of a dinner invitation at Tangshan outside Nanjing to lure Hu Hanmin — then president of the Legislative Yuan of the Nanjing government — out to Tangshan, and had then, without warning, given the order to shut him up there. The country was in an uproar. Political factions of every stripe denounced Chiang's gangster methods, and the anger ran through government and public alike. Wang Yaqiao, a gang chief who had been squeezed by Chiang Kai-shek for years, naturally felt every sympathy for Hu Hanmin's plight; but it had never occurred to him that one day he might actually do something for the man. In those days he was worried that a tall tree catches the wind, and had already resolved to furl his banners and still his drums.

Seeing that Wang Yaqiao still could not guess why he had come, Li Shaochuan let him further into the secret. "Brother Jiuguang, do you know that ever since Chiang locked Hu Hanmin up, Sun Ke has been stirring the anti-Chiang Cantonese officials to resign their posts in the Nanjing government in a fury and go off to Guangzhou?"

"That... I believe the papers had that too." Wang Yaqiao still could not see what his friend was driving at. He knew Chiang Kai-shek was nowhere near as ascendant as he had been two years earlier, when he was elected chairman of the National Government at Nanjing; Chiang had gone from the man everyone rallied to, to the man every official hated. As Chiang's name grew filthier by the day, Sun Yat-sen's son Sun Ke, seeing Chiang imprison Hu Hanmin, one of his father's chief ministers in his lifetime, had in his anger stirred up a whole flock of government dignitaries to fly to Guangzhou, where they formed the southwestern opposition. Among these Nationalist civil and military grandees were not only the famous republican general Li Liejun, but Tang Shengzhi and Tang Shaoyi as well. Their parting of ways left Chiang in a very bad way indeed. Wang Yaqiao looked at his old friend in perplexity. "I don't follow. What has any of this to do with us? I, Wang Jiuguang, am a commoner after all. When two factions of officials go at each other, all we can do is sit on the mountain and watch the tigers fight."

"Does my brother not hate Chiang Kai-shek, then?" Seeing Wang still dodging the point and talking around it, Li Shaochuan went straight at it and let out the true purpose of his visit. "As I recall, Chiang wanted to send Dai Li to assassinate you long ago. Which shows that Chiang will not only harm one Hu Hanmin who dares oppose his dictatorship; sooner or later he will have no room for the likes of you gang chiefs either. Chiang is a born king of murderers. Didn't you say as much to me yourself, long ago? And if so, why not stand up now and rid the people of this scourge?"

"You want me to stand up and rescue Hu Hanmin?" Hearing this, Wang Yaqiao felt a jolt. He had had a strong sense of justice from boyhood; how could he not hate Chiang Kai-shek, who used the power in his hands to crush the revolutionary forces wholesale? But to be asked to step out single-handed and assassinate Chiang Kai-shek — that came at him from nowhere. He shook his head again and again. "It won't do, Brother Shaochuan, it won't do. I take your meaning kindly, of course, and my hatred for Chiang is hardly the growth of a single day. But going for Chiang right now is nothing like Zhao Tieqiao. Who doesn't know that Chiang not only has the Ten-Man Team out gathering intelligence for him, but has set up a new Attendants' Office besides? There are dozens of sharp bodyguards in it, most of them marksmen who could put an arrow through a willow leaf at a hundred paces. Our Hatchet Gang are only rough men good for a brawl. How would we dare go making trouble in Nanjing? Besides, Hu Hanmin's business has little enough to do with either of us. Why should Brother Shaochuan want his younger brother to run such a risk?"

"No, this risk you have no choice but to run. Because I too am acting on another man's trust; I have come here to beg your help with all my strength." Seeing that Wang Yaqiao would not agree, Li Shaochuan understood that unless he told him the whole truth, Wang would never take the field. So he laid out in detail the bitter standoff between the two camps inside the Nationalist Party since Hu Hanmin's imprisonment at Tangshan. "Now that Sun Ke and the others are far off in Guangdong," he pleaded, "they are very close to setting up a National Government of their own and leaving Chiang suspended in mid-air. And Hu Hanmin is the spiritual leader of Sun Ke and the southwestern opposition. With two forces fighting this fiercely, it may well drive Chiang Kai-shek to break the cauldrons and sink the boats. I have come because Lin Huanting, Hu Hanmin's in-law, has had it on solid information that Chiang may put Hu Hanmin secretly to death any day now. You may know that Lin Huanting has been a close friend of mine for years. He came to my door himself to beg my help. How could I look on and let a man die?"

Only now did Wang Yaqiao understand why his friend Li Shaochuan had come so suddenly, and what he truly meant by asking him to strike at Chiang Kai-shek. He thought of all the evil Chiang had done him in the past, and of Li Shaochuan's years of friendship, and something turned over in him. "Chiang Kai-shek is a criminal of the ages, a man ten thousand fingers point at. By rights, for me, Wang Jiuguang, to kill him would be nothing but reasonable. But my brother must understand that killing Chiang is no small matter. If it is bungled, we may fail to beat the tiger and be swallowed by the tiger instead. And Chiang Kai-shek is no longer the little biesan scraping a living on the Shanghai waterfront. He knows now that he has too many enemies, which is why he keeps so many bodyguards about him who are good with a gun. They say a hundred men and more go with him the moment he steps out of doors; he has simply become a modern emperor. Even if my Hatchet Gang men truly wanted to act, I'm afraid we would be no match for him."

From Wang Yaqiao's words Li Shaochuan understood that though he had hated Chiang for years, he judged the hour for striking not yet come. Knowing that as a friend he could not force him, he said, "Brother Jiuguang, I too am acting on a trust; that is the only reason I came to you to smooth this through. Hu Hanmin's in-law believes that the first hero under heaven is Wang Yaqiao, and that you alone can save Hu Hanmin. But you have your misgivings, and that too is only reasonable. I won't press you to act. All the same I still believe that Wang Yaqiao will rid the nation of a scourge, because you are, after all, a Chinese with blood in his veins."

"Very well, Brother Shaochuan." With the thing put to him in those terms, Wang Yaqiao knew he could put it off no further. He thought for a long while and at last said, "This is a matter of great consequence. Allow your younger brother a few days to consider it seriously, and then decide. Will that do?" Li Shaochuan was overjoyed that he had agreed at all, and hurriedly took his leave. "Nothing could be better. Now that is how a hero talks. Only, whether you do it or not, I beg you to keep it absolutely secret."

After Li Shaochuan had gone, Wang Yaqiao sat alone drinking and thinking. Looking at the photograph on the wall opposite — the great Hatchet Gang assembly at Jing'an Temple — he found himself running back over all the perils he had come through since founding the gang. The one that made his flesh crawl above all was the autumn of 1928, when he attended the inaugural meeting of the National Government at Nanjing and Chiang Kai-shek sent Dai Li in secret to kill him. He knew that if Dai Li had not fired a warning shot in time as he led the Ten-Man Team down on his lodgings in Hongwu Street, he might well have died a wretched death that night at the Ten-Man Team's hands. And hating Chiang Kai-shek, he could not help thinking of Dai Li; and Dai Li left a desolate ache in him.

"Elder brother, you saved my life. In this life we could not be born on the same day at the same hour, but I would gladly die on the same day at the same hour with you." What rose before Wang Yaqiao's eyes was the scene in 1924 at the Anhui Guild Hall in Shanghai, when he and Dai Li smeared their lips with blood and swore brotherhood. It was not long after he had pulled Dai Chunfeng out from under a car — this fellow from Zhejiang who had come to Shanghai and lived by begging — and one day the man had simply turned up at his door. By then Dai Chunfeng had shed his beggar's rags and put on the new Western suit Wang had given him, and he looked strikingly handsome and martial. Wang Yaqiao looked up and thought at first he had the wrong man. Washed clean of blood and filth, Dai Chunfeng turned out to have a fine, commanding face, with a certain boldness between the brows. Wang Yaqiao looked him up and down and said at once, "Dai Chunfeng, from your face — you may be living under another man's roof now, but your day will come. If you don't think it beneath you, why not simply move into my guild hall and stay?"

"Elder brother, thank you for saving my life. If the day ever comes when your good words prove true and I really rise in the world, I will never forget what happened here today." Seeing how generously Wang Yaqiao treated him, Dai Chunfeng dropped to his knees on the spot and knocked his head on the floor.

From then on Dai Chunfeng looked on Wang Yaqiao as his benefactor and elder brother. He took Wang's word in everything he did, and soon enough made a name for himself along the ten-li foreign strip. Wang Yaqiao found that although Dai Chunfeng came from the bottom, he was a quick, resourceful man with both nerve and cunning, and he used him everywhere. Sometimes he even took him along to banquets full of men of standing, so that from that time Dai Chunfeng, through Wang's connections, came to know Dai Jitao, Hu Zongnan and others who would later be great men of the Nationalist Party. It was during this Shanghai period too that, after Dai Chunfeng had asked him repeatedly, Wang Yaqiao — who loved making friends — agreed to swear the eight-bow brotherhood with him. But then, quite against Wang Yaqiao's expectations, in 1925 Dai Chunfeng suddenly received a letter from Dai Jitao, who had gone to Guangzhou. It said: "Chiang Kai-shek is building his own strength here, and the Whampoa Military Academy has been founded on Mr. Sun Yat-sen's instruction. Why not come here and train, and give yourself a chance to make something of yourself..."

"Elder brother, if the gangs have less of a future in them than a soldier's career, then I might as well go to Guangzhou and join Dai Jitao?" It was around the autumn of that year that Dai Chunfeng finally told Wang Yaqiao the secret in his heart: he wanted to go to Guangdong and enter the Whampoa Academy. Wang Yaqiao knew that joining Dai Jitao was only a pretext, and that in truth he was going over to Chiang Kai-shek. Though Wang despised Chiang from the bottom of his heart, he did not want to use an elder brother's authority to force Dai Chunfeng out of a military career merely because they had kowtowed to each other. He said to him without hesitation, "We may be brothers, but I never force any man. Every man has his own ambition; go to Guangdong by all means. Only, when you are riding high one day, don't forget the bond we swore. If you can hold friendship high, then I, Wang Jiuguang, will not have known you in vain."

At this Dai Chunfeng dropped to his knees with a thud, knocked his head on the floor three times before Wang Yaqiao, and swore through his tears, "Elder brother, believe me — Dai Chunfeng is no ingrate. If ever my day comes, I will never forget that you saved my life."

After they parted, Wang Yaqiao asked after Dai Chunfeng's soldiering in the far south from time to time. But the news that reached him disappointed him again and again. Dai Chunfeng had indeed entered the artillery course at Whampoa on Dai Jitao's introduction; what Wang had not expected was that during the Northern Expedition he attached himself to Chiang Kai-shek. By the time the expeditionary army reached Wuhan, Dai Chunfeng had even changed his name — to Dai Li. They said Chiang had chosen the new name for him personally. From then on Dai Li was chief of the Ten-Man Team, the secret-service squad that gathered intelligence and assassinated the party's internal opposition, exclusively for Chiang Kai-shek.

"Elder brother, do you still remember me? I am that same Dai Chunfeng who nearly became a ghost under a car in Shanghai!" Around January 1927, after Dai Li had followed Chiang Kai-shek — by then risen to commander-in-chief of the National Revolutionary Army — from Hubei to Nanchang, he was sent on Chiang's orders to Shanghai to make contact with all the forces there. And so he came once more to the Anhui Guild Hall where he and Wang Yaqiao had exchanged pledge cards and sworn brotherhood. The Dai Li who now appeared before Wang Yaqiao was a dashing officer of the expeditionary army, uniform pressed sharp, bayonet slung at his belt. That Dai Li had not yet dared forget the debt to the man who first saw his worth; on meeting he still observed the manners of a disciple. Over the wine they talked with feeling, like brothers long parted, and Dai Li gave the Hatchet Gang chief a lively account of what had happened to him since he left Shanghai, dwelling especially on how he had come to know Chiang Kai-shek on the northern march. Of course Dai Li had not forgotten his real errand, which was to contact political forces of every stripe in Shanghai on Chiang's behalf, and he began praising Chiang to Wang Yaqiao — how Chiang loved talent, how he valued political men of every faction. At last he came out with what he had really wanted to say. "Elder brother," he said gravely, "with your talent and ability you can raise wind and rain on the Shanghai waterfront, but in the end you are only a gang chief. One hand alone cannot clap. You may have the ambitions of a great bird, but you will never make anything grand of it in the end. To my humble mind, elder brother, you would do better to come to Nanchang with me and go over to Mr. Chiang. I promise you that once you are under Mr. Chiang's command your prospects will be boundless. If you play it well, you might even end up a general, or a minister. Wouldn't that be a hundred times better than running a little Hatchet Gang in Shanghai? So my advice, elder brother, is that you..."

Wang Yaqiao thought little of this, but he did not reproach Dai Li to his face for going wherever the milk was. He only shook his head slightly. "Chunfeng, do you think that in the world as it stands, scraping up some post in the Nationalist Party is the highest honor a man can have? It is not. I still believe what I told you when you went off to Guangdong: every man has his own ambition, and no man should be forced. If you have an itch to be an official, go and beg an office from old Chiang. As for me, Wang Jiuguang, from the day I was born I was never the stuff officials are made of. Least of all under that fellow Chiang. He and I have never been carts running on the same road. You had better stop canvassing for him."

Dai Li knew Wang Yaqiao's character well enough to know that high office and fat pay would never move him. Two days of talk got him nowhere, and he had to withdraw with his tail between his legs. As they parted Dai Li again clasped his hands and struck his chest and swore over and over, "Elder brother, our roads may run apart from now on, but Dai Chunfeng is a man who does not forget old kindness. The day you have any use for me, I will spill my brains on the ground before I refuse."

Later Chiang Kai-shek moved his forces from Nanchang to Nanjing and, by every trick of politics, made himself chairman of the National Government and chairman of the Nationalist Party's Military Affairs Commission at once. And merely because Wang Yaqiao said a few words on the platform at the celebration meeting in support of Sun Yat-sen's Three People's Principles, Chiang set out to have him killed; had Dai Li not helped him in secret, he might really have died that night under the Ten-Man Team's guns.

Now, running through all Chiang Kai-shek's outrages since Sun Yat-sen's death and Chiang's rise, Wang Yaqiao was full of feeling. And just now his friend Li Shaochuan had come to his house on behalf of Hu Hanmin's in-law Lin Huanting and begged him bitterly for help, lighting new grievances on top of old. Kill this tyrant, this scourge of the people, and rid the nation of a plague! But when he thought of the attendants around Chiang with their German guns, he wavered again. He was still unable to make up his mind when a woman came quietly in. He looked up: it was his own graceful, quick-witted wife, Wang Yaying.

"Yaying, you have come at exactly the right moment!" Wang Yaqiao's eyes lit up.

### 16. Plotting in a Shanghai Back Room, Whetting the Knives in Nanjing

Wang Yaying, seeing her husband — who had not touched wine in days — drinking alone until his face was flushed, was privately alarmed, and asked, "Jiuguang, what has happened, that you drink like this today?"

With the wine in him, Wang Yaqiao told her the whole of it: how Li Shaochuan had come to plead for Hu Hanmin and to ask him and the brothers of the Hatchet Gang to rid the nation of a scourge by assassinating Chiang Kai-shek, and all his misgivings about it besides. At the end he said, "Yaying, if I followed the wish I have carried for years, I would have had Chiang cut into ten thousand pieces long ago, and only then felt the hatred lift from my heart. He hates me to the marrow too. It is only that killing Chiang Kai-shek carries enormous consequences. The slightest carelessness and his guards will shoot us down instead. That is why I cannot decide."

Wang Yaying understood her husband's difficulty: he wanted to kill Chiang Kai-shek and cut out a traitor for the nation, and he feared everything that might follow. An ordinary woman would have stepped in to talk him out of it, but Wang Yaying had been a woman of large ambition since girlhood. Whenever Wang Yaqiao meant to undertake a major job, he always asked her counsel before making his final decision. Now, facing the question of whether to assassinate Chiang Kai-shek, the great man of the party and the state, even Wang Yaying felt her nerves tighten. She came to the table, poured herself a cup of wine and drank it off, and her pale full face turned the color of peach blossom. She sat down, brooded a moment, and asked him suddenly, "Jiuguang, let me ask you one thing. Do you mean to live out your life as a mediocrity, or do you hope to leave your name in the histories?"

Wang Yaqiao was taken aback and did not answer for a long moment. His wife's deep eyes were fixed on him as though she already saw straight through him. He thought a long while, then nodded, resolute. "A man stands between heaven and earth. Of course I have no wish to be a muddled sack for wine and rice!"

"Then you do mean to accomplish something great?" she pressed.

Wang Yaqiao said seriously, "A name in the histories is more than I would dare claim. But Wang Jiuguang is no spineless coward, no soft-boiled egg. I don't see what this has to do with killing Chiang."

With the wine in her, Wang Yaying spoke what was in her heart. "If you truly mean to be a man who stands between heaven and earth, then what Li Shaochuan is asking of you is your best chance."

"How so?"

"It is very simple. Consider: since Chiang Kai-shek is a traitor to the National Revolution who moves against the tide of history, then if you put a bullet through him, who in this country would not applaud Wang Yaqiao?"

Wang Yaqiao had not expected his wife to come out with anything so startling. After a moment he asked, "Yaying, am I to understand that you have no objection to my firing on Chiang?"

"Of course not. If my husband brings off such a thing, he will certainly become a hero for the ages; there is no doubt about it. Everyone knows that Chiang Kai-shek got the leadership of the Nationalist Party only by trading on his standing as Mr. Sun Yat-sen's faithful disciple. And it is precisely because, once in power, he set about wrecking Mr. Sun's Three People's Principles and butchering revolutionaries, that he has raised the fury of the whole people. So in assassinating Chiang Kai-shek we are not merely saving one Hu Hanmin; what matters is that we cut out a great traitor for the nation!" Wang Yaqiao knew that when his wife spoke she spoke to the point, and that her schemes were a cut above other people's. Now that she had set her heart on the gamble of killing Chiang, it moved him all the more.

"Yaying, well said!" Wang Yaqiao gazed with admiration at the wife who had startled him so, and what she had said left him wavering no longer. He burst out, "If my lady, a mere woman, dares face a hail of bullets, why should I, a grown man, go soft in the heart and weak in the hand before a strong enemy? Even if I die gloriously for killing Chiang, I will have died in the right place. But killing Chiang is no child's game. We must plan it seriously. Until the plan is seamless, we do not move." Wang Yaying was steady and quick-witted both. She backed her husband in stepping forward to kill Chiang while the whole nation hated him, and at the same time she was cool and careful, uncanny in reading what was coming. Seeing that Wang Yaqiao's mind was now set on killing Chiang, she turned about and cautioned him instead: "In great matters, always be calm. Without calm you not only fail to kill Chiang, you put yourself past saving. So do not rush this. Let us first send several parties to Nanjing to learn the pattern of Chiang's movements, and only then strike."

"Good," said Wang Yaqiao, wholly convinced. "We shall do it my lady's way!"

While Wang Yaqiao and his wife were plotting Chiang's death in secret, Sun Ke and his people, far away in Guangzhou, were laying plans to kill him too. Wang Jingwei, a standing member of the Nationalist Central Committee just back from abroad, flew down from Nanjing to Guangzhou as well, and for a time the party's grandees were gathered thick in the far south, the various anti-Chiang forces drawn up in the City of Rams much as Sun Yat-sen had once formed his provisional government there. And while the anti-Chiang factions were planning to establish a provisional government at Guangzhou, in Wang Jingwei's villa below White Cloud Mountain another very great matter was being planned in secret: the assassination of Chiang Kai-shek, and as soon as possible.

"If we kill Chiang Kai-shek now, we not only save Mr. Hu Hanmin's life, we leave Chiang's Nanjing government a body of dragons without a head. And then our Guangzhou can become the forward post of the Chinese revolution." This was Wang Jingwei speaking at the secret meeting on killing Chiang.

Sun Ke was naturally the most eager of all for Chiang's death. "As for this Chiang Kai-shek, this traitor to the Three People's Principles, if my father were alive he too would approve our taking hard measures against him. The difficulty now is that Chiang lords it in Nanjing and has long since made himself out to be Yuan Shikai. He has so many attendants and guards about him, on duty twenty-four hours a day, that an ordinary assassin could hardly get near him. So there is nothing for it but to turn to that Anhui man in Shanghai who killed Zhao Tieqiao — Wang Yaqiao."

Tang Shengzhi sighed. "From what my sources in Shanghai tell me, Li Shaochuan has talked with Wang Yaqiao several times, but the man still has not made up his mind. It looks as though Wang Yaqiao is waiting to see where we in Guangzhou stand before he decides whether to act."

Wang Jingwei, who had once gone to Beijing to assassinate the prince regent, spoke like a professional. "That Wang Yaqiao is slow to move is understandable. When I went to Beijing to make my attempt there were plenty of twists and turns too; and how much more so now, when Chiang has heavy guards about him and is hard to get near? I think that since Wang Yaqiao has already tacitly agreed to rid us of this scourge, he must have the means to strike Chiang down. The problem now is that we cannot go on giving him nothing but verbal promises; we must offer some practical support. Once he has our deposit in hand, he will not go on delaying."

"Quite so, quite so. If we want Wang Yaqiao to remove this traitor and villain for us, there must be a fund for the operation." Only now did it dawn on Sun Ke. "Since we are asking him to get rid of Chiang for us, why not put together a proper operating fund for him at once?"

Tang Shengzhi said, "Our own funds are stretched thin as it is. How are we to pay out an expensive assassination fund?" Wang Jingwei brought his fist down hard on the table. "If it is a matter of cutting off the one head that is the enemy of the revolution, what does it matter if we go a few months short on army pay? And if we cannot raise the sum for the moment, then I will empty my own purse rather than fail to give Wang Yaqiao what he needs!"

Sun Ke at once backed Wang Jingwei's proposal and resolved to give freely. "Good. Since killing Chiang is our most urgent business, let those of us here simply open our own purses. We shall have no trouble making up that two hundred thousand yuan."

Before long Wang Jingwei, Sun Ke, Tang Shengzhi and the other grandees had raised more than two hundred thousand for the operation. After consulting together, Sun Ke and Wang Jingwei chose two of their most trustworthy confidants to carry the case of money in secret out of Guangdong by train, then by ship through Hong Kong, and so to Shanghai.

"Mr. Wang, this is the operating fund the southwestern generals of Guangdong have raised with everything they have. You can see from it how highly Mr. Wang Jingwei and Mr. Sun Ke regard you." When the two envoys came in secret to Wang's house on Rue Pottier in the French Concession and set the case stuffed with banknotes down in front of him, Wang Yaqiao was in the tensest stretch of his own secret planning. Seeing the great sum sent up from Guangdong, he refused it out of hand. "Mr. Wang Jingwei and Mr. Sun Ke think too little of Wang Jiuguang. Do they suppose I have held off all this time because I was waiting for their money? Not at all. It has been a month in the blink of an eye since Li Shaochuan entrusted this business to me. How would you know how many secret meetings we have already held here? Anything Wang Yaqiao means to do, he will do without a single copper of funding. And on the other hand, if the man you wanted killed were a just and upright gentleman, you could put up a million and I still would not touch it."

The men who had brought the money pleaded with him. "Everyone always said Wang Yaqiao was a man of honor. Now that I have seen it with my own eyes, I know the name was well earned. Since Mr. Wang hates Chiang as much as we do, why refuse the fund? You cannot do without it in an operation like this. And if Mr. Wang refuses to take it, will it not chill the hearts of all those supporters in Guangdong?"

"Very well." Seeing the sincerity of his visitors, and hearing how much Guangdong was pinning on the Hatchet Gang, Wang Yaqiao accepted the money in the end.

One afternoon in late May, a fine drizzle began to fall on the old city of Nanjing.

That same evening a passenger steamer from Shanghai came slowly in to the Pukou wharf. The rain pattered down and the whole ancient city lay under a thick black canopy of raincloud. Among the passengers coming ashore was a young man in a yellow oilskin, refined-looking, with a pair of shrewd eyes behind black-rimmed glasses. This was Sun Fengming, who had fled from the northeast to the south and joined Wang Yaqiao's Iron and Blood Traitor-Purging Corps during the Songhu fighting at Shanghai. Now he stood on the wharf in the fine rain and gazed through the smoky curtain of it at Bell Mountain rising in the distance, with Wang Yaqiao's parting words in his ears: "Fengming, we are doing a thing now that will go into the history books. When you get to Nanjing, find out Chiang's movements as fast as you can, and let us act as soon as we can."

"Fengming, we have been waiting for you a good while." Sun Fengming turned quickly to see a small car coming up the muddy lane. Out of it stepped a slender woman: his new bride, Cui Zhengyao. Behind her came a man he recognized at once as Yu Likui, the soldier Wang Yaqiao had sent to Nanjing to watch Chiang Kai-shek's movements.

Sun Fengming and Cui Zhengyao wasted no words; he took her hand and they got quickly into the car, and all the way in he talked quietly with Yu Likui about Wang Yaqiao's orders. Sun Fengming told him that since the beginning of May, Wang Yaqiao and the others had been pressing the planning of the assassination hard in Shanghai, and that they had now reached the final stage. Sun Fengming remembered that from early April he had been going in secret from Nanjing to Wang Yaqiao's little house on Rue Pottier in Shanghai, in company with Hua Kezhi, who led the student-union struggle at the University of Nanking, and had joined the Iron and Blood Assassination Corps that Wang led. It was a civilian anti-Japanese body, and in it Sun Fengming met many men of like mind. Most of them were Hatchet Gang men under Wang Yaqiao; but Sun Fengming, an intellectual by background, found that Wang Yaqiao was not the thug of popular report. Far from being a murderous demon whose name made men blanch, he struck Sun as a man of letters with a good deal of the scholar about him. Wang Yaqiao had made earthshaking bloodshed in Anhui, Nanjing and Shanghai, and had done killings with his own hands; yet when Sun Fengming came to know him he found him full of human warmth. One day the two of them went to eat at the Lao Zhengxing restaurant, and over the table Wang talked at length about all sorts of things that had nothing to do with killing. On the customs and character of the Jiangnan country in particular Wang Yaqiao was thoroughly at home, running through it all as if counting over family treasures, and Sun Fengming, who had been a soldier, came away feeling that Wang Yaqiao was a kindly elder.

Ever since Hua Kezhi introduced him to Wang Yaqiao, Sun Fengming had understood that Wang's assassination corps was in truth a body of patriots. Whenever Wang Yaqiao came into a substantial sum he never kept it for himself, but divided it among the brothers of the Traitor-Purging Corps. At the New Year and the festivals he even paid these young men who had thrown themselves into the resistance a special living allowance. And so, with the nation in peril, Sun Fengming had found a progressive assassination organization he was willing to give his life for. What excited him most came this spring, when Hua Kezhi gave him an important assignment. "Fengming, Mr. Wang may be about to take major action against Chiang Kai-shek. Do you dare take part?"

"Does he really mean to assassinate the tyrant and scourge of the people? Dare? Why should I not dare?"

"Mr. Wang has already made up his mind."

"Wonderful. If we can really be rid of this traitor to the country, I would spill my blood and my head for it and count the cost as nothing."

Sun Fengming had longed for revolution since he was a boy and burned for Sun Yat-sen's Three People's Principles; he had long hated Chiang Kai-shek's outrages with a deep hatred, and to learn that Wang Yaqiao was secretly plotting to kill Chiang set his blood boiling.

Through April, Sun Fengming and Hua Kezhi went to Wang's house on Rue Pottier almost every day. More than twenty patriots gathered there, among them Communists like Chen Wang. With the changing times, Wang Yaqiao's assassination organization had taken in many men from outside the Hatchet Gang. Among the patriotic young men Sun Fengming met at Wang Yaqiao's house were Zheng Baozhen, Yu Likui, Gong Chunpu, He Guangpo, Zheng Shaocheng, Liu Wencheng, Zheng Hailong, Xie Wenda and others. All of them felt deeply for Sun Yat-sen's Three People's Principles, and all of them were outraged at Chiang Kai-shek's butchery of the workers in Shanghai.

"Now we must turn the plan into action at once, and the sooner the better." Wang Yaqiao held secret meetings at home every night.

"There are only two roads to killing Chiang. One is to kill him in Nanjing; the other is to blow up his special train on the road when he goes out to Nanchang or Lushan. There is no third way. From nearly a month of watching from our post in Nanjing, Chiang keeps close and goes out rarely, and a chance to strike is extremely hard to find. The only routes he travels are between Nanjing and Lushan. We can only set the operation inside that ground." This was Yu Likui, the soldier Wang Yaqiao had sent to Nanjing to watch Chiang's movements.

"But going for Chiang in Nanjing is no easy thing either." Hua Kezhi was another key man in the plan to kill Chiang; years of running the student movement in Nanjing had made him steady and seasoned. "The most obvious problem is that Chiang has doubled the guard of the Attendants' Office, and these attendants scarcely leave his side from one day to the next. The moment Chiang steps out of the Huangpu Road residence, that long motorcade comes trailing after him. What makes it so hard is that Chiang's convoy runs twelve cars, large and small, and there is no way to know which one Chiang is riding in on any given trip. And the instant our gunmen so much as approached the convoy, the attendants inside would open up with their German pistols and rifles and lay down layer on layer of interlocking fire. We have nothing like that firepower to beat well-trained men."

Yu Likui added, "As for striking inside Chiang's residence, that is out of the question altogether. I have gone and looked over the neighborhood of that compound several times myself, and it is not just that you cannot go in the front gate; you cannot even get near the wall. There is no way to know the layout inside, and even if we could get in, we would never find Chiang's quarters, because we should certainly fall into the surveillance net the Attendants' Office has laid. So the only chance in Nanjing is when Chiang is about to leave the city on an inspection tour, at the Ming Palace airfield."

"Good, Likui. Go to Nanjing now and rent a house near the Ming Palace airfield, to be used when we move." In the end Wang Yaqiao approved the plan they had all agreed on: to take advantage of Chiang's departure from Nanjing and open fire on his car at the Ming Palace airfield, where his escort would be small.

Now Sun Fengming had come from Shanghai to Nanjing carrying that secret order. He and Yu Likui went to the rented house near the Ming Palace, a small gray Japanese-style building, and found seven or eight gunmen already lying up inside: all members of the special action group Yu Likui had put together in Nanjing. Among them were early Hatchet Gang men like Xuan Jimin, Wang Ganting and Niu Anru, and also newer strength Wang Yaqiao had developed in Shanghai, such as Zheng Shaocheng and He Guangpo.

"Here is the operating fund and the weapons." Sun Fengming set the heavy leather case down in front of Yu Likui and the others and delivered Wang Yaqiao's orders in one breath. "Guangdong is pressing us hard now. Mr. Wang wants us to strike at Chiang no later than the beginning of June. If we go on delaying, we may well lose the best moment. So he wants us to look for our opening at once."

The day after Sun Fengming arrived in Nanjing with Wang Yaqiao's orders, Yu Likui learned a piece of intelligence of the first importance from his contact inside the Attendants' Office: Chiang Kai-shek had decided to go to Lushan in Jiangxi at the beginning of June to escape the summer heat!

To the action group sitting in that house near the Ming Palace waiting for its moment, this was nothing short of wonderful news. Yu Likui and the others understood perfectly that if Chiang were going from Nanjing to Lushan, he would certainly take his private plane from the Ming Palace airfield; and when he did, their special action group could slip in close at that airfield, where few planes came and went at the best of times. Then, the moment Chiang stepped out of his car, they could fire on him at short range and drop him before he boarded!

"Now everything is ready; we want only the east wind." Listening to Yu Likui and the others lay out the ambush plan, Sun Fengming was tremendously excited. That same night he took Yu Likui's sealed report and slipped back to Shanghai, where Wang Yaqiao and Hua Kezhi were waiting for the operational plan he was bringing from Nanjing.

### 17. Guns Hidden Inside Nine Jinhua Hams

Lushan, at the southern end of Jiujiang on the shore of Lake Poyang, shows its full grandeur and bulk when high summer comes.

When Hua Kezhi brought a party of hard-picked assassins up from Jiujiang onto Lushan, disguised as tourists going up the mountain, it was the cool season of light mountain breezes. He and his eight special action men were all in Western suits and polished shoes, elegant to look at; they took soft sedan chairs through the Three Gorges Cave, past Guanyin Bridge, and made straight for Hanpokou. All the way he sat in the swaying, springing mountain chair with the look of a man drinking in the scenery in perfect contentment, while inside he was strung tight as a wire. He had come to Lushan to assassinate Chiang Kai-shek.

"Mr. Hua, I have decided to strike at Chiang along two lines. Do you dare go up to Lushan and carry out this assassination?" That unforgettable midnight two days before, in the dim-lit little Western house in the Shanghai French Concession, the room thick with smoke: a dozen and more of the core men of the plot gathered around Wang Yaqiao. Sun Fengming had brought back from Nanjing the precise intelligence that Chiang Kai-shek would go up Lushan on 6 June, and Wang Yaqiao called a secret meeting that same night to work through the operation about to begin. Hua Kezhi had not expected Wang Yaqiao to hand him the Lushan assignment; from Wang's grave expression he could see it was a mark of trust. Hua Kezhi had long felt every confidence about carrying out the killing, and when he saw Wang Yaqiao and all the members of the assassination corps turn their eyes on him, his blood came up. "Rest easy, Ninth Brother. If Hua Kezhi gets onto Lushan, I will kill this traitor to the country. And if I cannot kill Chiang Kai-shek, I would rather he killed me!"

Wang Yaqiao was delighted. "Good, Mr. Hua. Now that is my kind of man. Only there is risk on every side of this. We shall rise in two parties. One is Yu Likui and Zheng Baozhen with the first group, acting in Nanjing. They will lie in wait at the Ming Palace airfield; if Chiang is finished off there, then you come down off Lushan as soon as you hear. Second, if Zheng Baozhen and Yu Likui miss him in Nanjing, then it all rests on you finding your chance on Lushan, because by then Chiang will have gone up the mountain safely. You must get your men into position before Chiang goes up. Otherwise, once he and Soong Mei-ling are on the mountain, you can forget about getting in at all. So you must leave for Jiujiang at once, climb Lushan as fast as you can, and wait for word from me in Shanghai before deciding whether to act."

Hua Kezhi said, "In that case the sooner the better. I shall go to Lushan now and hide myself, and act the moment Chiang is on the mountain. I only wonder which of the brothers will be coming with me?"

"Mr. Hua, I'll go!" "I'll go too!" "Ninth Master, let me go up the mountain — I have been waiting for this day!" The men of the assassination corps, who had sat in the lamplight fretting for days, saw that Wang Yaqiao had named Hua Kezhi his commander, and to a man they rubbed their fists and clamored for the job.

"Now that is how my brothers should be." Wang Yaqiao himself named eight of the assassins — Chen Cheng, Zhao Shifa, Long Lin, Tang Ming, Zhang Xianting and the rest — to leave Shanghai with Hua Kezhi that same night.

Hua Kezhi accepted the command gladly, but not without misgivings. "With this in view, Chen Cheng and I quietly went up to Lushan a month ago. We found that the Meilu villa where Soong Mei-ling and Chiang Kai-shek spend the summer has almost nowhere around it for a man to hide. There are no inns for ordinary travelers anywhere near the villas of those senior Nationalist officials. So we can only lie up around Taiyi Village; there are some middling and cheap inns near Guling Street."

Chen Cheng was a quick assassin with a head for calculation. On that earlier trip up Lushan with Hua Kezhi he had quietly reconnoitered the whole perimeter of the high villa district, and had found the Meilu the least approachable house of all: Nationalist secret agents watched it from every side and a stranger could hardly get near it. Having reported the lie of the ground around the Meilu, he said, "In my view, striking at the Meilu is next to impossible. Even if we could get close to it once we were on the mountain, we could hardly break through the three lines of defense the Attendants' Office has laid in front of it. So the best way is to strike at Chiang and Madame Soong on the road, when they leave the Meilu to go sightseeing on the mountain. Those mountain paths are the best place for it."

Hua Kezhi agreed. "Chen Cheng's idea is a very good one. Once Chiang is on Lushan he is not going to shut himself up in the Meilu every day. The moment he comes out, we shall find our chance to shoot. The mountain is nothing but twisting paths. He may bring plenty of attendants, but on those paths they will have very little room to work. And then, from cover, we can hit our target. The problem now is how to get our weapons up Lushan, when there are checkpoints practically the whole way."

Wang Yaqiao heard out Hua Kezhi's and Chen Cheng's scheme for the ambush on Lushan with great approval. But when the talk turned to how to send guns and ammunition up the mountain, he too found himself at a loss. Hua Kezhi, Chen Cheng and the rest, nine men going up, would need at least eight or nine pistols. Yet Lushan had always been a forbidden zone where the great men of the Nationalist government and army passed the summer, so ordinary tourists who obtained a special pass to go up were rare as phoenix feathers, and with checkpoints all along the road it was next to impossible to carry guns in secret. Wang Yaqiao had worked his Nanjing connections and managed, in the end, to get nine passes for Lushan; but no amount of contriving would get guns up there. Ever since Chiang Kai-shek became chairman of the National Government and started taking Soong Mei-ling up Lushan every high summer, layer upon layer of tight military and police checkpoints had been posted along the road from Jiujiang. The agents made a specialty of searching tourists' luggage, and did not spare even the women; how much more conspicuous, then, were nine men like Hua Kezhi and his party, and how was their baggage to get past a dozen and more posts?

"It is a real problem. But you men just go up the mountain." Seeing Hua Kezhi and the others tying themselves in knots over the impossibility of carrying guns up, and knowing that no solution was going to come to them on the spot, Wang Yaqiao said, "As for how the guns get up there, leave it to me to think of something. In short, Mr. Hua goes up Lushan tomorrow and makes camp. I guarantee that before the operation I will send someone up with the guns and ammunition, on time."

Now, when Hua Kezhi's mountain chair reached the head of Guling Street, he ordered it set down, walked into a wineshop by the road and sat. Before long Chen Cheng, Zhao Shifa, Long Lin, Tang Ming, Zhang Xianting and the rest of the eight came in through the door as well. Hua Kezhi ordered a few dishes of seasonal fruit and delicacies to begin, and the nine of them sat around a round table drinking. As he ate, Hua Kezhi kept a careful eye on the street outside, and noticed the figures of secret agents passing along Guling Street from time to time. Quietly he said to Chen Cheng, Zhao Shifa and Long Lin beside him, "When we have drunk up, we split and go up separately. I noticed just now that our party has already drawn the sentries' attention on the road up from Jiujiang. We did not think this through before we came. Consider it: whoever heard of nine men going sightseeing up a mountain together? And we are all carrying Hong Kong passports besides. It is far too conspicuous."

Chen Cheng and Long Lin, thinking back to the many suspicious looks they had met coming up, agreed that nine men made too obvious a target. Hua Kezhi set down his cup. "In a while Chen Cheng, Long Lin and I will take chairs straight up to Taiyi Peak. The rest of you go up separately, and best on foot. That way the target is scattered and will not rouse suspicion up the mountain. And the Taibai Hotel that Chen Cheng and I booked below Taiyi Peak last time cannot be used any more. The best thing is for us to lodge in three separate small inns. Otherwise a tall tree catches the wind, and we would be asking for trouble when the mountain police make their night rounds."

"Right. Better to break the whole into parts and move separately." Chen Cheng thought Hua Kezhi's arrangement entirely sound.

"Remember: once we have taken rooms at the inns below Taiyi Peak, on no account contact each other openly by day. Watch for plainclothes sentries." Hua Kezhi divided them into three parties, with himself, Chen Cheng and Long Lin as group leaders, and picked three inns near the summit to stay at. He also fixed the signals and the meeting places by which the three groups would keep in touch. About half an hour later they had hurried through their midday meal and set off separately along Guling Street toward the mountain road that led to Taiyi Peak.

In Shanghai the rain fell without a break.

Since Hua Kezhi and the other eight had hurried off to Lushan, Wang Yaqiao had spent whole days in his French Concession house racking his brains over how to get nine German pistols secretly up the mountain. And just as he was at his wits' end, the slender, lovely figure of his wife came in at the door again. She was as serene and clear-faced as ever, not altered in the least by the killing her husband was plotting day and night. That instinctive unflappability of hers Wang Yaqiao much admired. Now, seeing his brow knotted, she asked, "Jiuguang, they have all gone up the mountain. Is something still troubling you?"

"Ah, it is a hard business." He had no choice but to lay out in full the impossibility of getting the weapons up Lushan. Wang Yaying listened, then stood a long while thinking at the French window, until suddenly her beautiful eyes flickered and she cried, "I have it! Yaqiao, this is no great difficulty for me. Why did you not tell me sooner?"

"Does my lady really have a way?"

"Of course there is a way. Simply let me go up Lushan myself."

"You yourself? There is a gate of hell on that mountain! It won't do; you absolutely must not go. Lushan is a place of trouble now. You are a woman. How could you take such a risk, going up with weapons?"

Seeing Wang Yaqiao shaking his head and waving his hands in agitation, Wang Yaying could not help laughing. "What of it that I am a woman? Does Wang Yaying not dare face a hail of bullets? Jiuguang, I have followed you all these years. Do you still doubt my nerve?"

"It is not your nerve I doubt," said Wang Yaqiao. "It is that carrying eight or nine pistols up that mountain is no child's game. If a gun were found on you, it would cost you your head! Understand that the men guarding that mountain for Chiang Kai-shek and the Nationalist grandees search even a woman's underclothes these days. And you cannot do Sun Wukong's seventy-two transformations. How would you get those killing tools up there? You absolutely must not."

Wang Yaying laughed coldly. "Jiuguang, I have to laugh at you men. All you can do is puff yourselves up with pride, and you have not a scrap of a woman's quickness. However hard the soldiers and police at the checkpoints search, there is always a place they cannot search."

Seeing his wife speak with such conviction, and knowing that Yaying never talked idly, Wang Yaqiao asked quickly, "What is your idea? Let me hear it. If it will really work, I shall agree to your going up. But if you have no extraordinary trick, don't go showing off and taking risks. It would be playing games with your own head."

Wang Yaying smiled a smile past fathoming, then struck her breast. "Jiuguang, do as I tell you and nothing will go wrong. First send someone to buy eight or nine Jinhua hams. I shall have a use for them!"

"What do you want Jinhua hams for? Is this a joke?"

"A Jinhua ham is a fine thing indeed. Just buy me the best there are, and when the time comes I shall have my use for them." Knowing his wife's exceptional cleverness, Wang Yaqiao pressed her no further, and sent men off to Jinhua in Zhejiang at once; they brought back nine hams overnight, superb ones, beautifully colored.

Wang Yaying took the nine glossy hams in her hands, looked each one over, and sent the servants away. Then she found a sharp knife and, working quickly, cut the meat out of the hams stroke by stroke. Then she quietly stripped down the nine German pistols Wang Yaqiao brought her; in no time at all they were a scatter of parts, which she wrapped carefully in oiled cotton and packed into the hollowed hams, dividing them among the nine. Then, with needle and thread, she sewed up every ham she had opened with fine close stitches, and sealed the seams over with oiled clay. When all this was done she set the nine hams out on the table for Wang Yaqiao to see. In the lamplight the nine hams gleamed with a rich, appetizing sheen, and not a flaw showed anywhere.

"Good heavens, Yaying, so you have been doing conjuring tricks!" Wang Yaqiao saw the beauty of his wife's scheme at once and clapped his hands again and again. "Marvelous, marvelous! Only, carrying those hollowed-out hams up Lushan is no small matter either. A person with any less nerve would not dare do it."

Wang Yaying clearly had it all worked out. "I have said it already: I shall take these weapons up Lushan myself, the whole lot of them. But I shall need a quick-witted woman assistant with me."

"Ah, so you mean to go up Lushan in the character of a travelling lady scholar? Then of course you would need a maidservant at your side."

"No, I am not a lady scholar. I mean to go up as the wife of a Nationalist dignitary."

By now Wang Yaqiao saw his wife's whole clever design. A person came suddenly to mind. "Yaying, let Yafeng go up Lushan with you. How would that be?"

Wang Yafeng was Wang Yaqiao's own younger sister, who had come to Shanghai from Anhui by this time. The suggestion suited Wang Yaying exactly. Wang Yafeng had studied at the Hefei Girls' High School; she was well read and well mannered, quick and clever and with a firm grasp of the great principles, and had long hated Chiang Kai-shek's outrages. So Wang Yaying nodded again and again. "Nothing could be better than Yafeng going up with me. But to make me a genuine official's lady in the eyes of those soldier-ruffians, I had best have two or three orderlies attending me as well. Then I guarantee I can take these hams up the mountain without the slightest trouble."

Wang Yaqiao too felt that his wife's method of sending the guns up was ingenious and safe. He immediately detailed Sun Fengming and two other members of the assassination corps to go up with Wang Yaying and Wang Yafeng. Two days later Wang Yaying took Wang Yafeng, Sun Fengming and the rest of the party by ship from Shanghai straight to Jiujiang, and from the Jiujiang wharf went up Lushan.

All the way up, she and Yafeng rode in hired mountain chairs. Wang Yaying wore a close-fitting purple-red qipao and red high-heeled leather shoes, with a snow-white Panama lady's sunhat on her head; with her clear, dignified beauty and elegant bearing, she looked every inch the great lady of some high official. Wang Yafeng, travelling with her, went lightly made up and plainly dressed, exactly like a second concubine. Sun Fengming played the accompanying aide-de-camp — Western suit, polished shoes, refined manner — running to and fro and busy every minute. The other two gunmen shouldered two cases of luggage and sweated their way up. Before the police and agents at every checkpoint they all bore themselves with perfect composure. In the eyes of these Nationalist soldiers, only rich ladies and concubines like Wang Yaying and Wang Yafeng had any business going up Lushan, that summer resort thick with officialdom and clouds of women. And that their luggage held first-class Jinhua hams naturally roused no interest in the sentries and police, since it was entirely reasonable that they should bring gifts up the mountain. Who would have imagined that guns and ammunition were hidden inside the hams?

When the Nationalist soldiers manning the checkpoints wanted to search Wang Yaying's luggage, she said grandly from her chair, "Let them look. There is certainly no opium and no pistols among my things. What is there to be afraid of?"

Sun Fengming was tense at first; but when the sentries opened the two trunks, what they found astonished them: letters in the hands of Chiang Kai-shek, Feng Yuxiang, Zhang Xueliang and Soong Mei-ling addressed to the lady of the house, along with fine women's cosmetics and jade and jewelry of the sort only overseas Chinese from the South Seas have, all of it dazzling to the eye. The soldiers did notice eight or nine glistening Jinhua hams among the rest, which struck them as odd, but they took them for gifts the lady was carrying up to some grandee, and not one of them dared rummage or look further. And so the party went through without a hitch.

Before long Wang Yaying's party had passed along the main street in front of the little town of Guling and were climbing the twisting mountain road straight up toward Taiyi Peak. Sun Fengming did not let out his breath until they were near the top. He noticed then that sweat had beaded on Wang Yaying's forehead too. As for Wang Yafeng, who had come up with them, she was pale with fright. But they all knew they had got through the gate of hell at last.

### 18. Hua Kezhi Musters His Men on Lushan

Nanjing was fiercely hot; once June came it had turned into a great furnace where a man could hardly draw breath.

Yu Likui and his action group stayed hidden in a residential district not far from the Ming Palace airfield. On 3 June — the same day Wang Yaying, Sun Fengming and the others made their dangerous climb up Lushan with the weapons for the assassination — Wang Yaqiao sent Zheng Baozhen in secret from Shanghai to Nanjing to tell Yu Likui and the rest that Hua Kezhi, Chen Cheng and their seven companions were on Lushan awaiting orders, and that Wang Yaying, Wang Yafeng, Sun Fengming and the others had gone up the mountain to support the operation.

"Of course, the Ninth Master still hopes you succeed in Nanjing." Having passed on Wang Yaqiao's orders, Zheng Baozhen added, "Because Lushan carries far more risk. In Nanjing you need only a few shots at long range as Chiang boards the plane and it will finish him, and then everyone can scatter and get away easily; but after an assassination on Lushan, getting down the mountain is dangerous in itself."

Yu Likui was greatly taken with the new weapons Zheng Baozhen had brought to Nanjing. The dumdum guns Wang Yaqiao had bought in Shanghai through foreigners had a special effectiveness at long range; once one of their dumdum bullets struck a man's body, poison would spread through him and kill him within half an hour.

On 6 June, in the first light of dawn, Zheng Baozhen, Yu Likui and their action group were already in the firing position they had chosen beforehand: a thick stand of maples on a sunlit slope at the foot of Purple Mountain. They lay hidden deep among the trees, from which they could look down on the runway of the Ming Palace airfield. If the intelligence from the Attendants' Office of the chairman of the National Government was accurate, then at nine that morning Chiang Kai-shek, his wife Soong Mei-ling and their party would take off from here for Lushan in Jiangxi to escape the heat.

As the light strengthened, Yu Likui and the others saw that a passenger plane was indeed standing on the runway, and recognized it as Chiang Kai-shek's own aircraft, the Meiling. At the sight of it their blood came up, because in a moment they would be firing on old Chiang from here with their dumdum guns. Once one of those heavy-hitting dumdum bullets came flying out of the dense wood at Chiang as he boarded, it was easy enough to imagine: the great man of the age would become a ghost under the bullet in an instant!

But the minutes and the seconds went by and the plane showed not the slightest sign of taking off. No mechanics or ground crew appeared on the field, and there was none of the tight security they had expected. Zheng Baozhen kept taking out his pocket watch to check the time, and when the hour hand reached half past nine he had a premonition that all the preparation Yu Likui's Nanjing group had put in had turned into so much meaningless labor.

Yet Yu Likui and the others still believed their intelligence absolutely. And so the assassination group waited in vain in the thick woods of Purple Mountain for a whole day and night, and did not return to their quarters until the small hours of the next morning. It was then that word came that dashed them all: Chiang Kai-shek and Soong Mei-ling had left Nanjing the night before, secretly, from the Pukou wharf aboard a warship, and sailed for Jiujiang. And so the killing ground Yu Likui and his men had laid out at the Ming Palace airfield came to nothing.

The news that the Nanjing chance had been lost now reached Taiyi Peak on Lushan.

Hua Kezhi's feelings on receiving it were naturally complicated. He was sorry that Wang Yaqiao's airfield ambush had come to nothing, and at the same time he understood that his own responsibility had grown much heavier, since Wang Yaqiao's whole plan for killing Chiang now depended on his group alone. Before long it was confirmed that Chiang and his wife were already on Lushan. A sense of sacred mission left Hua Kezhi and the gunmen in the three inns around Taiyi Peak both elated and tense: their target was within arm's reach, and the nine long-range German pistols had reached their hands on time. In these circumstances Hua Kezhi decided to put Wang Yaqiao's plan into effect at once.

Hua Kezhi first went to reconnoiter the Meilu near Guling Street. What he found was a forbidden zone that could not be approached at all. Hidden in the grass a few hundred meters off, he looked across at the little European house standing in a dense shade of green beside the Changchong stream, and found it very well placed indeed and heavily guarded. The Meilu backed onto the craggy Dayue Mountain, with rolling cliffs and precipices to the north and a deep ravine mouth to the south, and no ordinary person could simply walk in.

Until then Hua Kezhi had known only that the Meilu was a mountain villa built in 1922 by a famous English physician, a Mrs. Heli [UNCLEAR: 赫莉太太 is a Chinese transliteration; the underlying English surname is not recoverable from the characters — Herley, Hurley and Halley are all possible], and that it had originally been called No. 13 Guling Street. In 1930, on the eve of her return to London, the Englishwoman had given it to Soong Mei-ling as a Christmas present, and from then on it had been where Chiang and his wife spent the summer. Because Soong Mei-ling thought thirteen unlucky, the name was changed to the Meilu. Now, finding sentries with loaded rifles posted all around the Meilu, Hua Kezhi felt that to strike at Chiang there would be harder than climbing to heaven.

Later he and Chen Cheng went to scout the "Meiling Bridge" not far off, and found that although a man could lie in wait there, the whole place was running water, and if shots were fired, escape would be a serious problem. So Hua Kezhi finally gave up the idea of striking at Chiang where he lived.

Wang Yaying had completed her task of delivering the guns, but she did not go straight back down the mountain. Lushan was beautiful, its scenery lovely and all its own, but she had no heart for looking at it; her whole mind was on how to get Chiang killed on Lushan as fast as possible. Through those days Wang Yaying kept to her room at the Mountain Village inn below Taiyi Peak. She dared not show herself openly, and came out only after dark to make contact with Hua Kezhi and Sun Fengming. She made the White Deer Grotto Academy below Wulao Peak their meeting point. Under the quiet moon Wang Yaying would pace in front of the Book Repository Hall.

Hua Kezhi's report was this: in the last few days Chiang Kai-shek and Soong Mei-ling had gone out very little, and when they did it was only to Donglin Temple on the northern foot of Lushan, to see the Spring of Wisdom, the White Lotus Temple and the other ancient sites there, and once to Longshou Rock. Even on those two outings a great crowd of guards went with them, and other tourists could not get near them anywhere along the way, let alone fire on Chiang and Madame Soong in their mountain chairs.

"What makes it harder still is that Chiang's movements are never fixed. He goes one place today and another tomorrow, and it all looks decided on the spur of the moment. So laying an ambush in advance is next to unworkable. Besides, he has too many attendants about him — at a rough count, forty at least. And every one of them carries two guns and is watching all around him constantly. Let them see a suspicious passerby and at best they berate him, at worst they arrest him on the spot and put him to the question. Chiang travels with tighter security these days than a feudal emperor on progress. Still, in these few days we have found one pattern: Chiang goes out mostly in the morning, and in the afternoon, when the weather turns hot, he rarely goes out at all." "Mr. Hua, whatever you do, don't be impatient. This is already a real gain. If we know Chiang's daily hours, we can find the pattern of his movements." Wang Yaying, staying on at Lushan, had become Hua Kezhi's staff officer for the operation.

"We have Chiang's pattern now," said Hua Kezhi excitedly, meeting Wang Yaying again at the White Deer Grotto Academy one night. "For the first few days Chiang moved as the mood took him. After that he and Madame Soong became regular. Chen Cheng and I have found that the couple leave almost every morning for the northeastern slope of Lushan. They seem never to have gone to the southern slope or the western, because Donglin Temple is on the north. If we post men on the northeastern slope, sooner or later we shall meet Chiang going up there to burn incense."

"Very well. So that is what your days of shadowing have produced." Wang Yaying thought it over, then demurred. "But you must also be on your guard against Chiang; he suspects everything. If he goes to the north slope too often, he will worry that it has become a pattern, and once outsiders know it, that is exactly where it will be unsafe. Yesterday morning I went to the north slope on the pretext of sightseeing, and found the ground there very badly suited to an ambush: broad flagged paths everywhere, with no line of retreat if we were spotted. So it is best to expect nothing from the north slope. To my mind, the ideal place for an ambush would be the southwestern slope of Lushan."

"The southwestern slope?" Hua Kezhi was startled.

Wang Yaying laid it out point by point. "What I have found is that the southwest of Lushan is nothing but goat-track paths, and most of them steep and hard going. And wherever there is a path, both sides of it are mostly under tree shade, which makes hiding easy. That is why it is the right ground for us."

Hua Kezhi sighed. "A pity that Chiang simply will not go southwest. Perhaps it is exactly because the roads are hard and very unsafe that he will not go there."

But Wang Yaying shook her head. "I think he will go to the southwestern slope one of these days. Chiang is a man whose movements are unpredictable, and there are a great many historic sites on the southwestern slope; he will get there sooner or later. So my advice is that you make preparations there without fail: go over the ground early and set your ambush. As the military classics say, come out where he does not expect you, strike where he is not prepared. That is the principle exactly."

Hua Kezhi found her reasoning sound, and since an attack on the northeastern slope really was extremely dangerous, he agreed. "Very well, madam. We shall station men on the southwestern slope, and the day Chiang shows his head there, one shot will finish him!"

After the meeting with Wang Yaying, Hua Kezhi went back to the Taiyi Grand Lodging House and called a special action meeting that same night, redeploying his men and drawing up a plan to kill Chiang on the mountain paths of the southwestern slope. This time his dispositions were thorough. He posted assassins in hiding along all the roads Chiang might travel, dividing them into groups of two so that they formed a mutually supporting net for the killing. Specifically: on the roads that had to be taken going up and down the southwestern slope — Lulin Bridge, Hanpokou, Wulong Pool, the Imperial Stele Pavilion, Tianchi Temple, Taiyi Village, Lion Rock and the rest — he posted gunmen in hiding, disguised as ordinary tourists, sitting and waiting for Chiang Kai-shek to pass. The moment Chiang appeared, whichever group he came upon was to fire on him without letting the moment slip.

Hua Kezhi and Sun Fengming made one group, and to his group he swore this oath: "Whoever kills Chiang, get away fast. If you fall into Chiang's hands, die before you tell them anything. Give your life to fulfill your duty; lay down your life for what is right!"

That same night the assassins under Hua Kezhi's command drank cup after cup of blood-wine in a suite in a small inn below Taiyi Peak and swore a common oath: they would not rest until they had taken Chiang Kai-shek's head!

And the very day after Hua Kezhi, following Wang Yaying's advice, had spread his whole force across the southwestern slope of Lushan, Chiang Kai-shek — who since coming up the mountain had never once gone sightseeing on the southwestern slope — at last showed his head there!

Why had Chiang suddenly gone southwest? For that, thanks are due to Soong Mei-ling.

Inside the Meilu the setting was elegant and quiet. The garden in particular Chiang and his wife found peaceful and delightful. Sitting on the balcony of the little house you could see the twenty-meter magnolia tree in the garden. And the trumpet creeper the English doctor had planted with her own hands years before had climbed the wall of the house all the way to the third-floor balcony, a solid mass of green.

"Darling, how can it do, our going round the northeastern slope like this every day? If it goes on, will it not turn into a pattern? And then, if someone is watching our movements from cover, it will be far too dangerous!" This was Soong Mei-ling. On the very afternoon when Wang Yaying and Hua Kezhi met under the moon at the White Deer Grotto Academy to lay their plans, Soong Mei-ling suddenly raised a question that startled the guards badly. She said to Chiang Kai-shek, who was sitting reading telegrams, "Even before we came up the mountain, did Dai Yunong not send urgent telegrams again and again, saying that the Hatchet Gang people in Shanghai are watching your movements at all hours? I wonder — could Wang Yaqiao be in league with those opposition people in the south?"

What Chiang Kai-shek happened to be reading was precisely a coded telegram from Dai Li in Shanghai.

The message said that word had lately come from a highly reliable channel that the southern government was doing everything it could to send men secretly to Shanghai, and that there were also reports of heavy money being paid to buy the Hatchet Gang. Suspicious signs had been detected, but the particulars were not known. It was hoped the Generalissimo would be on his fullest guard on the mountain and watch for assassins coming up.

Chiang Kai-shek read Dai Li's telegram over several times. At first he thought little of it; only after his wife's warning did he take it seriously. He knew Dai Li's telegrams were never wind out of an empty cave. If Wang Jingwei and Sun Ke were secretly in league with Wang Yaqiao, the consequences would be past imagining. Chiang thought of the Anhui man who in 1928 had dared to attack him openly to his face, and felt a stab of alarm. Nor could he forget the photographs of the scene where Zhao Tieqiao, the man who had come over to him in those days, died wretchedly in front of the gate of the China Merchants offices in Shanghai. That bloody corpse still gave Chiang a turn whenever he thought of it, even now.

"What did you say, my dear?" Chiang wiped the cold sweat from his forehead with a handkerchief and turned to find several of his senior guards standing at attention behind him, hands at their sides, listening respectfully to Soong Mei-ling. "I said there is no great harm in caution. Why is it that since we came up Lushan we go round the northeastern slope every morning? Keep that up and the time and the place both become a pattern in other people's eyes." Soong Mei-ling was every inch the quick-minded, formidable woman who had made her name on the political stage. Ever since Chiang imprisoned Hu Hanmin and the Nationalist opposition under Sun Ke and Wang Jingwei had resigned their high posts in a body and flown off to Guangdong, she could not help watching in secret against whatever might befall her husband. Even when she went sightseeing with him she kept an eye on everything around her, and wherever the two of them passed, the slightest thing out of the ordinary and Soong Mei-ling would order the attendants to investigate it to the bottom. Today she had suddenly realized a very serious mistake: going to the same place to sightsee every day.

"Madame is over-suspicious." Yu Jishi, the Attendants' Office commander who had been standing behind Chiang and his wife and had not dared to speak for some time, now stepped forward to answer. Yu Jishi came from Chiang's own Fenghua in Zhejiang and was related to the Chiang family besides, so that as attendants' officer at their side he was about the man Chiang and his wife trusted most. Now, seeing Soong Mei-ling question the sightseeing route he had selected, he came forward quickly to defend it. "It is like this, madame. Before Chairman Chiang came up Lushan we had already gone up and studied the ground, and we found that although the southwestern quarter has more scenic sites worth visiting, the terrain is treacherous. The paths for going up and coming down are all narrow as goat tracks, and there is no way to protect the Chairman's safety and madame's. Whereas the northeastern slope is level ground with broad roads. In places like that, even if someone wanted to lie hidden, it would be simply impossible. For the sake of the Chairman's safety and madame's, we had no choice but to choose the northeastern slope for the outings."

"Your intentions were good, of course. But if we always take Mr. Chiang to the same place, then boredom is the smaller matter; what if it draws someone's attention and something happens?" Soong Mei-ling's words were so well grounded that Yu Jishi and the other attendants could only nod along; not one of them dared contradict her to her face.

"You may all withdraw. Since madame says the southwestern slope, then tomorrow we shall go sightseeing there!" Chiang waved a hand at Yu Jishi and the others, signaling that they might do as Soong Mei-ling wished. But Soong Mei-ling called Yu Jishi and the rest back. She was plainly taking Chiang's safety on Lushan even more seriously, and she charged them with special emphasis: "But this by no means implies that going to the southwestern slope allows any slackening of the guard. What I am saying is that the pattern of Mr. Chiang's outings must on no account be detectable by the tourists on this mountain. That would be extremely dangerous. As for how the southwestern outing is to be conducted, Yu Jishi, you and your men are still to produce me a route plan that cannot go wrong. Otherwise, if anything happens, I shall hold you answerable!"

After the guards' officers had withdrawn with nervous faces, Chiang Kai-shek let his wife read Dai Li's telegram. As she read it her heart rose into her throat. "From Dai Yunong's telegram, the southern opposition has already begun to act. That they are in league with Wang Yaqiao in particular shows how serious it is. Darling, now that we are here on the mountain we should be more careful still. If Wang Yaqiao's people have got up here among the crowd, even our Meilu is not safe."

"No, no, my dear." Though the telegram had unsettled him too, Chiang did not really believe that Sun Ke and Wang Jingwei would dare buy assassins to attack him, and he laughed drily. "Wang Jingwei and Sun Ke may hate me in their hearts, but I judge that neither has that kind of nerve. As for that Anhui fellow named Wang, I have long been on my guard against him. If he came up Lushan, Dai Yunong would be bound to know."

Soong Mei-ling was still full of dread. "Darling, there is still no harm in caution. Lushan has been calm enough these last days, but for some reason my heart is tight with fear all the time, as though some terrible shadow keeps flickering before my eyes. I worry that Hu Hanmin's people may have bought killers and sent them up the mountain."

"They will not, my dear. Does Hu Hanmin have that kind of strength to set against me?" Seeing his wife so overwrought, Chiang laughed coldly and made light of it. That night Chiang Kai-shek had a nightmare in the Meilu. In the dream he saw a big man with a heavy beard all over his face rushing at him and Soong Mei-ling with a pistol in each hand, roaring: "Chiang! You have killed so many innocent people; today I, Wang Yaqiao, have come on behalf of all those wronged ghosts to collect the blood debt!" Chiang fled, but Wang Yaqiao came after him fast, and before Chiang could get out of the Meilu a volley of shots rang out behind him. Chiang cried out in terror and started awake.

At daybreak the next morning Chiang told Soong Mei-ling about the terrible dream. She was already uneasy, and said at once, "Darling, since we both have this feeling of ill omen, why do we not simply stay in today?"

But by afternoon Chiang was bored and restless cooped up in the Meilu, and said to her, "My dear, in truth the dream at night came of listening to too much of your advice by day. Who would dare come up Lushan to make trouble with us? Let us go out for a walk. Where is the harm?"

### 19. A Crisp Shot Rings Out on Lushan

That afternoon Chiang and his wife and their party went out of the Meilu again.

Since it was the first time Yu Jishi and the large body of guards had gone toward the southwest of Lushan, they took double care with the security all along the way. Chiang Kai-shek and Soong Mei-ling came to the Immortal's Cave and found jagged boulders there, and among the rocks an ancient pine standing behind an enormous toad-shaped stone, blue-green and straight. He took Soong's hand and they went in, and found spring water plinking inside the cave and clear streams trickling down the rock walls, with four characters left by the ancients on the wall: Jade Nectar of the Cave Heaven. It was a fine place indeed for immortals to rest.

Chiang Kai-shek and Soong Mei-ling enjoyed themselves greatly in the Immortal's Cave; but they never dreamed that on the way back Yu Jishi would suddenly notice a strange heap of something in the grass beside the path, buried in the earth. He hurriedly called the guards over and they scratched about in the grass, and out came eight or nine empty Jinhua ham skins! Yu Jishi lifted one on the end of a stick: the ham, dried and shrunken in the summer wind, was nothing but a hollow bag of skin.

Yu Jishi did not dare alarm Chiang or Soong Mei-ling. That night he called together several of his trusted men and they examined the ham skins found in the grass over and over. In the end they concluded that these ordinary-looking casings had all been carefully cut open and stripped with a knife, and that sharp objects had once been hidden inside them. He said, "I would stake anything that something metal was concealed inside these ham skins. Even if it was not weapons, it was contraband forbidden on the mountain — opium, or the like. If it is opium, no great matter. What I am afraid of is guns and ammunition; and if that is what it was, then we are in for it!"

The attendants' officers, hearing Yu Jishi's analysis, all felt the gravity of it. "We had better advise Mr. Chiang to change his route. Otherwise, if something serious happens, we guards will find it hard to escape the blame." "Yes, yes; and the discovery of the ham skins ought to be reported to madame. She may change her mind once she sees them." Everyone in the discussion put in his own suggestion. But Yu Jishi had his own view. He knew that inside the Meilu it was not so much Chiang Kai-shek whose word was final as Soong Mei-ling, whose single word carried the weight of nine tripods; and since Soong Mei-ling had already decided on sightseeing on the southwestern slope of Lushan, any opinion of his that ran contrary to hers would certainly be opposed by her. He cut the others off. "If there really are men with evil designs on this mountain, then even if we do not go by the southwestern slope they will strike on the north slope. That being so, the best course is simply to tighten the guard. There is no other way."

That very night Yu Jishi settled on a new plan for protecting Chiang and Soong Mei-ling: one party in the open and one under cover, two lines of attendants moving in parallel, to keep suspicious persons away from Chiang at all times.

Yu Jishi said, "We divide the guard regiment into two detachments. One will accompany Mr. Chiang and madame openly on their outings as before. And I shall take the other detachment and follow quietly in the deep woods, so that if there really is an assassin bold enough to try something on Mr. Chiang, then even if our men out in the open fail to see him, the party hidden in cover will pick him up."

"An excellent plan!" Everyone at the meeting approved Yu Jishi's arrangement.

On the morning of 17 June, under a sky heavy with raincloud, a fine drizzle came down over Lushan and added a breath of coolness to the stifling air. Once Chiang Kai-shek and his wife had left Lion Rock escorted by a party of armed attendants, Yu Jishi took a dozen or so men and slipped quietly into a green bamboo grove nearby. This was the first time since the open-and-hidden plan had been put into effect that Yu Jishi had led men in person to guard Chiang from cover. His party spread out in a skirmish line deep in the bamboo and followed step by step behind the column making its slow way along the crags under umbrellas toward the Meilu.

For days now Yu Jishi had not been able to sleep, forever afraid that something unforeseen would happen on Lushan. Today, with the drizzle coming down fine as ox-hair, he felt more tense and anxious than ever, though he could not have said why. They had gone about two hours when the rain began to come down harder. And just then Yu Jishi caught sight of the back of a man in a Yunhu shirt flickering in the bamboo not far ahead. He started; the man showed for an instant and was gone. Men dressed like that were not rare on Lushan — the tycoons and big merchants who came up from Southeast Asia for the summer mostly liked the Hong Kong-made Yunhu shirt. But among Yu Jishi's own attendants very few wore that conspicuous short-sleeved shirt. And it was raining now: the only people walking quietly in dense bamboo were the plainclothes men of the Attendants' Office under Yu Jishi, ordinary tourists having gone back to their inns long since when the rain came. Could it be an assassin? The moment the thought flashed through Yu Jishi's mind his heart began to hammer. He could see the stranger in the Yunhu shirt now, edging quietly forward out of the bamboo toward a great bun-shaped boulder ahead. Yu Jishi quickened his pace, and when he had come within a few yards of the boulder he suddenly saw the man's hand come out from his waist, and he was certain the man held a gun in it! Through the dense green bamboo Yu Jishi could already see, on the narrow path high in the rocks opposite, a file of plainclothes men coming along the mountain road with umbrellas up, and among them two hand-borne mountain chairs, and in the chairs, under raincoats, Chiang Kai-shek and Soong Mei-ling! God, the man was an assassin! For an instant Yu Jishi's heart nearly leapt out of his chest; he was afraid that his own agitation would give warning to the assassin lying behind the bun-shaped boulder to ambush Chiang. And at that very moment the assassin in the Yunhu shirt spun around and showed him a cold hard face! In terror Yu Jishi shouted to the attendants on either side of him and they all rushed the assassin together!

The man was Chen Cheng, deputy leader of the special action team!

The night before, Chen Cheng and Hua Kezhi had conferred in secret until three in the morning, and Chen Cheng had concluded that Chiang Kai-shek would very likely go from the Meilu to Lion Rock the next morning. He reckoned that of the sights on the western slope of Lushan Chiang had been visiting these last days, only the Lion Rock area remained unseen, and Lion Rock was a place of fine scenery, thick with famous sites and ancient remains: the Hundred-Fathom Stair was there, and the Square Seal Rock. He said to Hua Kezhi, "If Chiang really does go there, it is a good place to kill him. I have studied the ground around Lion Rock; it is mostly cliffs and precipices, deep caves and ravines. Even where there are paths in the hills, most of them are wide enough for one man alone to pass. Once they really do go to that place, I can finish him single-handed."

"All the same, we must on no account be careless." Listening to Chen Cheng's description of the ground at Lion Rock, Hua Kezhi at once judged it the ideal ground for the assassination. "If Chiang really does go there, we ought to send more men. If you lie in wait there alone, then even if you succeed you will never get out."

On Hua Kezhi's firm insistence, Chen Cheng finally agreed that he and Hua Kezhi and Sun Fengming would go to Lion Rock together and lie in wait. The next morning the three of them had not long been out when the sky suddenly clouded over. Before long a rain as fine as ox-hair began to drift down, and the air turned cool at once. Following Hua Kezhi's arrangement, Chen Cheng hid himself at the edge of the thick bamboo on the left side of Lion Rock. From this hiding place he could see, on the crags opposite, the road that had to be taken from Lion Rock back to the Meilu: a goat track halfway up the slope. If Chiang Kai-shek came that way today, Chen Cheng could easily break out and fire on him at close range as he made his difficult way along the rocks.

Chen Cheng had not been long in the bamboo when the rain began to fall in a steady patter. In the hiss of it the bamboo grove was uncommonly still. Then Chen Cheng suddenly heard a confusion of footsteps ahead. He looked toward the sound and his heart began to hammer. On the path halfway up the slope in front of him a weary column was moving slowly through the netting rain, listless and dispirited. Most of the guards had nothing to keep the rain off and pistols tucked in their belts; only a few held umbrellas, keeping close around two hand-borne mountain chairs that were moving along the wet rock path toward the Meilu. Chen Cheng knew that the chance he had waited days for had come at last: he had made out the man sitting in the forward chair, and it was the target they had thought of by day and dreamed of by night, Chiang Kai-shek! Chen Cheng let nothing else weigh with him then; in a single bound he broke out of the deep green bamboo and hid himself behind the bun-shaped boulder nearer the crags.

From there he could see the column of attendants moving through the rain clearly enough, but for a shot aimed at Chiang in his chair the distance was still a little too great. Chen Cheng had one thought only: to seize this chance in a thousand years and shoot dead the Chiang Kai-shek he had waited so long to meet!

But just as he had pulled the pistol from his waist and was preparing to fire on the approaching Chiang, he heard a faint rustling in the bamboo behind him. He took it for Hua Kezhi and Sun Fengming, lying in wait in another quarter, coming up at the alarm. But when Chen Cheng looked around he saw a stranger come bolting out of the bamboo. At the same moment he saw seven or eight agents in bowler hats step out of the bamboo nearby with American-made Mausers in their hands.

"Done for!" Chen Cheng realized in a flash that there was no getting out of this. He had meant to wait until Chiang's column came a little nearer before he aimed, so as to be sure of his target; but the agents were already rushing him with their guns out, and he had no time to weigh anything. In his haste he sprang up, leveled at the people moving along the mountain path, and squeezed the trigger — crack!

The crisp report woke echoes down the silent gorge!

Hua Kezhi, hidden in the pine woods on one side of Lion Rock, heard the shot ring out from the bamboo opposite and knew Chen Cheng must have sighted the target. After that single sniping shot he was about to rush up the crags to support him when suddenly a rattle of gunfire like beans popping broke out from the same direction. Hua Kezhi judged that Chen Cheng was now surrounded, and that the volleys must be Chiang's attendants swarming an assassin they had found. He ran all the way to the top of the ridge and, through the branches of the pines, saw the green bamboo grove below Lion Rock swarming with heads. He wanted to charge down and save Chen Cheng, but it was already too late!

In the mountain wind, in the fine rain, a choking reek of blood spread all at once. Chen Cheng was dead!
