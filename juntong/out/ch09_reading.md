## The Traffic Police Corps: What Kind of Reactionary Force It Was

The fourth volume of the *Selected Works of Mao Zedong* mentions the Nationalist government's "Traffic Police Corps." Just what kind of reactionary force this was, many people still do not entirely understand. Here, so far as my own knowledge runs, I offer a little material for reference.

The "Traffic Police Corps" was an armed secret-service force given special training by the American secret-service organ, the Sino-American Special Technical Cooperative Organization (SACO for short), and equipped throughout with American weapons. It was one of the products of the merging of the American and Chiang secret services, and a pocket trump-card army of which Chiang Kai-shek was particularly fond.

From 1943 to 1946 the American secret-service chieftain Miles, deputy director of SACO, besides training great numbers of American-style operatives for the Juntong in Chungking — and, after the victory, training traitor operatives at Shanghai and Peiping to reinforce the Juntong's criminal activities against the Communists and the people — also ran, one after another, more than ten "Sino-American special technical training classes" in various places, training and equipping an armed secret-service force of more than forty thousand men. That force was the predecessor of the Traffic Police Corps. The first training class was set up at Xiongcun in Shexian, Anhui, with Dai Li as concurrent head and Guo Lüzhou as deputy, specially to train and equip the "Loyal and Patriotic Army" — the secret-service force Dai Li had built up in Shanghai at the start of the war against Japan out of the secret societies and street toughs. Each course ran about three months, its main content the use of American weapons, demolition, reconnaissance, guerrilla work and the like, and on finishing a course a unit was re-issued American equipment. The second class was set up at Nanyue in Hunan, Dai Li head, Tao Yishan deputy; the troops trained there were those led by He Jiyuan of the sixth column of the Nationalist Military Affairs Commission's Special Operations Army command, together with the troops of the Hunan bandit-operative Chen Shihu. The third class was first set up at Fengxue Temple in Linru, Henan, and later moved to Niudong near Xi'an, Dai Li head, the deputies being Wen Qiang and then Yang Wei; it trained the special-operations columns led by Liao Zongze, Yue Zhuyuan, Yang Rong and others. There were besides a fourth class at Shanba in Suiyuan (Gao Rong deputy head), a fifth at Xifeng in Guizhou (Deng Kuangyuan and He Efang deputy heads), and further classes at Xiushui in Jiangxi, Jian'ou in Fujian, Rui'an in Zhejiang, Zhangzhou in Fujian, Linquan in Anhui and elsewhere — more than ten in all. Except for the tenth class at Linquan, which Tang Enbo headed concurrently, every one had Dai Li as its concurrent head. The weapons issued included carbines, Thompson submachine guns, UD submachine guns, automatic pistols, revolvers and rocket launchers.

After the victory in the war against Japan, these secret-service troops were all merged into the General Administration of Traffic Police and reorganized into some twenty Traffic Police corps. The men who served in succession as corps commanders — Guo Lüzhou, Zhang Guoliang, Zhou Wenxin, Cao Tiesheng, Peng Ziqiang, Yang Yuchun, Bao Buchao, Chen Jiesheng, He Jiyuan, Tang Yisheng, Zhu Gengyang, Tian Dongyun, Li Xiang and the rest — were all Juntong operatives. Chiang Kai-shek set great store by this force: besides using it to guard the railways and highways, he threw it repeatedly into the key campaigns against the Communists and the people. In the spring of 1949, after his "retirement," Chiang Kai-shek even specially ordered several Traffic Police corps moved to Fenghua, Ningpo, to protect him. The directors-general of Traffic Police who led these forces — Ji Zhangjian, Zhou Weilong, Ma Zhichao in succession — were likewise all Juntong operatives. In the last stage of the war of liberation the ordinary traffic police of the railways and highways everywhere were mostly folded into the corps as well, and just before the liberation several traffic-police brigades were also formed.

The marks of these troops were heavy firepower, high mobility and quick, flexible deployment. Because they had been trained by the American secret service, were equipped throughout with American weapons, and were officered for the most part by Juntong operatives, they were more stubborn than other reactionary troops, more savage and brutal, and a greater menace to the people. In the war of liberation they not only fought in attack and defence on the various fronts alongside the reactionaries' regular formations, but also carried out harassment in the rear, sabotage of communications and the like, and joined the Juntong's local secret-service organizations in the whole range of criminal activities of hunting down and slaughtering revolutionaries. The repeated mass killings inside the SACO compound at Chungking before the liberation, the massacres at Shanghai, Chengtu and elsewhere, and all manner of destruction in other places — they had a hand in every one. In the war of liberation this secret-service force, like every other force Chiang Kai-shek had, was in the end wiped out by the People's Liberation Army, leaving only two or three corps under Zhou Wenxin and others to flee in panic to Taiwan.
