## How I Was Ordered to Assassinate General Yang Jie

In 1949 I was the National Defence Ministry's special commissioner stationed in Yunnan and concurrently chief of the Bureau of Secrets Preservation's Yunnan station. In August, the Bureau director Mao Renfeng twice sent me a cipher telegram for my personal decipherment, ordering me to assassinate at once five men then in Kunming: Yang Jie, Chen Fuguang, and the Yunnan provincial civil-affairs chief An Enpu, the Security Command chief of staff Xie Chongwen, and the security-brigade commander Long Zehui. This was one of Chiang Kai-shek's measures for removing those who differed from him and straining every nerve to hold Yunnan as an anti-Communist base, so as to collude with the imperialists abroad and "recover" the already-liberated areas. I was then living in Jingyuan New Village at Sanjieqiao in Kunming. Yang Jie lived diagonally across from me, and almost every day passed the big lawn before my gate on his way to eat and play cards at the home of Yunnan's "tuo-tea king" Yan Xiecheng, often not returning until deep in the night from some engagement outside. Besides meeting him often at Yan Xiecheng's, I saw him at many other social gatherings, and we were well acquainted. Each time Mr. Yang crossed the lawn before my gate and saw my small children, he would stop to play with them; the children all liked him and called him "Uncle Yang."

To send agents to assassinate Mr. Yang then was indeed the easiest of things, but I did not carry it out. First, because Mao Renfeng had ordered me to assassinate five men, and if I killed Yang Jie alone the others would take alarm and be hard to reach afterward. Second, because my whole family, old and young, was then in Kunming, and if an assassination occurred Lu Han would surely guess it was I; even if I could get away, my family would be implicated. Third, because I saw that the Nationalists' cause was lost, and killing a few men would not necessarily hold Yunnan. So I made all manner of arrangements yet never brought myself to the point of decision.

Before long Mao Renfeng sent me another cipher telegram for my personal decipherment, saying in substance that, by Chiang Kai-shek's own word, Yang Jie was making large moves, drawing in senior Nationalist generals for the Revolutionary Committee, and that he must be removed first to forestall future trouble. I was given three days to carry it out, on pain of severe punishment under the discipline of the organization — that is, the Juntong.

On receiving this order I weighed it again and again. I reckoned that to kill only Yang Jie might not turn Lu Han against me at once; for although Lu Han and Yang Jie were on friendly terms, Lu was not a member of the Revolutionary Committee and did not support Yang's democratic activities, and so long as I did not at the same time assassinate men closely tied to him, he would not fall out with me over Yang Jie. To guard against the worst, I nonetheless prepared to send my family first to Chungking after Yang's assassination, and to move myself, with the household radio, to the headquarters of the 26th Army at Beijiaochang. If I did not kill Yang Jie, Mao Renfeng would have no way to answer to Chiang Kai-shek and would be bound to punish me; so I made up my mind to do away with Yang Jie.

That afternoon I gathered several agents who specialized in assassination in a small reception room at my home to plan it in secret, directing them to kill Yang Jie that very night. The first plan was this: when Yang Jie passed the big lawn opposite my house on his way home at night, to close on him there and fire at his head with a silenced pistol. Since the streetlamp did not reach the middle of the lawn, one could wait there and see Yang Jie clearly as he came from the lit side. He was quite stout and walked slowly, and the agents all knew him and would make no mistake. Once he was hit there would be no saving him, for the bullets had poison packed in their heads. The second plan was this: if he did not go out that night, to strike at his home. We knew his household well — only an aide, a driver and a cook, and a girl of ten-odd years. Whether by knocking or by going over the wall, the shooting of Yang Jie could be carried out at leisure, for the neighbours all lived in high-walled separate compounds and no one would come to interfere.

The arrangements made, the agents had just saluted me and gone when the door from the small reception room to the balcony was suddenly pushed open. I looked up to see my mother come in in a fury and fling the book in her hand down hard on the desk. I had thought no one was outside, never imagining my mother was sitting reading on the balcony and had heard all we said. Seeing her face full of anger, I hastily knelt before her, meaning to explain. Grinding her teeth, she jabbed her finger hard against my head, and, so angry she could scarcely get her breath, upbraided me: "For years I have taught you that a man may hold no office, but he must be a human being! And today, for your own advancement and riches, you would kill a man? Are you still human? I ask you: when you have killed Mr. Yang, and tomorrow your sons and daughters ask you who killed Uncle Yang, what will you answer? If they learn it was you, how will they look on a father who kills good men as he pleases? And how am I, your mother, to face anyone? You think of none of these consequences — you think only of your own office, not of being a man! I will not have you for a son; I shall go this instant to Taiwan, to your brother and sister!"

Hearing my mother's words, I could only promise her that I would sooner take my punishment than kill Yang Jie, and begged the old lady to calm her anger. So I called the agents back, said the situation had changed, declared the arrangements just made suspended for the time being, and left it for later. My mother, fearing I would try some other scheme, watched every day to see whether Yang Jie was still there. And so the assassination of Yang Jie was put off in this way.

Before long Mao Renfeng ordered me to send my whole family to Hong Kong, the better to take action later. On 6 September Lu Han went to Chungking to see Chiang Kai-shek, and I made ready so that, the moment Lu Han was detained in Chungking, I would rise together with the 26th Army, seize Kunming, arrest Yang Jie and all Lu Han's trusted men, and disarm the several security regiments. On 8 September I received an urgent telegram from Mao Renfeng in Chungking saying Lu Han had agreed to a great roundup in Kunming, and I called together the ten or so agent chiefs in the city and organized the nearly one thousand agents and armed men we could command to make every preparation for it. The several agents watching Yang Jie were made ready too, to arrest him at a word.

On 9 September, no sooner had Lu Han's plane returned to Kunming than Xu Yuanju, the second-section chief of the Southwest Governor's Office whom Mao Renfeng had sent, flew in behind him with a large body of agents. Getting off the plane, Xu Yuanju came to my home and told me that the first thing Mao Renfeng had directed him face to face to do in Kunming was to arrest Yang Jie. But when Xu Yuanju and I went with several agents, we drew a blank, catching only one of Yang's staff, and then Professor Chen Fuguang, who had gone to Yang's home. Xu Yuanju took Chen up to the reception room on the upper floor of my house and let him hear the screams of the tortured coming from a downstairs room turned into an interrogation chamber. We soon learned from the two of them that Yang had flown to Hong Kong that morning — just as Xu Yuanju's plane landed, the plane Yang was on had taken off. The ticket had been bought under a false name by Zhu Jianfei, who had also driven a car to Yang's home to fetch him, Yang hiding under the rear seat, so that the agents on watch had not noticed.

When Chiang Kai-shek learned that Yang Jie had got away, he was greatly enraged and sent Mao Renfeng, with Zhou Yanghao and others, hurrying to Kunming. Mao Renfeng came to my home and flew into a rage at me, blaming me for not having killed Yang Jie earlier. Afterwards, from the diaries and letters we seized at Yang's home, we found out Yang Jie's connections in Hong Kong, and Mao Renfeng urgently telegraphed the Taiwan Bureau's operations chief Ye Xiangzhi to lead the agent Han Shichang and three others — four in all — to Hong Kong to assassinate Yang Jie.

One morning I saw in the paper the news that Yang Jie had been assassinated in Hong Kong, and hurried with the paper to Mao Renfeng. When I showed it to him, he drew from his files an urgent telegram from Taiwan to show me. It was from Ye Xiangzhi, saying the killing of Yang was accomplished and all the agents had returned safely. Only afterwards did I learn that Ye Xiangzhi and the others had first forged a letter of introduction from Li Zongli and had an agent take it to the home of a friend of Mr. Yang's on the fourth floor of No. 260 Hennessy Road in Hong Kong; there the agent saw Mr. Yang and handed him the letter, and just as Yang bent his head to read it, fired two shots into his head with a pistol and killed him. For their "merit" in assassinating Yang Jie, Ye Xiangzhi and the rest were even given a bonus by the Bureau.

Such is the whole course of Mr. Yang Jie's assassination.
