NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: the Louza police station
NOTE: The <i>Louza police station</i> (老闸捕房), at the corner of Nanking Road and Guizhou Road, was the first branch station of the Shanghai Municipal Police in the International Settlement, and a place of grim fame: on May&#160;30, 1925, constables firing from it into a crowd that had gathered to demand the release of arrested students killed several people and touched off the May Thirtieth Movement. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: it had crossed Fenglin Bridge
NOTE: <i>Fenglin Bridge</i> (枫林桥), in the Xujiahui district, was the site of the Nationalist garrison's prison and execution ground from the 1927 coup until about 1928&#8212;the chief place of Communist detention and death before that office passed south to Longhua. The prison van thus carries Ling Wen past the older killing-ground on the way to the newer. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: make up a table of mahjong
NOTE: <i>Mahjong</i> (麻将) needs four players; Miss Tao's wish for a third cellmate 'to make up a table' is the idle joke of a bored woman used to the game. (Texture.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: I've got malted-milk extract
NOTE: <i>Malted-milk extract</i> (麦乳精) is a sweet, nourishing milk-and-malt powder; in the early 1930s the prized versions were imported brands such as Ovaltine (阿华田) and Horlicks (好立克), luxuries a prisoner could indeed come by only 'through someone with connections.' (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: smoothed her cheongsam
NOTE: The <i>cheongsam</i> (旗袍, <i>qipao</i>) was the close-fitting one-piece dress that became the standard modern women's garment of Republican Shanghai; Miss Tao's, and the sapphire-blue woolen one that later hides the letter, mark her out as a woman of fashionable means. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: a satin-faced quilt rolled up on a sheet patterned with mandarin ducks
NOTE: <i>Mandarin ducks</i> (鸳鸯), thought to pair for life, are the standard Chinese emblem of wedded love; on Miss Tao's bedding, with the satin quilt and the drift of perfume, they are the furnishings of a kept woman. (Texture.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: her hair bobbed to the ears
NOTE: Hair <i>bobbed to the ears</i> (剪发) was the mark of the emancipated 'New Woman' and of the female student or revolutionary; in these years it could itself draw official suspicion, so that Dong Huiwen's cut, 'like a schoolteacher,' is also a quiet sign of what she is. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: Longhua is not Nanjing Road
NOTE: <i>Nanjing Road</i> (南京路) was the great shopping street of the International Settlement, the very emblem of modern commercial Shanghai&#8212;its department stores, cinemas and crowds; You Tianxiao sets it against Longhua, the garrison and its killing-ground, to menace Dong Huiwen. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: You know Xu Zhenya
NOTE: <i>Xu Zhenya</i> (徐枕亚, 1889&#8211;1937) wrote <i>Jade Pear Spirit</i> (玉梨魂, 1912), the great bestseller of the 'Mandarin Ducks and Butterflies' school of sentimental romance. That Miss Tao's idea of a famous novelist is Xu Zhenya sets her taste pointedly against Ling Wen, whose <i>Winter</i> belongs to the new revolutionary literature. (Corroborated.)
