ANCHOR: anti-aircraft searchlights
NOTE: After the Japanese attack on Shanghai in the 'January 28' war of 1932, the city's garrison was hurriedly fitted with air defenses; the sweeping <i>anti-aircraft searchlights</i> over Longhua are a lingering mark of that fighting, a year on. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: no more than a hundred-odd silver dollars
NOTE: A <i>silver dollar</i> (银元, the 'big money,' 大洋) was the standard silver coin of Republican China; that the pooled pockets of eleven arrested men come to only 'a hundred-odd' of them is meant to show how thin a gambling stake it makes. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: the walls have ears
NOTE: <i>The walls have ears</i> renders 隔墙有耳 (literally 'beyond the wall there are ears')&#8212;the same warning as the English idiom, that one may be overheard anywhere. (Idiom.)
