NOTE-ADD p024
ANCHOR: and to be without heirs is the greatest
NOTE: A-Chu quotes one of the most famous sayings of Mencius (&#23391;&#23376;, 4th c. BCE): 不孝有三，无后为大 &#8212; &#8216;there are three unfilial things, and to have no heir is the greatest of them.&#8217; In the classic scheme of filial piety, a man&#8217;s first duty is to continue his father&#8217;s line, for only a son can carry on the sacrifices at the ancestral altar; to die without a male heir is to let the family&#8217;s &#8216;incense-fire&#8217; (&#39321;&#28779;) go out and abandon the ancestors. This is why the Empress Dowager&#8217;s gift of a concubine to beget a son is, in the logic A-Chu reconstructs, an act of favor and not of cruelty. Corroborated; the enumeration of the &#8216;three&#8217; is the Han commentator Zhao Qi&#8217;s.
WHY: the whole first deduction turns on a Mencian maxim about heirs and ancestral sacrifice that a Western reader cannot supply for himself.

NOTE-ADD p089
ANCHOR: keep a fast and fulfill a vow
NOTE: To &#8216;keep a fast&#8217; (&#21507;&#26025;, <i>chizhai</i>) is to take up the Buddhist abstinence from meat, and to &#8216;fulfill a vow&#8217; (&#36824;&#24895;, <i>huanyuan</i>) is to return to a temple to make the offering one promised a god or bodhisattva in exchange for a granted wish. A retreat of some days at a temple for these devotions was an ordinary act of piety for a woman of the household; the sinister point here is that it gave the plotters their chance, the birth mother murdered and replaced during just such a stay at the Ciyun Temple. Corroborated.
WHY: the fast-and-vow temple retreat is a specific devotional custom, not a generic holiday, and the murder is timed to it.
