# ch18 The Maochang Coal Company — R2 densification

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: even a film studio had put up its sound stages nearby
NOTE: A real film-studio district lay on this southwestern fringe of the Concessions: the Lianhua company (联华影业公司, from 1930) had its photographic studio on Caoxi Road in the Xujiahui area, the site later taken over by the Shanghai Film Studio. (The studio district is corroborated; the novel names no particular studio.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: squatted on the waste ground nearby and thrown up shacks
NOTE: These squatter shacks are the <i>penghu</i> (棚户, &#8216;shack households&#8217;) settlements that grew on Shanghai&#8217;s margins as country people fleeing the lower-Yangtze floods poured into the city; thrown up first of planks and then of cinder-and-mud brick on waste ground the authorities ignored, they housed much of the city&#8217;s unskilled labor. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: from Rue Père Robert into Xujiahui Road
NOTE: <i>Rue P&#232;re Robert</i> (金神父路) was a French Concession street, laid out about 1907 and named for a French missionary priest, Father Robert; it became Ruijin Er Road (瑞金二路) in 1943 and keeps that name today. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: a house near Gujiazhai Park
NOTE: <i>Gujiazhai Park</i> (顾家宅公园), the &#8216;French Park,&#8217; opened on Bastille Day 1909 in the French Concession, laid out in formal French-garden style and at first reserved for French residents; it was renamed Fuxing Park (复兴公园) in 1946, the name it bears today. The &#8216;iron gate into the French Concession&#8217; mentioned below is the boundary between the Concession and the Chinese city. (Corroborated.)
