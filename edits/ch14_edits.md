# ch14 New Year's Eve — R2 densification

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: a crock of Shaoxing wine
NOTE: <i>Shaoxing wine</i> (绍兴酒) is a <i>huangjiu</i> or &#8216;yellow wine,&#8217; a fermented (not distilled) rice wine from Shaoxing in Zhejiang, amber and mellow and far milder than grain spirit; the classic banquet and festival drink of the Jiangnan region, served warmed, which is why a crock of it is brought to the New Year&#8217;s Eve table. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: a few Mauser pistols
NOTE: The <i>Mauser pistol</i> is the German Mauser C96, known in China as the 盒子炮 (&#8216;box cannon&#8217;) or 驳壳枪 for its wooden holster-stock &#8212; a bulky, long-barreled semi-automatic recognizable by the box magazine set before the trigger. Because the arms embargoes of the 1920s restricted rifles but not pistols, it poured into China by the hundreds of thousands and became the favored sidearm of soldiers, gangsters and revolutionaries alike. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: the Fourth Company of the Fifteenth Bao in Fengxian
NOTE: A <i>bao</i> (保) was a unit of the <i>baojia</i>, the old household-registration and local-defense system in which about a hundred households made a bao under a headman, used for policing, conscription and mutual surveillance; Fengxian (奉贤) is the marshy coastal county south of Shanghai. The Nationalists revived the baojia in full, as an anti-Communist control apparatus, only from 1932, so a formally numbered bao belongs to the system as it was being systematized around this time. (Corroborated; the numbered-bao apparatus is a c.1932 development.)
