NOTE-ADD p045
ANCHOR: To save the world and ferry men across
NOTE: A-Chu&#8217;s creed, 救世渡人, borrows a Buddhist image: <i>du</i> (渡/度) is to ferry living beings across the sea of suffering to the far shore of release, the work of a bodhisattva. He turns it to a doctor&#8217;s vocation of healing and rescue, which is why he sets it flatly against killing: to ferry men across and to cut them down are opposite motions. Corroborated.
WHY: &#8216;ferry men across&#8217; reads as a riddle without the Buddhist metaphor of carrying souls to the far shore that defines A-Chu's whole moral stance.

NOTE-ADD p126
ANCHOR: building the Great Wall
NOTE: &#8216;Building the Great Wall&#8217; (砌长城) is the standing jest for a game of mahjong: at the start the tiles are stacked face down into four long walls that the players demolish as they draw, so a table of mahjong looks like four little Great Walls going up. The Madams are at their tiles, the commonest pastime of a leisured household. Corroborated.
WHY: the literal phrase hides that the Madams are playing mahjong, the tiles &#8216;walls&#8217; the idiom is built on.

NOTE-ADD p228
ANCHOR: Qin by morning and Chu by nightfall
NOTE: The taunt renders 朝秦暮楚, &#8216;serve Qin in the morning, Chu by evening.&#8217; In the Warring States era small states shifted allegiance between the rival powers Qin and Chu as advantage dictated; the phrase became the byword for one who changes sides or lovers without constancy. Young Tang is sneering that He Yashu is fickle in her affections and so has no right to feel slighted. Corroborated.
WHY: without the note Qin and Chu are meaningless proper names; they are two rival ancient states, and the idiom means faithless inconstancy.

NOTE-ADD p242
ANCHOR: The Green and Red Gangs
NOTE: The Green Gang (青帮) and Red Gang (红帮, or 洪帮) were the great secret-society brotherhoods of Republican Shanghai, part fraternal order and part criminal syndicate, controlling the docks, opium, and protection rackets and reaching deep into business and politics; the Green Gang under Du Yuesheng was the most powerful. To ask whether A-Chu has offended them is to ask whether the bombing was underworld work. Corroborated.
WHY: names the real, dominant Shanghai gangs (distinct from the novel's invented Golden Dragon Society) that a Western reader will not know.

NOTE-ADD p259
ANCHOR: the road to the Yellow Springs
NOTE: The Yellow Springs (黄泉, <i>huangquan</i>) are the Chinese land of the dead, a shadowy realm deep beneath the earth to which the soul descends&#8212;the road there is the road into death. The narrator wonders whether Fourth Madam, now dead, found in that world the vengeance she never won in this one. Corroborated.
WHY: &#8216;Yellow Springs&#8217; is the classical name for the underworld, opaque to a reader who does not know the image.
