# ch24 Backstage — R3 densification

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: a pair of foreign-firm compradors
NOTE: A <i>comprador</i> (买办, <i>maiban</i>; from Portuguese <i>comprador</i>, &#8216;buyer&#8217;) was the Chinese manager who ran a Western firm&#8217;s dealings with the local market in a treaty port &#8212; broker, cashier and hirer of the whole Chinese staff, and often rich and Westernized. By the 1930s the word stood for the entire class of foreign-firm middlemen. A long gown beside a Western suit, passing as two such men, is just the moneyed, half-foreign look the disguise wants. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: the gold-and-red Cantonese-embroidered costume
NOTE: <i>Canton embroidery</i> (广绣, <i>Guangxiu</i>), the Guangzhou branch of Cantonese <i>Yue</i> embroidery (粤绣), is one of China&#8217;s four famous embroidery traditions, beside those of Suzhou, Hunan and Sichuan. It is known for bold, saturated color and for gold-and-silver thread couched flat on the ground (钉金绣), which gives an opera robe its glittering surface. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: the Dashatou airfield
NOTE: <i>Dashatou</i> (大沙头, &#8216;great sandbar&#8217;), the eastern-Guangzhou sandbar that also held the Canton&#8211;Kowloon railway terminus, was the site of one of China&#8217;s first airfields, laid out from 1917 and home to the Guangdong military aviation school. Through the 1920s it served chiefly military and official flying, so Teacher Ye&#8217;s hurrying in and out by air marks him as a man with government backing. (Corroborated; scheduled civil air service on the Shanghai&#8211;Guangzhou line dates only from 1929.)
