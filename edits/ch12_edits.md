NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: Chengzhong Middle School
NOTE: <i>Chengzhong Middle School</i> (澄衷中学) was a real Hongkou school, founded about 1900 by the Ningbo merchant Ye Chengzhong (叶澄衷, 1840&#8211;1899) as the first influential Chinese-run modern school in Shanghai; Cai Yuanpei served as its first dean of studies, and Hu Shi was among its early pupils. It added a middle-school course and took the name Chengzhong Middle School in 1928, and survives today in Hongkou as Shanghai Chengzhong Senior High School. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: the Xiahai Temple
NOTE: The <i>Xiahai Temple</i> (下海庙) is a real Hongkou temple, founded in the Qianlong reign (1736&#8211;95) by local boatmen and fishermen to worship the sea deities and later turned into a Buddhist nunnery; it is named for the Xiahai Pu (下海浦), a creek of the old Hongkou waterfront. Burned in the Japanese shelling early in the war and afterward rebuilt, it stands today at the corner of Kunming and Haimen Roads. (The temple is corroborated; the &#8216;Maohai Road&#8217; corner is the novel&#8217;s own geography.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: The training school had once been the manor of some old aristocrat
NOTE: Chen Qianli&#8217;s three years at a <i>training school</i> in the Soviet Union reflect a real practice: the Comintern trained Chinese Communist cadres in Moscow at the Communist University of the Toilers of the East (from 1921), Sun Yat-sen University (1925&#8211;30), and the elite International Lenin School (from 1926), whose core course ran three years. The novel leaves the school unnamed; the &#8216;old aristocrat&#8217;s manor&#8217; and the &#8216;Siberian forest&#8217; beyond the wire are atmosphere, not a real campus, all three schools being in or near Moscow. (Soviet cadre-training corroborated; this particular school the novel&#8217;s own.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: Give them one striking feature first
NOTE: Putting on &#8216;one striking feature&#8217; &#8212; here a conspicuous russet hat and scarf, swapped in a crowd for a plain gray homburg &#8212; is real tradecraft: a tail fixes on the memorable detail, so shedding it effectively erases the man the pursuer was told to follow. (Texture.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: Letters from Afar
NOTE: <i>Letters from Afar</i> (远方来信) is Lenin&#8217;s series of five letters written from Zurich in March 1917, on the eve of his return to Russia after the February Revolution; only the first was published at the time. The manuscript Chen Qianyuan is translating, headed &#8216;First Letter: The First Stage of the First Revolution,&#8217; is that actual first letter, whose title is exactly &#8216;The First Stage of the First Revolution&#8217;; it gives this chapter its Chinese title, 远方来信. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: boil tangyuan
NOTE: <i>Tangyuan</i> (汤圆) are boiled glutinous-rice balls, often filled with sweet sesame or bean paste, eaten at New Year and the Lantern Festival; their roundness stands for family reunion. (Corroborated.)
