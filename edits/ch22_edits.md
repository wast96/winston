NOTE-ADD p304
ANCHOR: a shikumen house
NOTE: The <i>shikumen</i> (石库门, &#8216;stone-gate&#8217;) is the characteristic Shanghai row house of the concession era, a brick terrace dwelling entered through a heavy stone door-frame and ranged along narrow interior lanes (弄堂, <i>longtang</i>). With doors front and rear giving onto a warren of connected alleys, such houses suited the Communist underground exactly: lookouts could watch every approach, and members could slip out the back at the first sign of danger &#8212; which is precisely how the meeting-house here is meant to work. Corroborated.
WHY: the safe-house's escape logic (front and rear doors, "open in every direction") turns on the shikumen form, unknown to a Western reader.

NOTE-ADD p184
ANCHOR: the stout fellows of Liangshan Marsh
NOTE: Liangshan Marsh (梁山泊) is the marsh-ringed mountain stronghold of the hundred and eight outlaw heroes of the classic novel <i>Water Margin</i> (水浒传), the standing Chinese byword for bravos of unbreakable toughness and sworn brotherhood. Xiong Zida means that not even those legendary hard men could hold out under his instruments of torture; the &#8216;108&#8217; of his boast in the same breath is itself the outlaws&#8217; number. Corroborated.
WHY: without the Water Margin allusion the torturer's boast — and the pointed "108" beside it — is opaque to a Western reader.
