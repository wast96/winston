ANCHOR: golden-turtle son-in-law
NOTE: A &#8216;golden-turtle son-in-law&#8217; (金龟婿) is a husband of wealth and high rank, a prize catch. Under the Tang, officials of the third rank and above wore a gold tortoise ornament as the badge of their office; the phrase, made current by a line of the poet Li Shangyin (李商隐, c.&#160;813&#8211;858), came to mean the winning of a rich and powerful man. Rong Sheng means that Yashu, by her costly dress, has plainly landed one. Corroborated.
WHY: &#8216;golden-turtle son-in-law&#8217; is an opaque calque in English; the note supplies the Tang badge-of-rank behind it and the sense &#8216;a rich, high-placed husband.&#8217;
