NOTE-ADD p019
ANCHOR: a blank window where the censor has been
NOTE: To &#8216;open a skylight&#8217; (开天窗) was for a newspaper to go to press leaving a conspicuous blank where the censors had struck out an article &#8212; a silent, visible protest that told readers something had been suppressed. It was a common sight in the tightly censored press of Republican-era Shanghai. Xiong Zida&#8217;s point is that the Communist voice reaches the street even so, in the papers that print the blank rather than the lie. Corroborated.
WHY: without the note a Western reader reads "blank window" as a bare metaphor and misses that this was a recognized, era-specific practice of protest against press censorship.

NOTE-ADD p122
ANCHOR: four treasures of the scholar’s studio
NOTE: The four treasures of the scholar&#8217;s studio (文房四宝, <i>wenfang sibao</i>) are the writing brush, the ink-stick, the paper, and the inkstone &#8212; the essential implements of Chinese calligraphy and ink painting, grouped as a set by this name since the Song dynasty. The ink is ground fresh with water on the inkstone before use, so that the ink here being &#8216;not yet dry&#8217; tells the visitor someone has lately been writing or painting at the desk. Corroborated.
WHY: "four treasures of the scholar's studio" is an opaque set-phrase in English; the note names the four implements and lets the reader see why the still-wet ink is a clue.
