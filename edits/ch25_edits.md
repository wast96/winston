NOTE-ADD p137
ANCHOR: a Shaoxing opera
NOTE: <i>Shaoxing wenxi</i> (绍兴文戏, &#8216;Shaoxing civil drama&#8217;) is the early name for what is now <i>Yue</i> opera (越剧), a lyrical opera form that grew up in the Shengzhou countryside near Shaoxing, in Zhejiang. Soft-sung and given over almost wholly to romantic scholar-and-beauty love stories, and in its mature form performed by all-female troupes, it swept Shanghai in the 1920s and 1930s and drew an especially devoted following among women. Corroborated.
WHY: a Western reader hears only "opera" and misses that this is a specific, then-fashionable Shanghai genre with its own romantic repertoire and largely female audience.

NOTE-ADD p256
ANCHOR: to the clouds and rain
NOTE: &#8216;They went together to the clouds and rain&#8217; renders 共赴巫山 (literally &#8216;went together to Mount Wu&#8217;), the standard classical euphemism for the act of love. It descends from the &#8216;Rhapsody on Gaotang&#8217; (高唐赋) attributed to Song Yu, in which the goddess of Mount Wu comes to the King of Chu in a dream and, on parting, names herself the morning clouds and the evening rain; ever since, &#8216;clouds and rain&#8217; (云雨) has meant sexual union. Corroborated.
WHY: the phrase is an opaque allusion in English; without the note the euphemism, and the fact that it names a famous literary source, is lost.
