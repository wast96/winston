# ch21 The Tanglong Door — R3 densification

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: the Jardine steamship company's Fusheng
NOTE: Jardine, Matheson (怡和洋行, the &#8216;Ewo&#8217; firm), one of the great British trading houses of the China coast, ran regular coastal passenger steamers between Shanghai, Hong Kong and Guangzhou through its Indo-China Steam Navigation Company. The <i>Fusheng</i> here and the <i>Guisheng</i> that later carries Chen Qianli back are the novel&#8217;s own vessels, but their names follow Jardine&#8217;s real ship-naming series, whose coast-and-cargo liners ended in &#8211;<i>sheng</i> (生; Cantonese &#8211;<i>sang</i>). (The firm and its coastal line are real and corroborated; the two ships are invented on an authentic pattern.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: a clipping bureau, which kept press-cuttings sorted by category
NOTE: Ling Wen&#8217;s route to the old news rests on real press geography. Guangzhou&#8217;s newspapers clustered in the wharf-districts of Xiguan (西关), the city&#8217;s &#8216;newspaper street&#8217;; the Seventh and Eighth <i>fu</i> (甫) there were merged and renamed Guangfu Road (光复路) in 1931, the street&#8217;s correct name in the novel&#8217;s 1933. The Guangzhou Press Association (广州报界公会) was a real body, founded in 1908. (Street and association corroborated; a press-association back-number clipping service is plausible for the period but not independently attested.)
