# ch26 The Guisheng — R3 densification (osmanthus taro relocated from ch31, its first appearance)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: by the Qinhuai River
NOTE: The <i>Qinhuai River</i> (秦淮河), the tributary threading through old Nanjing past the Confucius Temple and the Zhanyuan, was for centuries the city&#8217;s pleasure quarter, its painted boats, singing-houses and cultivated courtesans (the famed &#8216;Eight Beauties of Qinhuai&#8217;) a byword in Chinese letters. Nanjingers call it the city&#8217;s mother river. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: Minnan tangerine-red cakes
NOTE: <i>Tangerine-red cakes</i> (橘红糕, <i>juhonggao</i>) are a soft glutinous-rice sweet, tinted red and white and scented with candied citrus peel, chewy and mildly sweet, dusted with rice flour so the pieces do not stick. The <i>Minnan</i> (闽南, southern Fujian) kind named here is one attested regional version; Wenzhou and the Jiangnan towns claim the same sweet. (Corroborated; the regional attribution varies by source.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: osmanthus-sugar taro shoots, always cried for sale
NOTE: <i>Osmanthus-sugared taro shoots</i> (桂花糖芋苗) are a traditional Nanjing sweet: small taro corms simmered with a pinch of alkali, which reddens them, then finished in osmanthus-sugar syrup and thickened with lotus-root starch. They are counted among Nanjing&#8217;s best-loved street sweets. (Real; the tie to Mafu Street is the novel&#8217;s local color rather than a documented association.)
