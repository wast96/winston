### ch07 authority: Wusong bar -> mouth of the Wusong River TOUCH

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: the mouth of the Wusong River
NOTE: The <i>mouth of the Wusong River</i> (吴淞口, Woosung), where the Huangpu joins the Yangtze, was the outer anchorage of the port of Shanghai; incoming steamers lay there to take on a river pilot and be boarded by the Settlement's foreign police before proceeding up to the city wharves. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: the smell of salted fish and cured meats
NOTE: Against the New Year, Shanghai households hang out <i>salted fish and cured meats</i> (腊味) to dry&#8212;the winter provisioning that, with firecrackers and the settling of debts, marks the approach of the festival. (Texture.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: circling the Post Office building
NOTE: The <i>Post Office building</i> Chen Qianli circles is the Shanghai General Post Office (上海邮政总局), the landmark at the north end of the Sichuan Road Bridge over Suzhou Creek, built in 1922&#8211;24 with a columned façade and a Baroque clock tower&#8212;then reckoned the finest post office in the Far East. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: the Shuimo Bookshop and the Xinken Bookshop in Gongyi Lane
NOTE: The <i>Shuimo Bookshop</i> (水沫书店) and <i>Xinken Bookshop</i> (辛垦书店) in <i>Gongyi Lane</i> (公益坊) were real: Gongyi Lane, off North Sichuan Road in Hongkou, was a nest of small leftist and modernist presses, and the Shuimo Bookshop was founded in 1928 by the writers Shi Zhecun (施蛰存), Dai Wangshu (戴望舒) and Liu Na'ou (刘呐鸥). This was the ground of the leftist literary world the novel keeps returning to. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: straight on to Shengmuyuan Road next to it
NOTE: <i>Shengmuyuan Road</i> (圣母院路, 'Convent Road,' the French Concession's Route des Sœurs, today Ruijin Yi Road) was named for the Xujiahui convent; Old Fang's escape runs from Pu'enjishi Road through the through-lanes to it. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: cloth banners calling for a boycott of Japanese goods
NOTE: The banners call for a <i>boycott of Japanese goods</i> (抵制日货): the anti-Japanese economic movement that surged after the Mukden Incident of 1931 and the January&#160;28 war, when patriotic associations pressed shops to drop Japanese wares&#8212;so effective that Japanese imports to Shanghai for a time collapsed. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: in July of the eighteenth year of the Republic
NOTE: Dates are here reckoned by the <i>Republican calendar</i> (民国纪年), which counts 1912 as year one; the <i>eighteenth year of the Republic</i> is thus 1929 (1911&#160;+&#160;18). (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: the Great Revolution had been in ruins two years
NOTE: The <i>Great Revolution</i> (大革命, also 国民革命) is the standard name for the years 1924&#8211;27 of the Nationalist&#8211;Communist alliance and the Northern Expedition, which collapsed in the 1927 split and purge; 'in ruins two years' places Yi Junnian's transfer to Shanghai in 1929. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: was making ready a modern-style ceremony
NOTE: A <i>modern-style ceremony</i> renders 文明结婚, the 'civilized wedding' that spread in Republican cities&#8212;a reformed, partly Westernized rite, with an officiant and vows, that replaced the traditional 'six rites.' (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: this stretch of shikumen houses
NOTE: <i>Shikumen</i> (石库门, 'stone-framed gate') are the terraced lane-houses that were the characteristic dwelling of old Shanghai, built in long rows behind a stone doorway, each with its small courtyard-well (天井) and a flat rooftop drying-terrace (晒台). Because the rows abut roof to roof, they make the over-the-roofs escape Chen Qianli takes. (Corroborated.)
