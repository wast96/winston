NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: the New Stage on Penglai Road
NOTE: The <i>New Stage</i> (新舞台) was a landmark of the modern Chinese theater: opened in 1908 by the reforming Peking-opera actors Pan Yuejiao (潘月樵) and the Xia brothers, it was the first Chinese-built playhouse with a Western proscenium stage and a home of the reformist &#8216;civilized drama&#8217; (文明戏). Its documented sites, however, were at Shiliupu and, later, the Nine-Mu Field inside the old city, and it had closed by the late 1920s; a working theater on Penglai Road in 1933 answers rather to the later Penglai Grand Theater (蓬莱大戏院), so the novel may conflate the two. (The New Stage is real and corroborated; its placement on Penglai Road here is uncorroborated.)

ANCHOR: the Military Commission's secret investigation group
NOTE: Two rival Nationalist secret services divided this work. Ye Qinian&#8217;s Special Operations Headquarters grew from the Kuomintang&#8217;s party-run Party Affairs Investigation Section (the forerunner of the 中统); the body Mu Chuan is joining is the military one &#8212; the Military Commission&#8217;s investigation apparatus, built on Dai Li&#8217;s (戴笠) Special Services Department, established in 1932 and later nicknamed the 军统. The descriptive phrase is accurate to 1933; the label &#8216;军统&#8217; itself dates only from 1938. Their turf rivalry is the friction beneath this cordial scene. (Real; corroborated.)
