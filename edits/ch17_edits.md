ANCHOR: harmony, reverence, purity, tranquility
NOTE: These four words &#8212; <i>wa, kei, sei, jaku</i> (&#21644;&#25964;&#28165;&#23492;) &#8212; are the canonical four principles of the Japanese Way of tea, by tradition distilled by the great tea master Sen no Riky&#363; (&#21315;&#21033;&#20241;, 1522&#8211;1591): harmony between guests and things, reverence for others, purity of heart and setting, and the tranquility that the first three yield. NOTE-ADD p160
ANCHOR: Lord Guan patrolling the city wall
NOTE: &#8216;Lord Guan patrolling the city wall&#8217; (&#20851;&#20844;&#24033;&#22478;) and &#8216;Han Xin numbering his troops&#8217; (&#38889;&#20449;&#28857;&#20853;) are the set names of two motions in Chaozhou-style gongfu tea: pouring in swift circles round the ranked cups so that all fill evenly, then dealing out the last strong drops one cup at a time so none is slighted. They borrow the names of two famous generals &#8212; Guan Yu (&#20851;&#32701;), the god of war of the <i>Three Kingdoms</i>, and Han Xin (&#38889;&#20449;), the great strategist of the founding of the Han. A-Chu&#8217;s flawless performance of them is his proof that the art of tea is Chinese. Corroborated.
WHY: these are named ritual motions of gongfu tea, alluding to two generals, that read as mere decoration to a reader who cannot place them.
