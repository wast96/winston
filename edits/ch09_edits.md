NOTE-ADD p002
ANCHOR: a black spirit-tablet
NOTE: A spirit-tablet (灵牌 or 牌位) is an upright wooden tablet inscribed with a dead person&#8217;s name&#8212;here &#8216;Spirit-Seat of Yang Muchu&#8217;&#8212;believed to house the soul and to be the focus of offerings: the incense, fruit, and flowers set before it in this little shrine. Households kept such tablets for their ancestors on a domestic altar and tended them as the abiding presence of the dead. That a family should enshrine a photograph and tablet for a child said to have died in infancy is the first sign that his death is not what it seems. Corroborated.
WHY: the spirit-tablet, its inscription, and the offerings before it are a specific cult of the dead a Western reader will not recognize.

NOTE: The death-day (祭日, <i>jiri</i>) is the anniversary of a death, kept each year with mourning and offerings to the dead&#8212;a fixed occasion of the domestic ancestral cult, quite apart from any birthday. Madam Yang&#8217;s anguish is that her daughter&#8217;s birthday falls on the very day she must instead mourn her son. Corroborated.
