NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: that round, bridge-knobbed pot of vermilion zhuni clay
NOTE: <i>Zhuni</i> (朱泥, 'vermilion clay') is a prized, iron-rich red variety of the Yixing (宜兴) purple-sand (紫砂) stoneware from which China's most esteemed small teapots are made; the 'bridge-knobbed' lid names the little arched handle on top. A zhuni pot and a good rock tea mark Mu Chuan for a man of cultivated leisure. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: He rinsed the cups with the first steeping
NOTE: Mu Chuan brews in the <i>gongfu</i> (工夫茶) manner: a tiny pot, a first steeping poured off to rinse the cups and rouse the leaf, then repeated short infusions. The unhurried ritual is itself part of his self-presentation. (Texture.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: You used that German kit
NOTE: The Nationalist government did import quantities of German military equipment in these years, under a German advisory mission (Wetzell, then von Seeckt); but that materiel was for the army, and no record supports the garrison's being sent a German 'electric-torture apparatus.' The device belongs to the novel, hung on a real German-military backdrop. (The German equipment corroborated; the torture apparatus the novel's.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: this period of political tutelage
NOTE: The <i>period of political tutelage</i> (训政) was the stage of one-party rule the Nationalists proclaimed in October 1928: under Sun Yat-sen's three-stage scheme (military government, tutelage, then constitutional rule), the Party would exercise power on the people's behalf until they were judged ready for it. Mu Chuan's point is that under tutelage the courts, however cowed, could not simply be dispensed with. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: took a bottle of chemical from a drawer
NOTE: The line of writing brought up on the letter is <i>invisible ink</i> (密写): a message in a colorless fluid&#8212;here developed by a chemical reagent, elsewhere in the book by rice-water and iodine&#8212;that shows only when the paper is treated. It was a staple of underground correspondence. (Texture.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: the Xujiahui postal sub-office
NOTE: <i>Xujiahui</i> (徐家汇, the old 'Siccawei/Zikawei') was a district in the southwest of the city, long the center of the Jesuit mission&#8212;cathedral, observatory, orphanage and convent. The 'box to be called for at the counter' is a poste-restante box, a classic dead-drop for the underground's mail. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: Brother Fang, as though we met face to face
NOTE: The letter's opening, '<i>as though we met face to face</i>,' renders 如晤, a conventional salutation of the classical Chinese personal letter (literally 'as if seeing you'), used to an intimate or a junior; it sets the close, resolute register of the whole message. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: Nanjing's Xiaguan Station
NOTE: <i>Xiaguan</i> (下关) was Nanjing's railway-and-wharf district on the south bank of the Yangtze; its station, opened in 1908, was the Nanjing terminus of the Shanghai&#8211;Nanjing Railway, so that a traveler up from Shanghai arrived there. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: the Blue Steel Express
NOTE: The <i>Blue Steel Express</i> (蓝钢快车) was the luxury express of the Shanghai&#8211;Nanjing Railway, named for its blue-painted steel carriages; introduced about 1930, it ran the route in some five and a half hours and was the fastest and costliest way between the two cities. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: something more like a family law held sway
NOTE: <i>Family law</i> (家法) is the private disciplinary authority a household or clan wielded over its own; here it is the metaphor for the secret service's internal code, under which 'even execution was possible.' (Texture.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: doesn't much seem to be their central special commissioner
NOTE: A <i>central special commissioner</i> (中央特派员) was an agent sent down directly by the Party Central to a local body with special authority&#8212;the role the enemy is trying to fix on one of the prisoners. (Texture.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: wealthy merchants of the two Guang provinces
NOTE: The <i>two Guang</i> (两广) are Guangdong and Guangxi, the two southern provinces taken together. (Texture.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: They're in single-line contact
NOTE: <i>Single-line contact</i> (单线联系) is the underground's basic security rule: each worker knows only the one comrade above and the one below, so that a captured link cannot betray the whole chain. (Texture.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: I'll send you some new trainees from the Hangzhou training class
NOTE: The <i>Hangzhou training class</i> points to a real institution: the Zhejiang Provincial Police Officers' School (浙江警官学校) at Hangzhou, founded in 1928, which from 1932 became a training ground for Dai Li's Nationalist secret service. (Corroborated.)

NOTE-ADD
ANCHOR: must be the man who opened fire on Pu'enjishi Road
NOTE: <i>Pu'enjishi Road</i> (普恩济世路) was a real street of the French Concession&#8212;Route John Prentice, laid out in 1926 and today Jinxian Road (进贤路)&#8212;running near the Lyceum Theatre; the bun-shop rendezvous on it is the novel's. (Corroborated.)
