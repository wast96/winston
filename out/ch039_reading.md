### Peddling Obscene Books

The selling of obscene books and obscene pictures has always been against the law, and once a man is caught out and hauled into court he is punished as the statute directs. But punish them as you will, the selling goes on all the same; this class of small hawker seems to feel that without the obscene-book trade there is no other line of business left to it.

For a while they even set up a stall in the open and laid out a whole array of obscene books — such as The Apricot-Blossom Sky, The Candlewick Monk, A History of Sex, The Art of Sex, The Carnal Prayer Mat, and the like — wretchedly printed and yet asking a stiff price. A book he names at five mao, say, you beat down to one, and he lets it go. And so, selling three or four a day, they can keep body and soul together. Later, when the authorities took to arresting them in earnest, they changed their tactics, no longer daring to sell in the open, but hiding the various obscene books away and laying out on the stall a few very ordinary novels — these last all in copies gone yellow in the paper and broken at the cover — not meaning that a customer should buy them at all, but only to hang up a front and keep out of the eye of the law. A man passing by chances to halt his step and give the stall a glance, and the hawker says softly to him: "Sir, I've a good book here in my bundle — care to buy one and have a look?" This is the secret catch by which they do their trade, and the "good book" they speak of is just The Apricot-Blossom Sky, The Candlewick Monk, and the rest of that kind.
