## Chapter Seventeen

There was no wind. On the pale blue-green curtain of the sky a few white clouds had come to rest, and the moon's smiling face peered down through the rifts to spy out the secrets of the world below. The Huangpu was like a shining band of greyish-yellow, very peaceful, very glad. A little steam-launch slowly clove that glassy surface and gave one imperious, resounding blast. On her deck a string of little red and green electric lamps had been hung, vying in the cool of that night with the thronging stars of the sky. This was a boat gone out to make merry.

This was just the stretch off Gaoqiao sand, where the river runs broad; the little launch drove solemnly northward, and industrial, financial downtown Shanghai fell away further and further behind. The tall chimney of the waterworks-and-power plant was the last sentinel of industrial Shanghai, and in the blink of an eye it too had slipped past. On either bank the sleeping fields lay under the moonlight as though wrapped in a thin veil of pale-grey smoke.

The revellers on the deck of the little launch were all somewhat half-drunk. More than twenty minutes of unbroken hilarity had tired even their tongues, and now they lay back quietly, their faces turned up to the mysterious grandeur of nature on this moonlit night; and on their wine-flushed faces there gradually surfaced the lonely, idle vexation of men with nothing left to do. And this crew, who day after day were plunged head over heels in the great turning-wheel of life, now that they were far from the centre of the struggle, opening their eyes all at once upon those tranquil fields and that boundless dusk, felt the wounds that life's battle had left on their hearts gently soothed, and could not help sighing over a thousand things. So on top of the lonely, idle vexation there was added the grief of the mutability of human affairs, and the feverish, itching craving for some fresh and startling sensation.

This mood was felt most keenly of all, among this crew, by Wu Sunfu. Tonight's fine outing of pleasure had been of his proposing; a few close friends—Sun Jiren, Wang Hefu, Han Mengxiang, and one woman besides, Xu Manli. And tonight's elegant gathering was for Xu Manli too. By her own account, twenty-four years ago, when this same moon was first rising, she had come down into this dusty world. The lamps strung about the boat, the wine and dishes laid on the table, were all for this birthday of hers! And it was for this that Sun Jiren had specially wired for this newly built launch of the Zhenjiang–Yangzhou line to be brought out!

The boat was going slower still. The chug—chug of the engine came crawling up from the hold below like a lullaby. The mate, gauging the mood of the bosses, had throttled down to half speed; the deck was so steady one could have stood an egg on end upon it. Suddenly Wu Sunfu turned his face and asked Sun Jiren:

"If this boat opened up full throttle, how many li would she do in an hour?"

"Forty li, say. Riding light as she is today, maybe forty-six or forty-seven li. But she pitches horribly! What of it? You want to open her up?"

Wu Sunfu nodded and gave a little smile. Sun Jiren had put his finger on his secret trouble. His stifled heart was craving just that—some violent stimulation of speed and force. But over on the other side Wang Hefu offered an objection that was also, at bottom, a deeper insight:

"It's all empty out here, only our one boat; you'd get no flavour out of opening her up! Let's go back—somewhere lively, off the Bund and the Public Garden, out on the crowded water, and let her show her paces there. That would be something!"

"Don't be in such a hurry! Let's run out to the Woosung mouth and turn back to Shanghai—now, first, full speed ahead!"

said Xu Manli in her most silvery voice. At once the whole party applauded. In the thick of their earlier unbridled joking there had been a "compact": tonight no one might gainsay a single frown or smile of this youthful "birthday-mother." The order for full speed was passed down on the instant; the engine broke into a clattering roar, and the hull shuddered as though it had the ague; the white surf flung up at the bow stood a foot high, and to left and right two white ribbons of wake uncoiled and trailed far behind. Splash! Splash! The waters of the Huangpu roared their wrath. On deck those several half-drunken bosses all turned up their faces and laughed aloud.

"Let's make the most of today, and leave a lasting memento of it! Let's ask our Sun Jiren to rename this boat the 'Manli'! Is everyone agreed?"

Han Mengxiang cried out loudly, holding his wine-cup high; but just then the boat rounded a bend, and Han Mengxiang, swaying, lost his footing and lurched against Wang Hefu, his one brimming cup of champagne splashing full onto the head of Xu Manli, who sat next to Wang Hefu, and drenching her loose long hair through and through. "Ai—ha!" Wu Sunfu and the others cried out in dismay, and then burst into a roar of laughter. Laughing, and shaking the wine out of her hair, Xu Manli scolded him with a pretty show of temper:

"Mengxiang, you blundering fool! My hair's all wine now—you shall just have to suck it dry!"

This was of course no more than a joke, but Wang Hefu, as it happened, heard it very distinctly; he clapped his two hands together with a smack and called out in a loud voice:

"Did everyone hear that clearly? The Queen Mother of the West commands Han Mengxiang to suck the wine-stains from her hair! Suck them dry! Did everyone hear? Mengxiang! This is a plum of a commission, the very best under heaven—report for duty, quick—"

"Oh dear, oh dear! It was only a joke, it doesn't count!"

Xu Manli hurriedly cut Wang Hefu short, and gave his shin a soft little kick with her foot, to make him leave off his nonsense. But Wang Hefu made as though he did not understand, and went on crying out over and over, "Mengxiang, report for duty!" Wu Sunfu and Sun Jiren clapped and cheered. Here at last was a fresh stimulation to scour away their grey moods, and they were not going to let it slip lightly; the more so as three parts of wine had veiled their faces. Han Mengxiang grinned with a shameless, fawning face, and seemed not in the least unwilling. It was the seasoned Xu Manli, of all people, who by way of exception grew bashful. With a feigned smile she shot Wu Sunfu and the rest a glance. Six wine-reddened eyes were fastened on her, as though watching a monkey do its tricks. A faint sense of being toyed with rippled gently across her heart. But only for a moment, and then the feeling was gone. She pressed her lips and tittered. To be commanded, and watched into the bargain, while she did this thing—she did after all feel it a little awkward.

Wang Hefu, however, had already given the order to move. He seized Han Mengxiang's head in both hands and pushed it forward to Xu Manli's face. Xu Manli, tittering, swayed her upper body to the left and so came to rest against Wu Sunfu's shoulder, and Wu Sunfu, roaring with laughter, put out his hand, caught hold of Xu Manli's head, and steered it straight to Han Mengxiang's lips. Sun Jiren played master of ceremonies, and in the din of laughter chanted:

"One suck! A second suck! A third—suck! The rite is done!"

"Well, thank you, the whole tribe of you! My hair's dirtier than ever now—champagne, and spittle on top of it!"

said Xu Manli coquettishly, smoothing her hair, and laughed again. Wang Hefu, as though his sport were still not spent, answered at once:

"Then let's have it all over again! Only mind you don't put on airs and make believe you're too shy!"

"Enough of that! Manli broke the compact herself—let's all frame up a rule of penalties!"

Wu Sunfu had changed his tack; feeling that the sting had gone out of the thing before his eyes, he demanded something newer still. Han Mengxiang, who loved dancing, proposed that Xu Manli give them a foxtrot. Sun Jiren, staid and cautious, and afraid of an accident, hurriedly headed it off:

"That won't do! The deck pitches too badly—it's no joke to go over into the Huangpu! And the rule of penalties needn't be settled today either; let everybody think it over at leisure."

By now the little launch had reached the Woosung mouth. Off the bar three or four foreign warships lay at anchor, the mast-head lights on their mainmasts glittering high in the air like several very large stars. On one of the warships a bugle wailed up out of the dark, and then, of a sudden, fell silent again. On every side, stretching away without bound, was the desolate moonlight and the colour of the water. The little launch throttled down to half speed and came about in a great winding circle—she was turning her head, making ready to go back to Shanghai. Suddenly Wang Hefu said, very gravely:

"This afternoon two American gunboats and three Japanese torpedo-boats got urgent orders and sailed for Hankou; nobody knows why. Jiren, has your office had any telegram from Changsha? They say things are very touch-and-go there again!"

"A telegram did come, but it said nothing about all that!"

"Perhaps it had been through the censor and couldn't go into detail. The word I heard was something about the Communist bandits meaning to attack Changsha! Hmph!"

"That's another of the Japanese rumours. The news agencies the Japanese run are forever saying how formidable the Communist bandits are in the two provinces of Hunan and Jiangxi—how touch-and-go it is at Changsha, and at Ji'an too! There was that rumour on the exchange today as well, but it had no effect on the market; the market was steady enough today!"

said Han Mengxiang, and yawned. It was catching; Xu Manli was the first to be infected; Sun Jiren opened his mouth wide, then checked himself just in time, and turning his face to Wu Sunfu said:

"What the Japanese say isn't necessarily all rumour. Things really are in a bad way in those two provinces! With the great war between North and South locked in stalemate, the troops of the two provinces are only ever drawn off to the front, never brought back; the garrisons are thin, and while they mind the one thing they lose the other, so that the Communist bandits raise disturbances everywhere. What it will all come to in the end, no one dares to say!"

"Things really are past all telling nowadays. At the outset everyone reckoned the war would be over in two months at the most; who would have thought two and a half months would go by and it still could not be settled. But the dead and wounded at the front really are past belief! Staff Officer Lei Ming, who has long campaigned, only shakes his head when he speaks of it. By the reckoning of those army men, the two sides have mobilized three million men this time, and the dead and wounded already come to no less than three hundred thousand! Truly an unheard-of great war!"

As Wu Sunfu said this his manner was utterly dispirited; he closed his eyes and stroked his chin. Xu Manli, who had been silent a long while, suddenly cried out in alarm too:

"Oh! Those wounded soldiers, how dreadful they are! Hardly like human beings any more! Boatload after boatload, trainload after trainload, brought in day after day! Why, all along the Shanghai–Nanjing and the Shanghai–Hangzhou railways, in every town great and small, there are hospitals for the wounded; the temples are packed full, so they lodge them in the guild-halls; the guild-halls are packed full, so they lodge them in the schools; and sometimes, with nowhere to put them, they pass a few days out on the station platforms, in the wind and rain! Ai, 'above there is heaven, below there is Suzhou and Hangzhou'—and now the whole Suzhou–Hangzhou country has turned into a world of wounded soldiers!"

"By about the end of July, by the solar calendar, it ought to be settled, oughtn't it? With the dead and wounded so heavy, it can't be dragged out very long!"

Wu Sunfu ventured another note of optimism, and forced a smile. But Wang Hefu shook his head and said, drawing out his words:

"I doubt it—I doubt it! They say new-style trenches have been dug near Xuzhou, with foreign advisers overseeing the work, warranted to hold out a year! A year! On these trenches alone, they say, three million was spent—some say five million! It looks as if they're bound to go on fighting right through the New Year this year; a wretched business it is!"

"And besides, however many are killed and wounded, fresh recruits are being enlisted too! At Zhenjiang, Suzhou, Hangzhou, Ningbo, there are recruiting-commissioners everywhere; every day there are new recruits—three to five hundred at the fewest, a thousand at the most—sent up to Shanghai and on to Nanjing to be trained! At the North Station in Shanghai there's a great recruiting-banner too, and every day they take in two or three hundred!"

Han Mengxiang, half by design and half not, dealt yet another mortal blow to Wu Sunfu's optimistic line.

Nobody had anything more to say. Was the great war between North and South going to drag on beyond all expectation?—that was the sentence written in the eyes that these four men and one woman crossed with one another on the deck. The sound of the launch's engine had changed from a clatter to a heavy thudding, and each stroke fell into the hearts of the five and made their hearts the heavier. But with the two of them, Xu Manli and Han Mengxiang, this was only a passing thing, gone in a moment; those in whom it would not pass, and grew heavier and heavier, were the three bosses—Wu Sunfu, Sun Jiren, Wang Hefu. If the war was to drag on without end, their enterprises were done for!

Just then a thin mist rose off the water, and far away there was lightning again, and a stirring of thunder. The wind rose too, a southeast wind, blowing full in their faces, mighty strong. The little launch charged furiously on into the wind, and the sound of the water was like the shouting of a thousand armies and ten thousand horse, while the lights on the two banks of teeming Shanghai, drawing nearer and nearer, flickered in the thin mist.

"I'm bored to death! How is it you've all turned mute at one stroke?"

Xu Manli's coquettish voice throbbed through the stifling air. She was much put out, feeling that a merry evening had been ruthlessly befouled by wounded soldiers and trenches, and she meant to work her own peculiar magic to retrieve the deadlock! Han Mengxiang, who was the readiest to fall in with a mood, answered at once:

"Let's all drink one cup together, and then each of us drink a cup in homage to the birthday-mother—how's that?"

There was no one who did not agree. Though the stifled gloom and dejection in the hearts of Wu Sunfu and the others was nothing that a few cups of wine had power to dissolve, still, wine could turn their care off into another channel, and could change that care into merriment. Thereupon Wang Hefu said:

"We've drunk all the wine; let's have a little entertainment. Jiren, tell the skipper to open her up full speed, all she'll do! Manli, you stand up on this table, one-legged like the golden cockerel, and mind you don't put the other leg down. Afraid of a tumble? Never fear! The four of us will guard the four sides, and whoever's side you fall on, his is the lucky star, he'll make his fortune this very month!"

"I won't! With the boat come to a busy stretch, what would it look like!"

Xu Manli made a show of refusing, twisting at the waist as if to slip away. The four men laughed aloud, and answered her, one and all, with applause. Laughing, Wu Sunfu of a sudden caught Xu Manli round the waist and, smack, swung her up onto the table, and held her there, and would not let her come down, crying:

"Every man guard his own post! Manli, no cheating! Quick, quick!"

Xu Manli had no thought now of escaping, but she was so weak with laughing that her legs would not bear her up. The four men guarded the four sides and, roaring with laughter, urged her on. The boat drove madly forward, like a horse gone wild. Xu Manli had scarcely stood up straight and lifted one leg when the wind caught her clothes and rolled them up backwards, right over her face; she gave a twist at the waist and toppled off at a slant. Sun Jiren and Han Mengxiang both sprang over and caught her. "First prize drawn, drawn! Two winners! Up with her again! Now the second prize!"

Wang Hefu shouted, roaring with laughter, and clapping his hands, when all at once the boat's siren gave a hideous shriek that startled the whole revelling party, and then the hull jolted violently backwards, as though it would leap sheer into the air, and the cups and plates on the table were flung down clattering onto the deck. The five of them all lurched. Han Mengxiang, who was standing furthest out, all but went into the Huangpu. The faces of all five had gone livid. The boat had stopped too, and the sailors were racing along both gunwales with long bamboo poles. Across the water there came faintly a shout:

"Help! Help!"

A sampan had been run down and capsized. And so Xu Manli's "second prize" had, perforce, to go undrawn. Wu Sunfu knit his brows, and sneered coldly to himself.

The sailors first made the sampan fast, and a man, drenched to the skin, came up out of the water, clinging to the sampan's stern-sweep. He was the one who had been sculling her, and he alone had gone in. Ten minutes later Sun Jiren's little launch was driving ahead once more, straight for the Bronze-Man Wharf. The five aboard laughed on as before; they could not be still, for the moment they fell still they would feel the unbearable oppression, and the darkness of the times ahead and the crisis of their own affairs, which set them shivering to the very marrow, would begin to gnaw savagely at their hearts.

It was midnight now, twelve o'clock. The greater part of the men of industrial and financial Shanghai lay moaning in nightmares of a struggle of flesh and blood, but in the bar of the night-club there was a tinkling of knives and forks and the pop and hiss of bottles being opened. Wu Sunfu, his right hand cupped over his wine-glass, his left propping up his head, watched aimlessly the people passing in and out of the bar. Though the two of them, he and Wang Hefu, had already drunk half a bottle of dark grape wine, there was not the least flush on their faces; the wine might have been clear water, for all it could stir their leaden gloom. And they themselves could not tell why they were so leaden with gloom.

After going ashore at the Bronze-Man Wharf they had raised a half-hour's uproar at Xu Manli's, and had called besides at the notorious secret bower at Number Ninety-Four, where they set the madam there a poser of a problem; and now, in this night-club too, they had spent half an hour, and had had their turn at pai-gow and at bao. But every one of these devices for driving off care was of no use! Both of them felt as though their chests had been stuffed full of india-rubber, so that the one heart within stuck fast and could not be worked loose; both felt as though they were grown all over with invisible thorns, and had no road anywhere. What galled them most of all was that those cunning, scheme-hatching brains of theirs seemed to have been glued up by something too—as good as dead; only a violent stimulation could stir them a little, and even then only for a moment.

"Ai! Not an ounce of strength in my whole body!"

Wu Sunfu muttered to himself, and picked up his glass and drank a mouthful, his eyes still gazing dazedly at the shifting shadows of the people coming and going in the bar.

"Can't rouse myself, ugh! It's a good five or six days now—can't rouse myself!"

Wang Hefu answered with a yawn. The eyes of the two of them met for a moment, then parted again, and each went on with his aimless staring. Those two remarks of theirs were lost in the air. Speaker and hearer alike seemed not to be their own selves speaking, their own selves hearing; the world of their consciousness was an absolute blank!

Suddenly three or four men came in, thronging about a tall, big-framed fellow, chattering and laughing, and ran past the edge of Wu Sunfu's table and off, like a gust of wind, to the back of the bar. On the numbed nerves of the two of them it was like the sudden jab of a needle! The eyes of the two men met in one place, and a wry smile showed at the corners of both their mouths. Still muttering to himself, Wu Sunfu said:

"There now—wasn't that Old Zhao?"

"Old Zhao!"

Wang Hefu echoed the two words back, and instinctively glanced towards the inner part of the bar. And in the same instant he instinctively asked:

"And who were those others?"

"I didn't see clearly. At all events that old fellow Shang Zhongli wasn't among them."

"One of them, the one in spectacles, looked like—oh, I have it, it was Li Yuting, the one who's forever coming to your mansion!"

"Was it he? Heh, heh!"

Wu Sunfu laughed softly, and picked up his glass and drank again. But a man in spectacles came running out from within, straight up to Wu Sunfu's table—it was indeed Li Yuting. He had come over on purpose to pay his respects to the two bosses. Wang Hefu laughed aloud:

"Talk of Cao Cao, and Cao Cao arrives! How is it you university professors go the rounds of the night-clubs too, eh? I'll put you in my paper tomorrow!"

"Oh, oh, it was Lawyer Qiu who dragged me here. Have you seen him?"

"No. But we saw Old Zhao come in along with you."

Wu Sunfu had let this fall merely at random, yet in a flash a ring of red flushed up over the roots of Li Yuting's ears. There rose in his heart, too, the very fluster and unease of a woman who has been playing her husband false and is caught in the act by the man himself. He forced a smile, and cast about for something to say:

"They say the capital is to be moved to Hangzhou! Maybe it's only a rumour, but it's all over town outside; haven't you heard it?"

The two of them both shook their heads, though within there was a strange taste in their mouths, part gladness, part gloom. Li Yuting went on:

"In the North they want to set up a government, and here there's this talk of moving the capital to Hangzhou—which means that neither side will make peace, both mean to fight it out to the end and settle who wins and who loses! Sunfu, the war is going to drag on! It may well be a year or half a year! Since the founding of the Republic, this must be reckoned the fiercest war of all; the numbers mobilized, the length of time it drags on, are alike unheard-of! The battle-line is long, too, and several of the central provinces have been drawn into the whirlpool! And the Communist bandits, into the bargain, are raising disturbances everywhere. The general situation is truly one to be pessimistic about!"

"Live one day, and count it a day!"

said Wang Hefu with a sigh; never before had he been so cast down. Li Yuting felt very wretched to hear it, and turned his eyes to look at Wu Sunfu, and there was a face all bewilderment and feverish anxiety. This too was a thing Li Yuting had never seen before. Li Yuting could not help sighing himself, and cast about again for something to say, to dispel the unbearable murk:

"But of late the bond market has steadied and stood firm—no great slump; which shows that people in general are still optimistic about the times ahead!"

"Ha, ha! Quite right!"

Wu Sunfu said suddenly with a savage grin, and threw Wang Hefu a look. Wang Hefu had not yet taken it in, but Li Yuting had seen the meaning first; he perceived on the instant that he had blundered again without meaning to, and had touched the hidden sore of Wu Sunfu and his friends. He hastily covered it over with a spell of dry laughter, and, taking Lawyer Qiu once more for his theme, turned the talk into another channel:

"A native bank in the South Market has gone under—short by four hundred thousand and more, of which deposits make up four-fifths. The depositors have now jointly retained Lawyer Qiu to go to law for them. Sunfu, your kinsman Fan Bowen has been caught by this bad debt too! Lately he's given up writing poetry and taken to studying the Code of Civil Procedure! They say that bank came to grief through playing the bonds as well!"

Wu Sunfu nodded and smiled—he was smiling at the thought of Fan Bowen taking up the study of law only after being caught by a bad debt. Wang Hefu said flatly:

"If nobody goes bankrupt, how is anybody to make a fortune! The ones who have the worst luck of it are those scattered little depositors!"

"Just so! I've been thinking myself that the flourishing of the Shanghai money-trade these last few years is no wholesome sign. It's the flourishing of industry that is the true, proper road for a nation's economic life! Yet of late Shanghai's industry has been going downhill fast, like a river to the sea. Take even the cigarette trade, a luxury trade—it's not doing so well either; these two or three years the new cigarette factories opened in Shanghai really are not few, but in point of business they fall short of the foreign firms after all. And they've been hit by the war besides. Even the China Tobacco Company, the oldest brand of all and the largest in capital, is going to shut down its Shanghai manufactory for the time being. And this in a luxury trade!"

Li Yuting delivered this little discourse as though moved past bearing, and rose to his feet meaning to go, when suddenly he bent down again, put his mouth close to Wu Sunfu's ear, and said in a low voice:

"Old Zhao has a great scheme on foot, and would like to talk it over with you—suppose you go over and have a word? It's a bit quieter over there than here."

Wu Sunfu was struck dumb, and for a moment made no answer at all. Li Yuting gave a chuckling laugh, as though to say "please yourself," and turned and went off.

Gazing at Li Yuting's retreating back, Wu Sunfu sank stock-still into a brown study. He could not fathom what Zhao Botao meant by sending word to him, nor why Li Yuting had been so furtive and hole-and-corner about it, as though he wished to keep it from Wang Hefu. He turned and gave Wang Hefu a glance, and then made up his mind to go and see what game Old Zhao was playing.

"Hefu, Li Yuting said just now that Old Zhao has something he wants to talk over with us; let's go and have a word."

"Oh!—You go, then. I'll go over and watch a round of bao. Old Zhao wants to play at being Napoleon—win himself a battle, and then bring out the diplomatic despatches!"

The two of them looked at each other and burst out laughing, feeling the gloom in their hearts a little lightened for the moment.

And so Wu Sunfu went alone to meet Old Zhao; and having settled himself face to face with Zhao Botao at a little round table in a corner by the wall, he made an effort to put on a composed smile. Since their last "collaboration," in the month and more that had passed, the two men had met a good many times in the way of society, but only to exchange a few offhand civilities; and now they were once more to begin a confidential talk face to face. Zhao Botao was in his usual brisk, high-spirited vein; without beating about the bush, he opened by asserting his own superiority, straight off, out of all their past entanglements:

"Sunfu, it's high time we said a few frank and open words to each other. Our old accounts can be wiped clean at a stroke! But there are one or two things I can't help declaring to you first: in the first place, this banking-syndicate trust—I have a share in it, and we have a whole plan; but for one thing, we don't refuse to let others come in with us, and for another, we won't gulp down our food the moment we see it; we never once thought of using our whole force against you, we take no interest in the silk-reeling trade; Sunfu, that was your own over-suspicion!—"

Wu Sunfu gave a little smile and shrugged his shoulders. But Zhao Botao did not smile; his eyes shone with a piercing light. He drew hard at his cigar, and went on:

"You don't believe me? Then have it your own way. Frankly, that affair of Zhu Yinqiu's mortgage-loan—I was only having a bit of fun with you, I'd no set purpose of doing you a mischief. If you will have it that I've some tremendous scheme up my sleeve, well, no matter; perhaps if I'd been in your place I'd have taken the same view. Let's go on to the second matter. You people suspect me of using tricks everywhere to wreck the Yizhong; ha, ha; I did use a trick or two, only a trick or two, and I never used tricks 'everywhere.' You guess it was I who directed the 'economic blockade' from behind the scenes—ai, Sunfu! It's not that I couldn't have done it, but I wouldn't! When it's our own people, why fight to the death—what's the good of it!"

"Ha, ha, Botao! It seems it was all just our own over-suspicion! We misjudged you? Is that it?"

Wu Sunfu said with a wild laugh, cocking an eyebrow. But Zhao Botao stayed perfectly grave, and answered at once, weightily:

"Not so! I'm not saying all this to declare that everything in the past was a misunderstanding! What I want is for you to understand in your heart: between you and me there's no unresolvable feud, nor are we travelling wholly two different roads, nor is it a case of no room for me once there is you—even should the Yizhong prosper and thrive, it's not likely, by the look of things, to do me any easy harm, and so it's not worth my while to bring my whole force to bear against you! Nor, in fact, have I ever done so!"

This was downright the tone of a conqueror well pleased with himself. Wu Sunfu could bear it no longer, and shot back sharply:

"Botao! You surely didn't call me over here just for the sake of these few words?"

"Quite right, half of it was for these few words. Let it be, Sunfu, we'll say no more of the old accounts—as a matter of fact I'd another thing I meant to clear up with you in passing, but since you're out of patience with the listening, we'll let it drop. I'm a plain-spoken fellow, I don't beat about the bush; asking you over just now, what I had in mind was to see whether, after all, we two can still work together—"

"Oh, but, Botao, is there still another matter you'd clear up with me? That I should like to hear first."

Wu Sunfu cut Zhao Botao short, and made a point of smiling, to show how composed he was; yet his heart was in an uncommon flutter of unease. He thought all at once of the time, long ago, when he had first begun his fight with Old Zhao, and Du Zhuzhai had tried to mediate between them—"one must first win a battle, and then open negotiations, so as not to be held to ransom by Old Zhao": on that strategy he had, in those days, refused Du Zhuzhai; and little had he dreamed that things would now be turned clean about, host and guest changed places, so that it was Old Zhao, in the character of the victor, who proposed the "collaboration." That the affairs of men should be so mutable, and to such a degree—Wu Sunfu simply could not believe his own ears.

Zhao Botao too gave a faint smile, as though he had already seen clean through Wu Sunfu's state of mind. He said, very crisply:

"This third matter now, that really was a misunderstanding. You people always suppose that Du Zhuzhai was pulled over to my side; the truth is, I never pulled Du Zhuzhai over at all, whereas my own Han Mengxiang was truly angled away by you people! Sunfu, over this affair I take off my hat to the deftness of your hand!"

As Wu Sunfu listened his heart gave a jump he could not hold down, and his colour changed a little too; he covered it up quickly with a burst of wild laughter, and then, of set purpose, threw out a probe:

"So you only know of the one Han Mengxiang? I've bought over people more to the purpose than Han Mengxiang, I can tell you!"

"There may even be a woman or two! But that's neither here nor there. If you're willing to buy over the women, I'm truly much obliged to you! Women are too many for me, I can't cope with them all; heh, heh!"

It was Zhao Botao's turn now to hide his real feelings behind a forced smile. This too could not be hidden from Wu Sunfu's eye, and so Wu Sunfu felt in his turn some savour of victory; little by little he recovered his self-confidence after all, threw off the mood of defeat, plucked up his spirit, and went over to the attack. Straight off he turned the talk to that question of "collaboration":

"You've guessed quite right! Our buying-over policy is going smoothly enough as well! Botao, I fancy that even you yourself might be bought over! I too am a plain-spoken fellow, let's have no idle talk—you first put forward your terms of 'collaboration,' and if there's room to discuss them, I'll give you a frank and open answer!"

"Then, in one plain word—I'll introduce a banking syndicate to lend to the Yizhong Company! Three million in all, the first instalment of five hundred thousand to be paid down first, on condition that the entire property of the Yizhong Company stand as security!"

Wu Sunfu listened very intently, his eyes fixed hard on Zhao Botao's face. Suddenly he threw up his head and laughed aloud, and shrugged his shoulders. But Zhao Botao did not laugh; he smoked his cigar at his leisure, and waited quietly for Wu Sunfu's answer. When Wu Sunfu had laughed his fill, he asked with a straight face:

"Botao! Are you joking, or aren't you? The Yizhong holds to a policy of advancing step by step and entrenching at each; its plans may be large, but for the present it has no call for a loan of three million! The Yizhong has capital lying idle even now, and can find no outlet for it!"

"That's not the way to look at it. The total of the loan is three million; the first instalment of five hundred thousand is handed over first, and the handing-over of the second instalment is to be settled separately. You're an old hand, and you naturally understand that this loan is really only five hundred thousand—only, the syndicate that makes it gets the prior right to lend a further two and a half million!"

"But the Yizhong Company has no call even for a loan of five hundred thousand!"

"Is that so, indeed?"

"It is indeed!"

Wu Sunfu hardened his heart and answered with decision. But the words were no sooner out of his mouth than his heart fell to trembling. He knew that the very noose he had once slipped over Zhu Yinqiu's head was now taken up by Zhao Botao, widened out, and slipped over the Yizhong Company; he knew that, by this refusal of his, Old Zhao's grand economic blockade would now come in good earnest, and that the Yizhong Company, with the war unended and its eight factories glutting the market with over-production, if it met a grand economic blockade on top of it all, would have nothing before it but to close down or to sell out; he knew that this was the opening shot of that trust of Old Zhao's and his fellows!

Zhao Botao smiled and puffed out a mouthful of smoke, and pressed a step closer:

"In that case, then, we can't work together after all! The Yizhong Company has a great future before it, and it would be too great a pity to let it run aground and end like this! Sunfu, all your heart's blood shan't be thrown away for nothing; think it well over, the two of you, and then let me have your answer, how would that be? Sunfu, let's talk with the shutters open and everything above board: the Yizhong is short of ready money even now, I've known it this long while. And what's more, the war looks set to drag on and the front to widen further still, so that the goods of those Yizhong factories will find no sale within the year; think it well over, the two of you, and then let me have your answer!"

"Oh—"

Wu Sunfu answered thus, all vaguely, and of a sudden went soft; he seemed to hear a snap within his heart, as though his heart had been pulled to pieces and could nevermore be braced up again; he had lost his power of resistance, and lost his self-confidence, and there was but one thought turning round and round in his nerves: to surrender, then, on terms?

All at once he rose to his feet, sneering coldly. The last drop of strength had come back into him; and moreover he had no wish to let Old Zhao see clearly how bitterly wretched he was, and how ready to surrender; he clapped Old Zhao heavily on the shoulder, and said in a loud voice:

"Botao! As to how the situation will go in the end, every man has his own way of looking at it! It may well take a sudden turn for the better. As for the Yizhong Company, we on the inside are not one whit anxious. If there's a chance to take in capital and expand, why, so much the better. Tomorrow I'll lay your proposal before the board of directors, and later on we'll put our heads together again."

And then, with another wild laugh, Wu Sunfu, not waiting for Old Zhao to open his mouth, made haste to be gone. He sought out Wang Hefu, gave him the gist of what had passed, and knit his brows. For a good long while neither of them made a sound. At last Wang Hefu forced a single sentence out from between his teeth:

"Tomorrow morning Jiren and I will come round to your mansion and talk it over!"

It was already half past one when Wu Sunfu got home. A sky full of black cloud had blotted out the stars and the moon, and the garden of the Wu mansion lay dark and eerie, the wind stirring the leaves with a most mournful sound. The young mistress and all her party were not at home. The menservants and the maids were crowded into the gate-lodge, stealing a game of cards, laughing in a hubbub. It was not until the horn of Wu Sunfu's motor-car had sounded twice running outside the main gate that the crew of men and women in the gate-lodge heard it at last. The card-game broke up in a fright on the instant, and the menservants and maids scurried back each to his own post; but Wu Sunfu had noticed already, and so, the moment he stepped down from the car, his face was black as thunder. That the men and women servants should gamble at cards on the sly was a thing he absolutely forbade!

And that the young mistress and her party were not at home was oil poured on the fire of Wu Sunfu's wrath. "The mansion's no mansion any more!"—so he railed in the parlour, his eye sweeping over the parlour's appointments, and on the carpet, on the table-cloths, on the sofa-covers, on the window-gauze, hunting out a "flaw" in each and every one to storm and curse at those men and women servants. His awful voice rolled through the whole house; within the parlour and without were the panic-stricken faces of the servants, and the leaves soughed and wailed; and everything, everything, made the splendid Wu mansion seem the more gloomy and dreadful: "The mansion's no mansion any more!"

The manservant Gaosheng, with a great bundle in his arms of the plain white mourning-scrolls newly come in (the day of old Mr. Wu's funeral was drawing near), came blundering rashly into the parlour to ask Wu Sunfu to look them over; but a rebuff full in the face left Gaosheng not knowing whether to cry or to laugh. Only now did everyone understand that this evening the "Third Master's" fiery temper was not what it commonly was!

But this rash stroke of Gaosheng's did put an end to Wu Sunfu's roaring; he slowly stretched himself out on the sofa, and passed into deep reflection. It was not that he sat there casting up the account of the old gentleman's funeral; that was a matter for five days hence, and had long since been made over, with full powers, to the aunt and the young mistress to see to. It was that he had suddenly bethought himself of that time when old Mr. Wu first lay dead, and he and Sun Jiren and the rest had made their vow to organize the Yizhong Company! The old gentleman, dead and gone, was not yet buried, and already their grand ambition had turned to an empty bubble!

Thinking thus, Wu Sunfu came round again, in his waking dream, to that living melodrama in the corner of the night-club bar; and Zhao Botao's words, so charged with menace, came leaping out one by one in time with the throb-throb of Wu Sunfu's pounding heart. Nothing could have been plainer than Old Zhao's drift, and so the roads now left to Sunfu were but two: either to surrender to Old Zhao, or to let the Yizhong Company go bankrupt! These two thoughts alone went whirling round in Wu Sunfu's brain like the figures on a running-horse lantern, and would let him think of no third way at all; and never a bubble of struggle or resistance floated up into his consciousness. The Wu Sunfu of now was no longer the Wu Sunfu of two months ago, at old Mr. Wu's first lying dead! The fever to build up industry had cooled already in his veins! If even now he still meant to strive to keep the Yizhong Company from bankruptcy, that was no more than because he had over two hundred thousand of capital sunk in the Yizhong, and it was this one thought, too, that, turn it this way and that as he might, made him feel that, short of surrendering to Old Zhao, there was no second means of preserving the Yizhong—of preserving his two hundred thousand of capital!

"And yet two months of heart's blood are to go for nothing!"

Wu Sunfu hummed out this one sentence to himself, and in that hushed and silent great parlour it made a strange, ear-grating sound. He sprang up and stared about him in dismay, half doubting that this was his own voice. There was no one else in the parlour; the white glare of the electric light beat strongly on his face. Outside the window two black shadows of servants stirred and crept. Wu Sunfu knit his brows in a bitter smile. Lying back again on that sofa, he suddenly called to mind, too, those words with which he had, not long before, been coaxing Du Zhuzhai: "In Shanghai there's a kind of shrewd, calculating sharp who takes over an old house, puts in capital to whitewash and do it up, and then makes it over again at a good stiff price; in the running of these eight factories of ours, the least we can do is to take a leaf from those sharps who make a trade of taking over houses … and provided only we whitewash and do it up in the right style, the great and famous Zhao Botao is just the sort of moneyed buyer who'll pay a big price!" That had been a jest of the moment, thrown out to keep his hold on Du Zhuzhai, but now it had turned into a prophecy come true! Thinking of it, Wu Sunfu could not help laughing again, and felt that all things are foreordained, and man's strength can force nothing!

He grew somewhat easier in his mind. He felt that the timid Du Zhuzhai really was, at times, gifted with a good deal of foresight, and had thereby been spared no end of vexation. He went on to reckon, a step further, just what the whole property of the Yizhong Company was worth, and what terms he ought to put forward when it came to the actual bargaining with Zhao Botao—whether a clean out-and-out "sale," or a lingering "mortgage" with the root cut but the fibres clinging! The more he thought, the more spirit he found in it, and his face too grew flushed and glowing. Not only was he another man from what he had been two months before, when he was planning to launch out into large-scale enterprise; he was clean different, as well, from what he had been three hours before, on the little launch, craving after stimulation! Now he had a "way out." A way out by surrender, it was true; but any way out was better than none at all!

But this savoury reverie of his was of a sudden broken in upon. The Fourth Young Lady, Huifang, had stolen up before him like a shadow, and, halting at a distance of some three feet, stood gazing at him in a manner very fearful and ill at ease.

"Oh—is it you, Fourth Sister? You didn't go out?"

Once Wu Sunfu had made sure it was the real Fourth Young Lady and no phantom of his own conjuring, he asked the one question at random, with a good deal of the air of a man out of patience.

The Fourth Young Lady did not answer, but went and settled herself in the chair beside Sunfu, and suddenly heaved a sigh. Sunfu's brows drew together at once, and some few stern words had already rushed to the edge of his lips; but in the end he swallowed them down after all. He forced a smile, and was just meaning to fall back on gentler words, when the Fourth Young Lady had already spoken first:

"Third Brother! When Father's funeral is over, I mean to go back to the countryside after all!"

"What! Go back to the countryside?"

Wu Sunfu said with a start, and his colour changed too. He truly could not understand why the Fourth Young Lady should suddenly take up so strange a notion, and his fierce, astonished gaze fastened on the Fourth Young Lady's pitiably pale face. The Fourth Young Lady bowed her head, and after a while, at last, answered slowly and haltingly:

"I've always been with Father in the countryside; I'm not used to Shanghai—"

"You've lived here two months, and now feel you're not used to it instead? Ha, ha!"

Wu Sunfu broke in upon the Fourth Young Lady's words with a loud laugh, feeling that the Fourth Young Lady was really too childish. But this guess of his was wide of the mark. The Fourth Young Lady jerked up her head and looked at her brother with a piercing sharpness. That look of hers, too, had something of the awe-inspiring, all-radiating force of Wu Sunfu's own look when he had made up his mind. She and her brother shared the one strong-willed nature; only in her it had all along been kept close and never shown. Now this long-hoarded nature of hers was about to burst forth!

"Not used to it! To have lived here and to feel not used to it—that is the real not being used to it! Nor is it the house or the food I'm not used to; it's another kind of not being used to it, that I can't put into words! Every day is like walking through a wild dream, my very soul is unsettled; and yet every day I feel too idle as well, hands and feet with nothing to rest on! I've asked Sister Shan and the others, and it's not so with them! I've come to think it must be because I've always lived in the countryside, and am not fit to live in Shanghai!"

The Fourth Young Lady held to her opinion as she had never done before, and suddenly the rims of her eyes reddened, and a few teardrops fell.

"Oh—well then, Fourth Sister …"

Wu Sunfu murmured, and could get no further; his face had grown wonderfully gentle. Stern as he commonly was in his dealings with his younger brother and sisters, in his heart he was in truth full of tenderness for them; he was forever wishing to secure their lifelong happiness after those principles of his own that he held for fixed and sure, and so now, hearing the Fourth Young Lady tell out the misery of her life, he grieved over it as though it were his own suffering—yet the man of enterprise in him could not comprehend the tangled changes of soul and the conflicts of feeling in the Fourth Young Lady, a girl in her first youth!

The Fourth Young Lady, on the other hand, was a great deal more quick to feel. That gentle look on Sunfu's face made her feel, all at once, the loving caress of a mother, so long lost to her. Was this the first time in a dozen years and more that she had felt it? Ten whole years she had waited in attendance on the old gentleman, and never once had she felt so warm a caress. The old gentleman had treated her, from first to last, like a master handing down the doctrine of the Way; between father and daughter the inner life had been one of utter estrangement; and now that the Fourth Young Lady had come by this unlooked-for solace from her brother, her girlish tongue grew more nimble still:

"Third Brother! When I first came to Shanghai, I only felt very timid; meeting people, walking in the street, there was an unspeakable dread in it all. Now it's not like that any more! Now it's just that I always feel too dull and too idle; a while back, Sister-in-law taught me to play cards, but I very soon grew weary of it. My heart is often in a fret, my heart is as though it wanted some one thing, and yet I don't know, after all, what it is I want! I don't understand it myself, what it is I want; I only find everything savourless, and my heart and mind ill at ease!"

"Then it's that you have too little to do to while away the time? In that case, Fourth Sister, why didn't you go along with Sister-in-law today, to divert yourself a little?"

Wu Sunfu's face had grown gentler still, downright the face of a loving mother; yet the man of enterprise in his heart was, little by little, growing somewhat out of patience too.

"I don't care to go out—"

the Fourth Young Lady answered softly, and with a sigh drew in all the rest of what she would have said, and swallowed it down into her belly. Say what one might, a brother was still a brother, and a brother, moreover, who had all along been stern; certain of the tangled feelings of a girl's heart she could not well speak of to this brother. She bowed her head, and the rims of her eyes grew moist again; and before her eyes there floated up all at once a vision: a young man and a young woman, who looked to be Lin Peishan and Du Xintuo, chatting and jesting together, easy and natural. She felt that that was a very blissful thing; and yet she was alone, and there was in her heart a thread, a thread that had struck root there she knew not when, that ever held her fast and kept her from chatting easily and naturally with any man who came near her. She hated this thread, and yet she had no way to pluck it out! It was by just this contradiction and conflict of feeling that she was tormented! She longed to flee away, to be out of sight and easy of heart! But such misery as this there was, again, no place where she could tell it. She bit her lip, raised her head once more, and said resolutely:

"Third Brother! I know it of myself—there's no way but to go to the countryside! Maybe there's some other way as well, but the only way I can think of now is just this one way of going to the countryside! If I live on here, I shall go mad! Third Brother! I shall go mad!"

"Come, come! It really is the strangest thing!"

"I know myself it's too strange, I only can't understand why—"

"It's nothing at all! Live here a little longer and you'll be all right, you'll grow used to it! Look at Ah Xuan!"

Wu Sunfu's tone had grown a shade sterner; out of patience, he heaved himself up with a shake and rose to his feet, meaning to have done with this utterly pointless parley. But the Fourth Young Lady was uncommonly firm, and, very boldly meeting Sunfu eye to eye, answered coldly:

"If you won't let me go back to the countryside, then send me off to the madhouse! If I live on here, sooner or later I shall go out of my mind!"

"Come, come! It really is past all explaining! A grown woman like you, and still talking past all explaining! But I'll ask you this: if you go to the countryside, where will you live?"

"The old home would do well enough to live in!"

"Living there all alone, won't you be more moped than ever?"

"Why then, Fourth Aunt's house would do to live in too!"

Wu Sunfu shook his head, gave a snort down his nose, and fell to pacing with measured steps. There was nothing to be done with this sister's obstinacy, and he was in a most uncommon rage! He, who had always ruled over everything, whose commands no one dared cross! And he could never, from first to last, make out the reason why the Fourth Young Lady wished to flee from life in Shanghai; he only felt that the Fourth Young Lady had been too long at old Mr. Wu's side, and had caught that queer temper of the old gentleman's too: a hatred of modern civilization, a hatred of city life; and this stubborn hatred was the very thing that Wu Sunfu accounted the most "wrong-headed" of all. He came to a sudden stop, turned his face, and asked the Fourth Young Lady again:

"So you mean to hide away in the countryside for ever, then?"

"There's no saying! It's my belief a person's disposition is apt to change and change again! Only just now I'm sure that going back to the countryside is better than being in Shanghai!"

Wu Sunfu could not help laughing; he felt he had found a footing from which to storm the Fourth Young Lady's stubborn stronghold; but before ever he had opened his mouth, of a sudden a clamour of motor-horns came in from outside the main gate, and the manservant Gaosheng called out at the top of his voice in the garden:

"The young mistress and Miss Lin and the rest are all back!"

And then came a jumble of laughing voices and the click of high-heeled shoes. The first to come skipping into the parlour was Ah Xuan, a stage broadsword such as is used in the theatre in his hand. Plainly he had never reckoned on Sunfu's being in the parlour too, and, laughing, he flourished this precious weapon of his with a great air of triumph. But at one turn of his head he caught sight of Sunfu's fierce glare fastened on him, and thereupon his hand drooped down; and yet he still went on giggling boldly. Wu Sunfu knit his brows, feeling that this broadsword before his eyes was but a larger edition of that "dart" of the other day; here was Ah Xuan making so bold as to raise the standard of revolt in open day—forbidden his darts, he must needs go and take up a longer weapon still, and one more glaring to the eye; this was past all bearing!

At this the young mistress came in too, and, seeing at a glance that Sunfu was on the point of an outburst, made haste to come to Ah Xuan's shelter and said:

"It's not that he wanted to buy the thing himself; Xueshi gave it to him. Xueshi has taken lately to this fancy for knight-errantry and the like; swords and spears, he's got a whole heap of them!"

"Sister, wasn't there a telegram in from Fei the little-moustache in the town? It's still there in your purse!"

Lin Peishan, too, was lending a hand to Ah Xuan on the sly, and turned the talk aside. This served to shift Wu Sunfu's attention. Ah Xuan clasped his broadsword and made off with all speed.

The telegram said that in the town a round dozen shops had closed their doors at one and the same time, the owners having absconded, owing native-bank moneys on every hand to a total of as much as three hundred thousand, so that the native bank Wu Sunfu kept in the town had been dragged into it too and was likewise in imminent peril, and it begged that funds be remitted at once to relieve it. Wu Sunfu's colour changed; he drew a sharp cold breath, and, without a word, turned on his heel and quitted the parlour to go to the study and draft his reply; which was these eight great characters: "No funds to remit; act as the moment dictates!"

Land-mines, everywhere about him, on every side! Set one foot down, and up one goes with a roar!—Wu Sunfu, lying on his bed, could not for a long, long time fall asleep, and only these terrors of imagining went kneading and grinding over and over at his swollen, feverish head. And moreover, in society and in the family alike, his own authority was everywhere showing the marks of rout, was become a total collapse! The veins at his temples throbbed and throbbed; the wire-spring mattress beneath him turned all at once into a hill of knife-blades; while the young mistress beside him moaned and sobbed in her dreams.

Little by little a steam-whistle sounded faint and far away, and Wu Sunfu suddenly saw the Fourth Young Lady come running to make her scene again, clamouring to go back to the countryside, saying she would leave the world and be a nun, with her hair cropped clean away; and the aunt taking the part of the younger sister and the little brother, laying the blame on Sunfu sentence by sentence, demanding that Sunfu divide the property and let the Fourth Young Lady and Ah Xuan set up on their own; and then all at once he saw Ah Xuan and a whole crowd of others setting up a challenge-ring in the great parlour, the garden thronged with grotesque, misshapen fellows from the Three Mountains and the Five Peaks; and last of all, Sunfu saw himself in a certain hotel, lying on a bed, with Liu Yuying, her face all flushed, tittering, her soft white palm hot as fire, pressed against his breast, and moving down, a little and a little, moving down and down …

In his dream a long laugh, and Sunfu, with a clasp of his two arms, had caught fast a soft warm body, and heard, besides, a low delicate laughing. Wu Sunfu opened his eyes with a start; the window-gauze was all dappled with the mottled shadows of the sunlight, and she who sat beside him, in a bathing-gown, was the young mistress, smiling at him. Wu Sunfu of a sudden went red in the face, and sprang hastily up, only to see, on the little tea-table at the bed-head, that on the oval tray of imitation silver which bore up a cup of milk there were set, precisely and squarely, two visiting-cards: Wang Hefu, Sun Jiren. Over the hot milk in the cup a thin skin had but just gathered.

In the small parlour, the three of them—Wu Sunfu and his friends—began their gravest council. When Zhao Botao's manner of lending had been thrashed out in detail, Wu Sunfu was inclined to accept, Wang Hefu was neither for nor against, and Sun Jiren, for his part, was flat against it. That boss shook his slender, elongated neck, and said coldly:

"This business must be looked at in its several parts: is it a workable bargain, our making the Yizhong over to Old Zhao? That's the one. And ought we to sell it out at all? That's the two. Sunfu, you take it that this so-called syndicate Old Zhao speaks of is that trust there's been such long rumour of; but the way I see it, it doesn't look like it! Getting up an atmosphere is Old Zhao's forte! He lets out this atmosphere about some trust on purpose, so as to throw people into a panic, and make them feel there's no way out but by his door! Let's just take no notice of him at all!"

"But, Jiren, that trust of theirs—like as not it's no empty cannon; isn't he trying, this very moment, to slip a noose round our Yizhong?"

"Not so! Grant that he really means to lend, that trust is an empty cannon still! Old Zhao's whole fortune, every stick of it, is put into government bonds; it's hardly likely he still has the power to set up a company against the Americans; maybe he's in league with the foreign merchants, to make mortgage-loans to the Chinese factory-owners, and then he's no more than a broker! We've factories to sell out; are we not able, then, to go and find the principal buyer ourselves—what need to lean on this broker's good offices!"

"Just so! I too feel that Old Zhao, for all he's so terribly formidable, is at bottom a broker in disguise! In every undertaking that goes by the name of Sino-foreign joint venture, the Chinese shareholders, at the very marrow of them, are honestly and truly nothing but brokers!"

Wang Hefu came round to Sun Jiren's view, and Wu Sunfu thereupon no longer pressed his own, though he still said, not quite easy in his mind:

"If we can't find some other customer, then, when the time comes for us to go and treat with Old Zhao, we shall have to bear his squeezing; and if we don't go and treat with him, he'll really let us have an economic blockade in good earnest—wouldn't that be worse still? Jiren, have you any other channel in mind?"

"Nothing ready to hand, no; but with a bit of searching there's always some fair chance of it. I said just now that this business must be looked at in its several parts; now let's go into the second part of it—as things stand at present, how much longer can the Yizhong be kept up?"

No sooner were these words of Sun Jiren's out than Wang Hefu shook his head in deep dejection, and Wu Sunfu stroked his chin and sighed. There was no need to discuss it: nothing could be plainer. With no hope of peace, every added day that those eight factories of the Yizhong were kept up was one more day of added loss; so that the question was not, after all, whether Wu Sunfu and his friends had the power to keep them up, but whether they were willing to. And they were willing no longer; they had lost heart already for the whole enterprise!

The three of them looked at one another and smiled, and so, lightly, made an end of the feverish dream of more than two months. They felt, moreover, that a lingering "mortgage," with the root cut but the fibres clinging, was too troublesome by half, and were all of one mind to make a clean, out-and-out sale of it. The customers of Sun Jiren's imagining were two: a certain English trading-firm, and a certain Japanese company.

After a little, Wu Sunfu said with a dry laugh:

"To know when to advance and when to fall back—that too is to be no less than a hero! And besides, the thing came to grief through the dragging-on of the war, not through any want of skill on our part in the running of enterprise!"

Wang Hefu laughed aloud too, feeling that a heavy burden had been lifted from him, and that of a night he would have fewer troubled dreams. But Sun Jiren wore a wholly grave face, as though he were casting something up in his mind. Suddenly he slapped his thigh, and looked at his two friends very well pleased, and said:

"The eight factories sold out, with machines, fixtures, stock-in-hand and raw material all told priced at six hundred thousand, and the ready money the company has actually in hand a matter of seventy thousand and more, so that, reckoning it all up one with another, our capital's blood-and-bone is safe and sound; now we're left with an empty shell of a Yizhong Company, to take in deposits and bide our chance to launch out again by and by. Didn't Yunshan's telegram of the other day say he could get a little share-capital raised in Hong Kong? Let's wire him again and spur him to put his back into it—be it much or little, it's all to the good! —And another thing, Sunfu! What wrecked our venture in the factories this time was just the unquiet state of the times; yet a time like this is a rare good chance for the bonds! Let's take the capital we'd put into the factories and put it into the bonds instead! And have another bout with Old Zhao!"

As Wu Sunfu listened he nodded and nodded again; a hot ball of courage went spreading out once more from within his breast, and travelled over his whole body, till his very fingertips were a little a-tremble. On the bond side, they had not yet had the edge taken off their spirit. And what was more, they had already bought over the woman spy, and this was the very time to win by a masterstroke. Then and there Wu Sunfu declared his resolve:

"Then it must be done quickly, and it must be done with a bold hand and a broad axe! These last few days the bonds have rallied a little again—that's a trick of the 'bulls'; with the war dragging on undecided, the three sorts of scrip, the Customs and the Disbandment and the Reorganization, will all of them fall to three thousand for every ten thousand of face; today let's throw out several hundred thousand's worth!"

"Just so! That's my mind in the matter too."

Wang Hefu put in as well, stroking his moustache with the air of a man whose heart's desire is within his grasp.

In the old days, when they had wanted to run factories and to play the bonds both at once, they had for all that come safe through two perilous storms; now that they were putting their whole strength into the bonds alone, they naturally felt they had room and to spare, like a knife that plays easily where there is space between the joints. They had no reason not to let themselves be optimistic. And so this council of theirs, too, came to its end amid excitement and hope. Sun Jiren said at the last, rousing himself:

"Then I'll go straightway and find the channels and open the negotiations. Whether the buyer of the eight factories be one firm or several, the total we hold firm to is five hundred and twenty thousand; anything less, and it's off, and we'll find some other way! The Yizhong Company carries on as before, doing trust business only. Hefu! Those hundred thousand and more of deposits you've got some hint of, go and pull them in with all speed; savings, too, we mean to take up. Huang Fen's news, that shall be Hefu's to keep touch with. There's one weighty matter left over: the directing of the bond market—Sunfu, this I must trouble you with! You alone can carry it!"

When the three had parted, Wu Sunfu at once put through several telephone calls. He got into touch first with the broker Lu Kuangshi, and after that gave Han Mengxiang a good spell of instructions. The state of the bond market made Wu Sunfu very hopeful; the god of good fortune had not yet forsaken him. But he meant to hear the report of the woman spy Liu Yuying before he settled how much to throw out; and so he was ringing round again everywhere to run this Liu Yuying to earth, wild bird that she was, till he forgot even that his belly was empty.

At eleven o'clock Wu Sunfu's motor-car began to move slowly along the asphalt drive within the garden; and in the car Wu Sunfu was all aglow with ruddy colour. He was going out to take his own place at the very front of the bond-market battle! But before ever he reached the main gate, something went wrong with the car's engine; the chauffeur cranked her three times over, and the car only wheezed and panted and would not budge a single step. "This is no good omen!" Even Wu Sunfu, who had always prided himself on being purged of superstition, could not keep the thought from him. In a pet he got down from the car and went back into the parlour; but at that same moment there was suddenly a blaring of motor-horns outside the main gate, and a car came driving in, and the two within it were Du Zhuzhai and his wife.

The elder Miss Wu, Mistress Du, had come on purpose to find Sunfu over the matter of old Mr. Wu's funeral, and she opened straight off:

"Tomorrow the Imperial Litany is to be chanted at the Jade Buddha Temple! Today let's first go and take a look at the sutra-hall."

"Oh, oh, Second Sister, do please act for me in it. I've a matter of some urgency on hand. But for the car's going wrong, I'd have been away from the house long since."

Wu Sunfu answered with knitted brows, and, his eye lighting on Du Zhuzhai, of a sudden hit upon a capital idea: to draw Du Zhuzhai into an "offensive and defensive alliance" over the bonds, and so make his own force the mightier, and never fear then wherever Old Zhao might flee to. But how was he to lead up to it? On the instant Wu Sunfu's whole mind was turned upon this problem.

"That will do very well. I and Peiyao will go, then. Only, tomorrow the Litany opens at nine o'clock, and you must without fail come and offer your stick of incense! Peiyao, Fourth Sister, Ah Xuan—every one of them must go!"

"Ah! Now that you speak of Fourth Sister—don't you know? She wants to go back to the countryside! This creature, there's no making her out!"

Wu Sunfu had missed the whole first half of the aunt's speech; only the two words "Fourth Sister" had fallen into his ears, and they had roused this care of his.

The aunt, however, was not in the least surprised, but answered merely, in an offhand way:

"Young people are all fond of gadding about. She's lived a few days in Shanghai and grown tired of it, and now she'd like to go and amuse herself in the countryside again!"

"It's not only to go and amuse herself! Second Sister, I was just meaning to ask you to go and reason with her; maybe she'll listen to what you say! It's most odd! There's no telling why she's like this! Second Sister, one talk with her, and you'll understand it. Perhaps it's a kind of nervous disorder!"

Wu Sunfu, seizing the chance to pack the aunt off out of the way, laid hold of Du Zhuzhai and went forward with his diplomatic negotiations for the "offensive and defensive alliance." He set forth, with much exaggeration, how the war was bound to drag on, and how the bond funds would be seized to swell the military chest, so that bond prices could only fall lower day by day, and to go "short" was a chance beyond all price. He did not propose that he and Zhuzhai should "form a company"; he only dwelt on how profitable the going "short" would be, and pressed Zhuzhai to fall into the selfsame step with him.

Du Zhuzhai listened, taking a pinch of snuff the while, and smiled and nodded his head.
