### Scavenging

Every day, when dusk deepens into the dead of night, there are to be seen in the lanes and alleys certain ragged and filthy creatures, each with a little glass lamp in one hand, its flame no bigger than a bean, and in the other a pair of bamboo tongs, an empty basket slung across the back; and they go picking up bamboo-ends and wood-shavings along the way, or stoop double over the rubbish-bins, groping out old rags and scraps of paper and the like—men and women both. Whatever they have gathered they set to rights a little and carry to the old-goods stalls to change for ready cash, and so scrape a living. This calling, which lies outside the three hundred and sixty trades, goes by the name of "scavenging." But their aim, over and above the odds and ends of rubbish, is the hope of some unlooked-for windfall—a dropped needle, a fallen hairpin, a gaily coloured banknote!
