# Chapter Six: Two Murders, Unrelated in Their Causes, Fall Together at Shanghai North Station

### 20. Wang Yaqiao Warns Dai Li: "Men Whose Roads Differ Cannot Take Counsel Together"

Dai Li came to Shanghai again.

The Huangpu in front of him ran the same muddy yellow it had run when he was a ragged *biesan* on its banks, roaring away eastward. Near the Bund the huge buildings still reared up like mountain peaks. But the Yongfeng and the Salt Bank towers, which had once seemed to Dai Li impossible to scale, had all at once become small things. He was a man in high favour under Chiang Kai-shek now, and he had been in the middle of organizing the Special Services Section of the Renaissance Society when an urgent wire from Chiang called him up from Shanghai to Lushan in Jiangxi. Only a few days earlier, in front of Lion Rock on Lushan, there had been a shooting that came within an inch of costing Chiang Kai-shek his life.

"Yunong, I called you up the mountain for one thing: to find out for me who this assassin was and who sent him. A killer walked up to my very door — God damn his mother, they dared to fire at my forehead!" In the reception room of the Meilu villa Chiang Kai-shek, since the attack, had not been outside for days. Even now the scene rose in front of him with perfect clarity, and it still turned his stomach over: a fine rain drifting down, he and Soong Mei-ling both in raincoats, seated in the mountain chairs their attendants carried, going slowly up toward the Meilu.

Then, all at once, the crack of a shot, and Chiang was so startled he nearly went off the chair. Behind him Soong Mei-ling cried out, "Darling, an assassin!" In the confusion Chiang saw a short, thickset man a little way off behind a great boulder shaped like a steamed bun, holding a pistol level in both hands, teeth clenched, firing at him. The second bullet came whistling past his ear, close enough to have gone through his head. At the same moment Chiang saw Yu Jishi burst out of the depths of the bamboo at the head of a body of plainclothesmen, every pistol firing at once — bang, bang, bang, bang — and in a heartbeat the packed bullets had punched more than a dozen holes through the assassin in the Yunhu shirt. Blood flew up in a sheet, and where the man pitched forward across the boulder it sprayed out in a scatter of red blossoms. The sight was magnificent and terrible together; even to Chiang Kai-shek, long seasoned to the battlefield, it was an absolutely rare and savage spectacle.

"Search! Search for me!" Yu Jishi came running up in a panic to Chiang, whose face had gone bloodless, and saw him lift a shaking left hand high and point it into the deep bamboo, shouting until his voice tore: "Assassins — there is certainly more than one in there. You — search for me! Go and look!"

Yu Jishi and the other men of the Attendants' Office dared not be slow about it. With a rush they turned and charged into the bamboo, and another rattle of shooting broke out among the stems.

"Set your mind at rest, Commandant. We shall know very shortly who was behind it." What Dai Li was privately congratulating himself on was that he had not gone up to Lushan with Chiang this time. Had he been on the mountain when it happened, an intelligence officer could hardly have escaped his share of the blame. Once ordered up, his first task was to help Yu Jishi identify the body of the nameless assassin who had fired on Chiang from the bamboo. A whole party of forensic doctors and trace specialists was brought over from Nanjing and the body was opened up; but since the man had left nothing of any evidentiary value behind him when he died, his identity and his origins could not be settled at once.

Dai Li, however, was a sharp intelligence man for all that. On his second day on the mountain he led his detail in a sweep of every inn and lodging house on Lushan. He carried a photograph of the dead man and showed it to one proprietor after another, at the large houses and the small; and at an inn below Taiyi Peak called the Wan Ke Lai — Ten Thousand Guests Come — the woman who kept it knew him at a glance. The dead man was the guest from Hong Kong who had lodged with her for several days. Dai Li checked the register of arrivals and departures and established that the assassin's name was Ma Sanmeng, a native of Xiangshan in Guangdong, who had entered himself as chairman of the Wanmingli trading house of Hong Kong.

Dai Li at once sent a man from Jiujiang by night flight to Hong Kong to check Ma Sanmeng out. The answer came back from Hong Kong the following day. First: there was no Wanmingli trading house in Hong Kong at all. Second: three men named Ma Sanmeng could indeed be turned up in Hong Kong, but not one of them matched the body found at Lion Rock in trade, age, standing or appearance.

"I am certain the assassin who fired on me is connected with Hu Hanmin. Why? Because he hates me that much! This was without doubt an assassin sent up from Guangdong!" Chiang Kai-shek had heard Dai Li's report out and settled the killer's identity on the spot.

Hearing this, Dai Li could not help being taken aback. He had come with his own estimate of who the assassin might be. Now, hearing Chiang link the man to Wang Jingwei and Sun Ke, who were at that moment raising a rival government in Guangdong, he found himself remembering a piece of intelligence picked up in Nanjing not long before: a Guangdong man named Xiao Focheng had flown secretly to Shanghai a short while ago and met Wang Yaqiao in the concessions. Dai Li knew that this Xiao Focheng was a trusted man of Sun Ke's, a member of the Kuomintang's Central Supervisory Committee, and in his younger years a follower of Sun Yat-sen. If what had happened on Lushan had a Canton connection, then it very likely had a great deal to do with his old sworn brother from Shanghai days, Wang Yaqiao. For some reason, though, Dai Li did not bring the suspicion out. He only bowed from the waist in front of Chiang and said, "The Commandant means that the assassin came from Canton?"

Chiang said, "Yunong, whether he came from Canton I am not yet ready to say. But I think this man must have been a professional killer. If he were not, he would never have risked coming up to Lushan at all. This is not Shanghai; the mountain is full of our own people. Men who were not professionals would never have dared smuggle a pistol up here. From that one act alone I am certain the man who did this was no ordinary sort."

Dai Li's mind was a tangle. Chiang's words had reminded him of something: the day before, in Yu Jishi's keeping, he had seen the German-made pistol the assassin had used. It was a Powerful model, stainless-steel, a gun with which, in ordinary conditions, a shooter could hit the man he wanted to kill at five hundred metres. Dai Li had always been fond of pistols. Since leaving Whampoa he had taken part in every kind of secret work and handled every kind of foreign-made handgun, and yet he had never seen a Powerful like the one the assassin left at the scene. He knew that no ordinary hired killer would have such a weapon. And that brought a particular man back into his mind: Wang Yaqiao, the benefactor of his ruined days in Shanghai.

"It is him. It has to be him. Nobody else would have a weapon like that." So Dai Li thought; but he did not say in front of Chiang who it was he suspected. What made him certain that Wang Yaqiao's men had done it was that he had seen that same Powerful in Wang Yaqiao's own house years before. Wang had said to him then, "Chunfeng, this Powerful is one of the treasures I keep about me. As long as it is on me, nobody on earth need think of getting close."

Dai Li also knew where Wang Yaqiao's German pistols came from: an Englishman in the British concession in Shanghai named Ma Leqi, whose trade was running foreign arms and munitions from England into the Chinese interior. The Powerful could be had from no one but Ma Leqi. In his own mind Dai Li was eight or nine parts sure that Wang Yaqiao was in the case, but for a number of reasons he did not speak the name aloud in front of Chiang. It was less that he still felt the pull of the old Shanghai debt than that he was afraid to raise his old friendship with Wang in Chiang's presence at all, in case it set the man doubting and guessing.

"I can do more than guess that this assassination was worked from Guangdong. I can also tell you that the man who fired at me came out of Shanghai." Chiang Kai-shek was a politician of long calculation, and from his deck chair he laid out his reading of the case for Dai Li, standing in attendance before him. "Why do I say Shanghai? Because when Yu Jishi looked into the killer who lodged below Taiyi Peak, he found five or six others had disappeared along with him. His inquiries turned up that all of them had come to Jiujiang from Shanghai by train. So Shanghai is at the very least where this lot of assassins were based. And it is perfectly possible that the Guangdong people bought their killers in Shanghai. Therefore, Yunong, I am sending you to Shanghai in person, to trace out for me where these assassins went after they fled Lushan. If you find any track of them, arrest the whole lot at once. Shoot them out of hand — no softness!"

Now and then a long, drawn-out ship's whistle came up the Huangpu. Dai Li stood looking through the dusk at the clusters of light coming on along the far bank. The task Chiang had given him this time felt very thorny indeed. He had foreseen on Lushan that the assassin might be connected to Wang Yaqiao — but that, after all, was only his suspicion. What made it harder still was that even if Wang Yaqiao's men had done it, he would not dare move against him rashly. Nobody understood better than Dai Li how formidable Wang was. Wang held no office and never had; he was an idle cloud and a wild crane who thought nothing of Chiang Kai-shek or the National Government; and yet Dai Li knew that Wang's social power in Shanghai was beyond anything a government official could command. To set yourself against Wang Yaqiao in the great city of Shanghai — never mind that Dai Li had arrived with a dozen or so agents at his back; even Huang Jinrong, Du Yuesheng and Zhang Xiaolin, the three big bosses, gave way three parts before him. The thought sent a shudder through Dai Li. "How am I to lay my cards on the table with a man of that iron?"

He remembered, too, the awkwardness of the last time — his secret trip down to Shanghai to see Wang Yaqiao, after following Chiang on the Northern Expedition as far as Wuhan. That time he had praised Chiang Kai-shek's arts of war and government to Wang Yaqiao's face and been savagely dressed down for it; he had come near to being thrown out of the Wang mansion. Dai Li had assumed then that Wang Yaqiao would change gates as lightly as he had done himself, take service under Chiang, and pick up some post or other. It had never occurred to him that he would barely get out the words — how absolute Chiang's prestige was in the Whampoa clique, how well he treated junior officers of Zhejiang stock like themselves, how he cherished talent, how he gave real weight to strong local men who commanded a following — before the head of the Hatchet Gang went off like a powder charge. Wang Yaqiao dashed his wine cup on the floor and roared: "Dai Chunfeng! When I took you in in Shanghai in those days, it was because I thought I saw a man of some future in you. What I never expected was that you were the sort who calls any woman mother so long as she has milk. What is your purpose, coming to me now to sing Chiang Kai-shek's praises? I know that Zhejiang hoodlum better than you do, Dai Chunfeng. In the years when I was building up the Hatchet Gang here in Shanghai, what was your Mr. Chiang? A little *biesan*, the same as you. When he was scrabbling along on the Bund he stepped aside for Wang Yaqiao and bobbed his head like everybody else. Then he too went over to Mr. Sun. While Mr. Sun was alive he was a devout believer with the Three Principles of the People always in his mouth; the moment Mr. Sun died, he turned traitor to the revolution. Now you tell me — a man of that stripe, changing his face by morning and evening, with that heart in him — would I sit down and call him brother?"

"Enough, enough, Elder Brother. If you have a grudge against Mr. Chiang I shall not press you." Seeing the conversation had gone wrong, Dai Li changed direction in a hurry. "I meant it kindly. It seemed to me that great as my elder brother's abilities are, without leaning on some party or faction you might find a single palm cannot clap, and that nothing large would come of it. That was why I wanted to introduce my elder brother to Mr. Chiang. But since my elder brother has no such intention, let us each go our own road and be done."

"The ancients said: men whose roads differ cannot take counsel together." Wang Yaqiao, finding no common ground with Dai Li, showed him the door without further ceremony. "Since you are set on following old Chiang, I shall not make things hard for you. But from this day on, if you come calling again, we will not discuss anything to do with old Chiang. Otherwise do not blame Wang Jiuguang for forgetting the old friendship."

Now Dai Li had come to Shanghai under Chiang Kai-shek's secret orders, to trace the Lushan assassins in the dark. He guessed the case must involve Wang Yaqiao, but he had not yet met the man and could not make a final judgement. What put him in a corner was that carrying out Chiang's order meant a head-on collision with Wang Yaqiao — that was the only way to tell the true from the false; and yet whenever he thought of facing Wang Yaqiao he felt a certain thinness in his courage. He turned it over for a long while, caught between the two, and finally hardened his heart. The thought of a merit to be earned in Chiang Kai-shek's eyes won out in the end.

"Elder Brother — I trust you have been well?" Toward evening the next day a mysterious visitor walked into Wang Yaqiao's little Western-style house in the French Concession. Wang Yaqiao came forward to meet him and saw a man in a short-sleeved singlet and a pair of dark glasses. When he recognized Dai Li, whom he had not seen for a long time, it gave him a private jolt: for the man to arrive uninvited at so delicate a moment, there had to be some connection with the Lushan shooting. So he put his guard up and coldly showed Dai Li into the sitting room and to a chair.

"Dai Chunfeng — you who dance attendance on Mr. Chiang — why the sudden call at my poor house, where nobody bothers to come?" Seeing murder hidden in Dai Li, Wang Yaqiao decided to strike first.

To Dai Li, Wang Yaqiao's colour looked wrong that day. He also noticed unfamiliar men moving about inside the house and out. He knew perfectly well that Wang Yaqiao's house always had disciples coming and going and a constant stream of guests; but since his own errand was what it was, he could not help watching every one of them. After a pause he squeezed a few dry laughs out of his embarrassment and said, "Elder Brother, I really have been very busy this while. Mostly it has been following Mr. Chiang up to Lushan for the summer. Ai, ai — nothing like the easy old days when I followed my elder brother. Hold office and you are no longer your own man; you cannot even move where you please!"

Wang Yaqiao caught the faint sound of the sidelong probe in this. Of all subjects, Lushan was the one on which he was most sensitive. But Wang Yaqiao was a man long out on the rivers and lakes, who had been through every sort of hair's-breadth scene in his life; and although the attempt on Chiang on Lushan had failed and had cost him a brother in Chen Cheng, he believed the plot had been laid faultlessly from beginning to end. As Wang Yaqiao saw it, apart from one German-made Powerful pistol, Chiang Kai-shek could hardly have come away with any further lead. So he met Dai Li's reading of his face with his habitual reserve, and gave a short cold laugh. "Dai Chunfeng, I have always disliked men who talk like officials. Men like you especially, who borrow Chiang Kai-shek's tiger skin to play the fox — those I despise from the bottom of my heart. Whether you go up Lushan or not, what is there in it worth showing off? As a matter of fact, if I, Wang, wanted to pass a summer on Lushan in Jiangxi, I could come and go there whenever I liked. It is only that I still find nowhere on earth to compare with Shanghai."

Dai Li's idea had been to use Lushan as his opening, to watch Wang Yaqiao's face and bearing, and then to spring the assassination on him as a bluff. If Wang Yaqiao had indeed sent the man, he would be sure to leave some thread, some hair's trace, in what he said. And if Dai Li could really bluff out proof that Wang Yaqiao was in it, there would be a merit to claim from Chiang Kai-shek. What he had not expected was that Wang Yaqiao would be as flinty and unhandleable as ever, and would hand him a hard nail to run into with his very first words, leaving him thoroughly at a loss.

"Just so, just so — if my elder brother wants to go up Lushan, what could be easier? One word from you and I, Dai Chunfeng, will send men to Shanghai to bring you up the mountain." Dai Li piled the smiles on hurriedly. "Though Lushan is not so peaceful either. I hear a great affair happened up there not long ago. Somebody actually tried to assassinate Mr. Chiang."

"Oh? Was there such a thing?" This settled it for Wang Yaqiao: Dai Li had come with no good intentions. He had plainly become a runner and a hunting dog at Chiang Kai-shek's side, a very different creature from the man who had fired a rescuing shot in Nanjing in the autumn of 1928 out of feeling for the old days. Seeing that every word in and out of Dai Li's mouth was a sounding of his depths, Wang Yaqiao slapped the table and burst out laughing. "So there are heroes in this world after all! Even old Chiang can meet his match on Lushan. Dai Chunfeng — did you by any chance catch that hero, that second Jing Ke, up on the mountain? If I could set eyes on such a man, I should not have lived this life for nothing!"

Dai Li's face went red on the instant. He had never imagined Wang Yaqiao could be so quick, and the speed of the man's answer astonished him. Not only did Wang Yaqiao decline to avoid so sensitive a subject in front of him, he openly praised the assassin — which could not help shaking the judgement Dai Li had already formed. He said hastily, "Not at all, not at all, Elder Brother. The assassin was shot dead on the spot by Yu Jishi's men; I was not there myself. I heard afterward that he died a fine death, too. There were dozens of shots in him, and still he got off two at Mr. Chiang! Only — the pistol he left behind is rather a thing to think about."

"What is strange about a gun?"

"Elder Brother, what I find strange is that the assassin's pistol happened to be the German Powerful you yourself used to be so fond of. Tell me — a gun with killing power like that: if the bullets really had gone into Mr. Chiang, would there have been any chance of his living? It was only because Yu Jishi spotted it early that day. Otherwise there is no saying!" Dai Li half believed and half doubted that Wang Yaqiao had a hand in the case; he did not know the depth of the water. But he had come charged with Chiang Kai-shek's mission, and so he went on probing at the wholly unruffled Wang Yaqiao.

"Is that so? Excellent! That only proves the assassin was a genuine, thoroughgoing hero, one who went up prepared to die. Otherwise he would never have used the same German make as I do." Wang Yaqiao cursed Dai Li privately and went on facing the challenge with his customary arrogance. "Dai Chunfeng, do you suppose the assassin's pistol was a present from me? I can tell you that I truly did not know this man of resolve. And had I known beforehand that he meant to give his body up on Lushan and kill Mr. Chiang, I would have given him not only the German pistol but better bullets besides! Let me tell you: I have not only the Powerful here now, I have the more advanced dumdum pistols and dumdum rounds as well! If that man of resolve had got hold of those, I guarantee not a bullet would have gone astray, and Mr. Chiang might have been dead under that poisoned lead long ago. Ai, ai — what a pity of it!"

So far from being frightened by words with murder hidden in them, Wang Yaqiao had gone on to praise the assassin without the least restraint and curse Chiang Kai-shek in front of him. Dai Li understood that Wang Yaqiao's inner line was not one he could break by sounding sideways. Even so, the suspicion in him was not dispelled. But he dared not stay engaged. He could see that he would never have the upper hand in front of Wang Yaqiao, not ever. With that in mind he wound up the awkward call in a few sentences and took his leave, thoroughly deflated.

Dai Li stayed in Shanghai a month and more and got nothing at all. By then Chiang Kai-shek was back in Nanjing. Dai Li went to the presidential offices to present himself and reported that a month's investigation in Shanghai had not turned up the smallest trace of the assassins. It brought Chiang Kai-shek's rage down on his head. "Dai Yunong," Chiang swore at him savagely, "you will tell me the honest truth. How have I treated you these last years?"

Dai Li had not dreamed Chiang would blaze up at him like this. His voice shook with fear. "Commandant — speaking from the heart, you are the father and mother who gave me my second life. It is only that this case is peculiarly tangled. The men planned it in a sealed room and left no evidence afterward that could be used to run them down. So however much Yunong wants to take the killers and earn the merit, there is nowhere to lay a hand. Your student is not disloyal to the Commandant by so much as a hair!"

"Rubbish!" Chiang's fury only mounted. "Whether you take the killers or not, we can set aside. I ask you one thing, Dai Yunong. Is it I, Chiang, whose kindness to you is heavy as a mountain — or is that unpardonable villain Wang Yaqiao the father and mother of your second life? Tell me: since you swore your loyalty to me long ago, why do you shield Wang Yaqiao in every possible way?"

Dai Li went white with shock. He had believed Chiang knew nothing of his early friendship with Wang Yaqiao in Shanghai; it had never occurred to him that somebody would report it privately to Chiang. He knew that of everything in the world Chiang most hated a subordinate who was not loyal to him. All at once Dai Li dropped to his knees with a thud, threw up both hands and began slapping his own face left and right. Weeping and pleading at once, he poured out the reasons why he had not dared report the old tie with Wang Yaqiao. "Commandant," he sobbed, "Wang Yaqiao and I did have a friendship in Shanghai in the old days. It was because he saved me when I was down and ruined. But from the time I came over to the Commandant I broke with Wang Yaqiao entirely. When I went to his house this time, it was to bluff some lead about the Lushan attempt out of him and then report it to the Commandant, so that the killers might be netted at a stroke and I might repay the Commandant for seeing my worth. But who could know Wang Yaqiao is a stiff-necked, unbending creature? Whether your student went at him soft or hard, he never showed a flicker of fear. And what is more, he — "

"He what? Say it!"

Dai Li was trembling. "He cursed the Commandant to my face, and heaped praise on that unpardonable assassin as well. Tell me, Commandant — would a man with nothing on his conscience like that be the black hand behind the Lushan attempt?"

Seeing how bitterly Dai Li wept, Chiang Kai-shek concluded that his loyalty was undivided and left off scolding him; but the attempt on Lushan still stuck in his throat. He motioned Dai Li to his feet and charged him: "Very well, Yunong. Your loyalty or want of it we shall set aside for the moment. But from this day forward you are forbidden all connection with Wang Yaqiao, open or secret, root or thread. Since you are resolved to serve the party and the state, from now on you will watch this Anhui killer's movements for me at all times. The moment you have a hold on him, get rid of the man without hesitation!"

Dai Li climbed up off the floor and swore to Chiang with every oath he had: "Rest easy, Commandant. If he dares show the Commandant any further disrespect, your student will not spare him!"

### 21. Two Assassination Plans Go Quietly Forward at Once

Rain cloud covered the whole sky over Shanghai.

The day was 28 June 1931. As night came on, Wang Yaqiao took a mysterious telephone call. It came from his close friend in Guangdong, Xiao Focheng, the Kuomintang central committeeman who in the spring had travelled from Canton to Shanghai as Sun Ke's representative to urge him to go up Lushan and kill Chiang Kai-shek. He said one sentence into the telephone: "I have laid on a table for you at the Old Longxing. Come without fail — I shall not leave until you do."

Xiao Focheng had been a close friend of Wang Yaqiao's since Wang first made his name in Shanghai. And yet since the spring, when Wang accepted the two hundred thousand in banknotes brought from Guangdong and with it the commission to kill Chiang Kai-shek, this was the first time he had heard Xiao Focheng's voice. Wang Yaqiao did not keep him waiting. Taking only two personal bodyguards, he set off at speed in the household motorcar for a quiet back lane of Shanghai. In the car he turned over the reasons Xiao Focheng might suddenly have come up from Nanjing, and for a while could not guess what had brought him. But Wang Yaqiao felt he had let Xiao Focheng down badly. The last time, Xiao Focheng had placed such hopes in him, coming in person from Canton to Shanghai to beg him to kill Chiang Kai-shek, and paying handsomely for it; and the special action group he had sent to Lushan had not brought the work off. Even now the failure on Lushan sat uneasily in him.

Watching the streets slide past outside the car, Wang Yaqiao saw that with the sky so overcast many shops had already lit their lamps. Clusters of light played over the dim street, and his own mind was a blank haze. He knew that Wang Jingwei and Sun Ke, who had once come to beg him to kill Chiang, had for the moment given up their posture of confrontation and gone back to Nanjing. But now here was Xiao Focheng, holding no office in Nanjing, coming secretly to a Shanghai lying under war cloud. Could it be he has something else he wants of me?

Wang Yaqiao was still running on in this way when, without his noticing, the car reached the end of the quiet lane. From some distance off he could see three great characters glowing in the dark — Lao Long Xing, the restaurant's name set deep in a glass lightbox, uncommonly striking against the night. It was a house of some reputation, but out of the way, so there were few cars in front of it. Getting down, Wang Yaqiao ordered his two bodyguards not to come into the inner rooms but to wait in the front hall, where they could respond to whatever came. He himself was led by a waiter along a dim corridor to an inconspicuous private room. The light inside was low, and two guests in long gowns were already waiting at the round table.

Looking closely, Wang Yaqiao saw that one was his old friend Xiao Focheng and the other also a familiar face: Zhao Xingbei, the Kuomintang senator who had come up from Guangdong with Xiao Focheng the previous time to deliver the two hundred thousand. Since they were all old acquaintances, the three exchanged a few words of courtesy and then fell to eating and talking together.

"I am truly sorry. Last time Mr. Chiang's hour had come — who would have thought the men of his Attendants' Office were so formidable? The upshot was that Chen Cheng, whom we sent to do it, was shot down on the spot in a hail of fire, and Chiang got away with his life." Wang Yaqiao saw that everything set before him was the Wuxi cooking he liked — ham-larded green mackerel, herring belly and the rest — with old Shaoxing wine to lift it, and he drank two cups straight off. Then he said, a little shamefaced, "I, Wang Jiuguang, spent your two hundred thousand for nothing. When I have money I shall return it to the last note."

"Brother Jiuguang, who is asking you for money back?" Xiao Focheng filled his cup, sent Zhao Xingbei outside to see how things stood, and then dropped his voice. "We have come to Shanghai this time to bring you money again."

"What? More money for me?" Wang Yaqiao was startled, and could not think what Xiao Focheng and Zhao Xingbei had come for.

Seeing Wang Yaqiao sit there blankly, at a loss, Zhao Xingbei quickly set a heavy bundle in front of him. Inside were stacks of crisp new banknotes. Wang Yaqiao pushed it away at once. "How am I to accept this? I am already in your debt from last time. If there is something you want done, you have only to say the word — why bring money again? At this rate I, Wang Jiuguang, become a man who takes the money and does no work!"

"President Sun knows Brother Jiuguang's character perfectly well." Xiao Focheng put out a hand to block him and spoke gravely. "Lushan did not come off, but Brother Jiuguang certainly spared no pains over it. I hear even your good lady went up the mountain herself to carry the guns, and that meant risking her head at every moment. How can it be said you spent that money for nothing?"

Zhao Xingbei said as well, "Brother Jiuguang is a man of honour. Lushan did not succeed, but there were reasons for it. This time President Sun still rests his hopes on you, elder brother — and this is a chance to win back face."

Hearing this, Wang Yaqiao understood at last that Sun Ke, who was now president of the Executive Yuan in Nanjing, had sent men with money a second time because he wanted something more. He struck his chest with an open hand. "If President Sun still takes Wang Jiuguang for a man, a word passed on would have been enough. Why come specially to bring money? Am I some shameless creature who knows money and not men?"

"Not at all, not at all. President Sun's meaning is that if he is to ask Brother Jiuguang to act again, there must be funds for it. So let us hear no more about the money. This is fifty thousand, and before we left President Sun said that when it is done there will be a further reward." Seeing Wang Yaqiao so chivalrous and so indifferent to money, Xiao Focheng was pleased, and came to the point. "It is this way, Brother Jiuguang. Ever since Mr. Chiang decided on another period of cooperation with Wang Jingwei, he has had Sun Ke made a member of the Central Executive Committee and Wang Jingwei installed as president of the Executive Yuan. But before long Mr. Wang, in constant friction with Chiang Kai-shek, offered his resignation. Mr. Sun Ke had no choice but to accept Chiang's appointment and take the Executive Yuan as acting president. But who could know that everything old Chiang does is a snare? On the surface he had to let Mr. Sun have the Executive Yuan; underneath he has been egging on Finance Minister T.V. Soong to hobble him, to cross and thwart President Sun at every turn. So Mr. Sun's presidency of the Executive Yuan is an empty name. He has therefore decided to ask Brother Jiuguang to put out a hand and rid him of this T.V. Soong who lords it over everyone. What does Brother Jiuguang say to it?"

Hearing him out, Wang Yaqiao felt the long-unavenged grudge against Chiang Kai-shek take fire again. He knew T.V. Soong was Chiang Kai-shek's brother-in-law, that the two of them had been thick as thieves for years, and that Soong had the National Government's purse in his hands and had grown into a great financial magnate below one man and above ten thousand. Wang Yaqiao remembered the commission Sun Ke had given him at the start of the year and how it had failed, and saw that this was the best possible moment to make it good. Without another word he struck his chest. "There is nothing to discuss. Go back to Nanjing and report to President Sun: within one month I guarantee T.V. Soong's head comes off!"

That night Wang Yaqiao went back to the French Concession house with the fifty thousand Xiao Focheng and the others had brought. He told his wife Wang Yaying that Sun Ke had sent Xiao Focheng to Shanghai a second time with a commission to kill. Wang Yaying said: "To help Sun Ke kill T.V. Soong is a duty we cannot decline. Only this time the planning must be thorough. Better to spend longer in preparation than to have another heartbreaking business like Lushan."

Wang Yaqiao said, "That goes without saying. This time I shall go to Nanjing myself to lay it out. Until we have T.V. Soong's movements clear, no hand goes out."

Two days later, on a rainy night, a train ran out of Shanghai North Station for Nanjing.

In one of the compartments a man and a woman slept, two passengers out of the ordinary. The man was Wang Yaqiao, of course, and the woman was Wang Yaying. They were going to Nanjing expressly to plan how to get at T.V. Soong, who at that time lived in his residence at Jiming Temple in Nanjing.

When Wang Yaqiao and his wife reached Nanjing quietly with a few followers, they put up at the house of Yu Likui at No. 24 Xianhe Street, a deep, secluded courtyard well away from the noise of the old Jinling city. On two nights running Wang Yaqiao secretly convened his trusted men — Yu Likui, Hua Kezhi, Zheng Baozhen, Long Lin, Liu Gang, Sun Fengming and the rest — to work out how the Finance Minister T.V. Soong might be killed as quickly as possible.

Yu Likui, who knew Nanjing, heard him out and thought the difficulties serious. Having learned his lesson from waiting at the Ming Palace airfield for a hare to run into a tree, he was distinctly cautious this time. "T.V. Soong is only the Finance Minister; he has no crowd of guards before and behind him the way Chiang Kai-shek does. But the man is extremely wary. To protect himself he keeps four or five bodyguards at his side. There are not many of them, but every one is highly trained, and with a gun they could put a shot through a willow leaf at a hundred paces. In ordinary circumstances we should find it hard to get near. So there must be no carelessness."

Hua Kezhi said, "These last days I have been out with several of the brothers scouting the Soong residence in secret, and we found the Jiming Temple district full of guards. Those are the security forces Chiang Kai-shek specially ordered the Attendants' Office to detach to protect his brother-in-law. And besides them there are police from the Nanjing municipal force stationed around Jiming Temple. With all that, I am afraid there is little chance of success if we make our plans at Jiming Temple."

Wang Yaying, hearing this, grew gloomy herself. She said, "There is another thing against it. If we act in Nanjing, killing T.V. Soong will bring the army and police down hunting us at once. This is not Shanghai, after all. Nanjing is the Kuomintang's capital!"

That remark of Wang Yaying's set off Sun Fengming, who had been sitting silent to one side. He said, "When I was studying here I knew T.V. Soong had a house in Shanghai as well. If he has a home there, surely he does not stay in Nanjing forever?"

Wang Yaqiao's face cleared of its cloud at once and his eyes lit. "Yes — yes! How did I forget that T.V. Soong has a residence in Shanghai too? Soong Yao-ju made his start in Shanghai, after all. If we can find out exactly when T.V. Soong goes back to Shanghai, then Shanghai is the place to do it. That would be far and away the safest."

That night the meeting decided: Yu Likui, Hua Kezhi and the others would go on tracking T.V. Soong's movements in Nanjing, and above all establish exactly when he would return to Shanghai. Wang Yaqiao would take Wang Yaying, Sun Fengming, Long Lin and the rest back to Shanghai to make the preparations for the killing.

Before long Hua Kezhi came secretly down to Shanghai to report to Wang Yaqiao on the recent watch kept on T.V. Soong at Jiming Temple and elsewhere. Hua Kezhi said, "T.V. Soong has a house in Shanghai, but he and his wife Zhang Leyi live in Nanjing for long stretches, and the times he goes back to Shanghai are not fixed. What can be said for certain is that he manages to get back to Shanghai once a month. Barring anything unusual."

"Good." Wang Yaqiao heard Hua Kezhi's report out, thought a moment and then told him to return to Nanjing at once. "Kezhi, when you get back, tell Yu Likui to buy a man in the Finance Ministry at any price — a man who can know T.V. Soong's movements. Best of all one of Soong's own secretaries. We cannot stand about in front of and behind the Soong residence every day watching for him. That way lies the same mistake we made over Lushan. Only with a hidden sentry at Soong's own elbow can we know the pattern of his movements exactly."

Hua Kezhi travelled back to Jinling that same night to arrange it. Yu Likui was an old Nanjing hand, and thanks to his particular standing in military and political circles he very quickly picked out a target who could get close to T.V. Soong at any time.

Her name was Wei Xiuyan. She was tall and finely made, and although delicate and beautiful she was no flirt; her work never went wrong. She was one of the clerical staff about T.V. Soong, handling his incoming and outgoing papers. Years of trusted service had gone so far that this woman secretary even carried a key to T.V. Soong's office door.

Yu Likui had settled on Wei Xiuyan because she had been in the same class as a niece of his at Fudan University in Shanghai. Through the niece, Yu Likui invited Wei Xiuyan to dinner again and again, and gave her presents, until it had grown to the point where he could hand her banknotes directly. It was under this painstaking purchase that Wei Xiuyan at last agreed to help Yu Likui by supplying intelligence about T.V. Soong. And so Wang Yaqiao solved the great problem of knowing T.V. Soong's movements at any moment.

In the last days of June, with the preparations for killing T.V. Soong at their final stage, his wife Wang Yaying put forward a still more detailed plan of action. Her plan set out clearly not only how the men were to act once T.V. Soong had reached Shanghai North Station, but proposed establishing a temporary command post near the station; and it took in such details as how the gunmen were to get away from the scene afterward.

"The best thing would be to rent a house near North Station, and you direct it in person from close by. That way you can head off anything unexpected." Wang Yaying was a woman of fine judgement and a clear head, and had every detail that might arise in the killing at her fingertips. "The moment the shot goes home, the army and police will surround the platform. Our men should have bleaching powder ready in their hands to throw. When the time comes, one toss of the powder will put the whole place under a cloud. Even if the police come after them, our men can get out of the station in the confusion."

"Good — my lady's arrangements are almost too thorough!" Wang Yaqiao had not expected Wang Yaying to have planned it so completely. He decided on the spot to rent a room near North Station where the gunmen could rest or lie hidden before the killing, and had his men prepare a package of bleaching powder. With that, everything for the killing of Soong was ready and only the east wind was wanting.

While Wang Yaqiao was laying his secret plans against Soong in the French Concession, nobody would have imagined that two Japanese were also planning an assassination in Shanghai. It was toward evening. A motorcar came quietly over the Waibaidu Bridge toward the far side of Suzhou Creek. Before long the mysterious car turned off the crowded road into a little street with few people in it and pulled up in front of a Japanese restaurant, the Tamanoi.

The door opened and a Japanese in a coffee-brown Western suit got down. His name was Tanaka Ryūkichi, and his post was chief of the East Asia section of the Japanese Army Ministry. As far back as the eve of the Huanggutun affair, Tanaka Ryūkichi had slipped secretly out of Tokyo into China's northeast. First he worked at Dalian to bring Zhang Zuolin over to the Japanese; when that failed he went on to Beijing to make contact with the Japanese minister to China, watching for a chance to push Chiang Kai-shek's Northern Expedition army into forcing Zhang Zuolin back into the northeast. Then Tanaka Ryūkichi slipped back to Shenyang and, with Yamamoto Daisaku and other young hotheads of the Kwantung Army, laid the killing trap at Huanggutun outside Shenyang that blew Zhang Zuolin to pieces. Now Tanaka Ryūkichi was on another mission of importance, and had come by way of Nanjing to the great city of eastern China, Shanghai, without a soul knowing it.

Since arriving in Shanghai he had kept well clear of the Japanese Consulate-General there and had put up, in the character of a Japanese merchant, at a Chinese hotel near the Waibaidu Bridge. This evening he had gone quietly out to meet an important guest in secret at the Tamanoi.

A waitress went ahead and showed Tanaka Ryūkichi into an inner room. She slid the closed door back carefully; on the tatami inside, a bald Japanese in a yellow kimono was already sitting straight-backed. At the sight of Tanaka Ryūkichi in his Western suit and his air of haughty command, the bald man rose hurriedly from the low table to greet him. "Mr. Tanaka, to meet you in Shanghai is the greatest honour of my life!"

"Mr. Inamura, it is really quite natural." Tanaka Ryūkichi gave a cold smile, took off his shoes and settled cross-legged on the tatami. Two Japanese waitresses came in and set out plain-grilled dishes, sashimi and the rest with great deference. Tanaka picked up a bottle of sake and filled the other man's cup. "Because before very long the whole of Shina will be our great Japan's. We shall be able to meet whenever we like, not only in Shanghai but in Nanjing and Wuhan. What is there to make a fuss about?"

Inamura Kagorō was only a captain, and he knew that the Tanaka Ryūkichi sitting opposite him was a high officer with the rank of major general. So he flattered and fawned for all he was worth and pressed cup after cup on Tanaka. Then he waved the waitresses with their food and drink out of the room, shut the door, and they began to talk of great matters in secret.

"Mr. Tanaka, I do not understand. Why should a man of your standing, coming to Shanghai, refuse to lodge at our consulate and put up instead at an ordinary Chinese hotel that is not even luxurious?" Inamura looked at Tanaka's heavy face and cold manner in some perplexity, unable for the moment to make out the real purpose of his secret arrival in Shanghai.

"That is because I am charged with a special mission, and no one at the consulate is to know of it. Consul-General Shigemitsu Mamoru above all must not be allowed to know I am in Shanghai. Do you take my meaning?" Tanaka's words were startling, and his manner carried force.

"This — I truly do not understand."

"It is a high secret of our Japanese Army Ministry." Tanaka thought for a long while, and finally spoke. "I have come to Shanghai this time to kill a man here."

"Kill a man?"

"Yes. And not only kill a man — I want you to kill an influential personage for me! Do you dare?"

"Kill which one?"

"Which one I cannot yet say. I only ask you: do you dare, or do you not?"

Inamura's face went from grey to white, and a fine cold sweat came out on his bald forehead. From the murder in Tanaka's face he had already grasped that the man had come with no good in him. After a pause he stammered: "Surely not — like that Chinese killer Wang Yaqiao, striking at Chiang Kai-shek on Lushan?"

"What use is killing Chiang? We are going to kill a Japanese, and this Japanese must be a man of considerable influence." Tanaka had drunk several cups of sake by now, and his face had gone strangely pale; under the bright lamp it was almost frightening.

Inamura was aghast. "Kill one of our own Japanese?"

"Exactly so."

"But why? We came to China to build the Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere. If we want to kill somebody, surely it should be Chinese — China has so many people for us to practise our guns and blades on. Why should we cut our own flesh and bone?" Inamura assumed the devilish major general opposite him had drunk too much and was talking nonsense. Thinking his ears had deceived him, he blurted out: "You must have had too much, surely?"

"Too much of what?" Tanaka snatched the half-bottle of sake out of his hands in a fury and drank hard from the neck. Inamura watched the flush the sake brought up in Tanaka's cheeks and could not guess what medicine he was selling from that gourd.

"Of course, if you want me to kill, I can kill; if you want me to kill our own people, that can be done too. But what in the world is it for?" Inamura wanted to go and take the bottle from his hand but did not dare, and only muttered, "Mr. Tanaka, you must at least let me understand why we are killing, before I can be of service to you!"

"Very well. Listen to me." Tanaka turned grave now, and a solemnity and tension came into that long horse-shaped face that startled Inamura. "Mr. Inamura, I can tell you this: our Kwantung Army is at this moment preparing to set off an affair in Manchuria that will shake China and the world. After that we shall move fast to widen the gains. Once we have taken Shenyang, I guarantee that within a few months Rehe, Beiping and Tianjin will fall into our hands as well. And that is only the smallest of beginnings in swallowing China!"

"Good heavens — swallow a China of that size?" Inamura had been years in China himself, but his ambitions did not run to those of the major general across the table. He knew that for a Japan the size of a pellet to swallow the whole of China was as unimaginable as a snake swallowing an elephant.

Tanaka laughed coldly. "What is strange in that? Our great Japan is precisely now in its years of martial ascendancy. If we do not lay hands on China now, the chance will be gone in the future. So our Army Ministry's plan is to occupy the whole of eastern China as soon as possible; and since Shanghai is the great city of the east, the best course is to take this important city first!"

Hearing Tanaka's plan, Inamura understood that he was speaking for the highest levels of the Japanese military and passing on a special order. A moment ago he had been shocked and baffled by what Tanaka said; now at last he understood, and he cried out in delight: "Splendid! Shanghai has been the paradise of our dreams for years. Only — when can this all but visionary military plan be realized? It is not Mr. Tanaka's drunken talk?"

"A soldier does not jest. Should I talk drunkenness in front of you?" Tanaka saw that the moment had come, and brought out the secret he had been carrying. "And this is exactly what I have been telling you — the reason why a Japanese must be killed in Shanghai!"

"But I still do not understand. What necessary connection is there between our attacking the great city of Shanghai and our killing a Japanese?" "Manufacture an incident, to lay the ground for a military attack — the Nakamura affair in Manchuria is the best example there is." "Ah — ah, I see it!" At this Inamura let out a breath in sudden enlightenment. "You mean that we manufacture a Nakamura affair in Shanghai too, and then we can launch our attack on Shanghai in perfectly good name?"

"That is precisely my meaning." Tanaka drank several more cups; his face was flushed blood-red now and a greedy fire came up in his eyes. He snapped his chopsticks in two and said through his teeth: "So we must choose a Japanese with influence. Once he is killed, we announce to the world that the Chinese did it. I hear Shanghai has a Wang Yaqiao who likes killing people, does it not? Having him in Shanghai could not suit us better!"

Inamura was so delighted he waved his arms and clapped his hands, crying out over and over: "Wonderful, wonderful, it is too wonderful. This is what they call killing with a borrowed knife!"

"Not killing with a borrowed knife — killing with Wang Yaqiao's borrowed name!" Tanaka Ryūkichi had plainly heard of Wang Yaqiao's fame before he came to Shanghai. And he had thought and planned the killing through with care. He said with a cold smile: "Now you understand why I have come. And mark this: it is not my private notion but a top-secret operational plan of the great Japanese Army Ministry. You alone are to know the inside of it, and if a word gets out I shall send men to kill you first!"

"My mouth will be a sealed bottle." Sweat was standing on Inamura's bald crown, but he kept asking, "Then whom do we kill?"

"That is one of the things I want you to arrange for me. I shall choose a man of influence, and we must have his movements at once so that it can be done soon."

"In that case — how about killing Ōshima Takeo?"

"Ōshima Takeo?"

"Yes, yes. There is not a soul in Shanghai who does not know Ōshima Takeo the merchant. He runs textile smuggling here and in Hong Kong. In Shanghai or in Hong Kong, no other Japanese merchant operates on his scale. It is precisely because Ōshima Takeo is so rich that his name is so enormous. They say he has married three Chinese women alone. If we killed Ōshima in the streets of Shanghai, would the shock be small?"

Tanaka sat there and stopped drinking, thinking blankly. After a moment he shook his head, unconvinced. "Ōshima Takeo's name in Shanghai is certainly not small. But the man is a merchant, after all. Would killing a merchant bring on a war?"

"Was the Nakamura affair in the northeast not done the very same way?"

Tanaka shook his head stubbornly. "It will not do. A killing in Shanghai must have a direct political connection. Think: afterward we mean to hang the murder on Wang Yaqiao. Wang Yaqiao has always been a political figure. What use has he for killing a Japanese merchant?"

"But which political figure do we kill?" Inamura started.

"How about Shigemitsu Mamoru?"

"Shigemitsu Mamoru? Good heavens — the man is a senior official sent out by the Foreign Ministry, of the rank of army lieutenant general!" Inamura's face went white on the instant. Never in his dreams had he supposed Tanaka's nerve went so far as to bring even Japan's own consul-general in Shanghai into an assassination plan.

Only now did Tanaka show what he really meant, saying, in the manner of a great confidence: "The man we want to kill is precisely somebody of Shigemitsu Mamoru's calibre. Anything smaller will not serve, because it cannot start a great war!"

"But what of the consequences of killing such a man?" Inamura, who a moment before had been cheering Tanaka's murder plan, went suddenly paper-white with terror. It seemed to him that if he were the man who killed Lieutenant General Shigemitsu, then even when the Japanese army did occupy Shanghai he would not escape the reckoning. Cold sweat ran down his face.

"Ha, ha, ha! So my hero of assassination is really not the equal of the Chinese Wang Yaqiao. He dares go at Chiang Kai-shek himself, and you are afraid of one small army lieutenant general, Shigemitsu Mamoru?" Tanaka sat on the tatami roaring with laughter for a while, then turned suddenly serious. "In fact you have nothing whatever to fear. Because we are not going to have you touch Shigemitsu Mamoru at all."

Inamura was taken aback and thoroughly confused. "Not have me do it? Then who is to be the killer?"

"There is no need to be frightened to that degree." Before coming to Shanghai, Tanaka had already submitted the whole plan for killing Shigemitsu to Japanese Army headquarters. Only now did he give out the truth. "I want you to find a Chinese. Do you understand? You are only working this assassination on behalf of our Army Ministry. But you must stay in the dark and never appear. Because if the case is ever broken and it comes out that we Japanese did it, the whole fine performance is ruined!"

Only then did the light break on Inamura. All at once his confidence returned; he slapped the table and swore to it. "If that is how it stands, leave it all to me. I guarantee to find a Chinese scapegoat and put him into the field to do the killing!"

In the dim lamplight the two wine cups touched again.

### 22. Out of Level Ground Springs Another Assassin, and a Terrible One

In Shanghai a day was a year to Wang Yaqiao.

His temper grew more fretful still once high summer and July came in. A month after the failed attempt on Chiang Kai-shek at Lushan, the Hatchet Gang's vital force had still not come back. And if he broke faith a second time over T.V. Soong, how was he to hold his footing on the Shanghai Bund at all?

In the first days of July Yu Likui sent Hua Kezhi secretly to Shanghai again to report on the watch on T.V. Soong in Nanjing. Hua Kezhi said, "Reading the situation from what Miss Wei reports, T.V. Soong has been wanting to go back to Shanghai for some time. But there is always business in Nanjing he cannot get free of, so most likely he will find time to make one trip to Shanghai in the middle of July." With this in hand, Wang Yaqiao ordered Hua Kezhi back to Nanjing to keep watch on Jiming Temple, and to tell him by urgent coded telegram at the first sign of movement, so that Soong could be killed without delay.

After Hua Kezhi had gone, Wang Yaqiao held further meetings at his house in the French Concession, and it was settled in the end that the place to kill T.V. Soong would be the platform at Shanghai North Station. From what they had lately established, every time T.V. Soong came back to Shanghai from Nanjing he took the train from Pukou to Shanghai North, and his seat was always in the last carriage. Feeling the action to be imminent, Wang Yaqiao assigned Sun Fengming and Zheng Baozhen to direct the operation against Soong on the ground. He himself would wait in the rented house near North Station, ready to come to their support at any moment.

There Wang Yaqiao and Sun Fengming and the others made the final dispositions. Long Lin and Sun Fengming were to be the gunmen; Tang Ming and Gong Chunpu were to cover the withdrawal; Zheng Baozhen was responsible for the weapons and the tools of the killing. Liu Gang and Li Zhan were to wait outside with a car to pick up Sun Fengming and the rest as they came out of the station. With the preparations carried this far, what still concerned Wang Yaqiao most was the cover for the escape.

"Talcum powder would be the best thing for making a fog after the shooting." Zheng Baozhen, who had originally argued for buying smoke grenades, now came out with a bold idea. "If we buy specially made smoke grenades in Shanghai it is bound to make somebody suspicious. I think the best course is to disturb nobody: we simply throw ordinary talc."

"Good. Send someone out for talcum powder at once." Wang Yaqiao approved Zheng Baozhen's proposal.

Back at his own lodgings in Pudong, Zheng Baozhen immediately sent for his trusted subordinate Liu Haichuan. This Liu Haichuan was the right hand of the Iron and Blood Traitor-Purging Corps that Zheng Baozhen ran, and made it his particular business to gather intelligence in the concessions. Being a sociable man, Liu Haichuan had many friends in the French and British concessions, and dealings in particular with people of the Green and Red Gangs in Shanghai. That day, having his orders from Zheng Baozhen to get hold of two sacks of talc, Liu Haichuan went down to the Shiliupu wharf, where there was a builders' merchant called the Wanfeng company. He had a friend there named Huang Aliu. When he mentioned that he wanted two sacks of talc, Huang Aliu was astonished. "Now that is a strange thing. Why has talc suddenly come into fashion? Boss Chang sent someone round only a little while ago and took two sacks out of the shop. Has this stuff turned into a scarce commodity too?"

Hearing the three syllables *Boss Chang*, Liu Haichuan felt a faint jolt, but he thought no further about it; he took the two sacks of talc and delivered them straight to Zheng Baozhen. Incidentally, he passed on what Huang Aliu had said. Zheng Baozhen paid no attention to it at the time either, and went off to Wang Yaqiao's house; and there, having handed over the two sacks of talc, he was on the point of telling Wang Yaqiao what Liu Haichuan had heard in the shop at Shiliupu wharf, that Boss Chang had sent for talc as well. But Wang Yaqiao, all nerves, cut in with an instruction: "Old Zheng, why is there still no word from Nanjing? Go up to Nanjing this afternoon and press Yu Likui and Hua Kezhi. They must get the situation right. On no account let T.V. Soong slip out from under our guns. If we fail again, how are we to make our way on the Shanghai Bund in future?"

Home again, Zheng Baozhen hurriedly had his driver Liu Haichuan take a lorry and run him to the station for the midday train to Nanjing. Halfway there the matter came back into his head. Just before boarding, Zheng Baozhen said to Liu Haichuan, "Haichuan — that Boss Chang you heard about this morning. Would that be Chang Yuqing, the head of the Anqing Gang?"

"That is the man." It had not occurred to Liu Haichuan that his remark would catch Zheng Baozhen's attention.

"That one is no small matter. When we were in that fight at law with Du Yuesheng a few years back, it was this same Chang Yuqing who made a great deal of trouble in the middle of it." Zheng Baozhen, remembering the old business of the dispute over Li Guojie's *Jiang'an*, hated the man who had stirred things up at Du Yuesheng's elbow. Later — though nobody could have foreseen it — when the Japanese came, it was this same trusted enforcer of Du Yuesheng's who betrayed the patron who had made him, and went over to a new master, the Japanese consul in Nanjing, Tada Ichirō. Before long, with Tada's backing, he put together an "Anqing Gang" of his own; and now here he was raising a following that belonged to him on the Shanghai Bund. Thinking about Boss Chang sending men out for talc too, Zheng Baozhen could not help being suspicious. "What I find questionable is why Chang Yuqing should suddenly take an interest in talcum powder, exactly as we have. Can he have an assassination of his own in hand?"

"Impossible. Where would you find a coincidence like that?" Liu Haichuan laughed and shook his head.

The more Zheng Baozhen turned it over the less easy he felt, and he charged Liu Haichuan: "Care costs nothing. I think you had better find some way of finding out why Chang Yuqing wants talc as well. But when you ask, be extremely careful. Chang Yuqing's people must not get the least wind of it."

Liu Haichuan nodded and nodded again. As soon as he had seen Zheng Baozhen onto the Nanjing train he went off to look for a friend in the Anqing Gang, meaning to stand him dinner and drink and get at the business of Chang Yuqing's talc by roundabout questions.

The train ran on, rumbling and fast, across the green country south of the river, and by afternoon Zheng Baozhen was under Purple Mountain, where the tiger crouches and the dragon coils.

Reaching Yu Likui's house in Xianhe Street, Zheng Baozhen found him and Hua Kezhi anxious at having no word of when T.V. Soong would return to Shanghai. Not long after Zheng Baozhen had passed on Wang Yaqiao's instructions, Yu Likui took an urgent telephone call from Miss Wei, and everybody came alive at once, for the message was: "Tomorrow, the morning of 23 July, T.V. Soong returns to Shanghai!"

The particulars were these. T.V. Soong had suddenly received an urgent telegram from Qingdao: his mother, Ni Guizhen, was critically ill and he was to go with Soong Mei-ling at once to see her. By rights T.V. Soong should have gone to Qingdao with Soong Mei-ling together. But since his wife Zhang Leyi was in Shanghai, he meant to go back to Shanghai first and take Zhang Leyi with him to Qingdao.

"This is heaven's own opening. T.V. Soong's span is up." Yu Likui and Hua Kezhi, fretting a moment before over the delay, saw their brows clear. They knew that the great killing they had spent months preparing was now on top of them.

"The best thing is to get the intelligence to Shanghai at once." Zheng Baozhen knew Wang Yaqiao was waiting in Shanghai in a fever of impatience; but it was already dusk, and the evening train to Shanghai was gone.

"There is nothing for it but a telegram." Yu Likui thought and then added, "Though sending a telegram carries a risk."

Hua Kezhi knitted his brows and a plan came to him. "I have a way. I guarantee no outsider will be able to see a flaw in it." He fetched paper and brush at once and dashed off a line of text: FATHER GRAVELY ILL. TAKING TOMORROW MORNING TRAIN TO SHANGHAI ON TIME FOR TREATMENT. MEET TRAIN WITHOUT FAIL. URGENT. DO NOT ERR.

Yu Likui and Zheng Baozhen read the message over and agreed that it stated plainly the time of T.V. Soong's arrival in Shanghai the next morning while leaving no outsider able to find the smallest thread in such ordinary wording. A man was sent to the telegraph office at once and the wire went off express.

At nightfall Zheng Baozhen, Yu Likui and Hua Kezhi were eating by lamplight when the hidden sentry at the gate came in to report. "Mr. Zheng, there is a visitor from Shanghai outside. He will not be put off; he must see you at once. I told him there was no Mr. Zheng here, but the man is fighting his way in."

Hua Kezhi and Yu Likui were startled to hear that somebody had come to the door from Shanghai. Only Zheng Baozhen seemed to have expected it, and told the sentry hurriedly, "That will be Liu Haichuan. Let him in, quickly. There must be something urgent."

And in indeed came Liu Haichuan, from whom he had parted at Shanghai station at midday. He was travel-stained and out of breath. He drank a mouthful of water and then hurried out the important intelligence he had come by.

It appeared that not long after Zheng Baozhen left, Liu Haichuan had contrived to find Ma Fujian, one of the seniors of the Anqing Gang. In their younger days the two had been mechanics together at a motor works in Shanghai and their private friendship was warm. He was very pleased to have Liu Haichuan take him for a drink at a small eating house, and after three rounds of wine the talk was steered toward Chang Yuqing, the Anqing Gang boss then very much on the rise in Shanghai. Asked by Liu Haichuan why Chang Yuqing should have sent men to buy talc, Ma Fujian let the secret out.

"I will tell you something big, and you must not pass it on for the world." Ma Fujian, with several cups of thin wine down him, had already forgotten the Anqing Gang's prohibitions. He said that one evening not long before he had driven Chang Yuqing to a Japanese restaurant called the Tamanoi for dinner. Unexpectedly, two Japanese of very furtive bearing were there waiting for Chang Yuqing. While they talked in secret Ma Fujian was in the outer room and could not make out what was said inside. Later, over dinner, one of the Japanese invited Ma Fujian into the inner room as well, and there he happened to overhear Chang Yuqing asking the Japanese: "I never imagined I should be given a thing of this size to do. My worry is that if it does succeed, and you enter Shanghai, will somebody call me to account for it?"

"No, no, Mr. Chang. This is an order of the Japanese Army Ministry; nobody will dare do anything to you. So far from being cursed for it, you will be a meritorious founder of our Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere!" The Japanese in the Western suit clapped him on the shoulder and gave him his promise.

"Here is the payment. When it is done there will be a heavy reward besides." The bald man in the kimono set a weighty case in front of Chang Yuqing and opened it: it was full of gleaming gold bars and banknotes. Ma Fujian had never seen so much money in his life and his eyes went wide with shock.

"It is only that the man we are to kill is too important. Who in Shanghai does not know Shigemitsu Mamoru?" The heavy money in the case had moved Chang Yuqing, but his face was still tight with strain.

"Rest easy. Even dying at a Shanghai railway station, Shigemitsu Mamoru will be founding a merit for our great Japan." The Japanese in the Western suit spoke gravely. "One man falls, and countless Japanese soldiers may set foot on this land we have dreamed of for years. When that day comes, he will have died in the right place."

Listening to Ma Fujian, Liu Haichuan grew tense to his fingertips. He had never imagined that while the Iron and Blood Traitor-Purging Corps was secretly planning to kill T.V. Soong, another band of Japanese soldiers was buying Chang Yuqing of the Anqing Gang to manufacture a second killing, a bloody one, against Shigemitsu Mamoru. What the Japanese wanted to kill was a famous Japanese consular official. Liu Haichuan questioned him further, and so learned that the two sacks of talc Chang Yuqing had sent for that morning were for exactly the same purpose as their own — for a killing to be staged at Shanghai North Station the next morning. The difference was that the man Chang Yuqing and the Anqing Gang meant to kill was the Japanese consul Shigemitsu Mamoru, a man the Chinese hated to the marrow.

"So that is it. Then Shigemitsu Mamoru is in Nanjing too?" Hua Kezhi drew a cold breath as the light broke on him. Never in his dreams had he supposed the world so treacherous, or that the Japanese would manufacture a bloody outrage in Shanghai.

Yu Likui said, "Shigemitsu Mamoru comes to Nanjing often. My guess is that he will very likely be on the same train back to Shanghai as T.V. Soong tomorrow."

Zheng Baozhen said, turning it over without result, "What I do not understand is why Chang Yuqing should assassinate a consul on behalf of a pack of Japanese *rōnin*."

Hua Kezhi thought for a long while and said, "I have it. From the intelligence just given, the Japanese *rōnin* buying Chang Yuqing to kill Shigemitsu Mamoru may be a plot of the very largest kind!"

"A plot?" Everyone sat stunned. That Japanese should kill Japanese was genuinely beyond their imagining.

Hua Kezhi was quick-witted and full of stratagem, and he very soon built out of Liu Haichuan's scraps of intelligence an analysis of the grave events about to break in Shanghai. "The Japanese cannot wait to swallow China whole. If they want to occupy Shanghai they must have a pretext for opening hostilities. Let Chang Yuqing's men kill their own consul in Shanghai, and has the Japanese army not found its pretext for taking the city?"

Yu Likui, Zheng Baozhen and the rest listened to Hua Kezhi's analysis and their spirits sank at once. They said, one after another, "If this plot succeeds, will the great city of Shanghai not be laid waste?"

Hua Kezhi said, "That Japan means to seize Shanghai is Sima Zhao's intent, that every man in the road knows already. Even knowing the plot, I am afraid there is little we can do. What matters now is that this business is a grave threat to both the reputation and the safety of our Elder Brother Wang. If the plot succeeds, Elder Brother Wang is a traitor to the nation from that day on!"

"How so?" Yu Likui and Zheng Baozhen were aghast.

Hua Kezhi carried the analysis on. "Consider. If the Japanese want to assassinate Shigemitsu Mamoru, why buy Chang Yuqing to be their killer? Why do it in Shanghai? Because with Shigemitsu Mamoru dead, Elder Brother Wang becomes the goat. Once there is a killing in Shanghai there is not a soul who will not think of a certain king of assassins, Wang Yaqiao. And Elder Brother Wang is iron-hard against Japan. If they do not suspect him of murdering Shigemitsu Mamoru, whom will they suspect?"

The air in the room went tight; every man there was in deep alarm and dread at the urgent intelligence Liu Haichuan had just brought. There was no doubt of it: if Shigemitsu Mamoru and T.V. Soong were shot on the same morning, the Nanjing government would certainly issue a warrant for the murderer Wang Yaqiao, and the Japanese army would use it to launch a war of invasion — and the curses of the world would follow. For the man who lit the fuse of the war would be Wang Yaqiao.

"This will not do. I must get back to Shanghai tonight. How can such a thing be allowed?" Hearing Hua Kezhi's reasoning and thinking of the consequences that would follow, Zheng Baozhen could hold still no longer and wanted to start back for Shanghai that night.

"But it is already too late to get back to Shanghai!" Hua Kezhi was like a strategist unshaken on the eve of battle; facing an unavoidable bloodletting in front of the station, he was cool and steady. "Even if we could catch a night train to Shanghai, Elder Brother Wang would have no time to stop what is coming."

Yu Likui said, in a fever of anxiety, "Then let us simply send another telegram advising him not to act, so that he does not walk into the Japanese trap."

Hua Kezhi did not agree. "Suppose we could persuade Elder Brother Wang to break off at once. Can you stop Chang Yuqing from killing Shigemitsu Mamoru?"

"That is true." Yu Likui and Zheng Baozhen sighed and shook their heads, helpless to stop the Japanese plot.

Zheng Baozhen said, "The Japanese are too cunning by half. They buy Chinese to do the deed, so that even if our men never go near North Station we shall not escape the suspicion of the killing in the end. It looks as though all we can do is act at the same time as the Japanese and kill T.V. Soong."

Hua Kezhi, who had been sitting silent, suddenly found his way out of the corner. "I have it!"

"What is it?" Everyone hoped he had a turn to offer. Hua Kezhi beckoned with his hand and the others leaned in together. In the dim lamplight Zheng Baozhen, Yu Likui and Liu Haichuan heard him out, and their strained faces slowly settled. They said, "Things being as they are, there is nothing else for it."

### 23. Two Bands of Assassins Take Aim at the Same Stand-in Dead Man

Next day, at first light, Wang Yaqiao was already keyed up and putting his gunmen into their battle stations, for the night before he had received Zheng Baozhen's telegram from Nanjing with its "meet the train." The chance must not be let slip; he wanted the gunmen to succeed, because otherwise he could not face Sun Ke and the rest.

"Now — set your watches!" At six in the morning Wang Yaqiao was already in the rented house near Shanghai North Station. It was a small three-storey building, and from the top floor one could see several bright lines of track inside North Station and the trains shuttling to and fro; each blast of a whistle set the little house humming. Wang Yaqiao looked round at the dozen-odd gunmen sitting about him — Sun Fengming, Long Lin, Gong Chunpu, Xie Wenda, Liu Gang and the others — and took out his pocket watch. Everyone raised a wrist at the same moment: it was exactly a quarter past six. Wang Yaqiao showed them the telegram from Nanjing that had come the night before. "The train from Nanjing gets into Shanghai at half past ten. We divide now into three groups. Every man acts on my order. Be sure of your target before you move, and make certain of it."

Wang Yaqiao then fixed on Sun Fengming, Long Lin, Tang Ming and three others, six men in all, as the main assault, spread out over the ground between the platform and the exit. The six would buy platform tickets and go through early on the pretext of meeting friends and relatives. The moment T.V. Soong stepped down from the train, they were to shoot decisively while the alighting passengers were thickest, then scatter talc and get out of the station. The second group, principally Gong Chunpu and his men, would wait outside the exit on the stretch running to the street in front of the station, likewise with pistols and talc at their waists, ready to shoot at T.V. Soong outside the station should he by some luck escape Sun Fengming's party within it. The third group, four men under Xie Wenda, would wait on the road T.V. Soong must take to his residence, carrying detonators and explosives, ready to blow up his car if the first two groups both failed to hit him.

"Disposed like this, T.V. Soong is in a formation he cannot survive." Having set out his three parties, Wang Yaqiao spoke with entire confidence. "What I require of you all is that you not let slip an opportunity that will vanish in an instant. Understand that every one of your groups is vital. The favourable moment is really only a few minutes, perhaps only a few seconds. Slacken for an instant and the target is gone. I shall be directing from close by, and if anything unexpected shows itself on the ground, act on my hand signals."

There was no way on earth Wang Yaqiao could have known that while he was making his tense dispositions, in a large building not far from the station some twenty-odd men were also making ready for action, gathered in a shadowy sitting room. This was the secret base of a second band of assassins, and at its head was the Anqing Gang boss, Chang Yuqing.

"Every one of you pay a hundred times' attention. The man we are killing today is no ordinary sort. He is the Japanese consul-general in Shanghai, and his name is Shigemitsu Mamoru! Look well at this photograph and fix the man in your minds. When the moment comes you must not put your bullets into the wrong target!" Chang Yuqing was in high spirits himself that day, knowing that when the killing was over Tanaka Ryūkichi would pay him a fee running to several hundred thousand. But it was the first time he had done anything on this scale, and his manner was uncommonly strained.

"Rest easy, Boss Chang, I have this Japanese fixed in my mind!" "He won't get away!" "Damn them, the Japanese have got too fierce; it's high time somebody showed them a little colour." The gunmen shouted over one another. The photograph of Shigemitsu Mamoru passed from hand to hand among the twenty-odd assassins: a Japanese official in a white Western suit, handsome and elegant, no more than forty-odd years old, without the ferocious look people expected of Japanese soldiers and without the frightening little moustache, a square face and a pair of finely made gold-rimmed spectacles, and a dark bow tie under the collar of a snow-white shirt.

"And every man remember: if you do bring it off at the station, do not forget to drop that thing beside Shigemitsu Mamoru's body." Chang Yuqing might be committing his first assassination, but his design of every link in it was thorough and exact. Having had Tanaka Ryūkichi's hints about the whole background of the affair, he knew that a line of retreat after success was vital for the Anqing Gang. He handed two grenades to Ma Daxiang, the leader of the gunmen, charging him again and again: "These are the material evidence that will keep us all out of trouble hereafter!"

Taking the two grenades, Ma Daxiang found that Chang Yuqing had been at work on them beforehand: on each of them the two characters *hatchet* had been painted in red lacquer. That was the evidence to be left for the army and police who would investigate the case; anybody at all, from two grenades marked *hatchet*, would think of Shanghai's famous killer Wang Yaqiao, and nobody would suspect the ordinarily obscure Chang Yuqing.

"That is where our cleverness lies." Chang Yuqing gave Ma Daxiang and the other gunmen a cold, self-satisfied laugh. "So once you are on the ground, act in the Hatchet Gang's style. To leave the station staff and the patrolmen with an impression, every one of you is to speak Anhui dialect. Remember it — it is the best possible cover. Do exactly as I have told you and we shall kill Shigemitsu Mamoru and have somebody else stand in as the dead man for us as well. Tell me — where in the world is there business as good as that? And rest easy: when it is done there is a share for everybody!"

"Now that is a brilliant idea!" "This is a scheme without a seam in it!" "Damn my grandmother — let's see how Wang Yaqiao's Hatchet Gang gets clear of killing a Japanese after this!" "Boss Chang is bringing down two birds with one arrow: he ruins Wang Yaqiao and makes a fortune for our brothers. Where is there business like it in the world?" The twenty-odd Anqing Gang men were ordinarily idle and bored, and every one of them short of a stake for the tables. Having at last come by a piece of easy money, how should they not chance their necks for it?

"Look hard at Shigemitsu Mamoru's photograph and be sure you remember his face. The most important mark is this: the fellow is fondest of wearing a white Western suit. A jujube-red bow tie, red shoes." Chang Yuqing, in dread that his gunmen would shoot the wrong man, went over it again. "And be careful — Shigemitsu Mamoru may have staff with him. On no account take one of his staff for Shigemitsu Mamoru. Have you all heard me?"

"Heard you, heard you. It will go without a hitch!" They shouted it in a ragged chorus.

"But damn you, don't be pleased with yourselves too early. If any man of you funks it at the station, or fires at the wrong man, look out for me!" Seeing his followers bawling at Shigemitsu Mamoru's photograph, Chang Yuqing still would not let it go, and shouted them down. "Enough. The train comes in at half past ten. Now everybody to his post as I have told you. Be very careful, and let no man's gun go off before the train is in. Damn it, whoever ruins this business of mine, I shoot him first!"

"Rest easy, Boss Chang!" Chang Yuqing gave them a wave, and the twenty-odd gunmen slipped out of the building in ones and twos and made off toward the crowded station.

As for the passenger train that had left Nanjing at first light, it was at that moment running toward Shanghai at full speed. At its rear was a first-class carriage of compartments carrying senior officials of the Nanjing government with their families and staff. The Nanjing National Government's Minister of Finance, T.V. Soong, was sitting in that carriage.

T.V. Soong had always liked black or grey suits before; but that day, for reasons nobody could name, as though some spirit had had a hand in it, he had put on a suit of snow-white, with a bright red bow tie under the collar. More unexpected still, the confidential secretary travelling back to Shanghai with him was also wearing a suit of the same snow-white. This secretary's name was Tang Yulu, a brilliant Harvard graduate lately returned from studying abroad, whose build and bearing were extremely like T.V. Soong's; to an outsider's careless eye it would have been very easy to take Tang Yulu for the National Government's Finance Minister.

Master and man were not only remarkably alike in appearance; both wore white Panama hats as well. The only difference was that Tang Yulu, being the confidential secretary, had a red-brown leather case under his arm. Besides him, eight plainclothes attendants travelled at T.V. Soong's side, escorts specially assigned to him by Chiang Kai-shek. T.V. Soong would make a short stop in Shanghai and go on from there to Qingdao to see his sick mother. Weighed down with the thought of it, he said very little on the journey. But the Japanese consul-general in Shanghai, Shigemitsu Mamoru, in the seat next to his and travelling to Shanghai on the same train, kept looking for a chance to engage him in conversation.

"If Minister Soong wishes, I could accompany you to Qingdao." Shigemitsu turned an ingratiating, smiling face on the preoccupied T.V. Soong.

"No. Thank you, that will not be necessary." T.V. Soong put him off coldly; he had no wish for more conversation with Shigemitsu Mamoru.

Shigemitsu went on manufacturing talk to keep him engaged. "Your honoured mother is well advanced in years. I fear something may happen to the old lady. If her illness is grave, I could bring the finest physicians from Tokyo to attend her."

"Thank you. That will not be needed at present." Having said this, T.V. Soong closed his eyes.

Shigemitsu's own dress that day was very much out of the ordinary as well: for some reason he had left off the snow-white suit he generally liked and, in high summer, had put on silver-grey. The two consulate secretaries with him were also in dark clothes, so that in dress they stood in sharp contrast to T.V. Soong and his party. T.V. Soong's mind at that hour was wholly on his sick mother in Qingdao. He could not have dreamed that at that very moment Shanghai station was thick with assassins, and several black gun-muzzles were waiting to send their hating bullets into him.

In the same train, in carriage nine, there were three other passengers out of the ordinary: two members of Wang Yaqiao's brains trust, Hua Kezhi and Zheng Baozhen, and the driver Liu Haichuan, who had hurried up from Shanghai to Nanjing the night before to report to Zheng Baozhen. While Hua Kezhi and Zheng Baozhen talked over their great matter in the compartment, Liu Haichuan stood guard in the corridor outside, watching the passengers who went by.

"Rather than let the Japanese make use of us, we had better not kill T.V. Soong for the moment. We must not lose the great for the small and bring on a war between China and Japan. If that happened, Elder Brother Yaqiao and all of us would be traitors to the nation!" Hua Kezhi and Zheng Baozhen were still turning over how to stop the Japanese plot to manufacture a pretext for war.

Zheng Baozhen said, "But both sides now have the arrow on the string, and once we are off the train there will be no chance to report Chang Yuqing's plot to Elder Brother Wang at once. So there is nothing for it but the makeshift you worked out last night. Otherwise there is absolutely no way of stopping this frightful killing."

The emergency measure Hua Kezhi had thought out in the night was this: when the three of them arrived at Shanghai station along with T.V. Soong and Shigemitsu Mamoru, then before Chang Yuqing's hidden gunmen could fire on Shigemitsu Mamoru, Hua Kezhi and Zheng Baozhen would fire shots into the air on the platform. Hearing shots, T.V. Soong and Shigemitsu Mamoru would certainly not dare come down from the train. That would inevitably alarm the soldiers, police, gendarmes and agents about the station; and Wang Yaqiao's own prearranged assassination would likewise be brought up short by Hua Kezhi and Zheng Baozhen's sudden shots. Only so could a station killing that would bring on a war be prevented.

While Hua Kezhi and Zheng Baozhen were still discussing in the carriage how to get down first and how to get clear of the station after firing, the train rumbled into Shanghai North Station. Hua Kezhi and the other two at once crowded to the door to be first off. But to their consternation the attendant on that carriage could not get the door open, the key having rusted; and meanwhile the passengers from all the other carriages were pouring onto the platform in a dense mass. By the time the three of them jumped down, the best moment was long gone. Even so, Hua Kezhi could stand on no more ceremony. He pulled the pistol from his waist and fired two shots into the air — crack, crack! In an instant the platform was in an uproar.

At the moment Hua Kezhi fired in front of them all, Sun Fengming, Long Lin and the other gunmen already in position on the platform had just seen several passengers of striking bearing come down out of the last first-class carriage. Of all these assassins, only Sun Fengming had ever set eyes on T.V. Soong, once in Nanjing; Liu Gang, Long Lin and the rest knew the Finance Minister only from photographs Wang Yaqiao had given them beforehand. Now, seeing eight or nine unusual passengers come down from the train, they were dazzled for a moment. Sun Fengming, taking it in, quietly pointed forward for Liu Gang beside him. "Look — the one in the white suit. That is the man we want!"

Liu Gang nodded, but his eyes fastened by mistake on Tang Yulu, who walked in front in his white suit with a briefcase under his arm, and let pass the T.V. Soong following behind him.

Sun Fengming had recognized T.V. Soong at a glance; but as he put his hand into his coat and closed it on the butt of his pistol, he froze where he stood, for he saw that a group of senior Kuomintang officials headed by Wu Tiecheng had been waiting on the platform, and that these men, seeing T.V. Soong step down, all surged forward and closed round Soong, Tang Yulu and the rest on every side. Every one of these officials had staff of his own; with the eight guards T.V. Soong had brought from Nanjing they made what was very nearly an impassable wall of people. This was a scene neither Sun Fengming nor Wang Yaqiao had foreseen. So the hand Sun Fengming had on his gun had to come down again: he knew that to fire with such a crowd pressing forward could only hit the wrong man.

Wang Yaqiao at that moment was waiting in the station's passenger hall. He had not been able to go on waiting in the house in front of the station: the strain of it and the pressure of the failure on Lushan had cost him his composure. But once in the hall he found that the best moment for the killing on the platform was being lost, with crowds of passengers pushing in a dark mass toward the exit. Wang Yaqiao could hold back no longer and went up onto the platform in a few strides. And just as he was searching about for his target, ready to do it himself, he saw at a distance the Nanjing officials and the dignitaries who had come to meet them walking toward him. Before he could pick out T.V. Soong's shape among the group, there came a bang from somewhere in the milling crowd. Wang Yaqiao looked toward the sound and, in a crowd running like a tide, saw at a glance the man with his pistol raised and firing into the air: Hua Kezhi.

Sun Fengming, Liu Gang and the others mixed in with the crowd meeting the train were startled out of their wits by the shot. Seeing that the man who had fired was Hua Kezhi, they stood on no more ceremony either. Liu Gang got in first, rushing forward and firing a shot at Tang Yulu, who was walking ahead of the officials. Tang Yulu took the bullet where he stood, cried out and pitched forward. In the crush Sun Fengming did not see clearly whom it was Liu Gang had hit, and fired two more shots into the white-suited man lying in his blood. Seeing the shots had brought a mass of station patrolmen down on them, and knowing better than to fight it out, he tore open the sack of talc in his hand and threw it up into the air; a ball of snow-white fog spread instantly through the crowd. With no time for anything else, he took hold of Liu Gang and ran. The other assassins in the station, seeing Sun Fengming and Liu Gang pull out, fired as they went and pressed toward the exit through a crowd in complete disorder.

Hua Kezhi and Zheng Baozhen, seeing a man down in his blood on the platform, took it that T.V. Soong had been hit and dared not linger either; they turned and ran for the exit. By then Wang Yaqiao was already off the platform, and when Liu Gang said to him, "We did it!" he asked nothing further, but turned and made for the road out to the street. The third line Xie Wenda had laid, seeing Liu Gang and Sun Fengming come out of the station in the confusion, likewise took it that the work was done, and withdrew in haste.

As for Chang Yuqing's Anqing Gang gunmen, they too had gone through onto the station platform before the train from Nanjing arrived, on the pretext of meeting a passenger. Their target, of course, was the Japanese consul Shigemitsu Mamoru. The first action group, under Ma Daxiang, watched the door of the first-class compartment from the platform — and never expected that the first man to show himself would be Tang Yulu. Since Ma Daxiang and his assassins had seen the Japanese consul only once, in a photograph, the instant Tang Yulu put his head out they took him for Shigemitsu Mamoru. The cause of it was that Chang Yuqing had insisted too heavily beforehand that the Japanese envoy liked to wear a white Western suit — and Shigemitsu Mamoru, coming down behind Tang Yulu and T.V. Soong, had of all days changed his clothes on that one. So although Shigemitsu Mamoru passed directly in front of Ma Daxiang and the other assassins, not one of them knew him.

Ma Daxiang first fixed his target on the two men in white suits. But as they followed them up, meaning to fire when they reached the exit, they heard Hua Kezhi's shot go off first. Ma Daxiang could not make out Hua Kezhi's face at that distance and took it that one of his own men had found the target and was signalling to fire. So he sprang forward and was on the point of shooting at T.V. Soong when a shot from Liu Gang knocked the secretary Tang Yulu, walking in front, to the ground.

Seeing a man in white down in his blood and dense firing breaking out all around, Ma Daxiang and his men took the fallen man for their own target, Shigemitsu Mamoru. So he too seized the chance and put three more shots into the fallen Tang Yulu; then he threw his packet of talc up into the air, and another column of snow-white fog went up and spread. In his haste he did not forget Chang Yuqing's charge: he dropped the two grenades marked with the characters *hatchet* beside the body of Tang Yulu, which by then had been punched through with a number of bullet holes, and made his escape in the confusion.
