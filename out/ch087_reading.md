### The Wheelbarrows

The earliest of all the various vehicles that ply the streets of Shanghai must be reckoned the wheelbarrow. It first appeared in the early years of the Tongzhi reign, toward the close of the Qing (some would call it the "dog's-head cart"); it runs on a single wheel, the carter pushing behind. At the outset it carried only goods, though it could also seat a passenger; afterward came the bicycle, the rickshaw, the horse-carriage and the rest, and by the end of the Guangxu and the beginning of the Xuantong reigns the motorcar and the tram appeared one after another. In recent years the single-wheeled barrow, the first of them all to appear, has long since fallen behind the times; the number of these carts grows fewer and fewer, and such as remain carry only goods and freight and hardly ever a passenger. Only about the factories of Zhabei, at the hours of going to work and coming off, are a few wheelbarrows still to be seen bowling along the road with their two sides packed full of women workers—and beyond that they are now a rare sight indeed.
