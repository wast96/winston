### Petitions on the Pavement

On the cement pavements to either side of the road there are often men calling themselves scholars fallen on hard times, who chalk up in white a long plea for pity to beg alms of the passers-by; and the writing is in neat regular script, well composed, while some write wholly in English in hopes of the pity of foreign-versed friends and of foreigners. Some say: "Since the man has such a good hand, he could tell fortunes by character-splitting, or write letters for people, and get by well enough—why make such a spectacle of himself and beg the world's pity?" Others say: "This is one of their ways of doing business; why would they give up the trade?" There are besides those who write out beforehand on a sheet of white paper the whole tale of their misfortune and of how they are driven against their will to beg, hoping some passing gentleman will take pity and give; and of this petition-style begging there are both men and women, and there are even those who kneel weeping on the ground with a troop of small children. Among them the truly stranded, cast up far from home, are not wanting—only they are the few.
