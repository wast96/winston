### Getting Around and Getting Away

My friend the lawyer Qu Shaoyi says that a man who would gain a footing in Shanghai must be on fair terms with people of all three ranks, high, middle and low, before he can get around and get away in whatever he sets his hand to. It is a true word, spoken from experience. By people of the three ranks he means such men as the great officials and grandees, the society notables, the lawyers, the doctors, the police and the plainclothes detectives, and the hoodlums and their sort; of each kind a man must know a few, so that if some day he suddenly runs into trouble he shall not come to unexpected grief. For in scratching a living on the Shanghai sea, however careful and law-abiding a man may be, there is always the chance of the old saying, "You may sit at home behind shut doors, and still calamity comes at you out of the sky"; and it is only when that hour arrives that one learns how precious a thing it is to be able to get around and get away.
