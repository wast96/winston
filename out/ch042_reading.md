### The Mending Women

The mending trade is plied for the most part by women of Subei stock. On the arm they carry a bamboo basket and a little stool, the basket holding scissors, a bamboo rule, balls of thread, scraps of cloth and the like, and up and down the streets they go touting for custom. Their chief business is darning the soles of socks, mending dropped stitches, and patching the holes worn in people's clothes.

The shop-hands, the factory workmen, the apprentices in the trading houses — their wives and elders being away from Shanghai — must all get their socks darned and their clothes patched by the mending women, and so the mending women do a brisk trade. As for the meaning of the two words feng qiong, "mending-poverty": it is that they do the work in the poor man's stead, and hence the name. Of the women who follow this line, were one to count them up, the number is truly not small.

There is besides, they say, a sort of young and flighty mending woman who powders her face and paints her brows and comes out tricked up like a fright to lure the hot-blooded fellows in short jackets, granting them their sweet dream to earn a little on the side and gratify her own appetite. Where such shameful doings are real, mending is in truth her main trade, and "selling flesh" the sideline.
