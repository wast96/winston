## Eighteen

For two days now the Fourth Young Lady, Huifang, had refused to come out of her room. Once the old Master's funeral was over and the Fourth Young Lady still could not gain her end of "going back to the country," she fell back on this last "protest" of hers; no one could argue her out of it, and in the end they had to let her be.

The Taishang Ganying Pian the old Master had left behind was once more the Fourth Young Lady's constant "magic treasure." Among the twenty-eight pieces of luggage that had come up with him two months before there had been a Xuande censer and a few bundles of Tibetan incense — the "ritual implements" the old Master had needed when he chanted the Taishang Ganying Pian devoutly; and now the Fourth Young Lady had hunted these out too. Morning, afternoon, and evening, three sessions a day, she burned this incense. Only the round hassock the old Master used to sit on could not be found, hunt as she might. There was no help for it: the Fourth Young Lady made do by folding her legs under her on the sofa.

After turning it over and over in her mind, the Fourth Young Lady had resolved to take up this bequest of her father's. It was not that she meant to "lay up merit," but that she hoped by it to cleanse her heart and quiet her desires, and so ease a little of the contradiction and pain in her spirit. The first day it had seemed to work wonders. The blue smoke of the Tibetan incense curled and wound in the air; the Fourth Young Lady murmured the Taishang Ganying Pian under her breath, and in her heart it was as though she were no longer in Shanghai but back in the study of the old house in the country. Her late father's saintly countenance came back before her eyes, and all at once she was so moved she nearly shed tears. She was steeped in sweet remembrance — the plain, tranquil life of those days waiting on the old Master in the country, down to the smallest detail, rose up again so clearly that she felt a comfort she had never known. A faint smile played at her lips; she forgot to chant the sacred phrases of the Taishang Ganying Pian. The pure fragrance of the Tibetan incense little by little intoxicated her soul too, and she leaned softly against the back of the sofa, neither asleep nor awake, thinking of nothing, empty of everything. So it went a long, long while, until that stick of incense burned out and she seemed to come to herself, let out a breath, and faintly smiled.

In just such reveries and dream-visions the Fourth Young Lady passed the first day of her quiet devotions, and never even noticed she was hungry.

But by the afternoon of the second day the Taishang Ganying Pian and the Tibetan incense had lost some of yesterday's mysterious power. "Remembrance" kept its appointment and came again, all the same, yet the Fourth Young Lady's relish for it had greatly cooled; it was like an old friend not seen for years — yesterday, at the first reunion, there was no end to all the pent-up feelings of parting, but today there seemed nothing left to say. Eyes on her nose, nose on her heart, she chanted the text of the Taishang Ganying Pian in dead earnest, over and over; but of a sudden the honk-honk of a motor-car burst upon her ears, and men's leather shoes went clacking past outside her door, and from the great drawing-room below the piano floated up sweet and clear, and the mingled, merry, boisterous laughter of men and women — every sound of it bored into her ears and burrowed straight into her heart, wriggling there and making mischief. One stick of the incense burned out, and she felt as if the sofa were stuck full of thorns, and the very air of the room stifling, unbearable; several times she wanted to run out and take a look. What it was she wanted to look at, she herself could not say. In the end she managed to settle down again, and sat in a daze holding that costly copy of the Taishang Ganying Pian in its reverent regular hand, sighing under her breath a good ten times over, her eyes a little moist.

That night it was a long time before she could sleep. And she dreamed a great deal. All those wild dreams that used to leave her sighing, bitterly smiling, even weeping when she woke — now they all came back together, throwing her this way and that, drunk and bewildered; and in that short summer night she started awake in fright three or four times.

When she rose the next morning her face was ashen, her fingertips were cold, and her heart would not stop swaying within her. The phrases of the Taishang Ganying Pian seemed now to be all irony aimed at her, and more than once she closed the book and heaved a long sigh.

The afternoon was very hot, and in her room the Fourth Young Lady was like an earthworm on a fire-baked brick, without a moment's peace. As always she took up the Taishang Ganying Pian and lit the Tibetan incense; but her ears were full of every sound outside her door, in the garden, and on the road farther off; and for each sound her heart made some guess, some interpretation. Whenever footsteps passed outside her door she pricked up her ears to listen, her heart beating unnaturally; with tears brimming in her eyes she wished with all her heart that those footsteps would halt at her door, wished with all her heart that there would come two soft knocks, tap-tap, and that she would go and open it, and that the one knocking would turn out to be her brother, or her sister-in-law — or Lin Peishan, that would do too — and that they had come to coax her out to ease her mind!

But every time she was disappointed. Every time the footsteps went straight past, past and away, and never came back. She had been forgotten, like an old-fashioned dress! And so, facing the curling blue smoke of the Tibetan incense, holding that costly reverently-copied Taishang Ganying Pian, she began to hate her brother, to hate her sister-in-law, even to hate that little bird of a Lin Peishan. It seemed to her that everyone else had happiness, had the freedom to be happy, and that she alone was the forgotten one, the one robbed of everything! It was not, she felt, that she herself wished to be shut up in her room in "quiet devotion" — others had forced it on her; they had put their heads together and by this cunning method stripped her of her rights as a living being!

She remembered a wretched tale she had heard back in her home country: of a young lady of an old "gentry house" like her own who, for "misconduct," had been locked away and forbidden to see a soul! And that young lady too had been said to be in "quiet devotion" of her own will! And that young lady had in the end hanged herself! "Is that not exactly, exactly my own case?" — and thinking it, the Fourth Young Lady felt her hair stand on end. Suddenly last night's dream came back. It was an old dream she had dreamed over and over several times, and just now the Fourth Young Lady took it for no dream at all but the plain truth; she seemed to feel that on that dusk three weeks before, the dusk before the great thunderstorm, after she and Fan Bowen had chatted a while in the hexagonal pavilion on the rockery across the fish-pond in the garden, she had truly, under cover of the dark, lost her precious maiden's red; she truly felt that among the many strange dreams, alike and yet not alike, that tormented her again and again, one was surely no dream but the truth; and that true dream was set in that hexagonal pavilion, on the dusk of the great thunderstorm, when the first violent downpour came pelting down and she lay languid on the rattan lounging-chair in the pavilion, and Fan Bowen sat facing her, and with her eyes shut she heard him come to her side, and all at once her whole body went limp, as though she were drunk.

"Ah — !" The Fourth Young Lady cried out, and the Taishang Ganying Pian dropped from her hand. She looked wildly about her, and by instinct picked the Taishang Ganying Pian up again; a bitter smile floated over her face, and two glittering tears hung on her lashes. She firmly believed that that preposterous dream was a preposterous truth; and she was quite certain that it was for this preposterous thing that they had "immured" her by their cunning method, while giving out that it was "of her own will"! And she felt too that there was only the one end left to her, the usual move: to make away with herself! To swallow gold, or to hang!

And then, for no reason, it came to her that even if she would not take that road of no return, her tyrant of a brother would one day come storming in to force her to it. Her heart pounded wildly, her fingertips were ice-cold, and yet her face burned. Clenching her teeth she asked herself over and over: "Why is my lot so bitter? Why, when it comes to me, is nothing allowed? Why, in other families, may men and women do as they please? Why do they play deaf and dumb over Ah-shan? Why must I bow my head and let them grind me down, with nothing I can do about it? Is there truly no second way for me?" All at once she stood up, her whole body a flame of revolt. But at once she sank down again, dispirited. She was alone; there was not a soul she could take counsel with, not a soul to help her!

Suddenly hurried footsteps halted at her door. There came a hard knock. For no reason the Fourth Young Lady made up her mind that this was her brother come to force her. She sighed in despair, flung herself on the bed, buried her face in the pillow, and the blood ran cold through her whole body.

"Fourth Sister! Are you asleep?"

A woman's shrill voice pierced the Fourth Young Lady's ears, unexpectedly clear. Her whole body jumped, she twisted her face round, and there standing at the bedside was none other than that vigorous, high-spirited cousin of hers, Zhang Susu! It was truly like another dream! The Fourth Young Lady rubbed her eyes and looked again, then all at once sprang up, seized Zhang Susu's hand in hers, and could not hold back the tears that poured down. At such a moment, had the newcomer been a cat or a dog, the Fourth Young Lady would have treated it as one of her own!

Zhang Susu, for her part, was so astonished she could only laugh. She sat down on the edge of the bed, shook the Fourth Young Lady by the shoulder, and asked impatiently:

"Eh? What's all this! Crying the moment we meet? Fourth Sister! Are you really a bit touched in the head? Come, come — why won't you speak!"

"It's nothing! Ah, it's nothing."

The Fourth Young Lady forced back the string of tears and answered with a shake of her head. She felt easier in her heart now; she understood that this indeed was no dream but the truth — a real Zhang Susu, a real self.

"Fourth Sister! I really don't understand you! They've all gone out, and you're the only one left in the whole house! Why don't you go out and ease your mind?"

"I can't — "

The Fourth Young Lady did not finish, but stopped short, sighed again, and squeezed Zhang Susu's hand tight, as if that squeeze spoke for her.

Zhang Susu knitted her brows and stared fixedly at the Fourth Young Lady's face, saying nothing. However you looked at her, there was nothing in the Fourth Young Lady's whole bearing that suggested a disorder of the nerves! But then why — shut up in her room, not stirring an inch, no nun yet nunlike, no Daoist yet Daoist-like? The thought vexed Zhang Susu a little. All at once she recalled the day old Mr. Wu had died, and the wager she had made with Fan Bowen and Wu Zhisheng and the others; and with a touch of feeling in her voice she said:

"Fourth Sister! A while back we — Zhisheng, Bowen, Peishan, and the Du family's Number Six — laid a wager over you! We bet on whether living a long time in Shanghai would change you into someone different. But this change in you now — none of us ever foresaw it!"

"You expected then that I would change? Ah, Sister Su! How did you expect me to change?"

"That I don't remember very clearly. In short, we thought you'd turn out different. But now you've changed and yet not changed, and that's the strangest thing of all!"

"But I know for myself that I'm no longer the girl who lived in the country! — "

"Pah! Fourth Sister, but you are! For a while there you seemed not to be, and now you've slipped back into the old rut!"

Zhang Susu cried out impatiently, more than ever convinced in her heart that the Fourth Young Lady was nothing of the kind, and that Sunfu and the rest had made far too much of it.

"Ai! Slipped back into the old rut, have I? But in the old days, when I was with Father in the country, I was not what I am now. Sister Su! The vexation in my heart just now — I'm afraid no one can understand it! And no one is willing to try to understand me!"

The Fourth Young Lady spoke very steadily, and into her lustrous dark eyes there came all at once a note of strength. This was the first time Zhang Susu had seen it, and she thought it very strange. But in a flash the Fourth Young Lady's gaze turned again to a lost, bewildered look; staring into the air, she murmured to herself:

"Oh — you even laid a wager over me! And Fan Bowen was in it. He — what did he say? Ai! Sister Su, let me ask you — but no, there's no point in asking. Never mind, let's talk of something else!"

Zhang Susu suddenly gave a peal of laughter. All at once she sprang up, threw her arm round the Fourth Young Lady's neck, and, as if biting at her ear, cried out loud:

"Why not ask! Why not talk of it! Fourth Sister! I know — I've long known you've had your eye on Bowen! But why be so timid, so shy? Sunfu interferes with you, does he? That too I've long known! In your affairs he has no right to interfere — you have your own freedom!"

In an instant the Fourth Young Lady's face flushed red. How bold and welcome those words were! Yet even had she held them in her own heart, she could never have got them past her lips. From the bottom of her heart she was grateful to Zhang Susu; she caught her hand and gripped it tight, and nearly wept again. But Zhang Susu abruptly flung the hand away, straightened her chest, fixed the Fourth Young Lady with a keen look, and said in a grave tone:

"So now you shut yourself up in your room and won't come out, hugging some Taishang Ganying Pian — and you call that resisting Sunfu's tyranny? Pah! Your method is pointless! Your spirit of resistance is fine enough, but your method won't do at all! What's more, I warn you again: this Bowen is a spineless jellyfish who can't stand up straight! He was in love with Peishan to begin with, the two of them together all day long; then Sunfu opposed it, and Bowen backed off and ran! Fourth Sister! If you want to resist Sunfu's tyranny and win your freedom, you can't pin your hopes on a spineless jellyfish who won't stand straight either!"

So saying, Zhang Susu laughed again, brought both hands down, and gave the Fourth Young Lady a hard slap on the shoulder. Caught off guard, the Fourth Young Lady lurched and all but toppled into the bed, and she too could not help laughing. But once the laugh had passed, her whole face at once turned grave again; fixing her eyes on Zhang Susu, she very much wanted to ask more about Fan Bowen's "spinelessness," and at the same time felt that to ask more would only invite Zhang Susu's derision; she looked on Susu now as a knight-errant, and did not wish to seem too spineless herself before this champion. In the end she struggled to lay bare her most secret thought:

"Ai! Sister Su! You've looked into my very heart! I've grown so used to constraint that when I have something in my heart, I can never get it out; and I have no one to tell it to, no one to take counsel with! I am a blind woman; I don't know which road is the good one to take. I feel it so stifling here, so bitter, that I only want to go back to the country; they won't let me go, so I could only think of shutting my door and paying no heed to any of them! But these two days I've been half stifled with the misery of it! I know well enough this is no way out! Sister Su, teach me — is there no other way?"

"Ha-ha-ha … "

Zhang Susu laughed long, gave a twist of her waist and sat down beside the Fourth Young Lady, took her face in both hands, and studied it closely. That face now was flushed red and burning hot, but the lips were pale and trembling faintly. Zhang Susu looked a while, then said gravely:

"That too rests with you. You must be bold and shrewd, and speak your mind plainly to Sunfu! Besides, you ought to go and study. Ask Sunfu to let you enter a school this coming autumn and study!"

The Fourth Young Lady shook her head hard and made no sound. Zhang Susu opened her eyes wide in surprise, and knitted her brows.

"You don't want to go and study?"

"It's not that! I'm afraid there's no school I could get into! Of the old Chinese books I've read cupboards full, but of the other sciences I understand nothing at all!"

"Never mind that! You can take coaching. But Fourth Sister, the more you hide in your room the more short of breath you'll grow! Come out with me and take a walk!"

So saying, Zhang Susu pulled the Fourth Young Lady to her feet, and pressed her to wash her face and set off at once. As she washed, the Fourth Young Lady could not help laughing to herself, and then, on the sly, let fall two tears. They were tears of happiness, and tears of resolve! Though she did not yet know what was really to be done, the Fourth Young Lady had made up her mind to do in everything as Zhang Susu directed!

Hailing a Yunfei taxi, Zhang Susu carried the Fourth Young Lady off to breathe some fresh air. It was a little past three, and the sun's power was at its very height. In the car the Fourth Young Lady shut her eyes and felt a touch giddy. And her heart began once more to churn and fret. Her future was, after all, still a "riddle"; she longed for that "riddle" to be answered soon, and yet she was afraid. The car darted out of the city districts and now ran along a not-very-even half-dirt road, throwing up an acrid, sun-baked yellow dust. On either side lay green oily fields, and here and there a desolate grave like an earthen dumpling. All at once the car gave a jolt, and the Fourth Young Lady, as if startled, opened her eyes; seeing herself out in the country, she thought it was another dream; she steadied herself, gave Zhang Susu beside her a push, and asked softly:

"Look here! We've not taken the wrong road, have we?"

Zhang Susu smiled and did not answer. This warm-hearted girl was herself lost in her own fancies. She felt today an unlooked-for success in having carried the Fourth Young Lady off; and she was busy too imagining for the Fourth Young Lady that unknowable future — a boundless, sky-wide future, full of strong and vivid colours.

From Zhang Susu's silence the Fourth Young Lady understood that they had not gone wrong, and that their destination was the countryside. At that she felt very glad. She gazed intently at the fields flying past, and for the moment her soul went back again to her home country. There was not much here to tell it apart from her own country — only that there were more motor-cars going mad in the yellow dust. But all at once the Fourth Young Lady gave a cry and nudged Zhang Susu again. Their car was already going very slowly, and up ahead there were many motor-cars, of every colour, drawn up in the shade of the willows. And there were women too with red lips and fine-drawn brows and bare white arms, leaning on the shoulders of men as they stepped down from the cars. This was still Shanghai after all!

Following Zhang Susu out of the car, and then in through the gate of a queerly-fashioned pleasure-garden, the Fourth Young Lady grew more and more amazed at every step, until it burdened her past bearing. Here was only ordinary countryside scenery, some trees with cicadas shrilling in them, and yet here too it was still "Shanghai"; the dress and the doings of the men and women were still the very things the Fourth Young Lady had always dreaded to see and at the same time longed for. And here the things that made the Fourth Young Lady blush and set her heart pounding were more numerous still: over there on the grass in the shade of the trees a man and a woman laughed wantonly, a white leg cocked up, the pointed toe of a high-heeled shoe aimed straight at the blue sky; and there, again, two figures seen from behind, pressed so close, so close together! The Fourth Young Lady shut her eyes a moment, her heart pounding so that she almost wanted to weep aloud.

Under a very large canvas parasol the Fourth Young Lady met with people she knew. There were three of them. The Fourth Young Lady would gladly have turned her face away and walked past, but Zhang Susu held her back.

"Aiyo, has the walled-in monk come out of her retreat, then? This is worth writing up in great strokes!"

A man under the big parasol sprang up as he said it, and all but overturned the little table crowded with soda bottles and beer bottles and plates of refreshments. The Fourth Young Lady blushed; and because this man was none other than Fan Bowen, that shameless "dream" came bursting back on her, so that after the first flush she suddenly turned instead an ashen, dead pallor. Her two feet seemed nailed to the ground; she wanted to go, and yet could not stir. With a desperate effort she turned her face away and greeted Wu Zhisheng.

"Well then, Bowen, make a poem to mark the occasion! The title shall be — "

"No good! Other poets grow polished once they're poor, but this poet Fan of ours grows bare once he's poor! How could he still make a poem!"

Before Li Yuting could bring out the title, Wu Zhisheng had already made game of Fan Bowen. Fan Bowen, however, did not mind, and said with a shake of his head:

"There's no help for it! Even the Muse of poetry follows the gold — there's truly no help for it!"

Everyone laughed, the Fourth Young Lady among them; only Zhang Susu, with a smile that was no smile, bared her teeth a little, then knitted her brows and asked:

"What have you come here in a gang like this to do?"

"But you and Fourth Sister, coming here — isn't that a gang come to do something too?"

Wu Zhisheng shot back; keeping company these days so often with Fan Bowen, he had picked up some of his smart talk.

"I? I've come for a change of air. And I've brought Fourth Sister along to let her see what games Shanghai's modern young men and women come out to the country to play!"

"Oh — well then, we've come to look on too. These few days Professor Li Yuting hasn't been able to get a mouthful of food down, always saying great upheaval is at hand and one day we'll die with no ground to be buried in; so today we've brought him along, to let him see how the exiled Russian nobles and the bourgeoisie manage to go on living all the same, day after day."

"Come, come! Old Zhi, a most serious matter, and you've turned it into a joke again!"

Li Yuting hastened to protest, scratching mechanically at his scalp. Listening and looking on, Zhang Susu found it both laughable and provoking. She gave the Fourth Young Lady a tug, meaning to go. Suddenly Fan Bowen sprang up and cried out very gravely:

"Did you all hear that clearly? Professor Li takes everything in earnest, and prepares for everything in advance. His idea is quite right! Look at that White Russian coming over there — likely some count or marquis too, who half a lifetime only ever watched others hold and open the wine bottles, and now it's his own turn to wait on other people; yet he's learned it quickly enough, and can carry six soda bottles now in a single hand!"

"The truth is that when our own turn comes we shan't have even this little bit of good fortune they have!"

Li Yuting said it all at once as if with real grief, which set Wu Zhisheng and the rest laughing again.

"Too tiresome for words! You three precious specimens!"

Zhang Susu sneered, took the Fourth Young Lady by the hand, and turned to go. They found a little table of their own in the shade of a tree near the riverbank and drank soda-water. It was very quiet here, and they sat facing the little river; just now the vicious sun hung overhead, the river-water shone with golden light, and not a pleasure-boat was on it. The Fourth Young Lady too was no longer so restless as before. She could not quite make it out — to drink soda-water and to flirt and jest, why go to the trouble of coming all the way out to the country for it? There was not the least uncommon scenery here! But she had to own that this bit of country, set off by all those gaudy men and women, seemed to have a special savour of its own.

Zhang Susu, though, seemed deeply moved by something, and sat silent and lost in thought. After a while she said softly to herself:

"All of them fallen! — and yet it's nothing to wonder at!"

Then all at once she laughed wildly, took a sip of soda-water, stretched herself lazily, and, patting the Fourth Young Lady on the shoulder, asked:

"If Sunfu absolutely won't let you go and study, what then?"

"Then you must teach me!"

"Then I'll teach you to take him to law!"

"Oh — "

The Fourth Young Lady cried out in alarm, her face reddening, and looked at Zhang Susu with hesitant eyes, as if to say, "You — you're not joking, surely!" Zhang Susu's small eyes rolled round, and she lifted her face in a smile. She saw that the one she had roused was wavering a little. Yet the Fourth Young Lady went on:

"Sister Su! That's too much worrying on your part. Things won't come to such a deadlock! Besides, I can ask Second Sister to put in a word for me."

"Very well — mine was only a last-resort way of putting it."

"But Sister Su, I don't want to go on living at home! Not one day more!"

"Oh! — "

Now it was Zhang Susu who cried out in surprise. She could not fathom what was in the Fourth Young Lady's heart. The Fourth Young Lady blushed again, looked about her in bewilderment, and then, as if for rescue, looked to Zhang Susu. At last, seeming unable to bear it any longer, the Fourth Young Lady bowed her head and said softly:

"You don't know how lonely I am at home!"

"Why! Lonely?"

"They all have someone. I'm alone! And I always feel my soul is not at rest. If I go on living there, I shall go mad!"

Zhang Susu began to laugh. She had guessed at last, in part, what it was that tormented the Fourth Young Lady. "Most likely it's mainly the vexation of sex!" — so Zhang Susu thought, and glancing at the Fourth Young Lady she could not help laughing again; and because she had a moment ago rated the Fourth Young Lady's spirit of resistance too high, she now felt a little let down. Yet the Fourth Young Lady's piteous look moved Zhang Susu to sympathy too; she thought a while, unable to decide how to dispose of this inexperienced woman. But before Zhang Susu had settled on a plan, the Fourth Young Lady herself said again, with resolve:

"I don't want to go on living at home! Not one day more! Sister Su, I want to live with you, and take you for my teacher!"

This was a cry full of the fervent hope of one seeking help, and the warm-hearted Zhang Susu could by no means refuse it. Though she knew well enough that she too had "someone," so that the Fourth Young Lady would probably still feel lonely and wretched, she had not the heart to say so and pour cold water on the Fourth Young Lady's high spirits.

The sun had gone into hiding. The wind that blew across from the far side of the little river now carried a distinct chill. The Fourth Young Lady, feeling the great question already settled, sat brooding on the freedom and happiness to come. She knew nothing of the particulars of Zhang Susu's life; she knew only that Susu had been studying at some university and was now, over the summer vacation, lodging in the YWCA hostel; yet she leaned on this cousin of hers as on her own mother.

Suddenly there came drifting over the water a lilting song. The Fourth Young Lady made out that it was a sound of her home country, and very familiar. She smiled at Zhang Susu without thinking. But the song came again, nearer and nearer little by little, and the Fourth Young Lady made out four lines:

Heaven and earth are the furnace, and the Maker the smith;

the yin and the yang are the charcoal, and all things the bronze!

The Fourth Young Lady remembered that these were lines from the Rhapsody on the Owl, and made out that the voice was Du Xintuo's. She could not help laughing aloud. She thought this Du Xintuo very droll, and at once her mind ran to Lin Peishan too. By now Zhang Susu had also caught the words, and she smiled; then all at once she sprang up and stole quietly to the river's edge, where she crouched under a tree. The Fourth Young Lady stifled her laughter and followed Zhang Susu's example.

A little boat came drifting slowly along, keeping to the bank on the Fourth Young Lady's side of the river. Du Xintuo was plying the oar; beside his thigh the brim of a brown straw hat stuck up, its pale-yellow ribbon fluttering in the wind. The Fourth Young Lady knew it for Lin Peishan's straw hat! The little boat came nearer still, no more than ten feet off. Zhang Susu picked up a lump of mud and flung it straight at the boat.

"Aiyo!"

It was Lin Peishan's voice. The brown straw hat stirred. The little boat came to a stop at once. Zhang Susu jumped up and, laughing loudly, called out:

"You two are too happy, too selfish with it — no wonder somebody has to complain of being lonely!"

Du Xintuo and Lin Peishan turned their faces together, saw Zhang Susu, but did not see the Fourth Young Lady. Amid the clear ripple of laughter the oar sounded again, and the boat drew in to the bank. Crouching behind the tree, the Fourth Young Lady heard Lin Peishan say with a coquettish pout:

"Su! Lady revolutionary! Haven't you been busy of late with great affairs? Ask you to come and play along, and one's sure to be scolded a few times for corruption and decadence!"

"But Miss Zhang, that hand-grenade of yours just now was a fine throw indeed! You've got the makings of it!"

"Guess, the two of you — who else is here? Guess wrong, and I'll take Ah-shan for my prisoner!"

"Oh-ho-ho! — your companion! Who knows but it's any Tom, Dick, or Harry!"

It was Lin Peishan's voice again. The Fourth Young Lady felt ashamed to show her face. At the same time she heard the little boat brushing rustling against the wild grass along the bank. All at once Zhang Susu came running up with a peal of laughter, seized the Fourth Young Lady, and pushed her out. And so the Fourth Young Lady stood revealed before Lin Peishan and the rest. Blushing, she greeted them:

"Shan! You come here often, I suppose? It doesn't seem so very amusing!"

"Aiyo! Sister Hui, this I never, never looked for! — I take my hat off to you, Su! The lady revolutionary's methods are formidable indeed; so many people coaxed her and couldn't budge her, and you with one tug have dragged her out here!"

And then the laughing chatter of the three young women blended into a merry hubbub. Only Du Xintuo stuck his oar into the mud and said nothing, smiling. In his eyes all changes were only what was to be expected, and none of them counted for anything: the Fourth Young Lady, thwarted in what she wanted, had of course fled to the Taishang Ganying Pian, and now was just as naturally casting the Taishang Ganying Pian aside and coming to this mysterious Liwalida.

Of a sudden thunder rumbled in the sky. Black clouds came flying up from every quarter like swift horses, gathering thicker and thicker above the little river.

"It's going to rain! Fourth Sister, let's be getting back."

Zhang Susu said it with her face turned up to the sky, and with one hand took the Fourth Young Lady by the arm.

"What's there to fear! There'll be no heavy rain. Su, come into the boat, the two of you, and play a while."

"We won't! — And if you still find it not lively enough, Fan Bowen and the rest are just over there; shall I run and fetch them for you?"

Zhang Susu suddenly turned her barbs on Lin Peishan, laughed long, and went off with the Fourth Young Lady.

Here Du Xintuo watched the retreating figures of Zhang Susu and the other, still smiling as if nothing in the world concerned him. He gave a light tap of the oar on a tree-root at the water's edge, and the little boat drifted slowly out again to midstream. The wind had freshened, setting Lin Peishan's dress flapping. Lin Peishan bowed her head, watched the shadows of the trees in the water, and toyed with the hem of her dress with one hand. After a while she raised her head and fixed her eyes on Du Xintuo's face; her gaze seemed to say: "What are we to do, going on like this!" Du Xintuo only smiled.

Their little boat now passed through the hanging withes of a row of willows, the gunwale scraping against the leaves of some reed or other with a rustling sound. Lin Peishan sighed faintly, shifted herself forward a little, and laid her head on Du Xintuo's knee. The oar leapt up out of the water and lay across the gunwale, and the boat drifted slowly on of itself. Lin Peishan cocked up a leg and gave a coy little laugh.

"But you really must think of some plan! … If only you can find a way to make Sunfu not oppose us — that would do it!"

Lin Peishan said it softly, by fits and starts, her liquid eyes fixed on Du Xintuo's face.

"Come, come, why do you never speak? Do you hear me? What I say is, only let Sunfu not oppose it! Think of some plan or other — "

"This Sunfu is a man there's no reasoning with!"

"Then how are we to make an end of it?"

"Live one day, and count that day done!"

"Aiyo! Live one day and count it done! And muddle on until what day, pray?"

"Muddle on until there's no more muddling to be done — until you've got yourself a proper husband!"

"Pah! What talk is that!"

"But, Shan! Think it over carefully and you'll see I'm not far wrong. To get them to consent would be harder than climbing to heaven; unless we run away together, one day they're bound to marry you off to someone else — isn't that so? And yet you, you feel that to run off would be to eat bitterness, and I, for my part, am none too fond of stirring about either."

"Come, come, you do talk absurdly! As if the two of us had never had our doings together!"

"True, we've had our doings! But Shan! What of it! You are still yourself, you've lost nothing! Your lips are still as red, your arms still as soft and smooth, your eyes still as full of speech! You have your full share of youth and beauty still, enough to make your future proper husband happy, and to make yourself happy too — is it not so?"

Listening, Lin Peishan could not help laughing. Was there not, after all, good sense in what Du Xintuo said? At Lin Peishan's age, that little soul of hers had awakened to nothing of love in any true sense of the word; it was all no more than childish play! A long willow-withe brushed against Lin Peishan's face; she reached up, snapped off the supple twig, put it in her mouth and bit it, then spat it out, and asked with another peal of laughter:

"And who, then, is to be my proper husband?"

"That there's no knowing yet. Bowen, perhaps — he'd do well enough!"

"But they mean to give me to your family's Number Six!"

"Now that's not much to my taste! This Number Six is the very best under heaven of his kind too — he won't do! But no matter — life is only a game!"

Laughing, Lin Peishan scooped up a palmful of water and flung it in Du Xintuo's face, shot him a coy, reproachful glance, but said nothing. The boat had passed clean through the thick hanging willows, and ahead the river narrowed. Du Xintuo laughed long, took up the oar and drove it hard into the water; the water splashed and churned, and the boat spun round and round in circles.

About five o'clock it began to rain. It was a slanting rain. The menservants and maidservants of the Wu mansion bustled about closing all the east-facing windows. The pair of windows in the Fourth Young Lady's bedroom took the rain too, but there was no one to close them. The rain came down harder and harder, the east wind was strong, and the raindrops drove hissing straight in through the window; on the table before the window that costly copy of the Taishang Ganying Pian was soaked through, and the vermilion ruling on its double-thick paper had begun to run and blur. The Xuande censer was a full bowl of water, and the water brimmed over and ran across the whole table, steeping that costly bundle of Tibetan incense; and the incense dissolved too, turning to a thin, waxy-yellow paste that crept slowly to the side of the Taishang Ganying Pian.

The rain drove the pleasure-seekers home too. The Wu young mistress was the first. Because the rain had brought a coolness, the young mistress changed her clothes the moment she reached home. Then Lin Peishan came back alone. Her gauze dress was a good four-tenths soaked, but she paid no heed to that, and ran upstairs and burst straight into the Fourth Young Lady's bedroom.

When she saw plainly that the slanting rain was the only master of this bedroom, Lin Peishan stopped dumbfounded. She put out her tongue, turned and ran, and in two or three quick strides had skipped into her sister's room, where all at once she laughed till her sides ached and she could not get a word out.

The Wu young mistress, well used to her younger sister's foolish airs, thought nothing of it, and went on holding her cup of tea, lost in a reverie.

The room felt a little dim. The sudden rain beat drumming against the glass, and out in the garden sounded the honk of Wu Sunfu's motor-car. Lin Peishan, having laughed herself out, sidled up to the Wu young mistress and asked in a whisper:

"Sister, do you know there's news in our house? Do you know where Sister Huifang, our Fourth Sister, has got to?"

The Wu young mistress seemed to start, but at once pursed her lips in a smile, taking it that Peishan was up to her tricks and telling fibs again.

"I saw her just now. I saw her at the Liwalida! — "

The Wu young mistress only laughed aloud, certain that this was Peishan telling fibs again for the fun of it. She threw her sister a glance and set down her teacup.

"I'm not fooling you! It's true! But when the rain came and everyone got back, she didn't come back! Her room is one whole roomful of water!"

Lin Peishan cried out shrilly, then all at once doubled over in wild laughter. The Wu young mistress felt her sister was carrying the joke too far, and knitted her brows, on the point of scolding her a little, when suddenly the door sounded, and Wu Sunfu strode in with a face full of anger, and his very first words were:

"Peiyao! How is it you simply don't know that Fourth Sister has run off?"

This was a rebuke, harsh in look and voice. Only now did the Wu young mistress understand that her sister had not been joking; but against Wu Sunfu's manner too a resentment rose in her, and she stood abruptly up and answered coldly:

"She's no prisoner, and I was never charged with keeping guard over her; a few days ago when she was in her queer temper, everyone was coaxing her to go out for a stroll, and you all reproached me for going out without asking her along; today she's gone off to the Liwalida for a jaunt on her own, and now here you come making a great to-do and scolding other people!"

"Then since you knew she had gone out, why did you not stop her, and have her wait for me to come back before she went?"

"Come, come, how very strange! I never yet knew you'd forbidden her to go out! Besides, when she went out I wasn't at home either; it was Ah-shan who saw her at the Liwalida. Ah-shan, isn't that so?"

"Pah! Who said she wasn't to go out for a stroll! But now she has run away! 'Run away!' Do you hear that plainly? Look at this note!"

Wu Sunfu roared it out, and flung a crumpled ball of paper before the young mistress's eyes. It was a hard throw. The paper ball bounced up off the table and fell to the floor. The Wu young mistress nudged it with the tip of her toe, but did not stoop to pick it up and look; her face had changed colour, and it flashed on her all at once that there had been something odd in Peishan's laughter a moment ago — could it be that she had seen the Fourth Young Lady with some man at the Liwalida? And now the Fourth Young Lady had "run away" as well! All these thoughts came so fast that there was no leisure left for the young mistress to weigh them; by instinct she looked at the floor again, meaning to find the paper ball. But Peishan had long since picked it up in her hand, and had it open. A bare three lines, in a very graceful Lingfei-jing hand — it was indeed the Fourth Young Lady's own writing.

"So when Ah-su came, Peiyao, you were already out? I fancy this whole business is some scheme of Ah-su's!"

Wu Sunfu's expression had grown a little gentler as he said this. But all at once he flared up again, snatched the note from the young mistress's hand, and read it over very carefully. The young mistress, on the contrary, was somewhat easier now; she stepped back to sit in the sofa and said gently:

"Such a little matter, why fly into a temper over it! Only Fourth Sister is odd too — one moment she must be a walled-in monk, the next she must go off and study, won't even stay at home, and off she goes to lodge in some topsy-turvy YWCA hostel — "

"Just so! If she wants to study she has only to tell me — do you suppose I'd forbid her? Why leave a note and slip off empty-handed, as though eloping in secret! Even if she must first take some coaching, is home no place for coaching? If there's no tutor, one can be engaged. Take coaching with Ah-su? What does Ah-su understand!"

"Let her go her way. In a few days she'll tire of it and come back of herself!"

Seeing that Wu Sunfu's gust of fury had blown over, the young mistress coaxed him again with gentle words.

Lin Peishan too put in a word:

"When I ran into Fourth Sister and Susu, Fourth Sister was just as usual, not saying much. And Susu never breathed a word of this business. Most likely they got to talking merrily afterwards and went off together. Only, last time I thought Fourth Sister very stubborn, and now I find her all lively and restless in her mind instead!"

Wu Sunfu nodded and said no more, but paced the room with his hands behind his back, as though still unwilling to let the matter drop, still turning over a way to deal with it. He half understood now what it was the Fourth Young Lady was resisting. Such resistance, wounding his authority, he could of course by no means sit and watch; but Peishan's remark just now, that "the Fourth Young Lady is all lively and restless," had struck yet another chord of unease in Wu Sunfu. He knew Zhang Susu to be a giddy, meddling creature who made friends recklessly, and now this "all-too-restless" Fourth Young Lady wanted to be together with Zhang Susu — the danger in that was no small one! As her elder brother, he could on no account sit and look on!

And so he stopped short, and Wu Sunfu turned his face to the young mistress; in the dimness his look seemed the more sombre, and his eyes flashed with the fire of anger. He took a step toward the young mistress. It was the posture of a beast about to spring and rend! The young mistress did not understand what fresh thing was about to break out; her heart gave a jump, and a thread of cold ran down her spine despite herself. But out of nowhere came an interruption: Amah Wang came in to announce "a visitor." Wu Sunfu's eyes rolled up, he turned to go, but at the very door he halted after all, and looking back said to the young mistress:

"Peiyao! Go at once to the YWCA hostel and bring Fourth Sister back! By hook or by crook she must be called home!"

"Why be in such a hurry! Fourth Sister is headstrong; she's only just gone out today, and will surely not be willing to come back."

The Wu young mistress, unexpectedly relieved, answered soothingly. But she had not reckoned that Wu Sunfu would at once blaze up to the skies again. He shouted:

"No more talk! Go at once! By hook or by crook she must be called home! If she's not called home today, tomorrow she'll never come back again!"

Merely commanding thus, and giving no reason, Wu Sunfu hurried downstairs at a quick pace to meet his visitor.

The visitor was Wang Hefu, who had grown very impatient with waiting; the moment he saw Wu Sunfu come out he did not exchange half a word of civilities, but flurriedly pulled him into the small reception room, swung the door to behind him, and only then said in a low, most confidential voice:

"A piece of urgent news! Xu Manli came just now to report it! Old Zhao has found out we're playing the bears, and he's using his tricks to make trouble for us! The fellow! Dead set on being our enemy! But, according to Manli, Old Zhao himself is none too easy either — he's a bit hard put to turn his money over!"

When Wu Sunfu had heard Wang Hefu out, only then did he let out the breath he had been holding. For the moment no trouble had broken loose, and half his mind was set at rest. Old Zhao "using his tricks"? He had had a taste of that several times over, and it came to nothing much! But Old Zhao himself was feeling the pinch of money too? Serve him right! Who told him to be so dead set as an enemy! — thinking so, Wu Sunfu grew cheerful again, and answered with a smile:

"That Old Zhao is dead set on being our enemy stands to reason! Hefu, only think: when we sold off those eight mills, didn't we all but madden Old Zhao to death? By then we'd already settled matters separately with such-and-such a foreign firm and such-and-such a company, and yet we kept dangling Old Zhao along for sport; in the end not only did his broker's commission fall through, but he must have got a fine dressing-down before that backer of his! That time Jiren's play of the game was truly a fine bit of fun! We managed to peel back Old Zhao's bragging skin and let his backer see for himself. How should Old Zhao not hate us! — But, Hefu, how is it Old Zhao himself can't turn his money over?"

"Not so fast! Let me first tell you the trick Old Zhao is playing against us. He's just now laying his plans. He means to use the name of the 'Domestic Bond Maintenance Association' and wire the government to forbid short selling! Lawyer Qiu has got wind of it from another quarter: on the one hand they mean to petition the Ministry of Finance to order the Central Bank, the Bank of China and the Bank of Communications, and the other banks specially licensed to issue notes, to accept the mortgaging and discounting of all classes of bonds without fail, shirking or refusing none; and on the other hand to petition the Ministry to order the exchange that every account selling futures short must first pay in the actual goods as security, and where no actual goods are handed over as security, no short sale shall be allowed at all — "

"That can never be brought off, whatever happens! It would simply be closing down the exchange's business under another name! Hefu, I take it Old Zhao is putting this rumour about on purpose, to hearten the bulls!"

Wu Sunfu put in, still smiling very calmly. But with Wang Hefu it was quite the reverse; whether from talking himself into a heat, or from the anxiety in his own heart, in short he was streaming with sweat from head to foot. He opened his eyes wide, waited for Wu Sunfu to finish, and then cried out loud:

"Not so, not so! This is bad enough already! And there's more to come besides! Old Zhao is also going straight to work on the exchange's board of directors and the brokers' association, egging them on to issue an order this very day to raise the sellers' margin! To double it! Sunfu, this can be brought off!"

"Ah! — Truly? And the bulls' margin stays as before?"

Wu Sunfu leapt straight up, and his face changed colour. He felt once again that Old Zhao was, after all, not to be made light of.

"Truly, of course! This is news reported by Han Mengxiang. And Lu Kuangshi says besides that the thing is already decided behind the scenes, and the order will come tomorrow!"

"But this too is against the law! Buyers and sellers, both are in business — how can one discriminate! This is against the law!"

Wu Sunfu said it with a shake of his head, the veins standing out at his temples, yet queerly enough without a drop of sweat. Wang Hefu slapped his thigh and heaved a sigh.

"Say all you like that it's against the law — what use is it? Sunfu, Old Zhao and his lot at every turn bring out the great hat of 'safeguarding the credit of the bonds and steadying the market,' at every turn say that those who speculate on short sales are endangering finance and throwing the market into disorder; with a great hat like that pressed down on them, the exchange's board of directors will of course have to bow and obey!"

"This is plainly squeezing the bears till they collapse — it's monstrous!"

Wu Sunfu said it with his back teeth clenched. His alarm now was in truth twice what Wang Hefu's had been a moment before.

For a while neither of them had a word to say, and they looked at each other with knitted brows. A motor-horn sounded in the garden, and then sounded away out of it. "Likely that's Peiyao gone to fetch the Fourth Young Lady? But why is she so slow!" — so ran the thought that rose in Wu Sunfu's mind as his ears caught the honk of the car. Then he thought of Du Zhuzhai again. This brother-in-law of his was a timid man; in such a case as this, would he still dare to sell short? Wu Sunfu had no confidence of it, and his heart grew very dark. At last Wang Hefu took up the thread again:

"I've talked it over with Jiren, and his view too is much like yours: as for handing over the actual goods as security before one can sell futures, that likely can't be brought off; but as for doubling the margin, that's bound to come! In that case Old Zhao's five thousand in silver would stand for our ten thousand! In a twinkling, when the settlement comes, it will be the easiest thing in the world for him to squeeze the bears! Aren't we then done for?"

"Then how if we quickly cover our positions? By the time Old Zhao has all his plans laid, the price is sure to have risen!"

"But Jiren's opinion is a little different. He feels that if we cover now, all our earlier effort is thrown away; he holds for a fight with our backs to the wall! Things being as they are, the price of the bonds will never rise to any great height; let us hazard one bold stroke and seek life in the midst of death! If in truth the worst comes, Jiren says, then it is as though a steamer had gone down, and his two hundred thousand and more is thrown into the water and no more thought of! — I feel this word of Jiren's is a way too."

Wang Hefu said it firmly, his two round eyes opened very wide and fixed straight on Wu Sunfu. An argument as forceful and iron-willed as this would, two months before, surely have come from Wu Sunfu's own mouth; but the Sunfu of now was no longer what he had been, and at every turn his mind ran to caution, to compromise. Even goaded so by Wang Hefu, Wu Sunfu still wavered, still was at his wits' end. He asked with knitted brows:

"But how are we to fight with our backs to the wall? The five hundred thousand and more we got for our eight mills has all gone into short positions; and I've close on two hundred thousand more stranded in those two lines of dried cocoons and stored silk; there's no ready cash — so how is it to be done?"

"That too I've talked over with Jiren. The plan is this: we three scrape together another five hundred thousand between us, and besides that you are to do your utmost to egg on old Du Zhuzhai to sell short once more — then, squeezing them from both sides, we may perhaps ride safely through the strait!"

"On the Zhuzhai head there's no counting. Last time I arranged with him to sell short together, and he did indeed throw out three million; but only the day before yesterday did I learn that he had covered again long since; a bare twenty dollars' profit on every ten thousand lots, and he covered! And what's more, those twenty dollars of profit were made for him at the very moment when we threw out our two million! Hefu, think of it — a man as timid as that, what's to be done with him! We asked him into an offensive-and-defensive alliance, thinking each would give the other a hand, to share the luck and share the blows alike, and instead he's the first to come sponging on our advantage — what more is there to say!"

"But Sunfu, you go and try him all the same. The settlement is extremely near now; even if Zhuzhai won't sell short, so long as he does not play the bull, but keeps neutral, that will be of the greatest good to us!"

So saying, Wang Hefu laughed aloud and stroked his beard, as if the victory were as good as in his grasp. And so Wu Sunfu had no choice but to agree. Then they fell to reckoning again how the three of them were to piece together five hundred thousand. Unhurriedly Wang Hefu counted it off on his fingers:

"The deposits newly drawn into the Yizhong come to about two hundred thousand; that leaves three hundred thousand, one hundred thousand each for the three of us — need we fear we can't raise it? If Yunshan's floating of shares in Hong Kong shows any promise, and he wires up two or three hundred thousand within these five or six days, then there's less to fear still! Besides — there's fresh news again today from Huang Fen's quarter: the whole tide runs in favour of the bears; Sunfu, this is a chance hard to come by and easy to lose! How is it you've been so short of decision of late?"

Wu Sunfu was silent and made no sound. After a while a flush of colour came out on his face, his eyes lit up, and slapping the arm of his chair he cried out sharply:

"Very well! Since you and Jiren are both in such high spirits, I'm in too! But I'll do it in real cash this time. I mean to put my mills up for a mortgage loan! And this house I live in, reckoned by the land price, is worth over a hundred thousand too; let's simply mortgage it together with the mills for two hundred thousand!"

Wang Hefu laughed aloud and cocked up his thumb toward Wu Sunfu, but Wu Sunfu went on again:

"But Hefu! For mortgaging the land I have my own channels; but for mortgaging the mills, it can't be done without Jiren's help!"

"Enough! I'll go and tell Jiren, and have him talk it over with you face to face. That settles it, then; and on the Zhuzhai side, you must do your utmost!"

Wang Hefu said it in great good humour, and stood up to go. But just as he was about to duck into his car at the steps of the great drawing-room, Wang Hefu turned his face back and called out:

"Sunfu! One word more! That woman surnamed Liu, they say, is not to be trusted; she's playing both ends against the middle!"

"Oh — how do you know she's spying for Old Zhao too?"

"It was Han Mengxiang who said it. Xu Manli too bids us take care. And Manli had it from Staff Officer Lei."

"Then I'll be on my guard against her. — But how has she fastened herself onto Staff Officer Lei as well?"

Wu Sunfu answered, nodding and musing. Wang Hefu laughed aloud and ducked into his car.

By this time the heavy rain had long since stopped, and the sky, on the contrary, showed bright; there were many long streaks of yellow cloud in it, turning the vault of heaven into a tiger's hide. Wu Sunfu stood musing on the stone steps of the great drawing-room, thinking of the "fight with backs to the wall" that was coming on the bond market, thinking of mortgaging the house, mortgaging the mills — his thoughts many and tangled, and yet somehow he was listless and could not rouse his spirits. He stood there a long while, until the honk of the young mistress's returning car recalled him: he had still to go and work his "diplomacy" on Du Zhuzhai.

"Fourth Sister would not come after all! I found the place quiet and respectable enough; let her stay there a few days, and we'll see."

The young mistress said it, seeming out of breath, as she stepped down from the car, expecting that Sunfu would not fail to fly out at her once more. But unexpectedly Sunfu only nodded once, and drew the young mistress back into the car with him, saying meanwhile to the chauffeur:

"To the mansion of Master Du, the son-in-law! — Master Du's mansion! Haven't you got it yet!"

Sitting beside Sunfu, the young mistress could not help a smile. Never in the world would she have guessed that Sunfu was going to see the son-in-law about the matter of the bonds; she supposed all along that Sunfu meant to drag out the married sister to go with them and fetch the Fourth Young Lady home. And that, she thought, was really making a great fuss over a trifle. And what was more, she found she actually felt a real sympathy for the Fourth Young Lady's move; did she not herself find the mansion dreary and hateful? At that the shadow of a smile faded from her face, and gave place to a grey look of grievance and helplessness. Suddenly she felt her hand caught in Sunfu's grasp, and so she forced a smile.
