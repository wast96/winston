### The French Park

In the district of Koukaza in the French Concession stands a park laid out by the French Municipal Council, which everyone calls the Koukaza Garden. For breadth of ground, elegance of arrangement, abundance of trees and beauty of prospect, no other park but Jessfield Park can match it. And so the visitors are many every day, and above all in summer, coming and going past all counting. Among them are a good number who take the cool of the tree-shade to see out the hot season and savour the natural charm of the place; and there are no few lovesick men and pining women besides, who murmur their endearments two by two and pair by pair and, on the sly, play out their whole living drama here; while a troupe of prostitutes, tricked out gaudy as blossoming boughs, ply their hooking arts and their trade in flesh, weaving to and fro without a break.

The International Settlement has four parks in all, the French Concession but this one; and though the French Park does sell tickets at the gate — one dollar for a strip of five, all to be used at a single visit and void the next day — most people buy a season pass beforehand (a season pass, good for a year, costs one dollar in silver) and may then go in to stroll whenever they please. The Settlement parks, besides selling season passes, also sell single tickets by the piece, two jiao a ticket, as the visitor pleases.
