### The Song Case, Looked Back On

Mr. Song Jiaoren of Taoyuan was a man of upright and self-possessed temper, learned and richly gifted. He had first held the post of feature writer at the Minli Bao, where his commentary cut sharp and struck straight to the heart of things, and Yu Youren leaned on him as on his own two hands. In the early days of the Republic, when Yuan Shikai held the reins of state, Song was for a time named Minister of Agriculture and Forestry, but he firmly declined and would not take it up. Later, spying out that Yuan harboured designs against the Republic, he held fast to his opposition; and Yuan, jealous of his talent and unable to rein him in, passed from jealousy to spite and from spite to murder, so that the secret plot to "destroy Song and be rewarded for the merit" was resolved upon.

On the night of the twentieth of March, in the second year of the Republic, Mr. Song went with Huang Kexing to the North Station, meaning to take the night train to Nanjing on official business. But just as he was reaching the platform a pitiless bullet came out of nowhere and lodged in his waist; he was rushed to the railway hospital and cut open for treatment, but the wound was mortal and he did not recover, dead at just thirty-two. With his passing the whole nation went into mourning, and his comrades of the Nationalist Party, their hair fairly standing on end with rage, brought on what became the "Second Revolution," and set about the punitive war against Yuan in earnest.

As for the principals in Song's murder, everyone knows them for Zhao Bingjun, with Ying Guixin, Hong Shuzu, Wu Shiying and their sort for accomplices. But in truth Zhao killed Song at Yuan's command: Yuan worked through Zhao, and Zhao in turn through Ying, through Hong, through Wu, so that the real principal was Yuan, and all the rest did no more than carry out their orders, hoping to secure their places and curry favour, and to pocket a little money into the bargain.

Earlier, after Mr. Song had declined the Agriculture and Forestry portfolio, he had gone again and again through the provinces of the southeast, lecturing on the merits of party cabinet government; and Zhao, hearing of it, grew fearful and uneasy, and once said, "Let this man once have his way, and it is all up with us." Zhao's secretary Hong Shuzu put in a word: "I have an old friend, Ying Guixin, a man full of resource and cunning, who can take on the heavy work of killing Song." So the deed was plotted, and Hong wired Ying in secret to act as opportunity offered — "Liangshan runs riot" and "destroy Song and be rewarded" were the cant phrases of the telegrams. Ying then laid out a great sum and recruited a bravo, one Wu Shiying, a coarse fellow who, knowing nothing of the real business, agreed readily enough. Ying first handed him a small photograph of Huang Kexing and Song standing side by side, and charged him over and over: "Kill both and you get the top reward; kill one, the middle reward; miss both, and there is nothing." Wu bobbed his assent — but the names inscribed on the photograph had been switched, and Ying never noticed. Wu, who at first knew neither Huang nor Song by sight, went at the appointed hour by the picture, hunting his men as one hunts a horse by its portrait, and hurried to the North Station; there he ran his quarry to earth, and fired — Huang was untouched, but Song dropped and did not rise.

At that time Cheng Dequan was serving as military governor of Jiangsu; on the report he had Ying Guixin and Wu Shiying arrested and jailed, and then sent men to search Ying's house, where they turned up letters and secret telegrams by the packet. The principal was thus in hand, and Cheng asked the courts to deal with the case in the full rigor of the law. Huang Kexing and the others jointly published Zhao Bingjun's crimes and pressed him to come south and answer for them. Zhao took fright, and secretly dispatched a certain Long with a heavy purse to Shanghai; Long, by an ingenious scheme, poisoned Wu Shiying in his cell to stop his mouth. The thing done, Long got a great reward; and though on the advice of friends and kin he afterward went to ground in his native village, Zhao had him hunted down and killed all the same. The next year Zhao went out to govern Zhili; Ying broke jail and fled to Tianjin, where he came dunning Zhao for office and money, and Zhao made a show of obliging him while quietly setting a trusted orderly to knife Ying aboard a train. Before long Zhao too died suddenly at his post. Later Hong Shuzu was arrested in his turn, and under interrogation the facts came out; he was put to death by the electric garrote, and only then was the whole of this great atrocity brought at last to the light of day.

Ever since the Song assassination, the papers — the Minli, the Tianduo, the Minquan, the Zhonghua Min — had all been beside themselves with fury at Yuan and Zhao. The Minquan Bao was the fiercest of them: day after day it ran leaders reviling the pair, and went so far as to print plate photographs of Yuan Shikai and Zhao Bingjun, each captioned "the felon Yuan Shikai" and "the felon Zhao Bingjun." Ordinary readers, too, admired the Minquan Bao's director Zhou Hao (Shaoheng), who feared no power on earth, and was as bold as he was shrewd.
