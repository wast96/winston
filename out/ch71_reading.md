## About the Author

Shiba Ryōtarō was born in the city of Ōsaka in Taishō 12 (1923), and graduated from the Mongolian department of the Ōsaka School of Foreign Languages (now Ōsaka University). In Shōwa 35 (1960) he was awarded the 42nd Naoki Prize for <i>The Castle of the Owl</i>. In Shōwa 41 (1966) he received the Kikuchi Kan Prize for <i>Ryōma Goes His Way</i> and <i>The Tale of the Land-Takers</i>. In Shōwa 47 (1972) came the Yoshikawa Eiji Prize for Literature, for the body of work centered on <i>The Days We Lived in the World</i>. In Shōwa 51 (1976) he received the Imperial Prize of the Japan Art Academy; in Shōwa 56 (1981) he was made a member of the Academy. In Shōwa 57 (1982) came the Yomiuri Prize for Literature for <i>The Footsteps of the People</i>; in Shōwa 58 (1983), the Asahi Prize, for his achievement in the "renewal of the historical novel"; in Shōwa 59 (1984), the Grand Prize for Japanese Literature for <i>On the Highways: "The Nanban Road, I."</i> In Shōwa 62 (1987) he received the Yomiuri Prize for Literature again, for <i>On Russia</i>, and in Shōwa 63 (1988) the Osaragi Jirō Prize for <i>A Record of the Tartar Whirlwind</i>. In Heisei 3 (1991) he was named a Person of Cultural Merit, and in Heisei 5 (1993) he was decorated with the Order of Culture. Among his books are <i>The Collected Works of Shiba Ryōtarō</i> and <i>The Complete Short Stories of Shiba Ryōtarō</i> (Bungeishunjū), and a great many besides. He died in Heisei 8 (1996).
