### Thirteen O'Clock

The winter before last, a lodger came to live at the author's house—a husband and wife and an adopted daughter (a foster-daughter, that is), some fifteen or sixteen years old; and the pair of them, in calling to their foster-daughter, would forever say "Thirteen O'Clock" this and "Thirteen O'Clock" that. The author, hearing it, was truly at a loss to understand, until, asking a friend who knew Shanghai society well, he learned that there is a kind of person of whom, call him a dullard and he is no dullard, call him quick and he is not quick—a person, as it were, like a clock or a watch that has struck past its proper measure; and so people have hit on a name for such a one, mocking and a little cutting, and call it "Thirteen O'Clock." Only, this name is always used of a man behind his back; call it to his face, and it will brew up a quarrel and a scolding, sure enough!
