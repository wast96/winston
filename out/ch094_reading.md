### Calling the Doctor, Signing the Guarantee

Ever since the fashion for kidnapping came into full swing, the doctors who happen to own some property and have had a taste of trouble now require, before they will pay a house call on a stranger who sends for them, that a guarantee be signed and stamped with a shop's seal; only then will they go. But this being so, when a patient's family wishes to call one out and cannot produce a guarantee, there is nothing for it but to die. There are, besides, a few doctors who study to keep themselves out of harm's way, and who—the ways of the world being so steep and perilous—simply refuse point-blank to answer a stranger's call, and tell the family to bring the sick man to them instead. If the illness is slight this is no great matter; but where it is grave, and the man lies on his bed unable to stir, and they wish to bring the doctor to him, and he again refuses—there too is nothing for it but to die.
