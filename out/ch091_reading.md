### Fake Ginseng

Ginseng is the most precious of all the herbs in the Chinese pharmacopoeia; that a single small root should be worth several hundred dollars is nothing to wonder at. Its virtue is above all to restore the vital spirits, and it has the power to turn back the very work of Nature: suppose a man is at the point of death, yet has business still unfinished and cannot be let to breathe his last—give him a draught of ginseng broth at that moment, and his life may be drawn out for a good while longer. As a tonic, though, it must be given to suit the man: take ginseng when it does not agree with you, or greedily swallow a whole tael when five mace would have done, and it will do no good at all, but great harm instead.

Ginseng is grown in the province of Jilin, and after that in Korea; the truest and finest sort grows of itself, deep in the earth among the wild mountains and open country, and is dug up by the gatherers only after many years. This is why the ginseng shops hang out the sign "Wild Mountain Ginseng"—for once it is not wild ginseng it loses all its worth and all its power. There is a spurious kind that is planted and raised rather than grown of itself, and this is called "garden ginseng." In Jilin and at Yingkou, whenever the ginseng comes to market, the country people come swarming in with carrying-poles and baskets, all wanting to be rid of their loads, and the price is very cheap: two or three dollars will buy a whole picul—that is, a hundred catties—much as one might buy turnips at a Shanghai produce house. They buy the stuff up, sell it on wholesale to the ginseng dealers, and after a further round of skilful roasting and dressing-up it can be passed off as wild ginseng; brought to Shanghai, its worth multiplies a hundred times, a thousand times, in a single move. This is how so very many of these dealers rise from nothing to swell into fat rich men. There is yet another sort—men who once clerked in the trade and have set up their own sharp-dealing ginseng houses, buying a few catties at wholesale from the dealers at second hand, adding their own brand-name and dressing, and selling it off bit by bit; their profit too is not small, and as for whether the buyer who eats it gets any good of it, that is no concern of theirs.

In short, the moguls who have the standing to eat ginseng, when they wish to build up their bodies and replenish their vital force, had far better go and buy from an old-established ginseng house or a famous herbal shop; only so will they escape being badly cheated and made fools of.
