### The Arrest of Dai Jitao

Mr. Dai Jitao, President of the Examination Yuan, was a newspaperman by origin. In the last years of the Qing and the first of the Republic he served first as chief writer of the Tianduo Bao and then as principal writer of the Minquan Bao. The pieces he published in those days he signed with the two-character pen name "Tianchou," and his commentary, sharp and forthright in the extreme, took Yuan Shikai's abuses of power and denounced them in essay after essay. When the great loan of the Republic's first year went through, Dai refuted it point by point in the Minquan Bao, day after day, and so galled Yuan into a fury that one day he was arrested and locked up in the Central Police Station on Fourth Avenue. His comrades of the Nationalist Party, hearing the news, were incensed, and wired Yuan at once to demand the grounds for the arrest; and Yuan, finding public opinion hard to flout, hastily wired back an order to release him. So he tasted no more than a single night behind bars; the next day, before he had even been brought before the Mixed Court for examination, the whole case of Dai's arrest quietly came to nothing.
