## The Zhang Xiaolin I Knew

{d} By Yu Yunjiu

Zhang, given name Yin and styled Xiaolin, was a native of Hangzhou in Zhejiang, born in a remote village outside the Qiantang Gate of Hangzhou into a family of fallen landlords. His parents died early; being an only son, he had been doted on in their lifetime and given his way in everything, so that from boyhood he was arrogant and overbearing. Grown up, he loafed about all day, and the company he kept were all wastrel youths and idlers, forever gathering to gamble and make trouble and to molest women; and because they had men in the police station to back them, they threw their weight about as they pleased. They used three dice for their gambling gear, cunningly setting up such names as "Green Dragon" and "White Tiger" — the game commonly called diandianqiao — which was in truth a swindle to cheat people of their money. The Hangzhou people gave this pack of fiends the nickname "liaoguir," a bit of Hangzhou dialect meaning good-for-nothing.

Notorious in Hangzhou, Zhang Xiaolin then followed Wu Hong to Shanghai. Wu Hong had once been a subordinate of Xia Chao, chief of the Zhejiang Provincial Police Department, and had served as head of the Second Police Station at Menggu Bridge in Hangzhou; he had a great many acquaintances in the Shanghai Green Gang. After the two of them reached Shanghai, Wu Hong went about calling on all his connections in the Green Gang, got in touch with a batch of middling hooligans, and had people introduce Zhang, one after another, to the "Swallow's Nest" (a low-grade opium den) in Baoyu Li in the city, and to the big gambling house "Rongsheng Company" in western Shanghai, where he served as a "checker" (in slang, a "table-leg-holder"). Before long Zhang had put by a little ill-gotten money, and he rented an old-style shikumen house in Male Li on Chongqing Road, where every day he called his friends together to gamble and took his cut of the stakes; and he brought his wife, née Lou, to live there with him.

In the early years of the Republic, Zhang Xiaolin chanced in Shanghai upon Tang Guanjing, a friend of his late father's. This man was a disciple of Huang Jinrong and had once managed the Great World amusement park in Hangzhou. Before long, through Tang's introduction, Zhang Xiaolin came to know the speculating druggist Huang Chujiu, and through Huang's introduction he took Fan Jincheng, of the Green Gang's "Da" generation, as his "old man." From then on he used the power of the gang to deal in opium in partnership with others, while taking disciples widely and building up his strength. Du Yuesheng was by then just coming to the fore, and the two became bosom friends. Later they each built a residence on Rue Wagner (today's Ninghai West Road), with hidden rooms connecting the upper and lower floors, kept expressly for the two men's secret meetings. Once Zhang had made his fortune, his ambitions swelled: he cultivated the Japanese rōnin Doihara, and through Doihara's introduction came to know the Japanese officer Nagano Osami, who often came and went in secret at Rue Wagner. In those days carriages and horses crowded the gate before the Zhang and Du residences on Rue Wagner, which were guarded by concession-police detectives.

In the years of the warlords' wars, Zhang Xiaolin, under the banner of "saving the nation through industry," joined with Fan Huichun, Zhou Bangjun, Huang Chujiu, and others to set up a great racecourse, the "Far East Public Sports Ground Company, Limited" — in reality a large casino, located at Jiangwan, with its offices on Songshan Road in the French Concession, and Bao Lanwen, Hong Jiujiu, and others engaged to run the daily business. On Avenue Edward VII (today's Yan'an East Road) in the French Concession, opposite the Gongwutai theater, he further opened a Hangzhou restaurant, the Rongguan. After China and the Soviet Union restored diplomatic relations, Zhang joined with Du Yuesheng to set up the Linji Timber Firm, which contracted with the Soviet trade-agency office in China for the exclusive distribution of Russian timber in the Far East. By this time he had long been colluding with the Japanese army in the northeast and had reached a tacit understanding with the traitor Yin Rugeng; he had the whole of the Soviet-shipped railway sleepers diverted to be unloaded at the Liao-Shen docks in the northeast, the Linji Timber Firm's business manager Zhao Nansheng sending a trusted man to sit as agent in the northeast and handle the foreign goods and their transfer. The Linji Timber Firm stood at No. 97 Avenue Edward VII, with Zhang Xiaolin as chairman of the board, Du Yuesheng as general manager, and Yu Zhaotong as managing supervisor in charge of daily operations. Later, within the firm, he organized the Great Wall Record Company, entrusting it wholly to Zheng Zibao, the aim being to draw in and win the applause of people in literary and press circles. Zheng, whose sobriquet was "Master of the Plum-Blossom Studio," was skilled in calligraphy and in prose.

Zhang Xiaolin had one wife and three concubines, all living together on Rue Wagner. His first wife was née Lou. The concubine Zhang Xiuying had originally been a noted courtesan and had consorted with many high military and political men, whose connections Zhang used to enlarge his power. None of the wives and concubines bore children; his four sons and two daughters were all adopted in infancy. His eldest son, Fayao, and his two daughters all lived on Rue Wagner; his second son, Zhongyao, lived apart on Route Cardinal Mercier (today's Maoming South Road); and the whereabouts of the other two sons are unknown. Bent on setting up his own domain in Shanghai, Zhang, besides taking disciples widely, wished also to make use of the French Concession's Municipal Council and its police, and so he sent his eldest son, Fayao, to study in France. Fayao took a doctorate some years later, but on his return, to everyone's surprise, he stayed unknown, and when he hung out his shingle no one came. To save Zhang's face, Du Yuesheng found Zhang Fayao a place as assistant manager at the Zhonghui Bank — a post in name only, which he gave up within a few months.

To push his son forward, Zhang Xiaolin courted Wellington Koo, Chu Minyi, and others, and took Fayao to Nanjing for an audience with Chiang Kai-shek, hoping to get him into political life. The strings had been pulled beforehand through Koo and the rest, and Chiang had tacitly agreed to arrange a bureau-level post for Fayao in Shanghai; but at the audience Fayao and Chiang did not hit it off, and they parted on bad terms, so that the hope of office came to nothing.

Later Zhang Xiaolin and Du Yuesheng fell out. Du Yuesheng organized a "Heng Society," and Zhang, unwilling to be outdone, organized the "China Mutual Progress Society" and the "Ren Lu" and other bodies to set against Du.

Before the war, when the Tibetan Panchen Lama came to Shanghai on a visit, Zhang Xiaolin, on a sudden whim, invited the Panchen and his party to his residence in the name of the Shanghai Buddhist Association. The reception was lavish in the extreme: from the front gate all the way to the main hall of the Bairen Tang the whole path was laid with yellow carpet, and from the corner of Rue Wagner his henchmen and the concession's Chinese and foreign detectives were ranged in ranks to make the welcome.

When the war broke out and Shanghai fell, Zhang Xiaolin, introduced by Huang Chujiu and Zhou Bangjun, took up with the traitors Chen Gongbo, Zhou Fohai, Jiang Kanghu, and others, often giving banquets for them at his own residence. Later he held secret talks with Doihara and was preparing to go over openly to the enemy when, before long, he was assassinated by Lin Huaibu.
