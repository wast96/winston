### Ticket Toss

Besides their usury (the little pawnshops mostly reckon interest by three periods to the month, two to three fen the period, though a few count by the month, and those are the exception), the little pawnshops of the town have another dark racket: the "dropped ticket." How is the ticket "dropped"? When some dead pledge nearing the end of its term — out-of-date clothes, say, or old clocks and watches and oddments — finds no customer to take it up, they work the dropped-ticket trick: they make out a pawn ticket and drop it in the street, and a passer-by picks it up, sees that the term has nearly run and that it is for clothing, or a watch, or some such useful thing, and goes off in high glee to redeem it. But once the goods are in his hand and he looks them over — worth little, and no use to him — and thinks to pawn them back at the price he paid, the pawnshop clerk will no longer meet his request; and let him try another shop, it is the same there. For the things are all cast-offs and out-of-date stuff that, once the term is up, nobody will deal in; and that is why they hit on this "dropped ticket" trick to prey on people.
