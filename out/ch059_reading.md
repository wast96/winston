### Fire Under the Ground

Not long after Shanghai was opened as a treaty port, the "self-coming fire" was brought in. People in those days, who saw little and wondered at much, clicked their tongues and marvelled; and because the fire came up out of the ground, they called it not "self-coming fire" but "earth-fire." The earth-fire had not long been in use when a Westerner, Lide, brought in the electric lamp besides. When it was first set going, our authorities, taken in by rumour and afraid the electricity would strike men dead, actually went and petitioned the foreign officials to forbid it, and lifted the ban only later, once they had learned it did good and no harm — such was their benighted folly. In the eighteenth century, high and low alike were mostly without the plainest knowledge of science, and whenever they saw the Westerner set up some new contrivance, they could not tell benefit from harm, but rose up in a body to decry it, and some even took it for a heterodox and devilish thing. To think of it today — is it not enough to make a man laugh in spite of himself?

There is a saying among the common people, "when the iron tree blooms." Ever since the "self-coming fire" came into wide use, people have taken to saying, besides, that the iron tree has bloomed and the saying come true. Such far-fetched and strained talk is worth, in truth, no more than a knowing man's smile!
