### Eating Tofu

The three-character phrase "eating tofu" is a bit of cant among the hoodlum set; when they say it they do not mean they want any tofu at all, but that they are out to hook a woman by the arm. In the parks and the amusement halls one is forever hearing the cry of "eating tofu"—which is to say, the work of accosting some woman is under way. But let them run up against a seasoned woman, and she answers them coolly: "This old girl keeps no tofu shop; if you fellows are so fond of tofu, be off to a tofu shop and eat it!"—and those few words are enough to send the tofu-hunting hoodlums packing, not daring to press their liberties any further, for they know their match is an old hand herself and not so easily led on.

There is another sort of man who sets himself up as a Lu Zhonglian, a guide and go-between, and when the business has been brought off must be treated to a round of drinks and a meal for his pains; their cant for this is also "eating tofu." Tofu being white, the sense is taken from the look of the thing—it means, one supposes, "eating white," a free meal at another's cost.

Not long ago old Mr. Wu Zhihui, over the matter of the manufacturer Rong Zongjing's cash-flow troubles, wrote to Chen Gongbo, the Minister of Industry, to plead for support; and the fourth sentence of his letter ran: "There are too many tofu-eaters in China, and too few who will take a risk." Further on he wrote: "These men are indeed experts, yet their nature is to eat tofu, and they are hemmed in besides by a tofu-eating environment . . ." What Mr. Wu meant by "eating tofu," if one teases out his sense, was something like making one's success off the backs of others—quite a different thing from the cant of the hoodlum.
