### Osmanthus

On the tongues of Shanghai folk, if they wish to name a thing that is second-rate and bottom-grade, they can stand for it with the two words "sweet osmanthus." To say a "sweet-osmanthus widow-woman," for instance, or a "sweet-osmanthus lawyer," is to signify a no-good woman and a bottom-grade lawyer; but why people should use so fine a flower to describe some shoddy, inferior thing is truly hard to fathom.

Among flowers the osmanthus, though it cannot match the peony for fragrant splendour or the chrysanthemum for braving the frost, is no common cheap bloom either. Guangxi produces the most cassia, which is why Guangxi is called the Cassia Province. The gui comes in several kinds—musk-osmanthus, golden osmanthus, silver osmanthus, red osmanthus, cassia; and the bark of the cassia, called guizhi, can be used as medicine. Other osmanthus may be set, branch and all, in a vase to stand on the desk, filling the nose with fragrance and seeping into the very heart; steeped in sugar it can also serve as a flavouring. Put this way, the osmanthus too is a precious and useful thing, and to liken it now to a bottom-grade plant—would that not make the osmanthus cry out at the wrong done it?
