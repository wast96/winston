### Thirty Cents to Piss, a Dollar More

By the regulations of the concession (that is, the Land Regulations of the Settlement north of the Yangjingbang), the seventeenth article reads: "There shall be no easing of nature upon the roads. In lanes where there is no privy, one may not ease oneself either. Offenders shall be haled to the police station and fined a dollar for the greater need, three jiao for the lesser." And so, when a man is suddenly taken short and can find no privy in time, and eases himself on the road or in a lane, should a plain-clothes constable catch sight of him he is hauled off to the station house (that is, the police station) and punished according to the article; and if he has no money, they will lock him up for four hours.

To be sure, not to go to a privy for one's needs is both unseemly and bad for the health, and any man of the least self-respect would not willingly break the rule; but when he is suddenly taken short and can find no privy, there is nothing for it but to let go the once (this way of relieving oneself is for the small need only; the great need is seldom seen). The author has a method: should a man, walking along the road, be suddenly taken short and unable to find a privy, let him seek out a teahouse or an eating-house and step inside to relieve himself, and that will serve. For teahouses and eating-houses have not only urinals but chamber-pots as well, and whether it is the small need or the great, you have only to be civil in the asking and they will always let you relieve yourself.
