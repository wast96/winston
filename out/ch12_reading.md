## Chapter Twelve

The careless smile faded slowly from Wu Sunfu's face and gave way to a brooding look; presently a few of the purplish pimples on his face quivered faintly, and the fine beads of sweat at his temples grew thicker. He turned his eyes away from Liu Yuying's gaze, showed the whites of his eyes as he stared toward the window, and with the middle finger of his right hand traced a cross on the tabletop.

Outside the window someone passed by. He seemed to halt, and on the patterned glass of the window the shadow of half a head was thrown. Then he moved off again, and a second head-shadow came. All at once the cry of a bulletin-seller went flying past the window: "Wanna read how Yan Xishan's marching his whole army out? Wanna read about the big battle at Dezhou? Jinan hard-pressed! Wanna read... the circular telegram from beyond the Pass..." And then came a second bulletin-seller's voice, shouting and running past.

Wu Sunfu's eyebrows seemed to leap; abruptly he rose, walked a half-circle across the room, and then came and stood before Liu Yuying, standing very close; his sharp gaze fastened on her powdered face, fastened on her eyes with their faint bluish shadows, as though he would see into her very heart.

Under his stare Liu Yuying neither smiled nor spoke, but waited patiently for what would come of it.

"Yuying! You must do as I tell you—"

Wu Sunfu said it slowly, without a trace of wavering, still watching her as keenly as before; yet he did not go straight on, as though weighing which things he ought to charge her with first. Liu Yuying pursed her lips in a smile, knowing that "what would come of it" had come; she was so happy that her bosom heaved gently, and she could not help breaking in to ask:

"But do you understand where my difficulties lie, Uncle?"

"I understand it all. You want to guard against the chance that Old Zhao sees through what you are doing, and to leave yourself a way out in advance—isn't that it? Ah—leave all that to me. We are kin of a sort, after all, and ought to help one another. Now listen to me, Yuying: first, you keep a firm hold on Han Mengxiang. I know you have the knack for it. You mustn't—"

Liu Yuying smiled again, and a flush of colour flew across her face.

"You mustn't go telephoning all over the place after me any more, and you mustn't come looking for me at the Yizhong Company again! If you do that, Old Zhao will know at once that you and I have dealings together, and then Old Zhao will be on his guard against you—"

"That I understand too. Today was the first time I came after you, so I had no choice but to telephone everywhere; from now on I'll be careful."

"Ah, you're a clever one! Now, then, the third thing: go and take a room by the month at some quiet hotel, and whenever we have something to say we'll meet there. I'll come to you. Every afternoon around six o'clock you must wait for me there—can't you manage that?"

"It's the waiting there every single day that I'm afraid I can't manage. There may be times when something holds me up."

"That doesn't matter. Just snatch a moment to telephone the Yizhong Company and let me know, and that will do."

"And what if you're not at the Yizhong Company either?"

"Between four and five I'll certainly be there. Should I happen not to be at the Yizhong, ask for the one named Wang—Wang Hefu, He-fu—and you can tell him instead. He's a northerner, with a very loud voice; you're not likely to mistake him."

Liu Yuying nodded, pursing her lips in a smile. Suddenly a head-shadow flashed once more across the patterned glass of the window, and then, with a smack, the head struck against the pane and all but knocked the casement open. Wu Sunfu whipped his face round to look, and his colour changed a little. Now two head-shadows showed on the patterned glass, one tall and one short, swaying to and fro. A sudden suspicion seized Wu Sunfu; he ran quickly to the window, threw it open without warning and looked out—only to see two furious faces, eyes glaring as if to devour each other, neither willing to give way. It was two ragamuffins at fisticuffs. Wu Sunfu shrugged his shoulders, shut the window fast, went back to the table, signed a cheque and handed it to Liu Yuying, and added in a low voice:

"But mind—not a room like this one! Far too noisy! It must be upstairs, with no passageway under the window!"

"Set your mind at rest, I'll see to it properly. But, Uncle, have you finished your instructions? I have something to say—"

"What is it?"

Wu Sunfu tilted his head, his brows lifting a trifle.

"With Xu Manli you must draw the strings tighter, so that Old Zhao keeps suspecting her and keeps having nothing to do with her. That way the rumour I put about last time won't come unstuck, and I'll be able to keep going to Old Zhao's freely! If you've never been very close to Xu Manli, then please hurry and make yourself close to her!"

Wu Sunfu knitted his brows tight, but he nodded all the same.

Outside the window the two ragamuffins suddenly fell to cursing each other, and it too seemed to be over money. "Go ahead and cut the ground from under me if you dare! I'll give you what for!"—these two lines leapt out clear as could be. Wu Sunfu, inside the room, heard them too; he knitted his brows tighter still, threw a glance at Liu Yuying, swayed his body and rose to his feet. But by now Liu Yuying had already put forward a second request:

"There's more, Uncle. I have ways of keeping a hold on Han Mengxiang, but my one tongue alone isn't quite enough; he'll want something solid to go with it. Old Zhao is very ready to spend money buying men over. Uncle, tell me roughly first what advantage you'd be willing to give Mengxiang, so that I can watch for my chance and egg him on."

"That, for the moment, I can't settle. Let's talk of it again tomorrow or the day after."

"Well, then, there's one more thing to say—"

As she spoke Liu Yuying broke into a suppressed giggle, and her face too flushed all at once; her glance slid across Wu Sunfu's face, but she did not go on.

"What is it? Say it!"

Wu Sunfu asked, hesitating, having caught something odd in that smile and that glance of hers; he felt that this lady detective had rather too much to "say," and, matters having come this far, he had in fact grown a little doubtful of her—at the very least he no longer dared trust himself utterly sure of "keeping a hold" on her.

"It's only—what am I to call you when you come to the room I've taken?"

Liu Yuying said it softly, her smile now settled, her jet-bright eyes full of an alluring glimmer.

Understanding at last that it came to no more than this, Wu Sunfu gave a little laugh; yet he felt no strong allure, and, breathing more easily, he stood up and answered as though it were nothing at all:

"We are kin, after all; I'm still your uncle!"

So saying, Wu Sunfu waved a hand and hurried away. Only when he had run out of the hotel and settled into his motor-car did he taste again that smile of Liu Yuying's just now, that flush, that play of her eyes, and all their allure, and he could not keep his heart from giving a jump. But this stirring of his senses lasted only an instant; at once his whole mind swung back to Old Zhao and the bonds, and to the chauffeur, who had turned his face round for orders, he barked:

"To the exchange! Quick!"

It was now getting on for three in the afternoon. The venomous sun had baked the asphalt of the road soft, and where the motor-car tyres rolled over it they pressed out patterns of every kind. The ragamuffins and small boys with black sweat streaming down their faces, hawking bulletins of every kind along that asphalt road, cried out too in every kind of tone every kind of contradictory news.

In the car—which had flashed like lightning into the exchange for a turn and was now bound again for the Yizhong Company—Wu Sunfu, his whole mind laid to the plotting of his enterprise, all at once discovered a great contradiction in himself as well. He was a man of industry; he cherished the grand ambition of developing the nation's own industry; he had always been against men of great capital like Du Zhuzhai, who dealt in nothing but land, gold, and government bonds—yet here he was, burrowing into the bonds himself! He hoped to see a genuine democratic government come into being, and so he hoped too that the military action of the "Northern Enlarged Conference" would swiftly succeed, would swiftly reach Jinan along the Tianjin–Pukou line, would reach Xuzhou; yet now, from Liu Yuying's own mouth, he had confirmed that Old Zhao was "bearing" the bonds and, moreover, was preparing to use his "old method" to make victory certain—so that he dreaded the northern military power spreading too fast! He was utterly unwilling that within this month—within these five or six days—the situation in Shandong should shift! And upon these contradictions was heaped yet another: that the slender capital of the Yizhong Company was to be laid out on bonds and to expand the eight newly bought mills both at once! Only a month before, he had racked his brains to seize Zhu Yinqiu's dried cocoons and modern reeling machines; yet now that he had seized them into his grasp, now that one more "new mill" lay beneath his iron hand, he felt it, too, to be a "wet shirt," and knitted his brows whenever he thought of it!

All these contradictions had come on so fast, so abruptly, that Wu Sunfu seemed to have sunk into them before he knew it. Now he saw them clearly, plainly—but he could no longer pull himself out! He knitted his brows and gave a grim laugh.

Yet he was not much cast down. His self-confidence could still hold him up. The contradictions before him were a stage on the road to victory, an unavoidable ordeal of demons—so he argued to himself. Was it not precisely in order to resist the "trust conspiracy" of Old Zhao and his kind that he, Wu Sunfu, must "cross swords" with Old Zhao and seek to bring him down in the bond market? This was the crux within the crux! And so Wu Sunfu fitted a "reasonable" explanation onto his own contradictions. Only there was one point: the contradiction in the Yizhong Company's finances—to deal in bonds and expand the eight mills both at once—must find some real solution! What was more, Du Zhuzhai's withdrawal from Yizhong was now past all remedy, and the hoped-for help from the banking and native-bank world would be affected by it too; this was the greatest difficulty of the moment, and this pass must somehow be broken open before there could be any talk of a second step!

The car halted, and Wu Sunfu's train of thought paused for the time; carrying his mood, not yet despairing but fretful, he hurried into the Yizhong Company.

In the business department downstairs a man was withdrawing a deposit, wrangling stormily with the department's clerks. Was there some question of the "seal"? Or an error in the sum? To turn so red in the face and neck over it! Wu Sunfu knitted his brows and threw the man a glance in passing, then made straight for the second floor and burst into the general manager's office. Office it was called, but its arrangement was more like a meeting hall; the general manager's real place of work was another "confidential room" next door. Just now, because he had come in such a rush, Wu Sunfu wore a somewhat flurried look; and Wang Hefu and Sun Jiren, who had been in close, knee-to-knee talk in that office, were startled, and all at once sprang up together, their eyes wide with alarm. Wu Sunfu gave a little laugh, to show there was nothing amiss. But Wang Hefu's words, coming straight at him, set his heart pounding.

"Sunfu, Sunfu! A hitch has come up, no small one and no great one either! We telephoned everywhere and couldn't find you—you've come just in time!"

"I too was driven here by one telephone call of Hefu's after another. We're just now discussing what to do. The business itself isn't so very grave; only, it's turned up right at this awkward pass when we can't turn round, and that makes it a nuisance!—The one hundred thousand taels from the Yuanda that we arranged last week: today the other side has suddenly gone back on it, in the smoothest of tones. That's the whole of it."

Sun Jiren went on, still in that slow, cool tone of his, only with a few traces of anxiety showing through on his face.

Wu Sunfu's heart settled too. Though the thing had come rather too early, it was no great surprise; that sum from the Yuanda native bank had been Du Zhuzhai's connection to begin with, and now that Zhuzhai had broken away from Yizhong, for their side to refuse the loan was only human nature. So Wu Sunfu made an effort to stay calm, set aside for the moment the question of the bonds in his mind, and turned first to discussing how to meet the one hundred thousand that had suddenly fallen short.

The intended use of this sum was to pay the wages of the two thousand five or six hundred workers in the eight mills all told, and to buy in the various newly added raw materials.

Wang Hefu brought out a great many account-books and vouchers for Wu Sunfu and Sun Jiren to look over, and explained briefly:

"The wages come to fifty thousand-odd dollars in all, paid out at the end of the month—that's still five or six days off, so it's no great matter. What is urgent is the goods newly bought in: rubber, umbrella-ribs, electrical stock, rosin, sulphuric acid, and the like, seventy thousand-odd dollars all told. And that's all to be paid within two or three days."

Wu Sunfu stroked his chin and pondered, and threw a glance at Sun Jiren. The end of the month was near, and his own mills, together with Zhu Yinqiu's mill now under his management, had wages to pay too. He would have to bestir himself somewhat to raise the money. Though his enterprise had expanded, never had he been so pinched for ready cash as now! Reckoned over his whole estate, he had leapt ahead these past two months, by two hundred thousand at the very least; yet the dried cocoons in his warehouse had a hundred thousand and more locked up in them, and, with the price of silk lately plunging wildly and no bearing the pain of dumping the stock, this silk in store had another hundred thousand and more locked up in it too; and last of all, another hundred thousand and more lay idly tied up back in his native place. So although Yizhong was short of only one hundred thousand at present, he pondered and pondered, and could not dispose of it.

"Well, then, the seventy thousand is wanted tomorrow or the day after; good, I'll go and think of something!—"

Sun Jiren looked back at Wu Sunfu, and very handsomely took up that responsibility; he knew Wu Sunfu's difficulties. He paused, turned over those vouchers and account-books, and went on:

"Still, doctoring the head when the head aches, borrowing here and scraping together there, is no way to go on. We've taken in the eight mills, and on top of that Chen Junyi's silk-weaving mill is leased to us, the contract fixed for a year; the scope of our enterprise—call it large, it's not large, call it small, it's not small either. We must have a comprehensive reckoning. When the company was formed the paid-up capital was eight hundred thousand; then we took over this Yizhong and bought those eight mills, and now that old Du Zhuzhai has drawn out his shares and withdrawn, there's only some four hundred thousand in ready cash, and that has all gone bit by bit into bonds. I have long thought it: to run those mills and to deal in bonds both, this bit of capital of ours isn't enough to turn round on. Of the two, we can only settle on one and go at it; but the trouble is that both, as things stand now, have become a case of riding a tiger and not daring to dismount."

"To run those eight mills alone, four hundred thousand and more might just barely scrape by. But do we not mean to expand? We have Chen Junyi's silk-weaving mill besides. Four hundred thousand and more is still not enough! And just at this moment, the fighting has cut the lines of communication, the goods the mills turn out can't be shipped away, and this month we shall have to pay out clear expense for nothing; we really must plan the whole thing through!"

Wang Hefu, being in sole charge of the mills, laid his stress on the side of the mills.

As he listened Wu Sunfu was thinking, and of a sudden a look of resolve came out on his face. He shot a glance at the two of them, Sun Jiren and Wang Hefu, and in his gaze there burned a flame of courage and optimism. This gaze of his could often fan the ardour of his two colleagues into a blaze, stir up their fancy, stiffen their will; this gaze of his had a magic in it! This gaze of his was the cannon that overawed the foe with its first shot, each time he came to settle a great plan or resolve a great doubt!

But just as Wu Sunfu was about to speak, there came two soft knocks at the door yonder.

"Who is it? Come in!"

Wang Hefu turned his face toward the door and called, and rose to his feet as though out of patience.

The one who came in was the head of the business department downstairs; bent at the waist, he came tripping nimbly on tiptoe with quick little steps up to Wang Hefu, and said in a low voice:

"Here's another fixed-deposit holder whose term isn't up yet, wanting to draw his money out. We showed him the new regulations, but he flatly won't submit; he says he'll sacrifice four months' interest and more, but making him take the 'discount' terms won't do. He's been making a row down there for a good long while. What's to be done—may the general manager give his orders!"

Wang Hefu gave a soft snort through his nose and, without answering the department head just yet, turned his head to look at Wu Sunfu and Sun Jiren. These two had heard and understood as well. Wu Sunfu knitted his brows; Sun Jiren stroked his chin and smiled. Wang Hefu turned back and asked the department head:

"How much is the sum?"

"Ten thousand."

"Ah—ten thousand! Let it go, then; don't make him take the 'discount' terms. What a nuisance!"

The department head nodded with a smile and withdrew, again tripping nimbly on tiptoe. The door, fitted with a Yale automatic closer, swung softly shut of itself; after one small click the room fell all at once into a deathly silence.

"What a nuisance! Every day there's something of the sort!"

Wang Hefu, muttering to himself, went back to his seat and lit a Garrick. He blew out a thick mouthful of smoke and went on:

"These odds-and-ends depositors were all taken on in the old company's day! By now they've drawn out a good sixty per cent of it, bit by bit."

"Ah!—and the new ones we've taken on?"

"They just about make up for it. Yunshan drew in a hundred thousand and more, current and fixed both. On the side of drawing in deposits, the outlook is very sure."

Even as he answered Sun Jiren, Wang Hefu was turning over those account-books again.

Wu Sunfu gave a laugh, and his gaze turned all at once very ferocious; he looked at Wang Hefu, then at Sun Jiren, and said with decision:

"Tomorrow we'll send out letters notifying those old depositors that, if within the half-month they wish to draw money not yet due, we can make a special accommodation and reckon the interest by the day! Jiren, am I right or not: it's not worth our while to be figuring these petty sums! The way I see it, those old depositors all rushing in to draw their money is certainly not without cause! Most likely they've heard some rumour that damages our credit. Zhao Botao is a past master at spreading rumours! He's over there devising every kind of way to make trouble for us. He said long ago that we'd not be able to bear it the moment the banking world tightened on us even a little; that was no idle talk of his—he's over there laying his plans, he's over there working his tricks!"

"That's it! Today's about-face by the Yuanda native bank was most likely stirred up by Old Zhao too. I caught something behind their tone."

Wang Hefu put in hastily.

"Take this business of Zhuzhai, then. On the face of it, the reason he pulled out of the company was that he disapproved of buying those eight mills; but at bottom it may well have been the talk Old Zhao put about that Zhuzhai took fright at. Zhuzhai wouldn't say so plainly to me, but I could see it. Once he learned that Yunshan had gone off to Hong Kong, he pressed again and again to bring Shang Zhongli in. I flatly refused, and the very next day he made up his mind to draw out his shares!"

"Ha, ha; old Du Zhuzhai is a bit timid, a bit timid. But then old Du Zhuzhai really has no love for running mills of any sort."

It was Wang Hefu again who spoke, and he glanced at Sun Jiren. Sun Jiren nodded and mused. A dark shadow spread slowly and widened in Sun Jiren's heart: just as Du Zhuzhai truly had no love for running mills, so he, Sun Jiren, had no great relish for dealing in bonds and the like—it was not that he loathed this sort of "speculation" at root, but that his energies were not equal to it, and he always felt that to attend to his own proper line of trade was tiring enough; and now not only had both bond-dealing and mill-running become a case of riding a tiger with no way to dismount, but step by step new dangers were coming to light, step by step it was being borne out that Old Zhao's deliberate "economic blockade" had already become a fact; hemmed in on every side, he really felt he had no great assurance of being able to fight his way clear. Yet, other than to press forward, what other course was there after all?

Yet Sun Jiren was still very composed; he knew Wu Sunfu was waiting for him to give his opinion, and he knew too that Wang Hefu had no fixed opinion of any kind, and so, looking coolly at Wu Sunfu's spirited, purplish face, he said, slowly as was his wont:

"So long as we plant our own feet firm, we've nothing to fear. Credit is one thing, rumour another; we too must keep unhurried and unflustered. Sunfu's proposal to leave off figuring petty sums—I heartily agree! Since those old depositors believe the rumours, let's set off a loud firecracker for them to hear. But first our own feet must be planted firm, and quickly; first the foundations of those mills must be laid solid. Let us reckon it up: overhaul those mills thoroughly and see how much outlay can be saved; expand what ought to be expanded and see how much capital must be added at the least; Hefu said just now that the four hundred and fifty thousand originally fixed on is likely not enough—well then, even calling back the capital we've put into bonds, we'd still fall a little short, and we'd have to find some other means. But just how much expansion-money will be needed, how much can be saved on outlay, and, with sales unlikely to be good these next two or three months, how much we shall lose clear—all this we ought to work out into a solid figure."

"The expansion-money has already been carefully reckoned: three hundred thousand allotted among the eight mills in all. That can't be cut any lower!"

Wang Hefu answered first on the matter under his own charge, though inwardly he was weighing the gains and losses on the bond side, for that three hundred thousand had all gone into bonds. He turned his face to look at Wu Sunfu, and was about to ask him how the bonds stood, when Wu Sunfu spoke first:

"Putting the whole of the company's capital into bonds this time was likewise something we could not help. On the third of this month we threw out only one million, meaning at first merely to seize the chance for a small turn; but then the situation changed and forced us to go on, until we became 'bulls'; and now we hold ten million in bonds! At today's closing price in the morning market of the exchange, say it's a lot—it isn't a lot; but a clear profit of three hundred thousand can be reckoned out of it! Just now, before I came here, I already notified our broker that at the opening of the afternoon market today we're to let out five million first!"

Wu Sunfu's face shone with the flush of victory, and he rubbed his hands, well pleased with himself.

"But, Sunfu, most likely it'll rise still further, won't it? From the 15th till today, hasn't it climbed step by step? Though each day it rises no more than two or three dimes."

Wang Hefu put in hastily, his whole face, like Wu Sunfu's, now all delight.

"That's not certain!"

Wu Sunfu answered with a smile, but his tone was uncommonly grave. He turned his face to look at Sun Jiren; the resolve and self-assurance in his gaze could make even the most irresolute of men suddenly make up his mind and follow him. In a voice quick and clear, every word like a block of iron, he said:

"First we must stand our own ground firm! But we are like men at war, with enemies before and behind: those little Japanese factories set up in Shanghai are the enemy to our face, and Old Zhao is the enemy at our back! We must first beat the enemies before us and behind us, and only then will our feet stand firm! Those eight mills of ours must be overhauled at once: the management must be made strict, a batch of shrewd and capable staff brought in to replace the old, the wasting of materials sternly forbidden, a batch of superfluous hands cut, and a batch of bad workers dismissed! I say the budget of every mill ought to be cut by twenty per cent!"

"Just so—from next month, the budget cut by twenty per cent! As for the original staff, I've long felt they're none of them any good; but talent is hard to come by, harder still to find in a moment, and so it's been put off day after day; now we can drag it out no longer. Hefu, you go round those eight mills every day—whom do you think we ought to cut first?"

Sun Jiren said it, cool as ever, and he seemed to pass over the chief point of the first half of Wu Sunfu's speech; but the fire in Wu Sunfu's eyes—the fire of optimism, the fire of doing active battle with Old Zhao—had already kindled in Sun Jiren's eyes. This Wu Sunfu saw very clearly; he seized fast on the chance and at once pressed a step closer:

"Just now I said we've already let out half of our ten million in bonds. We're in great danger! Old Zhao has laid his plans well, ready to 'slaughter the bulls'! By good luck his secret leaked this very day. One of his own intimates sold me this secret—sold it for two thousand dollars—and promised besides to be our inside line and pass us news often! By Old Zhao's arrangement, before the end-of-month settlement the bonds are to take one violent plunge! But we've let out half of ours this very day, which Old Zhao never reckoned on! Tomorrow we'll be clean out of them, and Old Zhao's fine scheme will be of not the least use!"

As he spoke Wu Sunfu shot to his feet; like a great general recounting the course of a decisive battle waged through death into life, he was so wrought up that tears all but fell. He looked at his two colleagues and added, with a smile:

"From now on we'll be all the surer in dealing with Old Zhao!"

And so the question of overhauling the mills was set aside for the time, and the talk gathered on Old Zhao and the bonds. Wu Sunfu had triumphed utterly. He had put his own line of battle in order; he had made Sun Jiren and the rest understand that to deal in bonds and run mills both was no contradiction but an unavoidable step in the history of their success; he had made plain that a passive "policy of self-reliance"—not leaning on loans from the banking world—was as good as sitting still to await death; only by first defeating Old Zhao and breaking the "economic blockade" under Old Zhao's command could they truly "plant their own feet firm"! He had deepened his two colleagues' understanding of Old Zhao and their enmity toward him. He had made the Yizhong Company over entirely into a great headquarters of "opposition to Zhao"!

At last they came back to that question of overhauling the mills. On this they naturally bent a redoubled effort. Cutting hands, lowering wages, lengthening working hours, drawing up several management rules strict beyond all bettering: everything was put forward, and within a little more than ten minutes it was in the main decided.

"Dismiss workers, three hundred to five hundred of them; cancel Sunday overtime; lengthen working hours by one hour; every worker to be searched going in and out of the mill gate; the mill to withhold ten per cent of wages each month as 'held-back pay,' up to a limit of sixty-five dollars, to be returned by the mill on discharge later on: all these can be done at once. But the last item—paying wages at only nine-tenths—there I'm afraid the workers will kick up a row! Sure enough, cancelling Sunday overtime already amounts to paying their wages at nine-tenths; and to pile another nine-tenths on now is a shade too hard all at once, and I'm afraid they really will get up some strike or slowdown, which only makes more trouble. I propose we put this one item off for the time—well, how do you see it?"

Wang Hefu said it hesitantly, scratching his scalp, his eyes on Wu Sunfu's taut face.

Wu Sunfu smiled, but before he could open his mouth Sun Jiren, over yonder, had already got in first and, for once, spoke very fast:

"No, no! Where we're strict we're strict, but where we treat them well we treat them better than other firms do. Hefu, haven't you seen we have a bonus rule as well? When the work is especially good, above the work-standard we've set, we give a special bonus. Take the bulb factory: we've provisionally set it now that each worker in the bulb factory is to make two hundred bulbs a day, and that figure is really a very considerate one; a worker with a good hand, who doesn't slack, can easily make 250 a day, and then we give him a special bonus of one dime and five cents, so that when accounts are settled at the month's end, isn't his wage even more than before?"

"Ah, ah, Jiren, what you say is true enough; we do treat them well. Only, more's the pity, the workers aren't very reasonable—what's docked they can see, the special bonus they can't! Sunfu, it's not that I'm timid and afraid of trouble, but truly we must think this over with care."

Wang Hefu's tone still did not relent; he was the one in sole charge of managing those eight mills, and he knew the two thousand and more workers in those eight mills had for some time been in a somewhat restless state.

The two of them, Wu Sunfu and Sun Jiren, made no answer for the moment. Once more a deathly silence fell in the general manager's office. Out on the road beyond, the sound of a tram came rumbling up, and rumbled away again. The westering sun, like a wash of blood, wrapped the snow-white table-cloth and sofa-covers in the room.

A look of deep deliberation settled on Wu Sunfu's face. He made nothing of any slowdown or strike; his own mills were forever getting up such tricks—had they not been smoothly settled every time? But his own experience told him that only with loyal and capable clerks in a mill was victory sure. And these eight mills under the company's management had as yet no such "good" staff, and, being eight mills each standing on its own, the difficulty would surely be greater still. Wang Hefu's misgivings could not be wholly dismissed!

Just then Sun Jiren happened once more to voice an opinion that "chimed in secret" with Wu Sunfu's own thought:

"Well then, as for paying wages at nine-tenths, to put it off for a month or so will do. But we must first, and quickly, get the management office of each mill in order! The frivolous, the doddering and muddle-headed—cut them, every one! Bring in a batch of good men at once! I fancy a few might be drawn out of Sunfu's own mills. We'll allow a month's work to overhaul the management offices of the mills, and the month after we can post notice of the nine-tenths wage. Our special-bonus rule, though, must be put into force at once, so the workers know from the first that we are clear in reward and punishment: whoever has the skill, and doesn't slack, can grab a fat handful of money!"

Wu Sunfu listened and gave a nod. But all at once a burst of hurried, heavy boot-steps came rolling like a drumbeat right up to the door of the office, and mixed in with them the flustered challenge of a tea-boy: "Whom d'you want? Don't go rushing about!" Inside the office Wu Sunfu and the others started to hear it. At the same moment the office door had already flown open, and a man burst in, his head streaming with sweat, a very large document-satchel under his arm; kicking the door shut behind him with one leg, he shouted as he came:

"Yan's whole army is on the move! Dezhou's in chaos! How's the business Yunshan went to Hong Kong to see to—have you had any telegram from him here?"

This man was none other than Huang Fen, the famous "Big Cannon."

Wu Sunfu's colour changed on the instant. But Wang Hefu sprang up laughing aloud and hastily asked:

"Is it true? When's the news from?"

"News of half an hour ago—who says it isn't true! Has any telegram come from Yunshan?"

Huang Fen said it panting, and slapped hard the document-satchel under his arm, as if to show the "news" was packed inside the satchel and could not possibly be wrong.

"And Jinan? To reach Jinan there'll surely be a great battle, won't there?"

Wu Sunfu asked, pressing a step forward, his heavy eyebrows twitching and jumping.

"Within four or five days they'll fight their way into Jinan. There'll be no great battle! The great battle will be on the southern stretch of the Tianjin–Pukou line!"

"Four or five days? Ah! No great battle! Heh, heh!"

Wu Sunfu laughed wildly to himself, stepped back, and dropped into the sofa; his colour turned all at once to ashen grey, and his eyes looked as though they would devour a man. The military turn on the northern stretch of the Tianjin–Pukou line had come too fast! So fast that even a hand as nimble as Wu Sunfu's could not keep pace with it!

Sun Jiren too took the point; he let out a heavy sigh, threw a glance at Wu Sunfu, and looked again at the great clock in the room—it was exactly four. At once he pictured the exchange, where at this moment perhaps, amid a roar of ten thousand voices, the bonds had crashed to the limit halt. His heart leapt, and he dared not think further.

"No telegram has come? That's the strange thing! Hefu, if one arrives, let me know at once!"

Even as he spoke Huang Fen turned and went, as abrupt in going as in coming.

Wu Sunfu leapt up again abruptly, his jaws clenched tight, both eyes glaring round. In his agitation he strode a half-circle, then suddenly turned and stood still, facing the astonished Wang Hefu and the wry-faced, brooding Sun Jiren, and said, very excited and very flustered:

"The way I see it, there's only one course left. Work on the brokers to bring the settlement forward by two days! Didn't he say it'll still take four or five days to fight into Jinan? Call it four days, then—then, then, bring the settlement forward by two days, and it falls just before Jinan's collapse. By then, by then, though there be rumours in the market, the bond price may still not crash outright! Bring the settlement forward by two days—that means the market closes the day after tomorrow, and then, then, the 'bears' can't go on fighting it out past tomorrow, and the five million we have left we'll let out tomorrow too, so it looks as if we can just about break even, neither in nor out!—Ugh, what are they about? All of a sudden the whole army's on the march!"

"Lucky we got the news early. Last time, when the Zhang-Gui armies pulled out of Changsha, wasn't it just because we got the news early that we pulled through?"

Sun Jiren was the first to voice approval of Wu Sunfu's plan, half of it to console himself against his will.

"Sunfu, that's the very way to do it! Set to at once!"

When Wang Hefu had understood, he was as high-spirited as ever; he had great faith in Wu Sunfu's clever devices.

"Well then, I'll first put through a telephone call for Lu Kuangshi—man proposes; a little squeeze money may bring it forward by two days."

Wu Sunfu's tone was steadier now; knitting his brows, he spoke and watched the great clock at once. Now was indeed a critical time when "an inch of daylight is an inch of gold"! He gave a grim laugh and hurried off to the "confidential room" next to the office to telephone.

Here Wang Hefu and Sun Jiren, the two of them, both kept silent. Sun Jiren looked at the flower-vase on the great dining-table before him, then raised his face to look at the map of the "Industrial Plan" hung on the wall. He was composed as ever, only now and then stroking his chin with his hand. Wang Hefu, though, was somewhat unable to sit still. He ran to the window and gazed a while, then all at once ran back and pressed the electric bell. At once a young man put his head in at the office door and looked to Wang Hefu for instructions. He was a typist in the correspondence section under the general manager. Wang Hefu beckoned him in; then, pointing to a Chinese typewriter by the window, told him to sit down, and gave his order:

"I'll say it out, you type: Newly Drawn-Up Bonus Regulations of This Mill. This mill—whereas—by way of trial—of scientific management,—to increase production,—Bah! You're no use, so slow! To increase production,—and for the encouragement of the workmen,—the new provisions are as follows,—Bah! A bit quicker! The new provisions, have you got that? are as follows,—Bah, new line—"

"How goes it, Sunfu?"

Over yonder Sun Jiren suddenly cried out. Wang Hefu left the typist and turned to hurry across, only to see Wu Sunfu, both arms folded across his chest, standing by the great dining-table with a look of vexation all over his face. Wang Hefu gave a snort, and, turning, called at the typist's back:

"No more typing! Off you go!"

Now there were only the three of them left in the office, and Wu Sunfu, gritting his teeth, said softly:

"It's already fallen half a dollar!"

Wang Hefu felt the blood in his whole body freeze. Sun Jiren heaved a sigh. Wu Sunfu, head bowed, paced a step, then raised his ferocious gaze and went on softly:

"At the close it fell half a dollar. Our five million was let out at the very opening of the bidding, when the opening price was still half a dime better than the morning market's close; after that it fell all the way! On that five million of ours we can still reckon a gain of a hundred and twenty or thirty thousand, but for the five million left there's no assurance. Man proposes, and Heaven disposes!"

"Not necessarily so. There's still tomorrow! We'll go on as first planned. All lies in what a man makes of it!"

Sun Jiren said it with a forced smile, but his voice shook a little.

"That's it! All lies in what a man makes of it—there's still tomorrow!"

Wang Hefu said it too, like an echo, but without a smile. Suddenly he turned to the Chinese typewriter, tore off the sheet of the unfinished "Bonus Regulations," gave it a flourish in his hand, and, turning back, said loudly:

"The mill business I'll set in order tomorrow! In the eight mills, dismiss workers, three hundred to five hundred; cancel Sunday overtime; lengthen working hours by one hour; withhold 'held-back pay'; and—wages at nine-tenths! Post the notice tomorrow! The workers will kick up a fuss? Hmph! We'll shut the damned mill gates on them for a half-month and see! And Chen Junyi's silk-weaving mill that we've leased must likewise cut wages, dismiss workers, and lengthen the hours!"

"That's right! All lies in what a man makes of it! Let's do it just so!"

Sun Jiren and Wu Sunfu gave their approval with one voice. The faces of the three of them now all shone an iron-grey, for they had resolved to use every possible means to squeeze out of those nine mills the sum they might lose at the exchange; this was their one and only way of making it good!

At nine o'clock that night Wu Sunfu came home carrying a whole body's weariness. It was a very hot night. The sky was full of stars, and a crescent moon so thin it could scarcely be seen. Only under the shade of the trees did there seem to be a little breeze. The young mistress and the others were all in the garden taking the cool. They had put out every electric lamp in the drawing-room, so that of the great foreign house, five bays wide and three storeys high, only two window-holes on the third floor cast out lamplight, like a great monster crouching in the dark and flashing a pair of man-eating eyes.

The young mistress and the others sat under a row of trees by the pool. Of the electric lamps fixed along that stretch on the tree-trunks, only one or two were lit, and the pitch-dark shade of the trees threw into relief the white garments of the four of them. None of them spoke. Now and then came one or two low sighs.

All at once Lin Peishan sang, in a drawn-out voice, the plaintive popular ditty "Ramona"; and all at once left off singing.

Ah Xuan laughed softly. The laugh was faint, like the laugh of one who cannot weep and so laughs instead. In the pool a red carp broke the water with a splash.

The Fourth Young Lady, Huifang, felt that the little tune Lin Peishan had sung was very pleasant to hear, as though dug straight out of her own heart. Those who could sing, she thought, were blessed; to sing was itself to speak. When one had something to say and nowhere to say it, a song was like pouring out one's inmost heart to a dear one. She thought again of the things Fan Bowen had said to her that day, and her heart, half afraid and half glad, went pit-a-pat.

Silence pressed round the pool, and among these four people—four people with four different moods, savouring there the taste of that silence. All at once the silence broke! The centre of a storm, coming from afar, spread like ripples slowly out to the pool's edge, in among these four people. It was Wu Sunfu's angry, cursing voice, carried over from the house yonder.

"Turn on the lights!—It's like a ghost's den!"

Then Wu Sunfu, in his nightclothes, stood out sharply under the strong electric light. He stood on the veranda before the great drawing-room and looked about on every side, his whole face set for a quarrel. Though a bath just now had washed a little of the weariness from his body, his heart was still as violent as a volcano. He saw the four white-clad figures across by the pool. "The very image of four White Wuchang!"—rage leapt up within his breast. Just then Amah Wang, bearing a tea-tray, passed in front of Wu Sunfu on her way toward the pool; Wu Sunfu at once found his pretext, and shouted loudly on purpose:

"Amah Wang! What are you going over there for?"

"The young mistress and the others are all by the pool taking the cool—"

Without waiting for Amah Wang to finish, Wu Sunfu waved a hand impatiently, turned, and hurried into the drawing-room. He felt of a sudden that his temper had run to a ridiculous pitch; he had never been like this. But the strong electric light in the drawing-room only made him the more violent. Those several big lamps were like so many little furnaces, and the skin all over his body felt as if it were blistering. And there was not a single servant on duty in the drawing-room. Where had they all hidden themselves? These lazybones! Wu Sunfu, as if gone mad, sprang onto the stone steps before the drawing-room and roared:

"Someone come here! Damn you all!"

"Here, sir!—Master!—"

Two voices answered together from the foot of the five stone steps. It turned out the servants Gaosheng and Li Gui were both standing down there. Wu Sunfu, taken aback, gave a start, turned his face and shot them a sharp glance, and, unable for the moment to think of anything to say, asked at random:

"Gaosheng! Just now I told you to telephone the mill and ask Mr. Tu to come—have you done it? Why isn't he here yet!"

"I have, sir. Didn't the master say to have him come at ten o'clock? Mr. Tu has some matters still to see to, so it'll be half past ten—"

"Nonsense! Half past ten! You agreed to half past ten with him?"

Wu Sunfu flared into anger again, frightening Gaosheng's words to a halt midway. Over by the pool, Lin Peishan, one of the four, had begun once more to sing that plaintive little tune in a drawn-out voice. It was like oil poured on the fire of Wu Sunfu's rage. Stamping his foot and clenching his teeth, he shouted in fury:

"Damn it! Telephone again! Tell him to come and see me at once!"

Before the words were even out, Wu Sunfu had already turned and, in a fury, made for the poolside. Behind him Gaosheng and Li Gui put out their tongues.

The languorous, plaintive air by the pool turned on the instant into a hushed tension. All four felt that the centre of the "storm" was now sweeping straight toward them, and that they might well be in for a bout of groundless scolding. Lin Peishan, the quickest-witted, twisted her waist and slipped behind the trees, where, covering her mouth to hold in her laughter, she poked out half her head, pricked up her ears, and opened her eyes wide. Ah Xuan, most obtuse in such matters, still cradled in his hand that certain "dart" he had lately made a treasure of, striking a pose as if to fling it. The Fourth Young Lady, Huifang, bowed her head and watched the red carp that rose blowing bubbles to the surface of the pool. And the young mistress, who knew her husband's temper well, leaned lazily against the back of her chair and smiled.

But Wu Sunfu did not break out at once; he only knitted his brows and narrowed his eyes ferociously, as though reckoning up whom to pick out first for a bite. Just so—he wanted to take a bite! From the moment he came home till now, the bellyful of temper in him seemed to need biting someone before it could subside. Of course this would not be a real "bite"; but it had the same meaning as a real one. He glared a while, and at last his gaze fastened on the thing in Ah Xuan's palm. And then, in a composed voice, came the question. Just as a cat catches a mouse, the beginning is composed and shows neither claw nor fang.

"Ah Xuan! What's that thing you're cradling in your hand?"

Seeming flustered, Ah Xuan did not answer, but only held up the "treasure" in his hand for Sunfu to see.

"Bah! The devil take you! Who taught you to play at this trick?"

Wu Sunfu grew stern by degrees in voice and face; but Ah Xuan's manner was so comical that Wu Sunfu could not help baring his teeth in a grin.

"Oh, oh,—Old Guan taught me."

Ah Xuan answered with a stammer, drew back the hand that cradled the "dart," and turned to slink away. But Wu Sunfu at once loosed his stern authority and pulled him up with a shout:

"Stay where you are! Dart indeed! Throw it away! Throw it in the pool! A boy of seventeen or eighteen, still at these good-for-nothing games! It was old Mr. Wu spoiling you rotten while he lived! The summer holidays are nearly over—do you not mean to go to school and study in the latter half of the year?—Throw it in the pool!"

A sound—splash!—Ah Xuan stared blankly at the whole pool of wrinkled water, aching for his treasure.

Wu Sunfu's eyebrows shot up, and the fret in his heart seemed a shade eased. His stern gaze turned next upon the Fourth Young Lady, Huifang. He knew that of late the Fourth Young Lady and Fan Bowen seemed to be on very close terms. This he would not permit! And so a second wave of temper surged up from his breast. Yet he turned his face again to look at the young mistress. Leaning against the back of the cane chair, the young mistress lifted her face and gazed in bewilderment at the stars in the sky. Of late the young mistress had grown a little thinner, and those bright, expressive eyes of hers were often set and vacant now; even when, once in a while, they were as lively as before, they would be so red all over that they seemed about to take fire. Something was ceaselessly gnawing at her heart! The change had come on slowly, and Wu Sunfu had never marked it; and even when now and then he noticed, he paid it no heed; he forgot it at once. Now, all of a sudden, it was as if he saw it for the first time, and the temper in his heart doubled. At once he left the Fourth Young Lady and said sharply to the young mistress:

"Peiyao, they're your own true brothers and sisters—you needn't stand on ceremony! Whatever they get up to, don't go covering for them! I hate above all this being kept so thoroughly in the dark!"

The young mistress looked at Sunfu in bewilderment, pursed her lips in a smile, and said nothing. This angered Wu Sunfu the more. He snorted hard and went on, very sternly:

"Take the matter of Fourth Sister. I'm no old die-hard; in the great matter of marriage one may leave it to the person's own wishes. But I must be let know first, to see whether the two sides suit; there's no call to keep it hidden from me! What's more, I've always had this matter at heart, and there have been people proposing a match to me; if you all just keep me in the dark and carry on your nonsense, won't it end in a laughing-stock by and by?"

"Well, this is a strange thing—what nonsense is there? Have you found someone else suitable, then? Come, say who it is!"

The young mistress could not but speak now; but Wu Sunfu did not answer, and, wheeling round abruptly, asked the Fourth Young Lady with a grave face:

"Fourth Sister, whatever is in your heart, tell me and be quick about it! Once it's out in the open, things can be arranged."

The Fourth Young Lady dropped her face to her bosom, and not a single word came. Her heart pounded wildly. She feared this elder brother, and she hated this elder brother.

"Well then, you have none; I'll decide for you!"

Wu Sunfu felt the satisfaction of a cold arrow striking the enemy home, gave a long laugh, and turned to go. But when he had hurried into his study, that bit of satisfaction faded once more. He still wanted to "take a bite," to "take a bite" precisely at his real enemy. Not the aimless "venting of spleen" of a moment ago, but a "bite" at some place that could be bitten, to make good his own losses! Now his temper subsided by degrees, and his mood passed into the stubbornness, the cruelty and the coolness of one fighting for his life to break out of encirclement. Yet at the same time there arose a dark emotion that had no way out. His heart now pounded pit-a-pat in excitement, now lay still as death, unmoving. The swiftly spinning wheel of his thought seemed not quite to obey the rule of his will: no sooner had he been thinking of that soul-stirring talk in the general manager's office of the Yizhong Company than, cutting across it, in broke Liu Yuying's alluring smile, the flush as her eyes turned, that bewitching low-voiced "what am I to call you"; no sooner had he been thinking, all optimism, how to spread his line of battle and march openly and grandly against those eight mills, than there rose before his iron-grey face the desperate resistance and counter-attack of the two thousand and more workers of those eight mills,—

His thoughts, do what he would, could not be gathered together; above all it was Liu Yuying's bewitching smile, her coy words, the play of her eyes, that broke back in time after time to tempt away the mind he needed for plotting great affairs. This was against his nature! He had never been a man to lose his head at a pretty face!

"Bah! Devil's snare!"

He leapt up abruptly and cried out, slapping the table.

"—snare!"—the walls of the study rang back the echo. Outside the study window the trees rustled in mockery of his turmoil of heart and clouded wit. He sat down again in dejection, gritting his teeth in a wish to make one more effort to recover his true self and drive out the dishonourable cowardice, the decadence, and the pessimism, the sinking spirits, that squatted upon his heart.

But just at this moment the study door opened softly, and Tu Weiyue, his chest thrown straight, stood in the doorway, made a handsome bow, then turned and shut the door, and walked serenely to the front of Wu Sunfu's writing-desk, where he watched Wu Sunfu coolly yet alertly.

For a full two or three minutes neither of the two spoke.

Wu Sunfu deliberately drew a paper from the heap of documents on the desk and looked down at it, and twirled a pen between his fingers, letting his face grow calm, and with a great effort composed his spirit; then, raising his head, he waved a hand at Tu Weiyue, told him to sit, and asked in a very offhand tone, smiling:

"The first time I telephoned for you to come, didn't you say you had some business not yet finished? Is it finished now?"

"It's finished!"

Tu Weiyue answered in two words; but his flickering gaze said a great deal more, as though to say: he had already seen that Wu Sunfu had just now had a spell of agitation and distress, and that Wu Sunfu's deliberate show of leisure now was like the wheeling and posturing of a hawk before it strikes.

Wu Sunfu dropped his gaze, not letting this young man before him see through his mood; still twirling the pen in his hand, he asked again:

"I hear the several mills in Hongkou are in a bad way! You don't think there'll be trouble? If there is, will it affect us in Zhabei?"

"Not necessarily!"

Tu Weiyue's answer had grown by one word; he smiled very alertly. Wu Sunfu at once raised his eyes and cried out, feigning surprise:

"What! You too say 'not necessarily'? I thought you'd thump your chest and say: our mill has nothing to fear!—Come, Weiyue, 'not necessarily'—I won't hear it; what I want is 'for certain'! Eh?"

"I could well have said 'for certain,' but the moment I came in I sniffed something in the air; I guessed the Third Master had an order to dock wages to hand me, and so I said 'not necessarily.'—Now, since it's 'for certain' the Third Master wants, that will do too!"

Wu Sunfu listened very attentively, his gaze circling over Tu Weiyue's composed face. After a while, he asked again:

"You've made all your arrangements, I suppose?"

"Not quite yet. But no matter. Third Master! When we bring this knife down, resistance is bound to follow; but in a day or two, three at the most, it can be settled. Perhaps—"

"What! You mean there'll be a strike? Three whole days before it can be settled? That won't do! If the workers dare make trouble, I'll settle it that same day! That same day!—Perhaps? Perhaps what? Perhaps more than three days, is that it?"

Wu Sunfu cut Tu Weiyue short, his tone grown very stern, though his manner was still composed.

"Perhaps the little spark that bursts out from our mill will grow into a general sympathetic strike of all the silk-mill workers in the whole port of Shanghai!"

Tu Weiyue answered with a cold smile. This was the last ladleful of oil, and the anger Wu Sunfu had been holding down this good while at once blazed up again! He flung away the pen in his hand, glared ferociously at Tu Weiyue, and shouted as if gone mad:

"I care nothing for any general sympathetic strike! If there's the least stir in my mill, I want it settled, plainly and simply, within a single day!"

"Then the Third Master will have no choice but to use force—"

"That's it! I'll use force!"

"Very well! Then I beg the Third Master to grant me leave to resign!"

As he spoke Tu Weiyue rose to his feet and looked straight at Wu Sunfu, very firm and very bold. A short silence. Wu Sunfu's face passed slowly from astonishment into a careless-seeming coldness, and at last he asked impatiently:

"You don't hold with using force? Are you afraid?"

"No! Let the Third Master understand—I've never, I think, been afraid of anything! I can tell the Third Master honestly: I greatly prize the heart's blood I've put into the mill this past month, and I'm loath with my own hand to overturn a month's painstaking arrangements! But the Third Master is the boss, and to do as he pleases—the authority rests with the Third Master! I only beg the Third Master to grant my resignation at once! I say it once more: I am not afraid!"

Tu Weiyue proudly threw his chest straight, his gaze fixing sharply on Wu Sunfu's face.

"Your arrangements I know; now I'll put them to the test and see whether they're worth anything!"

"Since the Third Master understands, I may say a few words more. Now the Third Master orders me to use force and settle it within the day; that I can well do. Police, plain-clothes detectives, the militia—all are ready to hand. But if it's settled today, then in no more than ten days or two weeks the old trouble breaks out again, and that, most likely, the Third Master would not care for either; nor can I do the Third Master's business with so little credit; I greatly prize my own credit!"

And so for a time Wu Sunfu had nothing to say; he took up the pen again and twirled it between his fingers, fixing his eyes on Tu Weiyue for a good long while. Tu Weiyue let him look, letting not a flicker of expression show on his face; yet inwardly he wondered a little why Wu Sunfu was so irresolute this time.

Wu Sunfu pondered a while, and at last asked again:

"Well then, by your account, what should be done?"

"I too mean to use a little force. But it must be kept for the very last! The workers in the mill aren't all stamped from one block; there are a few of the very worst, most likely Communists, and a pack of muddle-heads who run at their heels. The greater part are the timid sort. I beg the Third Master to give me a term of three days, and I mean, amid the turmoil of the strike, to make out clearly which few are suspected Communists, and net the whole lot of them at one haul! Then a little force must be used! At one such stroke, I'm confident of at least half a year, six months, of quiet. This past month, it's on just this that I've spent my heart's blood!"

Tu Weiyue said it very calmly and with great assurance, smiling. Wu Sunfu too poured his whole mind into listening. All at once his eyes rolled, he gave a grim laugh, and, rising, cried out loud and excited:

"Weiyue! Able as you are, there are still places you don't see! That sort you can never catch to the last one! It's like moths breeding in furs in the plum-rain season—they come of themselves, in the course of nature! You catch them all today, and tomorrow they breed again! Not until the plum-rains are past!—But just now we're in the very midst of that plum-rain season, a long, long plum-rain season, with no knowing when it will end!—Enough! Keep your fine scheme for the future. The times before us allow us no such patience!"

Tu Weiyue made a bow and said nothing, thinking to himself that this time his "bad luck" was sealed; it would be either resignation, or the collapse of his "regime" in the mill, with Qian Baosheng's faction rising to take his place. But Wu Sunfu flared up again all at once, and gave his order, stern in voice and face:

"Strike or no strike, general sympathetic strike or not, my mind is made up! From next month, wages will be paid at eight-tenths! When the silk price climbs back to nine hundred taels and more, we'll talk again—there, off you go! I do not grant your resignation!"

"Then, Third Master, give me the term of three days!"

"No! No! Not one day even!"

Wu Sunfu roared it out. The flesh of Tu Weiyue's face gave a faint twitch, and his gaze grew strangely cold and hard. But unexpectedly Wu Sunfu shifted his manner all at once, waved a hand at Tu Weiyue, and went on impatiently:

"Fool! Do you think to sign a contract with me? Let's see how things stand when they've gone out on strike, and then we'll talk!"

Tu Weiyue, smiling, made another bow and said nothing; but in his heart he had seen clearly that Wu Sunfu was not this time as he used to be,—somewhat out of his usual self, somewhat flustered. Again he thought that this time he would probably be "out of luck." But he was a stubborn man, and he was resolved to struggle on.
