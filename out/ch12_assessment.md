# Chapter Twelve — synopsis and reliability

*Printed pages 239–258. Sections 41 ("A Suspicious Man and Woman on the Peak
Road"), 42 ("A Male Assassin, a Female Assassin?"), 43 ("What Lay Between Wang
Yaqiao and Yu Wanjun"). Chapter title: "The Assassin and the Lover."*

**Why this chapter matters more than it looks.** It is largely novelistic — a
stalking sequence, a mysterious woman, a romance — but it introduces **Yu
Wanjun**, and everything in chapter fifteen turns on her. The book is planting
the relationship that will later be used to kill him.

---

## What happens

**February 1934, Hong Kong, at the bottom of his fortunes.** The Fujian revolt
has collapsed and Wang is at his lowest. His wife is a patient at St Paul's
Hospital in Causeway Bay, and he comes down from the Peak almost every day to
sit with her, through a month of low cloud and rain.

**Then the watchers.** Walking back up toward the Peak at dusk he meets a man
and a woman coming the other way: a bald old man with a yellowish goatee, one
sleeve hanging empty in the wind, a dark seamed face and eyes with the cold in
them he knows from the agents who used to tail him in Shanghai. He cannot make
sense of what such a figure is doing here.

At the hospital he watches a woman in black in the big mirror opposite him
while the doctor talks about his wife's nerves — sees her turn, slip something
into her breast, and fix her eyes on his back. He gives no sign of noticing.
Then she begins shaking violently, stands, and comes toward him step by step.

**And the third strand,** which the chapter's last section names: what lies
between Wang Yaqiao and Yu Wanjun.

---

## How it holds up

There is very little here to check, which is itself the finding: this is the
book operating as a novel, in the gap between documented events.

**The setting is right.** St Paul's Hospital is real, in Causeway Bay, founded
by the French Catholic sisters — though the 1843 date attaches to the order
rather than to the Hong Kong hospital, which came later in the century. The
usual pattern: accurate furniture.

**The chronology is right.** February 1934 is exactly where Wang was and exactly
how he was placed. The Fujian revolt collapsed in January 1934 and he returned
to Hong Kong; his position was genuinely desperate, with a Nanjing bounty on him
and Dai Li's people looking.

**The stalkers are unverifiable** — as is everything about assassination
attempts on Wang in Hong Kong in this period. The one-armed old man and the
woman in black belong to the fiction.

**Yu Wanjun is the real question, and the book is doing something with her.** In
the documented account she is the wife (some sources say concubine) of Wang's
lieutenant Yu Likui, and she is the instrument of his death in October 1936 —
bought by Dai Li with money and a promise of her husband's release. She has a
Baidu Baike entry and that is the ceiling of her documentation.

What this chapter adds is a **prior relationship** between her and Wang
personally, which the chapter title frames as a romance. That is not in the
record, and it is doing obvious narrative work: it converts a bought informer
into a betrayal by a lover, which is a far better story and a far worse fit with
the evidence. Note that some accounts do muddle her as Wang's own concubine
rather than Yu Likui's, so the book may be building on an existing confusion
rather than inventing from nothing — but the romance itself is unattested.

## Verdict

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| February 1934, Hong Kong, after the Fujian collapse | **CORROBORATED** |
| St Paul's Hospital, Causeway Bay | **CORROBORATED** (founding date loose) |
| Assassins stalking him in Hong Kong | **UNCORROBORATED** |
| Yu Wanjun existing, married to Yu Likui | **PARTLY** — attested only at Baidu level |
| A prior romance between her and Wang | **UNCORROBORATED** — and load-bearing for ch15 |
