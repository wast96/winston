### Congee Shops and Tofu Shops

Among the shops of the city that stay open the whole night through, their doors never shut, there are—apart from a few wine-and-food houses—only the two kinds, the congee shops and the tofu shops; from the first of the year to the last they are never seen to close. The tofu shops do all their work at night and put the tofu out for sale at daybreak, so they keep open the whole night; and the congee shops, meaning to relieve the hunger of the carriage-pullers, likewise stay open all night and never bar their doors. The night-shift ricksha men run about the whole night, and when the fire of hunger burns in them they all go to the congee shops to fill their bellies; if the congee shops were not open all night, where, pray, would that crowd of drivers go to eat?

But the congee shops keep the whole night to do business, while the tofu shops keep it to work: though both stay open all night, the two are different in kind. There is, besides, a sort of down-and-out fellow who, in the dead of night, has not the wherewithal to hire a bed at a lodging-house, and so goes off to a congee shop, eats a bowl or two of congee, or a few cheap "pheasant" rice-dumplings, and then deliberately dawdles and will not leave, nodding off for a doze and dragging it out till daybreak before he goes—and thus the night's lodging money is saved as well.
