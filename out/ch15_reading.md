## Chapter Fifteen

Next morning the whole sky was a blind white fog. As the night-soil carts came rumbling along the road, the siren at the Yuhua Silk Filature set up its hooting. The police who were to guard the reopening stood ranged in a single line before the mill gate, long rifles, Mauser pistols, armed to the teeth. Pockmarked Li and Wang Jinzhen, heading the whole squad of inspectors and floor-overseers, were spread out through the reeling-hall and its approaches. Over their sleepless faces lay a livid film; their eyeballs were threaded with red, and lit with an excited gleam.

This was the last five minutes of the decisive battle! These toil-worn and much-deserving "heroes," lifting the "cup of victory" in trembling hands, could not for all that keep their hearts from a certain fluttering unease.

Over by the veranda of the management office Tu Weiyue paced to and fro like some great general awaiting the tidings of triumph. His look was resolute, self-assured; he knew already that Wu Weicheng and the others had been to Wu Sunfu's residence the night before, but there was nothing in that to fear! His dispositions had been laid with the nicest care. The night-long labours of the inspectors and overseers, too, had turned out to his satisfaction. One thing only gave him a slight sense of anticlimax, and that was that A Xiang, the scoundrel, had to this very hour still not come to "report his errand done."

The siren hooted a second time, longer and louder than before. When it had ceased Tu Weiyue still felt a droning in his ears. Over in the reeling-hall the electric lamps had all been switched on together now, and seen through the dense fog they made one blur of haloed light, ghostly as corpse-fire.

Gui Changlin came running up from far off, the small eyes that sat so ill on his long, oblong face fixed on Tu Weiyue from a distance.

"How goes it, Changlin?"

"The women hands are going in! Three or five, a dozen and more!"

At that the two of them exchanged a smile, face to face. The great matter was as good as won! Tu Weiyue turned and ran into the management office, snatched up the receiver and called for the number of Wu Sunfu's residence. He meant to send off the first telegram of victory. Wu Weicheng, Ma Jingshan and Zeng Jiaju, the three of them, stood to one side pulling faces out of the corners of their eyes. Tu Weiyue had called twice, and had just got the line through, when all at once a burst of shouting came flying in from without. The three of them, Wu Weicheng and the rest, darted out on the instant. Tu Weiyue too turned his face and glanced outside. He gave a cold little smile. He knew what that shouting meant. There were still some stubborn women hands who meant to "bar" the others at the gate! This he had foreseen long since. And he had given his orders long since as well: any who dared "bar the mill gate" were to be seized! There was nothing in it to fear. He put his mouth back to the receiver, but the line had gone dead again, and he was on the point of calling once more when a fresh and louder roar came flying in from without; and hard on the heels of it a figure came bursting into the room, bawling — it was A Zhen, her hair all loose and streaming.

"They're fighting! They're fighting!"

A Zhen shrieked it out as she flung herself to Tu Weiyue's side. The receiver dropped from his hand; Tu Weiyue gave a savage cry, thrust A Zhen off with a single shove and went flying out at a run, and just at the foot of the veranda steps ran full into Wang Jinzhen, who came fleeing towards him no less crazed, her face a corpse-like ashen grey.

"Barring the gate, are they? Seize them and have done!"

Tu Weiyue ran straight on, shouting all the way. His face had gone white with fury; he hated Gui Changlin, pockmarked Li and all that crew, hated them to the death — why must they be so useless? But when he drew near the cocoon-room he too came to a stand. Gui Changlin came running up, a gash on his face, breathless and crestfallen. Over at the mill gate a knot of people were locked and writhing in one mass. The police stood by remonstrating, but plainly it was a remonstrance got up for form's sake. In that press of bodies there seemed to be no women hands at all; outside the gate, rather, stood some dozens of women, far off in little clusters, gesticulating and clamouring. Gui Changlin barred Tu Weiyue's way and cried out in a rush:

"You mustn't go! Our men are all being beaten! You mustn't go!"

"Rot! Are you a clay Buddha, the lot of you? Where's pockmarked Li?"

"He's there, in that press of bodies!"

"The sly rogue! That's no way to settle it!"

Tu Weiyue cursed him shrilly, brushed Gui Changlin aside and ran on ahead. Gui Changlin turned and fell in behind him, still bawling "You mustn't go!" Over near the gate Zeng Jiaju stood up on a bench, with Wu Weicheng and Ma Jingshan in front of him; the three of them, their faces all triumph, were loudly bellowing "Strike!" — while at the right of the gate stood that Qian Baosheng, deep in talk with a man who had the look of a police sergeant. All this Tu Weiyue took in at one glance, and something of the truth grew plain to him; the fire shot straight up from his heart, and he sprang forward upon the three of them, on Zeng Jiaju and the rest, and shouted in their faces:

"Whom are you setting them on to beat? When the Third Master comes by and by, I shall make no scruple of asking him to deal with you!"

The three of them were struck dumb. Zeng Jiaju gave a roar and made to fling himself on Tu Weiyue; but before he was aware of it Gui Changlin had hooked a foot round his leg from behind, and down went Zeng Jiaju, heels over head. Tu Weiyue left the three of them and was already at the gate; with one hand he gripped Qian Baosheng's shoulder and thrust him aside, then turned to the man with the sergeant's look:

"I am the head steward of this mill, Tu by name! Over there is a gang of rowdies beating the people of our mill — I beg you, order your men to seize them!"

"Oh — but there's no telling which are your own mill's people!"

"Seize the whole lot and be done! We can reckon the account up afterwards!"

Tu Weiyue shouted it out, and turned once more to look for Qian Baosheng. But he was already gone. The man with the sergeant's look set his whistle shrilling; blowing as he ran, he made for the press of bodies. By now the press too had broken up, and a dozen and more men were fleeing out past the gate. Three or four police, hurrying up at the whistle's call, reached the gate at that same moment. Tu Weiyue saw that among the dozen and more who had fled was A Xiang, and now the whole thing was plain to him; he pointed A Xiang out to one of the police and said:

"That's the one! Take him to the mill counting-house, if you please!"

A Xiang was dazed a moment, and would still have argued his case; but Tu Weiyue had already turned and gone racing off into the mill.

This whole disturbance, from first to last, was no more than six or seven minutes; yet to A Zhen, trembling in her hiding-place within the management office, it seemed a hundred years. When Tu Weiyue came back to the office, this same A Zhen, her face still hung about with loose hair, sprang straight up and clutched at his arm. Tu Weiyue gave her one cold glance, flung off her hand and cursed her brutally:

"Haven't they torn the two flaps of skin off you yet? If everyone were like you, nothing would ever get done at all!"

"You didn't see how murderous those corpses were! They —"

"I won't hear it! It's over now — go and call Gui Changlin and pockmarked Li in!"

Tu Weiyue gave the order in a tone that would cut nails and slice through iron, then ran to the telephone, took up the receiver that hung there idle and called "Hello, hello." Of a sudden a thought turned over in him, and he hung the receiver up again and ran out of the office. A moment before, an idea had stirred in his mind, though still very dim; now it had fled clean away; he stamped his foot in vexation, and wheeled about angrily in a circle, and just then caught sight of Mo Gancheng — a cloth gown thrown over him, a pair of old shoes with the heels trodden flat flapping at his feet — come clattering up; and his very first words were:

"I say, Brother Tu, what do you mean by holding A Xiang under lock?"

Tu Weiyue set his face hard and gave no answer. Then all at once he fell to sneering, and shouted full into Mo Gancheng's face:

"Mr. Mo! Be so good as to tell them that I, Tu, yield to soft handling but not to hard! We reopen the mill today, and they call in rowdies to make a riot of it — what sort of thing is that! A Xiang is an inspector of this mill, and he too joined in the rioting; he must be dealt with, and there's no help for it! The Third Master is not yet come; till he does, everything rests on the responsibility of me, Tu!"

"Come now, won't you all make peace for the sake of my old face? We're every one of us of the same household —"

"It won't do! When the Third Master comes I can hand over my charge, roll up my bedding and clear out; but to have me make peace with troublemakers here and now — it won't do! — Only, Mr. Mo, be so good as to keep guard on the telephone, and let no one ring up anyone at all! If you grow slack, and fresh trouble breaks out, the fault will be yours!"

Tu Weiyue, his face gone iron-grey, pinned Mo Gancheng with a keen and driving look. He had judged to a nicety that one fright would melt the old man to jelly. Mo Gancheng, screwing up his rat's eyes, would have said something further, but by now pockmarked Li and Gui Changlin had come up, Wang Jinzhen and A Zhen behind them. Beside pockmarked Li's nose was a patch of livid bruise.

"Don't be in haste to tell the Third Master! By and by I'll fetch him myself, and we'll thrash the whole thing out plainly, every party face to face!"

Having thus earnestly enjoined Mo Gancheng once more, Tu Weiyue ran across to meet Gui Changlin and his party, and heard out their detailed report.

They all stood by the notice-board before the veranda. The blinding morning fog had thinned a little now, and the sunlight, piercing the thin mist, fell upon their faces. Tu Weiyue had heard Gui Changlin out through no more than a few sentences when all at once that dim idea which a moment ago had slipped out of his brain came circling clearly back. His face lit up on the instant; with a gesture he checked Gui Changlin's words and said to A Zhen:

"Tell them to sound the whistle once again — long, and loud!"

"It's no use sounding it! After the beating just now, the devil himself wouldn't come in to work!"

A Zhen, contrary as ever, would not obey the order. Tu Weiyue's face darkened at once. A Zhen hurried off. Tu Weiyue gave a soft snort, looked round at Gui Changlin and the others, and then, his whole face grown of a sudden resolute, spoke out a piece of his mind, hard as iron:

"I see it all, and there's no need to say more! Half of it was some of the women hands barring the gate, and half of it was that scoundrel Qian Baosheng's trick! This litter of dogs, caring nothing for the whole! A Xiang is already under lock; put him to the question and there's your hard proof! This cur, to play his sly games under my very nose — pack him off to the Public Security Bureau! And Qian Baosheng too shall be charged with inciting the workers to bar the mill and do violence! Till now I've borne with everything and to spare; but I can be slack no longer!"

"A Xiang's been wronged, surely? He was there trying to make peace!"

Pockmarked Li, all in a fluster, spoke up for his good friend. In truth he had not the least wish in the world to go on fighting Qian Baosheng and his crowd, only he found it awkward to say so outright. Tu Weiyue saw it at a glance and burst of a sudden into wild laughter. Gui Changlin, the duller of the two, argued hotly with pockmarked Li:

"He's not been wronged at all! I saw it with my own eyes — A Xiang's mouth was making peace, but his fists were helping Qian Baosheng!"

"Come, Changlin, better to loose an enmity than to knot one — take my advice and let it slide! To my mind, we send for Qian Baosheng and have it all out together. If he still won't come round, well and good! Then I, pockmarked Li, will stand on no ceremony! Eh, Mr. Tu, am I right or no? Let's first hail him over and see what he says!"

Just then the mill siren hooted out again, a full three minutes of it, like some wounded beast wailing for rescue.

"How many workers have come into the mill by now, all told?"

Tu Weiyue changed the subject, and smiled his cold little smile once more; but this smile was no longer his old composure — it was a thing put on.

"I counted them before the fighting began — forty-odd."

Wang Jinzhen answered, letting out a stifled breath, and stole another glance at Gui Changlin. This Gui Changlin now had the blue veins standing out all over his forehead; he ground his teeth and glared up at the sky. Tu Weiyue smiled again, and felt that his "sovereignty" was this time genuinely tottering. His methods were good enough, and he had spent every art of conciliation upon pockmarked Li; yet at this pinch, when the moment pressed, his conciliation was after all no match for the old bond between pockmarked Li and Qian Baosheng. He thought a moment, then shifted his tone and said:

"Very well, Old Li! For the sake of your face I'll let it pass! If Qian Baosheng has anything to say, let him come and say it to my face! Only the mill must open today, come what may! We've sounded the whistle again just now! My guess is that Qian Baosheng is in the little tea-house outside the mill — go and meet him there, Old Li! Tell him that whatever there is to say, we can talk it over amicably, for we're all of one household; but if he tries that same conjuring-trick again, then I've no choice but to deal with it by the book!"

"If Mr. Tu bids me go, then go I will! Best of all, let Changlin come along with me too!"

"No! For now go alone. I've other business to send Changlin on."

Tu Weiyue, not waiting for Gui Changlin to open his mouth, broke in with this, and with a shrewd sidelong glance at pockmarked Li turned and bade Wang Jinzhen take the whole squad of overseers to see to the reeling-hall; then he ran back to the management office, Gui Changlin following after. Within the office Mo Gancheng and the three of them, Ma Jingshan and the rest, were talking in undertones, and seeing Tu Weiyue come in they all shut their mouths and fell silent. Tu Weiyue, feigning not to notice, ran straight up before Wu Weicheng and the three of them, and said with a smile:

"You three have had a hard time of it just now. I've found out the whole rights of the matter, root and branch; rogue beating rogue is nothing to speak of — once the blows are struck you shake hands and it's over. There's but one thing amiss: the women hands took fright and ran. But no matter! In a little while they'll be back."

Wu Weicheng and the three of them stared blankly and could not get out a word. Tu Weiyue smiled once more, very handsomely, upon these three enemies, and ran out of the room. Gui Changlin was still loitering before the veranda. Seeing Tu Weiyue come out, and seeing no one about on any side, he drew close and asked in a low voice:

"Mr. Tu, are we really to surrender to Qian Baosheng, just like this?"

Tu Weiyue smiled coldly, gave no answer, and only walked on. Gui Changlin followed quietly after. When they had gone some way, Tu Weiyue said at last, low and cold:

"What sort of creature is Qian Baosheng? As if he were worthy of it!"

"But you've already sent pockmarked Li off to him."

"You sly rogue, and yet so dull! We put him off with a trick first, and once we've got the mill open we'll settle accounts with him afterwards! A Xiang is still shut up in the empty room out at the back — the proof of their rioting is still in our hands! Pockmarked Li won't play the hard man, so we must find another, and quickly; and that too will take some little time to manage!"

"Qian Baosheng's a crafty one too. He'll see through this stratagem of yours."

"Of course he will! But we can't refuse pockmarked Li a little face for all that. We give it him; and if Qian Baosheng won't, then pockmarked Li will help us with all his might."

At that the two of them both laughed, and stood on the open ground before the reeling-hall, waiting for pockmarked Li's answer to come back.

By this time the thin mist too had wholly cleared; the sky was blue, with a few white clouds; the sunlight striking on a man's body was growing by degrees a little scorching. It was about half past eight. Tu Weiyue had gone late to bed the night before, had risen at five that morning and not once been off his feet since; in truth he was somewhat weary; but he was not one to fear weariness. He stood waiting a while, then grew impatient, and of a sudden, calling to mind another matter, sprang up and cried:

"Ah! They've muddled my wits with all their uproar — I all but forgot! Changlin! I've an important errand to send you on! Go to the Public Security Bureau and lay an information: two people are to be arrested — He Xiumei and Zhang Axin! You're to be the informer! That dog's-head A Xiang deserves to die! Last night he was told to shadow them, and go he never did — he'd thrown in with Qian Baosheng's lot instead, and came today to work his mischief! Changlin, if there are others in He Xiumei's room, take them up as well — don't let a single half of one get away!"

With that Tu Weiyue waved Gui Changlin off, and turning on his heel went to the reeling-hall. Work had not properly begun there; the reeling-frames were spinning empty. The women hands, a hundred and more of them, had come in already, and sat glum and silent by the frames. The whole squad of overseers were posted like sentries throughout the hall. Tu Weiyue put on his most agreeable smile, and made a sign to A Zhen, who came forward to meet him, that she should stop the frames. At once the whole hall grew hushed and empty of the least sound, and only the water boiling in the basins and cauldrons moaned low. Tu Weiyue squared his chest and took his stand in the gangway down the middle of the hall, Wang Jinzhen on his left and A Zhen on his right; he swept his keen eyes once round about him, and then, in his gravest and most earnest tones, addressed his homily to those hundred women and more:

"Hear me, all of you, one word. I, Tu, have been at this mill two years and more, and I've always dealt gently and fairly with you; Mr. Wu made me head steward better than a month ago, and I've never given myself stinking airs. I know you're all of you very poor — I'm a pauper myself; wherever there's a way I can help you, help you I do! But the price of silk keeps falling, the mills are all running at a loss, and on a single bale of silk there's a clear loss of some four hundred taels! Do you all understand me? Four hundred taels of silver! Reckoned in silver dollars, that's six hundred! And a mill can't shit out gold — there's but one move left on the board, and that's to close down! Close the mill, and none of you has any rice to eat; you all know well enough that on Shanghai ground more than twenty mills have shut their doors already! Mr. Wu borrows money, mortgages his houses, tries every means to keep the frames turning and won't close down — and all to save the rice-bowls of you all! If he means now to dock your wages to eight-tenths, it's truly because he's driven to the end of his wits, and only so came to do it! You must all think a little too — a master has a master's hardships! Master and worker must help each other, and win through the strait we're in! You are people of sense, and you've come to work today. When you go back you must tell your little sisters that not to come to work is to smash your own rice-bowl with your own hands! Mr. Wu loses money and gets no thanks for it, and he too will lose heart. Once he shuts the mill, you'll have nowhere even to draw those eight-tenths wages! And if it's me, Tu, you can't abide, why, that's the easiest thing in the world — you needn't even strike; I can resign my place to Mr. Wu myself! I sent in my resignation long ago, and Mr. Wu has not yet allowed it, so I must go on tolling the bell as long as I'm the monk! Whatever you have to say, say it out to me, and never be afraid!"

Only the boiling water moaned low in the cauldrons and basins. The faces of the women hands, steamed red by the heat, were as void of any expression as faces of stone. In their hearts too a seething resentment boiled and turned, but it did not rise to their faces; it only choked, sobbing, in their throats.

Tu Weiyue felt an unlooked-for loneliness. Though the temperature of the reeling-hall stood at some ninety degrees, he felt a thread of icy shuddering rise along his spine and spread little by little through his whole body. Listlessly he turned in a circle, shrugged his shoulders, signed to Wang Jinzhen and the others that "the frames might be properly set going," and made his escape.

Before the veranda of the management office pockmarked Li stood with another man, looking out for him. Spying Tu Weiyue in the distance, pacing with his hands behind his back, pockmarked Li cried out gladly:

"Mr. Tu! I've been looking for you a good while! Baosheng's right here!"

Tu Weiyue halted at once, looked with the coolest composure at pockmarked Li and his companion, gave the faintest smile, and, throwing out his chest, walked slowly up to the two of them. The whole discomfort he had brought upon himself in the reeling-hall just now was gone now; in his mind on the instant a numberless array of stratagems and reckonings piled themselves up. Now it was Qian Baosheng he had to deal with — and this was a different thing from the workers; Tu Weiyue felt himself here to have room and to spare for the play of his blade, and would surely feel no taste of that ice-cold loneliness.

Qian Baosheng too said nothing, and only gave Tu Weiyue a smile. It was the smile of a man who feels himself the victor. Tu Weiyue, easy and unruffled, feigned not to understand it, but in his heart he raged.

The three of them, each carrying a different heart, went in silence round the range of buildings that made up the management quarters. Only pockmarked Li laughed aloud in high good humour and threw out a few irrelevant remarks. They came to Wu Sunfu's office, where no one else resorted, and there began their parley. Qian Baosheng, in his character of victor, spread out at the very outset every one of the "cards in his hand": he demanded that Tu Weiyue restore to their work the three of them — Xue Baozhu, Qian Qiaolin and Second Sister Zhou; he demanded that Gui Changlin be transferred away; he demanded further that thenceforth Tu Weiyue take on and dismiss no worker without his prior consent; he demanded that the mill's "secret fund" be handed over entirely to his own disposal; — and at the last he solemnly declared that all this was the will of the union.

"But Gui Changlin too is a committeeman of your union!"

said Tu Weiyue with a cold little smile, making no answer to those demands; his settled policy was to use this parley to spin out time, and give himself full leisure to prepare and to dispose his forces. The knots of flesh on Qian Baosheng's purple-flushed face bristled up at once; he pounded the table and roared:

"Committeeman, his mother's! True enough, Changlin's a committeeman of the union too — we humour him, we let him play at it! What the mother's use is he! There are five or six committeemen! What one man says by himself counts for no more than a fart! I speak for the whole body of them!"

"Baosheng, don't be hasty! Whatever's to be said, say it slowly — let's all talk it over!"

put in pockmarked Li, pressing down Qian Baosheng's fist where it pounded the table. Tu Weiyue, smiling composedly, turned the talk another way:

"Let it be! The affairs of your union you may settle among yourselves. Let us talk of the mill. The Third Master has fixed on the mill's opening today. We're all of one household, and must all lend a hand — first bring the workers to heel, first get the mill open. And besides, with the silk-mill women now on general strike all over Shanghai, the situation's a tight one; drag it out one more day and there may be great trouble. Your union, I dare say, would as little wish to see trouble break out? For if trouble really breaks out, you too must answer for it! Let us first talk over how to open every hall of the mill."

"Just so! We must first put this present disturbance to rights!"

Seeing that Qian Baosheng had nothing to say, pockmarked Li put in again to smooth things along. Tu Weiyue rolled his eyes, and quickly shifted the point at issue, saying coldly:

"Baosheng, your demands are none of them any great matter — all may be talked over. Only that game of yours this morning was a rash stroke, and it has roused the anger of the crowd. If the Third Master gets to know of it he'll surely take fire. I've forbidden them to go and report it to him. Let us first settle this business privately between ourselves. Let us agree here and now, to each other's faces, that that morning's game is never to be played again! For our own people to brawl sounds ugly in the telling, and it wrecks the reopening besides!"

"What! You're spreading lies!"

Qian Baosheng's face changed, and again he made to pound the table; yet behind that fierce show of voice and countenance there was plainly a touch of panic, a touch of shrinking. Tu Weiyue saw it at once, knew that his "diplomacy" had gained the upper hand, and pressed a step closer, cold as before:

"How am I the one spreading lies! Several of the mill's men were beaten — look, Old Li still carries the signboard of it on his nose!"

"That's because you first called in a crowd of men yourselves, and never gave me word of it; with many men and many hands about, it's likely enough a few blows were caught."

"We called men in to guard against the women hands' barring the mill —"

"My men too were guarding against the women's barring the mill! My men came to help!"

"You're simply denying it against all reason! There's A Xiang now to bear witness — it was you who began by beating the mill's own people! Our men were driving off the women who barred the gate, and you laid hold of our mill's people and set to brawling!"

"A Xiang talks arrant nonsense!"

Qian Baosheng shouted it, grinding his teeth, his temples all beaded with sweat the size of soya-beans. He paused a moment, then all at once shifted his tone too:

"The morning's business is over and done — what's the good of talking of it! Now I'll ask you plainly, in one word: my terms — will you grant them or no? One word and it's settled, and no shilly-shallying! The union's waiting for my answer!"

"But we must first have it agreed that that morning's game is never to be played again! It's not that I'm afraid — only that for our own people to brawl gives outsiders something to laugh at; and besides, once our own people fall to fighting, it's just so much gain for that pack of workers!"

"Then you're not to call in men either!"

"We called men in to guard against the women's making trouble! We couldn't but call them! Old Li, isn't that so?"

"Yes, yes! Baosheng, set your mind at rest — the men were all called in by me; how should they be squabbling with you!"

"There, isn't it so! How clear-headed is Old Li's word! Then it's agreed — there's to be no bringing about of what happened this morning again! Baosheng, be so good as to go first and give your men their orders — disperse them; and by and by when the Third Master comes, I'll lay your terms before him and we'll talk it over further."

Tu Weiyue seized the chance to press yet a step closer, and to bring out the second move of his time-spinning strategy. Pockmarked Li put in a word from the side to help it along:

"Baosheng, go first and tell them to make no more mischief, and set Mr. Tu's mind at rest too."

"There's no need to tell them! Without my word they'd never dare make mischief!"

said Qian Baosheng, thumping his chest. But the words were scarce out of his mouth when all at once, far off, there came the sound of shouting. Tu Weiyue's face changed, and he rose to his feet at once. At the same moment a rush of footsteps was heard outside the window, and a man came dashing in at the door — it was Mo Gancheng, who stammered out:

"There's — there's — there's trouble again!"

Tu Weiyue fixed Qian Baosheng with a look of deadly force, as if to say "There you are, making trouble again!" — then kicked over his chair and ran out as though he flew. Pockmarked Li too sprang up, his whole face flushed crimson, shot out a hand to seize Qian Baosheng, and, the spittle flying from his mouth, cursed aloud:

"Baosheng, this is beyond all bounds! Beyond all bounds!"

Qian Baosheng made no answer, his whole face iron-grey, and laid hold of pockmarked Li in his turn; the two of them, gripping each other, ran outside; and as he ran Qian Baosheng struggled out the words:

"Let's go and see! Let's go and see! — This pack of scoundrels of theirs deserves to die!"

The two of them, quick on their feet, had soon overtaken Tu Weiyue. From far off they saw a black mass of people outside the mill gate. The din of their shouting was louder than thunder. When the three of them charged straight up and saw it clearly, they cried out in dismay with one voice, and their faces went ashen on the instant! The most of these were women hands like maddened tigers! The three of them turned quickly to slip away, but it was already too late! The angry tide of women swept them off their feet and rolled them into the thick of the press! Along the road another crowd of women came shouting and flying, bearing down like a mountain, crushing in upon the thin line of defence within the gate. Overhead through all the air rang the slogans of these stormers:

"General strike! General strike!"

"To go to work is to be a running dog!"

"Stop your frames and come storming out!"

The thin line of defence within the gate gave back. The women storming the mill rolled forward like fire. They poured in through the narrow little iron wicket, and by main force wrenched open the great iron gate as well! All this was swift as lightning, mighty as an overturning of mountains and a tipping-out of seas! But of a sudden a body of men came charging from the flank and, like a pair of steel shears, sheared the ranks of women into two lengths. It was Gui Changlin, come up with a squad of police, neither too late nor too soon! The shrill of a whistle rose out of the shouting thunder. Bang! Bang! Shots to overawe them! Bang! Bang! Live rounds now! The thin defenders within the gate went over to the counter-attack. The women storming the mill had now no choice but to fall back. They forced back Gui Changlin's squad and made for the road.

"After them! Take them! See one, seize one!"

roared Gui Changlin. At the same moment the shrill, piercing note of whistles broke out on every side of the road; the police station near by had had its report, and had sent men hurrying up to make their catch in separate parties. Gui Changlin, with the same squad of police as before, drove straight at the mat-shed quarter, and left behind each broken bamboo door the terrible print of his claws. Twenty-odd he seized, and better than two hundred more he drove off to the mill to work!

Both Tu Weiyue and Qian Baosheng were hurt in the confusion. Qian Baosheng's shin had caught, into the bargain, a chance wound from those two "live rounds," and sacrificed a layer of skin. Yet for all that he could not but be thankful that Gui Changlin had come at just the right moment, and saved his life.

In Tu Weiyue's bedroom Gui Changlin said, in high delight:

"Three hundred and more workers are at work — listen to the sound of the reeling-frames! He Xiumei and Zhang Axin are taken too; and ten-odd more were caught along with them, by the way. No matter if they sit a few days wrongly in gaol! Their mother's, that pack of storming sluts — not one of them cares a rap for her life! And they're not even of our mill — the greater half are people from other mills! — But, Mr. Tu, how has your parley with Qian Baosheng gone?"

"Now it's we who've won! Changlin, telephone and tell the Third Master!"

said Tu Weiyue with a cool little smile; of a sudden he remembered there was still one man whose whereabouts he must ask after, but just then his wound gave another throb of pain, his face turned livid, cold sweat started at his temples, and he clenched his teeth and said nothing.

Thus was one powerful link in the general sympathetic strike of the silk filatures snapped through! By about seven in the evening, there came, together with the murky dusk, the imminent collapse of the whole general strike. The mat-shed quarter of the Yuhua women hands, under close watch, was now as still as the grave; let but a woman's livid-white face flash for an instant in the gloaming, or a low moan sound for once in the frozen-seeming air, and it would set the cordon of guards a-quiver, and then the bawling and the driving-off would break, for a while, that grave-like stillness!

Out of the deep shadow of this mat-shed quarter a black figure came creeping softly, sniffing, sniffing like a little dog that steals its food, — to sniff out the thin, weak point of the cordon. The starlight kept its watch on the deep-blue sky. The light breeze carried the startled crying of children in the sheds. A single blast of a whistle! The black figure, with slow but resolute movements, crossed the guard-line at last. Then its movements quickened a little. The stars in the sky kept watch, and saw the black figure run zigzagging into a filthy alley and tap softly three times on the back door of the last house of all. The door opened a crack, the black figure slipped in, and was gone within.

In the "front upper room" upstairs stood three broken bedsteads without mosquito-nets, but only a single square table. A fifteen-candlepower electric lamp showed, on the bed by the window, a woman lying, with another seated beside her, the two talking in low voices. The seated woman turned her head sharply and called out under her breath:

"Oh! Sister Yue'e, are you — are you all alone?"

"Xiumei and Axin have both been taken — didn't you know?"

"I did know! What I'm asking is about that one surnamed Zhu — Zhu Guiying, isn't it, the one newly joined — why hasn't she come?"

"There was no going to fetch her! I all but couldn't get out myself! The watch they keep is fierce indeed!"

said Chen Yue'e, shaking her head and spitting out a mouthful of spittle. She sat down by the square table, poured herself a cup of tea as she did so, and slowly drank. The woman on the bed patted her companion's shoulder and said:

"It's the same as on the Hongkou side. Ma Jin, this general strike has failed once again!"

Ma Jin made a resentful sound in her throat, but gave no answer; her pair of keen, spirited black eyes were fixed on Chen Yue'e's face. Chen Yue'e was plainly somewhat listless, or at the least bewildered, not knowing how the strait before them was to be broken open. Knowing that Ma Jin was watching her, she set down her teacup, turned her face and asked fretfully:

"What in the end is to be done! Tell me quickly!"

"When Old Ke comes, we'll open the meeting. — Cai Zhen, what o'clock is it? How is it Old Ke's still not here! Su Lun's nowhere to be seen either."

"It's twenty past seven! I can't wait much longer either. Over on the Hongkou side they're waiting for me to attend at half past eight! Ai!"

answered Cai Zhen, who lay on the bed; she gave her body a heavy heave, sat up, threw her arms round Ma Jin and softly bit her on the neck. Ma Jin, out of patience, tore herself free and said, cursing her with a laugh:

"What's this now! You erotomaniac! — But, Big Sister Yue, how is the 'fighting spirit' of the little sisters in your mill? Is it still good? The women hands here on the Zhabei side are all still staunch; this forenoon, when they heard that a part of your mill had gone to work, they stormed the mill of their own accord! Only let the little sisters in your mill hold firm, and the general strike can still be carried on. That you've now gone to work unconditionally is a wretched business! If this time we fail outright, we may give up all thought of the next!"

"This time isn't over yet, Ma Jin! My own proposal is that tonight we stake everything, stake everything on rousing them up, and storm the mill again tomorrow! Fight with our backs to the wall! Even if we fail, ours will be a glorious failure! — Ma Jin! I've thought it out point by point, and I come back after all to my first proposal: fear no sacrifice, and be ready for a glorious failure!"

Cai Zhen said, cutting in, and ran up to Chen Yue'e, flung her arms of a sudden about her and pressed cheek to cheek. Chen Yue'e blushed, twisted her body away, all abashed. Cai Zhen laughed wildly, hysterically, threw herself down again on the bed and shook with all her force, till the bed-frame rattled and creaked.

"Little Cai, be quiet a moment! … A glorious failure! Ai!"

Ma Jin scolded softly, sat down by the square table, and facing Chen Yue'e questioned her closely on how things stood at the mill. But they had exchanged only a few sentences when two men came running in, one behind the other. The one in front sat down with a slap at the edge of the square table, drew out an iron-cased watch and glanced at it, and gave his orders in haste:

"Half past seven! Quick! Quick! Ma Jin! Stop talking! Cai Zhen! Get up! There's not a scrap of urgency in you!"

"Old Ke! You've come late yourself! Quick! Ma Jin, Big Sister Yue! Half past eight, and I've still to get to Hongkou!"

said Cai Zhen, springing up and seating herself beside the newcomer, Ke Zuofu. He was a young man not yet thirty, a little taller even than Cai Zhen, with a pale, thin face of no particular mark, save that his two tightly-closed thin lips showed him a man of settled mind. The young man who had come with Ke Zuofu was a shade plumper, his eyes very quick, with a few tired wrinkles about their sockets; he grinned, smiling at Ma Jin, and sat down just below her shoulder.

The air in the front upper room grew tense. The yellow light of the fifteen-candlepower lamp swayed over their heads. Ke Zuofu spoke first to the plumper young man:

"Su Lun, your work has been very bad! At the mass meeting of silk-mill activists this afternoon, your leadership was mistaken! You could not grasp the revolutionary spirit of the masses, could not carry them from one struggle onward to another and ceaselessly widen the struggle; your leadership was tinged with Right deviation, you left all the work standing at its present stage — you made yourself the tail of the masses! The general strike of the filatures has now reached a grave juncture, and the first thing is to overcome this tailism! Ma Jin, report on the work in Zhabei!"

"Be quick, be brief — half past eight and I must go!"

Cai Zhen urged again, tapping the table with a pencil. So Ma Jin spoke for five minutes. Her manner was very composed, and she put forward a single main point: the repression was too fierce, more than half the progressive elements among the women hands were already lost, and the mass base was for the present comparatively weak. Ke Zuofu listened, and as he listened turned his eyes impatiently from time to time on Ma Jin, and then on the iron-cased watch in his hand; his two thin lips were pressed tighter than before.

"I'm against Ma Jin's conclusion! New progressive elements are forged in the struggle, and the mass base must be strengthened out of the struggle itself! That fearful frame of mind of Ma Jin's is just another manifestation of tailism!"

Cai Zhen said, breaking in, and shot a glance at Su Lun across from her. Cai Zhen now held out wholly for the "first proposal" in her own heart. For the featureless, unremarkable Ke Zuofu had at the outset denounced Right deviation, denounced tailism, and to Cai Zhen it seemed that Ke Zuofu was always and in everything right.

Ke Zuofu said nothing, and shut his lips tighter still; it was his way to draw the conclusions and give the orders last of all.

Su Lun, at whom Cai Zhen had shot her glance, was for his part in sympathy with Ma Jin's view. Naturally he would not own to any tailism of his own, and he said in a smooth, supple tone:

"What Cai Zhen speaks is theory, what Ma Jin speaks is fact. Nor ought we to slight the facts. Old Ke says that at this afternoon's meeting of activists I committed an error — well, I'll own it an error. But today's meeting of activists was rotten to the very root! Only half the people came, the work-reports were neither solid nor to the point, and the airing of opinions was in the wildest disorder. This lays bare well enough how poor is the ability of our lower cadres — they cannot lead at all! If I've committed the error of tailism, why then, the lower cadres one and all are tailism itself! And Cai Zhen and Ma Jin, who direct the strike movement at first hand, have made themselves the tail of the lower cadres too!"

"Why am I too a tail! —"

"No idle words! Come, decide the steps of the work at once! Big Sister Yue has something to say!"

Ma Jin cut short the wrangle between Cai Zhen and Su Lun, and drew Ke Zuofu's attention to Chen Yue'e. Ke Zuofu tilted his head a little, faced Chen Yue'e, and opened his eyes very wide.

"What in the end is to be done — tell me quickly! Two comrades of our mill have been arrested, and I'm the only one left! The little sisters — the little sisters went to work today, but they were driven to it by force! If only we have some good plan, we can still bring the mill out on strike again tomorrow! What in the end is to be done — tell me quickly!"

Chen Yue'e's look was full of anxiety, and full of excitement too; plainly the "terminology" of Ke Zuofu and Su Lun and the rest gave her much trouble, and she had many opinions but could find no fit words to set them forth. She felt that Ma Jin's words were very just — now that He Xiumei and Zhang Axin were both arrested, and she the only one left, was not their strength grown weak? Yet neither did she dare to fault Cai Zhen's words, for she dimly acknowledged those to be the scripture of revolution. With much labour she got her say out, and then fixed her anxious, longing eyes on Ke Zuofu's face.

Ke Zuofu's featureless thin face turned all at once severe. He looked once more at the iron-cased watch in his hand, and said with decision:

"You are to mobilize the whole body of you, press the work harder, raise the fighting spirit of the masses — no going to work tomorrow! The Yuhua mill above all must come out again tomorrow! Whatever the cost, overcome every difficulty and strike again tomorrow! To the masses put forward these slogans: Down with the capitalists' hiring of rowdies! Down with the arresting of workers!"

An instant's silence. Down in the hall the bamboo clapper of a wonton-pedlar's stand knocked out a few beats. The crying of a child in the house next door. The yellow light of the fifteen-candlepower lamp swayed over their heads. At last Ma Jin's composed voice rose once more:

"The core force in the Yuhua mill is very near wiped out, and the masses are under close watch; until they've been reorganized we cannot take the risk!"

"What! Reorganize, you say? This is the life-and-death pass of the general strike, and there's no time to let you reorganize at your leisure! This very night is your reorganizing, is your rousing of new fighting elements, is your opening of a fresh offensive!"

"One night is nowhere near enough! Our organization is utterly broken up, the enemy's watch is close — it would be a reckless gamble! Even if we forced it into motion, it would be crushed on the instant, and then the very shred of a base we've kept till now would be wiped clean out too!"

Ma Jin held firm, her black eyes flashing as she looked round at them all. Ke Zuofu had fallen silent, his thin lips pressed tight, and no less resolute for that. Things had reached a deadlock, when over on her side Cai Zhen of a sudden gave a cry, yet said nothing; the "second proposal" that had once drawn back in her heart now all at once broke out again to do battle with the "first proposal" she had chosen, and she bit her lip and smiled a bitter smile. Chen Yue'e opened her eyes wide in her anxiety. Su Lun came forward then to soften things:

"Ma Jin! What's your own proposal? Out with it!"

"My proposal is that the front of the general strike be shifted a little, and no harm done. The mills that can go on striking will of course struggle on with all their might; the few mills that have already suffered heavy losses cannot risk more, and must take breath a while! We'll go quickly and reorganize, and build the organization up; we'll husband our strength, and at the fitting moment we'll —"

Ma Jin had not yet done when Ke Zuofu sternly rebuked her:

"This proposal of yours is to liquidate the general strike outright! To flinch back in cowardice before the grave stage of the revolutionary high tide! This is a Right-deviationist view!"

"Just so! On the one hand to break the front of the general strike, and on the other to hope that the other mills can hold firm — this is a contradiction!"

Cai Zhen hastened to take up the word, and in her heart the "first proposal" was victorious once again.

Ma Jin's face flushed suddenly crimson, and still she held firm:

"How is it a contradiction? In fact it can be done! To go at it recklessly is to commit suicide!"

"If there were a good plan, our mill could come out again tomorrow. But our people are already few, and the masses are much afraid of the repression; if we set about it in the old way of the day before yesterday, it will never get off the ground! The one thing needful is a good new plan!"

Chen Yue'e, her eyes on Ma Jin, put in her word too; it was by a very great effort that she got her meaning into just this shape. But neither Ke Zuofu nor Cai Zhen gave any heed to her words. Su Lun, who was for Ma Jin and understood Chen Yue'e's meaning as well, once more came forward to soften things:

"What Big Sister Yue says is grounded on fact! When she asks for a good new plan, she means a shift of tactics — isn't that it, Big Sister Yue? I put forward a proposal: the organization in Yuhua has been broken, and in fact must be reorganized; one night is not time enough, so add another day, and come out on strike the day after tomorrow; then the front of the general strike can go on standing as before!"

"It won't do! If tomorrow the struggle isn't widened, the general strike is finished! If the Yuhua opens tomorrow, the whole mass of the workers will waver!"

Cai Zhen objected fiercely. Ma Jin too could be composed no longer, and said at once, sharp-edged:

"If it's to be put so, then plainly the hour for this general strike was never ripe! It's adventurism! It's a reckless gamble!"

Ke Zuofu's face changed at once; he slapped both hands down on the table and gave his order with decision:

"Ma Jin! You've carried your criticism up to the general line, and this Right-deviationist error of yours is a very grave one! The Party will resolutely sweep clean these Right-deviationist views! For the Yuhua mill not to come out tomorrow is to wreck the general strike, is to fail to carry out the general line! The Party will discipline it strictly!"

"But when in fact it does no more than deliver our comrades into the enemy's hands — what do you say to that?"

Still Ma Jin held firm, her face crimson but her lips gone white. Ke Zuofu gave an angry roar, and cried, pounding the table:

"I warn you, Ma Jin! The Party has an iron discipline! No one is permitted not to carry out an order! Go back at once with Big Sister Yue and set tomorrow's struggle going! Whatever the sacrifice, it must be done! This is an order!"

Ma Jin bowed her head and said no more. Ke Zuofu eyed her sternly, then turned his face to Cai Zhen and Su Lun:

"The work on the Hongkou side must be pressed harder, Cai Zhen! Carry out the order resolutely, and sweep clean every Right-deviationist view! Just now the 'Silk Union' passed several important resolutions on this struggle — Su Lun, do you tell them of it!"

Having said so much, Ke Zuofu looked again at the iron-cased watch in his hand, rose and went off first.

Those left in the front upper room were silent for a while. Cai Zhen stretched herself lazily, turned and flopped down again on the bed, so that the frame shook with a loud noise. Su Lun looked at the fifteen-candlepower lamp and smiled. Chen Yue'e gazed anxiously at Ma Jin. Out in the hall two people were quarrelling, and a stray dog yapped and snarled.

Ma Jin raised her head, smiled at Chen Yue'e, looked again at Cai Zhen on the bed, and called:

"Cai Zhen! We have our order now — whatever the sacrifice, it must be done! Let's divide up the work! It's late — look alive!"

"Oh, oh! At half past eight I've to be at Hongkou to attend! This is bad — it's nearly eight already!"

Cai Zhen, bawling as she went, sprang up, flung herself upon Ma Jin, dealt a slap in passing on the head of the drowsy-looking Su Lun, and shouted in Ma Jin's ear:

"Ma Jin! Ma Jin! There's a ball of something in my breast that feels ready to burst! A ball of something! Bursting out to burn up everything that's the enemy's! I want to find me an enemy and shoot him dead with a single shot! Feel my face — how hot it is! — But, Ma Jin, let's divide the work!"

Ma Jin, paying Cai Zhen no heed, squared her chest and said very gravely to Chen Yue'e:

"Big Sister Yue, do you go back first; find Zhu Guiying first, then the little sisters you're closest with; tell them that in Hongkou and Zhabei the little sisters of many mills have resolved not to go to work, and that if the Yuhua opens tomorrow they'll come and storm it; that everyone strikes together to help you, and that for you to be the first to go to work would be a rank betrayal of good faith! Hold out one or two days more, and the masters will give ground! — Big Sister Yue, throw yourself into rousing them, and harbour no thought of defeat! In half an hour more I'll come to you. Oh — it's eight now, half past eight at the very latest. Wait for me at home. And don't make a mess of it! Once we've met, we'll go together to the delegate conference of the General Strike Committee!"

"That's right! Get to that little inn at half past nine, not too early! I and the Hongkou delegates can't be there before half past nine ourselves!"

Cai Zhen put in hurriedly, then sprang away again, humming some song or other in high good humour.

"Good! It's all settled! There are still the delegates of several Zhabei mills, whom A Ying has gone to make contact with; they may get there a few minutes early, so let them wait over there! Big Sister Yue, off you go first! Cai Zhen, you can't dawdle any longer either! Mark it well! Half past nine, the delegate conference of the General Strike Committee! I'll wait here a little longer. If after another quarter of an hour A Ying still hasn't come, then she certainly isn't coming, and we'll get in touch with her at the conference and be done!"

"Not so fast, Cai Zhen! There are still the 'Silk Union's' resolutions for you to carry to the conference!"

said Su Lun hurriedly, fishing a sheet of paper out of his pocket. But Cai Zhen was in a great fret; she snatched the paper from him and glanced at it, then flung it back to Su Lun, and, catching hold of Chen Yue'e's hand, said:

"Writing like hens' scratchings — I can't make it out! Just tell Ma Jin and be done! — Big Sister Yue, come along! Ai, how I love you!"

Only Su Lun and Ma Jin were left in the room now. The explaining of the "resolutions" took up five or six minutes, and after it the two of them were for a while without words. Ma Jin paced slowly about the room, her face taut with hard thought. Then all at once she nodded to herself, and said low, as if talking to herself:

"Of course we must attack — but there's no doing without a rear either; I must find some way to keep the little base in Yuhua whole!"

Su Lun turned his eyes on Ma Jin's look of hard thought, then smiled, and, mimicking Ke Zuofu's tone, called softly:

"I warn you, Ma Jin! — Whatever the sacrifice, it must be done! This is an order!"

"Ai, you little clown! What imp are you playing at?"

Ma Jin halted, and scolded him softly with a laugh. But Su Lun's manner turned all at once grave again; he let out a hard breath and said in earnest:

"To speak plainly, I too often feel there's something wrong in charging ahead so recklessly, with no thought for before or behind. But what's to be done? The moment you open your mouth and put a contrary opinion, they curse you for Right opportunism, for Liquidationism; and there's the great high hat of an order to hold you down besides! Commandism!"

Ma Jin's quick, soft gaze came to rest on Su Lun's face, as though in warm sympathy with his words. Su Lun passed for a "theorist" of sorts, and was a first-rate talker; Ma Jin had always held him in a fair respect, and now, she scarce knew how, she suddenly felt Su Lun better than his wont — clear in the head, not forever speaking by "formula," often smiling shrewdly, and never given to horseplay; so that, over and above her everyday respect, a few degrees of warmer feeling were now added.

"How is it A Ying still hasn't come? It looks as though she's not coming after all!"

Ma Jin changed the subject, and went to lie down on the bed by the window, her face turned all the same towards Su Lun's side, gazing at him still, softly and in deep thought.

Su Lun followed to the foot of Ma Jin's bed, gazed at her without a blink, and then, smiling suddenly, said:

"A Ying certainly isn't coming! She's been busy of late with work on both sides!"

"What do you mean, work on both sides?"

Su Lun sat down on the edge of the bed, only grinning with his mouth wide. Ma Jin laughed too, and asked again:

"What are you laughing at?"

"Laughing that you don't understand what work on both sides means."

Ma Jin stirred her body on the bed, gave Su Lun an odd look, and said, as if carelessly:

"Don't spread tales!"

"Not a word of it's untrue! Hasn't she too grown thinner these last few days? Haven't you seen how Cai Zhen's grown thinner of late as well? The cause is one and the same. The demands of sex and the demands of revolution, both grown urgent at once!"

Ma Jin gave a laugh and shook her head, quite unpersuaded. Su Lun edged closer to Ma Jin's side and said again:

"Li the Eighth was all over the place looking for you again today!"

"That man's a nuisance!"

"He says he wants you transferred to his place to 'keep the station'! He's canvassing Old Ke to grant it him!"

"Hmph! That man's the dreariest creature alive!"

"Why don't you love him?"

Ma Jin laughed again and gave no answer. After a while Su Lun sighed softly once more and said:

"Little Huang turned her coat against me the moment she left Shanghai!"

Ma Jin laughed once more, gave her body a twist on the bed, looked at Su Lun's slightly plump face and asked, as if in jest:

"And so you've been a little down in the mouth of late?"

"Naturally one can't help feeling a little sad —"

Ma Jin laughed the harder, till she began to cough; she undid the button at her collar, and laughed and coughed together.

"One can't help feeling a little sad, Ma Jin, don't you say so? Though this business of love is nothing we hold to be of any great weight, still one can't help feeling a little sad! Even the loss of so many comrades of late — though they were sacrificed for the Cause, yet when I think of it, it always seems to me a very mournful thing!"

As he spoke Su Lun bowed his head, and Ma Jin laughed on as before.

"Ha, ha! Su Lun, you're no revolutionary — you've turned into a little girl!"

"Ai! Ma Jin! Sometimes I truly turn into a little girl, Ma Jin, Ma Jin! I need someone to comfort me, to hearten me; Ma Jin, will you? I need —"

Su Lun raised his head, and with one hand catching Ma Jin's hand, laid his own face against hers. Ma Jin did not stir, and laughed under her breath.

"Ma Jin! You're — you're like the head of a seven-centimetre shell!"

Ma Jin of a sudden flung herself over with a violent turn, pushed Su Lun off and sprang up, saying:

"It's late! I must go and find Big Sister Yue! —"

So saying, she pushed off Su Lun, who was feigning his way up against her again, and ran to the bed over by the wall, where she picked up a "worker's coat" and was on the point of putting it on; when Su Lun of a sudden darted a step forward and flung himself upon her, so violently that the two of them both fell on the bed. Ma Jin laughed, and cried out again and again:

"You savage thing! No — I have work to do!"

"What work! Devil a bit of work! Commandism! Adventurism! I've seen it through to the bottom!"

"Seen what through to the bottom?"

"Seen this through to the bottom: the work's a fart of work! The general line is a policy of suicide, the Soviets are Soviets on tour, and the Red Army a new kind of roving bandit horde! — But, Ma Jin, don't be so feudal about it …"

All at once Ma Jin gave an angry cry, thrust Su Lun off with all her force, opened her eyes round and glared at him in fury, sprang up and rebuked him sharply:

"Hmph! What talk is this! You've let your tail show! You breathe through one nostril with the Liquidationists!"

And with that Ma Jin ran downstairs like a gust of wind, out of the room, out of the hall.

The stars that filled the sky all kept their eyes on Ma Jin overhead. All along the way, Ma Jin thought of her quarrel with Ke Zuofu, thought of Su Lun's unseemly antics, and her heart was full of anger and hate together. But in a moment she thrust all these memories aside, and fixed her whole mind upon one thing only: her work, her mission. The mat-shed quarter drew near. Very carefully she crossed the guard-line, and came softly to the neighbourhood of the shed where Chen Yue'e lodged. Ahead there dimly showed the shadow of someone. Ma Jin grew warier still. She stood motionless in the dark, all ears, all eyes. The figure ahead, coming before Chen Yue'e's shed, likewise ceased to stir. The bamboo door creaked softly. Ma Jin understood, and with quick, light steps hurried up to the bamboo door, looked back once over her shoulder, and then slipped in.

Chen Yue'e and Zhu Guiying were both there. The kerosene lamp on the plank table gave only a flame the size of a soya-bean. In the dimness there was a snoring like thunder — that was Chen Yue'e's elder brother, a dock-worker. Ma Jin asked the two of them in a low voice:

"Have you made all your contacts?"

"We've made contact. It's well enough. — They all say that only let someone come to storm the mill, and they'll stop the frames and rally to the answer."

Ma Jin knit her brows. Outside there seemed to be some noise. The three of them started, and turned their ears to listen, but it was gone again. Ma Jin said softly:

"Then let's be off to the conference! Only I should still like to have a word with your little sisters. Whichever few are the good ones, take me to them!"

"It won't do! Things here are far too tight! The moment you stir abroad, there'll be someone shadowing you!"

said Chen Yue'e in a voice so thin it could scarcely be made out. But Ma Jin was stubborn, and would have them take her by all means. Zhu Guiying, tugging at the hem of Chen Yue'e's coat, said:

"Let me take her. I come and go, and no one's tailed me yet."

"It's only that you don't notice it yourself! Tu the Chamber-pot is a sharp one — as if he'd have forgotten you! Better send Little Sister along with her!"

As she spoke Chen Yue'e gave Ma Jin a push, bidding her look at a small little figure in the corner of the shed near the bamboo door. It was Jin Xiaomei; pricking up her ears at the word that she was to go along, her two eyes shone and sparkled with delight. Ma Jin gave a nod.

"Little Sister won't do either! This child's fond of chattering, and they've long been shadowing her too!"

Zhu Guiying objected again. Ma Jin, growing a little impatient, said:

"No more arguing — we'll all go! Guiying, you lead the way; I'll keep a good few paces behind you, and Big Sister Yue a good few paces behind me, following at my back. Whoever sees anyone shadowing us, let that one give the word first!"

No one objected further, and so they did according to the plan. The three of them had gone but a few steps out of Chen Yue'e's shed when they came to the home of one whom they took for a "progressive element"; Zhu Guiying went in first, and next, just as Ma Jin was edging her body up to the half-open bamboo door, there came of a sudden a shout out of the dark:

"What are you about!"

Behind them Chen Yue'e took fright, turned to flee, but was already caught by someone. Then a whistle set up its blowing. Pockmarked Li and Gui Changlin, with their men, groped their way into the sheds like a raging wind, and, asking no reason why, seized one wherever they saw one. In the mat-shed quarter there rose at once a terrible whirlpool. After some ten minutes the whirlpool too subsided, and the smiling-faced women overseers came upon the stage, warning from door to door those panic-stricken "little sisters":

"Don't fret yourselves for nothing! It's only Communists we're after! Go to work tomorrow and all will be peace and quiet, no trouble at all! Mr. Wu will give you all your due, soon or late!"
