### Catchphrases of the Brothel World

The technical terms of the brothel world have already been printed in the first volume of this book; what is set down here are the catchphrases in common use among them.

When a client gambles and lays on a banquet at a girl's house, the whole is called "getting up a spread"; having got up his spread, to reward the men and women servants with money is "footing"; the men and women servants themselves are the "hands"; to shift house at a festival is "turning the head." A client who does not gamble but only lays on a wine-party is throwing a "barefoot party." For a girl to make off with a client's money or goods is "lifting the small goods"; to dun the client for something over and above is "opening the hatchet" (those three words are really a bit of thieves' cant borrowed from the hoodlums' mouths). For a client to agree to a spread and then break the appointment at the last minute is "singing tanhuang." To demand handkerchief-money of a client at the festivals is "eating rice-balls." When a new client first comes to look about a yao-er house and all the girls of the house come out together for him to pick and choose, that is "moving the tea." In their cursing they will often bring in the two words jieshou; a client who goes to a flower-smoke room for his pleasure is "leaping the old tiger"; and a client who goes to a yao-er or a pheasant house to pass the night is taking a "night-box."
