### The Site of Lu Xun's Tomb

In the age of the Later Han and the Three Kingdoms, the Eastern Wu had in Lu Xun a valiant young general — the great man who, after Zhou Yu, held the supreme command of its forces by land and water alike; and yet, who would have thought that his grave should lie in Shanghai? Passing along Bubbling Well Road at the mouth of Mohawk Road, we caught sight, across the way, of two stone men standing up before the gate of the livery-stable at No. 340: within, they say, is the site of Lu Xun's tomb, and that pair of stone men are the guardian figures that once stood before it. But the grave is there no longer, having long since been made over into a stable; and whether his poor dry bones lie buried in the ground yet, or have already been shifted for reburial elsewhere, is a thing there is at present no way to make out.
