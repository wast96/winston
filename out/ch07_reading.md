### Chen Yangshan: A Life
Comrade Chen Yangshan — an outstanding member of the Chinese Communist Party, a tested and faithful communist fighter, a proletarian revolutionary, a distinguished leader of our country's political and legal front, former deputy procurator-general of the Supreme People's Procuratorate, adviser to it, and member of its consultative commission — died of illness in Beijing on February 22, 1991, at the age of eighty-five.
Chen Yangshan was born in Shangyu county, Zhejiang, on October 7, 1906. In June 1919, driven by his family's poverty, he went to Hankou to make his living. As a shop clerk there he came under the influence of the May Fourth movement, threw himself into the revolutionary campaign against imperialism and feudalism, organized a study circle of progressive young men, and set about learning and spreading Marxism. In October 1924 he joined the Socialist Youth League, serving as acting secretary of its Wuhan district committee and as distribution officer of its central leadership. In January 1925 he passed over into membership of the Chinese Communist Party, becoming propaganda chief of the Party's Fa'nan district committee in Shanghai and head of the distribution section of its Jiangsu provincial committee. In the summer of 1925, under the leadership of Ren Bishi and Yun Daiying, he helped organize Shanghai's May Thirtieth movement. In the spring of 1926, acting on the Center's instructions, he helped lead the strike of the printing workers of Shanghai's Zhabei district.
After the Great Revolution failed, in the fierce White Terror of the years 1928 to 1931, Chen Yangshan worked under Zhou Enlai and Chen Yun to help build our Party's intelligence and security apparatus. Fearless of danger and of death, he fought the Kuomintang reactionaries with courage and cunning, and did outstanding service in shielding the Party's organization in Shanghai and protecting the Party Center and its senior leaders. From June 1931 to June 1940, sent out by the Party, he carried on intelligence and united-front work in turn at Shanghai, Tianjin, Chongqing, Chengdu, and Xi'an, laboring hard and to marked effect against civil war, for the movement of national salvation against Japan, and for the building of the anti-Japanese national united front.
In June 1940 Chen Yangshan was recalled to Yan'an, where he served as head of the local-work bureau of the Central Department of Social Affairs, head of its intelligence training course, branch secretary at the Central Party School, and chief of its cadre section. He attended the Party's Seventh National Congress.
From early 1945 until the founding of the People's Republic, Chen Yangshan helped lead the political and legal work of the Jin-Sui Liberated Area, holding in turn the posts of director of the investigation bureau of the Party's Jin-Sui Sub-bureau, deputy director and then director of the Jin-Sui Border Region's General Public Security Bureau, member of the Party's Northwest Bureau, head of its social-affairs department, chief of the public security department of the Shaanxi-Gansu-Ningxia Border Region, and director of the Xi'an municipal public security bureau. In these years he carried through the general line of winning nationwide victory laid down at the Seventh Congress, led the body of political-legal cadres, boldly roused the masses, waged the struggle against the enemy, and helped direct the takeover of the large and middling cities; he did marked work in wiping out bandits and enemy agents, guarding the border region, and building and consolidating revolutionary power, and gathered a rich store of experience.
After the founding of New China, Chen Yangshan served in turn as a member of the Party's Shanghai municipal committee and deputy director of the city's public security bureau, and then as a standing member of the Party's Nanjing municipal committee, deputy head of its political-legal commission, and director of the city's public security bureau and chief procurator of its procuratorate. He did effective work to safeguard the newborn people's power, consolidate the people's democratic dictatorship, and win the struggles of the Campaign to Suppress Counterrevolutionaries and the Three-Antis and Five-Antis, and was commended for it by Chairman Mao. From June 1952 to the end of 1958 Chen Yangshan gave all his energy to the building of New China's system of justice, serving in turn as deputy head of the North China Political-Legal Commission, director of the North China public security bureau, vice-minister of justice of the Central People's Government, and deputy secretary of the ministry's Party group. He carried out the Center's directives on political-legal work, held to the policy of joining specialist work with the mass line, went deep into the actual conditions, investigated and studied, drew lessons from experience, strengthened guidance, and waged a firm struggle against every kind of unlawful and criminal activity that harmed public order and against the "Left" errors of thought and conduct that damaged the legal system, making a valuable contribution to the building of a socialist rule of law.
In 1958, under the influence of "Left" error, Chen Yangshan was wrongly criticized and disciplined. Not until after the Party's Third Plenary Session of the Eleventh Central Committee, in 1978, was he fully rehabilitated.
From February 1966 to July 1978, Chen Yangshan served as a vice-chairman of the Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region, deputy head of its revolutionary committee, and a standing member of the regional Party committee, taking charge of cultural and educational work; he played an important part in advancing the region's culture and education. Through the ten years of turmoil, though he was himself hard hit, he held to the principle of Party spirit and firmly resisted the interference and wrecking of Lin Biao and the "Gang of Four."
After the Party's Third Plenary Session of the Eleventh Central Committee, Chen Yangshan served in turn as deputy procurator-general of the Supreme People's Procuratorate, member of its Party group, adviser, and member of its consultative commission. Heedless of his advancing years, he threw himself with great zeal into the work of restoring and rebuilding the procuratorial organs. Together with the comrades of the Supreme Procuratorate's Party group he summed up the historical experience of procuratorial work since the founding of the Republic, thoroughly repudiated the slanders and damage that Lin Biao and the "Gang of Four" had done to political-legal work, cleared away the interference and influence of "Left" thinking on procuratorial work, and, in the spirit of the Third Plenum, worked out the policy and tasks of procuratorial work for the new era and looked into and settled the problems of staffing and professional development that faced the rebuilt organs, doing outstanding service for the recovery and growth of the procuratorial cause.
Chen Yangshan was elected a delegate to the Seventh and Eighth National Congresses of the Chinese Communist Party, a deputy to the Fourth and Fifth National People's Congresses, and a standing member of the Fifth National Committee of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference.
Chen Yangshan held a lofty communist ideal and a firm revolutionary conviction. He fought for sixty-seven years for the revolution and the socialist cause of the Chinese people. Through the First and Second Revolutionary Civil Wars, the War of Resistance, and the War of Liberation, whether under the White Terror or in the conditions of war, however dangerous the situation and however hard the circumstances, he held to the struggle bravely and doggedly and carried out with distinction the tasks the Party gave him. Under blows and persecution he never lost his firm faith in the Party and in the communist cause. He bore his disgrace and worked on for the Party conscientiously, and he poured his heart's blood into collecting and editing more than a hundred thousand words of Party-history material, showing the broad mind of a Communist and a boundless loyalty to the Party. After the Third Plenum of the Eleventh Central Committee he wholeheartedly upheld the Party's basic line of "one central task and two basic points" and its policy for the leadership of political-legal work, acted strictly by the law, held to the Four Cardinal Principles, opposed bourgeois liberalization, and kept in close accord with the Party Center in thought and in politics.
Chen Yangshan had the noble character and revolutionary temper of a Communist, and a strong sense of dedication and duty to the cause. He was open and aboveboard, held to principle, dealt fairly, and was ready to practice criticism and self-criticism; he kept the Party's discipline, accepted without condition whatever the organization assigned him, and guarded the Party's secrets closely. He kept the whole in view, grasped what mattered, could take a high post or a low one, bore hardship and grievance without complaint, and never weighed his own fame or gain; he united his comrades, kept close to the masses, was easy to approach, cared for and looked after his cadres, was strict with himself, honest in office, plain and frugal, and so won the love and respect of cadres and masses alike. He studied hard and thought hard, and in old age still read the theory of Marx and Lenin and the Central documents tirelessly and wrote articles and memoirs. Across his long revolutionary life he worked, for decade after decade, with the same diligence and care, kept to the facts, let nothing slip, and gave himself to the last, to the very end of his strength.
Chen Yangshan's was a life of revolution, of struggle, of whole-hearted service to the people. His death cost our Party a veteran of signal merit and took from our country's political and legal front an elder and a distinguished leader. His fine character, his high and unbending honor, and his revolutionary achievements will be remembered forever in our hearts. Let us turn our grief into strength, take Chen Yangshan as our shining example, carry through the spirit of the Party's Seventh Plenary Session of the Thirteenth Central Committee, and under the Party Center with Comrade Jiang Zemin at its core draw more closely together and strive on to build socialism with Chinese characteristics and to carry to victory the great aims of the Ten-Year Program and the Eighth Five-Year Plan.
Chen Yangshan lives forever in our hearts.
