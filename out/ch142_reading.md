### Selling Calligraphy and Paintings

On the wall of the Sanshan Guild Hall, at the west end of Fuzhou Road, there used of an evening to be a seller of scrolls and paintings, who would hang the whole place full of banner-scrolls and paired couplets—writing and painting both, some by living hands, some the surviving brushwork of the old masters, a dazzle of colours to bewilder the eye—and his prices were very cheap and his "padding" very thick. These fellows would never deal by day, but always waited for the dusk to open shop. And what was the reason for that? Their calligraphy and paintings, it is said, were every one of them counterfeit and forged, not easily to be got rid of in broad daylight, and so they had to come out and do their trade only under the dim of nightfall. Nowadays this scroll-stall is no longer to be seen.

The stationery-and-fan shops all deal in calligraphy and painting on the side, each piece with its price marked plain, for the customer to pick and choose.
