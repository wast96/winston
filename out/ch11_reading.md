## Chapter Eleven

At nine in the morning, all along the Bund, a gale was howling. In the night, when the tide came up the Huangpu, the water had ridden the wind's strength and climbed clear onto the wharves. Though it had ebbed by now, the waves out on the Huangpu still ran with noise and force. High on the meteorological tower that soared into the clouds at the corner of Avenue Edward VII, several black balls had been hoisted, high aloft.

This was the first assault of the year by the storms that came to call on Shanghai several times each summer!

A tram coming from the west to the Nanjing Road corner was struggling head-on against the contrary wind; every window in it kept juddering and rattling, as if in terror. At last the tram stopped at the halt before the door of the Cathay Hotel, and the first to step down was a young, modish woman who, as though swept along by the great wind, made straight across the road and sprang up the stone steps before the Cathay's door — and just then a handsome young man in a Western suit, a walking-stick hooked over the crook of his arm, came hurrying out from within. The gale caught up the lower hem of the woman's cheongsam, slit very high, and wrapped it round that stick; with a ripping sound, a rent tore open in the light silk gauze of the gown.

"Pig!" the woman swore under her breath; and, turning her waist to look back, she at once broke into a smile, for she knew the man. It was the broker Han Mengxiang. And the woman was Liu Yuying, the widowed daughter-in-law of Han Mengxiang's colleague Lu Kuangshi — a young married woman of the Western-beauty type!

"So early! Crawling straight out of a warm quilt into a wind like this — that's no joke!"

Han Mengxiang said it with a smile, narrowing his eyes, and drew himself aside to the edge of the semicircular steps. Liu Yuying followed him a step, put on an angry face and glared at Han Mengxiang, then all at once smiled again and said softly:

"Don't be fresh! Here, Mengxiang — I can't remember for certain which room number is Old Zhao's here."

The wind caught up the hem of Liu Yuying's cheongsam again and wound it round Han Mengxiang's legs. The wind turned her long hair about too, till it fell across her brows and eyes.

Han Mengxiang seemed to give a grunt, and put up a hand to hold down the Panama hat on his head. After a moment, as though he had just got his breath back, he said:

"What a wind! — It's a rising wind! Yuying, won't you buy in ten or twenty thousand on this 'rising wind' of ours?"

"I've no money — but come, tell me quickly: which number?"

"Do you really mean to look for him? The number's Room Four, as it happens —"

Another, fiercer gust swept full into his face, and Han Mengxiang hastily turned his head away, so that his sentence was never finished. Liu Yuying murmured a "Thank you," swept her hair back, swayed her waist, and ran on into the Cathay. Han Mengxiang turned to watch her retreating figure with a smile, then walked slowly to the street corner opposite and stood there reading the advertisement board of the North China Daily News.

"Reds threaten Hankow, reported!" — such was the startling headline set in the top line of that board. Han Mengxiang gave a careless shrug of his shoulders, looked back once more at the great door of the Cathay, and just caught sight of Liu Yuying coming out again, her face all discontent, standing on the stone steps and gazing about her on every side. She seemed to have seen Han Mengxiang too, when a tram suddenly ran up and cut the two of them off from each other. By the time the tram had passed, Liu Yuying had run over to Han Mengxiang and, stamping her foot, said:

"A fine one you are, Han Mengxiang!"

"And whose fault is it, being in such a hurry — running off before a man's had his say?"

Han Mengxiang answered with a sly smile, gave his stick a flourish, and set off south along the cement pavement, deliberately slowing his pace. Liu Yuying was in no hurry now; she followed a few steps behind Han Mengxiang, then caught up and walked shoulder to shoulder with him, but said nothing. She reckoned that Han Mengxiang must surely know Old Zhao's new place, and she meant to use a little art to dig the truth out of this wily young fellow's heart. The wind was really too fierce, damp and cold besides, and Liu Yuying's clothes were too thin; slowly she edged in close against Han Mengxiang. The wind played with her long hair, which pricked, downy and soft, at the roots of Han Mengxiang's ears, and in that hair there was a rich, oily fragrance.

"Can it be he never went to the Majestic?"

As they neared the front of the Customs House, Han Mengxiang said it with his head cocked to one side, and his left leg brushed against Liu Yuying's right.

"I waited till daybreak and never saw a shadow of him —"

Liu Yuying answered with a shake of her head, but a gust came straight down on her, and she choked on her breath and could say no more. She twisted her waist, turned her back to the wind, and let it blow the hem of her cheongsam high up, baring a pair of naked white legs. Biting her lip, she gave a smile, and with a sidelong glance at Han Mengxiang said venomously:

"This cursed, damnable wind!"

"But I tell you it's a 'rising wind'! The rising wind Old Zhao likes best of all!"

"Come then, tell me — where did Old Zhao stay last night? I shan't forget your kindness!"

"Hee, hee! Yuying, I'll tell you what: once I've found out, later, let's fix a place to meet —"

"Pah! —"

"Oh — oh, that was one word too many from me; you're an old hand, we understand each other without saying it, don't we!"

"Then out with it, quick!"

Liu Yuying's eyes rolled, and she smiled with great seductiveness. Han Mengxiang gazed hesitantly at the sky. White clouds flew past, patch upon patch, very fast. All at once he threw out his chest, as though he had made up his mind, and murmured a sentence into Liu Yuying's ear — and instantly her face changed colour, and her eyes glittered as though something were burning in them. She bared her white teeth in a dry laugh, and those even teeth looked as if they could bite. Han Mengxiang could not help a shiver: he had truly never guessed that a woman whose skin was as white and tender as cream could be so terrible when she was angry! But Liu Yuying's face at once turned faintly red again, and she pursed her lips and smiled at Han Mengxiang. Another gust struck fiercely, and, as though she could not keep her feet, Liu Yuying swayed to one side, took hold of Han Mengxiang's arm, and, making the most of it, said:

"Thank you. But I still want to find him."

"Take my advice and spare yourself the trouble! Don't fret — wait till he wants you and comes looking for you! I know Old Zhao has a vile temper; when he's not in the mood for a person he simply won't have anything to do with you! There's only the one exception, Xu Manli — Old Zhao doesn't dare ignore her!"

Han Mengxiang said it very earnestly, and, taking Liu Yuying's arm, walked on with her in step.

The wind blew fiercer still. Its howling roar drowned out every clamour of the city. The whole sky was full of grey-white cloud-heads racing, racing on like galloping horses! Moment by moment the wind grew damper and colder. Yet Liu Yuying still felt that what blew against her was not cool enough, for her thoughts were racing faster even than those cloud-heads in the sky. As they neared the corner of San Malu, she suddenly stopped, slipped her right hand free from the crook of Han Mengxiang's arm, stepped back, gave him a most bewitching smile, blew him a kiss besides, and, turning, sprang up into a rickshaw. Han Mengxiang stood stock-still, staring after her in a daze.

"I'll telephone you later!"

The wind carried back this one sentence of Liu Yuying's, and a ringing peal of laughter.

Half an hour later, Liu Yuying was already pursuing her venture in a five-storey "mansion" on Avenue Joffre. She handed a slip of paper on which were written the three characters "Xu Manli" to a "boy," and followed him to the door of the room, going over once more in her mind the three schemes she had prepared for dealing with Old Zhao.

The door opened. Liu Yuying slipped in all smiles, and gave a sudden start: for the person with Zhao Botao was no woman at all, but the old fellow Shang Zhongli! At once she felt that all three of her prepared schemes would hardly serve. Zhao Botao's face too changed abruptly; he sprang up and shouted harshly:

"So it's you? Who told you to come?"

"Why, it was Xu Manli who sent me!"

In her haste this was the only line Liu Yuying could hit upon. She felt her venture would fail today. But she had not forgotten a woman's "weapons": she smiled brightly, gave the old fellow Shang his greeting, and sat down in a chair by the window. The wind came in through the window-opening and beat hard against her head, but she did not feel it; she watched Zhao Botao's expression carefully, steadied her nerves, and laid new plans.

"Nonsense! Even if Xu Manli had traffic with the immortals, she couldn't know at once that I was here! It must be that young rascal Han Mengxiang you've tricked!"

Zhao Botao shrugged his shoulders and, with a cold laugh, in one breath laid bare Liu Yuying's secret. Liu Yuying could not keep her heart from pounding; but she guessed at once that Old Zhao could not have met Xu Manli these last few days, so her lie would not be exposed for a while. And she had a genius for lying, too: on the strength of what Han Mengxiang had told her of the relations between Old Zhao and Xu Manli, and of the various tales about Xu Manli she had picked up in the ordinary way, she at once set to composing a whole string of lies in her mind. She stopped smiling, and put on an angry air as well.

"It's the dog biting Lü Dongbin, truly! I came here of my own accord, yes, but this place of yours I heard of straight from Xu Manli's own mouth. Last night at the Majestic, when I waited and you never came, I grew so bored that I went into the dance hall to have a look. I know Xu Manli. But she doesn't know me. She was muttering away with a man for the longest time. I chanced to listen in — anyone else would surely not have understood whom they were talking of, but I understood the moment I heard it. She, she —"

Liu Yuying paused, unable to decide how best to put it. Just then a gust of wind turned her hair over and buried her very eyes; seizing the chance, she stood up and shut the window, and so managed to cover over her hesitation.

"Did she say I was staying here?"

Zhao Botao asked impatiently.

"Mm, she told the man you were staying here, that you had some new game on —"

"Heh, heh! Do you know the man? What sort was he?"

Zhao Botao cut Liu Yuying short, his eyes opened very wide. From that look Liu Yuying saw that Old Zhao not only wanted to know who the man was, but was already guessing who it must be. This she had not foreseen. For the second time she could not keep her heart from pounding. She knitted her brows, gave her neck a twist, and then suddenly broke into a laugh, saying:

"Ah, it must be someone you know well! Not so very tall, and no beauty in the face either — I seem to have seen him somewhere."

Zhao Botao's face changed all at once. He gave the old fellow Shang a meaning look. The old fellow Shang stroked his beard and smiled.

But Liu Yuying felt her whole body turn suddenly hot and parched. She stood up and opened once more the pair of windows beside her, and stood full in the window. A gust blew straight into her face, carrying in with it a small leaf from a tree. The trees along the roadside were all swaying there as though drunk; even here the wind had force enough!

"It must be Wu the Third! Xu Manli has got herself mixed up with him — how tiresome!"

Zhao Botao said it softly, his eyes on Shang Zhongli, and in his agitation slapped a palm down on the arm of the sofa. "Wu the Third?" Liu Yuying knew now who was meant. Him she really had seen. And she recalled, too, that her father-in-law Lu Kuangshi had lately spoken once of some faction or party of Wu the Third's, and that Han Mengxiang had let slip a word as well: that Old Zhao and Old Wu had fallen out. Her heart leapt with joy, and she nearly laughed aloud. This lie she had trumped up on the spur of the moment fitted so well that she felt surer of herself than ever. She resolved to push her monstrous, sky-covering lie a little further. She had the nerve for lying!

"I foresaw this move long ago; that's why I advised you last time to be patient and win Manli over."

Shang Zhongli said this softly too, slowly smoothing his beard, and ran his eye over Liu Yuying once again. Zhao Botao turned his face round and asked, coldly once more:

"What else did they say?"

"Some of it I couldn't quite make out, and so I've forgotten it; it seems they were talking of how things stood at the Exchange. But I did catch the word 'gun' — mm, it was as though they said someone deserved to eat a pistol, and I saw the man pull a fierce face and make a gesture —"

Liu Yuying had brought out one part of her prepared lies first, and was very pleased with herself; but to her surprise Zhao Botao suddenly threw back his head in a great laugh, and Shang Zhongli too narrowed his old eyes at Liu Yuying and shook his head. Was this disbelief? Liu Yuying's heart gave another jump. Zhao Botao's laughter ceased, his face turned all gravity, and, springing up abruptly, he gave Liu Yuying a hard slap on the shoulder and said loudly:

"You've a real heart in you! But we don't want to hear any more! There's someone through there whom you know — go and keep her company a while!"

As he spoke, Zhao Botao pointed to a door on the left, seized Liu Yuying by the arm, pushed her straight through, and shut the door behind her.

This was an elegant, refined bedroom, with a pair of French windows giving onto a balcony. A large bed took up the middle of the room, one end toward the window, the other toward the wall. On the bed lay a woman, her face to the wall, wearing nothing but a suit of white silk night-clothes. Liu Yuying looked, and stood there in a daze. From Old Zhao's sudden burst of laughter to his forcing her into this room, all this string of strange happenings — whether it boded good fortune or ill she could not, in her haste, make out at all! She turned her ear and listened hard for the two of them in the outer room. Not a sound! She stole a glance through the keyhole in the door: the old fellow Shang was smoothing his beard, and Old Zhao was smoking a cigar.

One leaf of the French windows opening onto the balcony stood open, and the wind blew in by fits and starts, tight then slack, like a bout of malaria. The woman's loose night-clothes on the bed were now and again puffed up by it, like a half-transparent soft shell, and her freshly waved long hair fluttered too against the pillow. Yet the woman slept on soundly. Liu Yuying steadied herself, and, tiptoeing to the head of the bed to look, all but cried out aloud. It was none other than her good friend Feng Meiqing! So it was this girl of sixteen or seventeen who had made her, Liu Yuying, keep watch a whole night for nothing at the Majestic! True, Liu Yuying was in the habit of thinking that, so long as she could still rake in her money as before, Old Zhao might have ten thousand mistresses and it would be no concern of hers. But now, for all that, she could not help a sour twinge in her heart, and had half a mind to wake Feng Meiqing and ask her what all this meant — and just at that moment Feng Meiqing woke. She rubbed her eyes, turned over, and lazily stuck up her two legs. She let her night-clothes slide down to her waist and, without a trace of shame, bared her thighs.

Liu Yuying laughed to herself, slipped aside, and hid on the balcony outside the window. She had meant to play a joke on Feng Meiqing, by way of a small revenge; but all at once a few sentences came floating to her ears, in Zhao Botao's voice:

"You're quite right there! This 'gun' they spoke of must mean that lot of arms. Damn it all! That day, as ill luck would have it, Xu Manli was still hanging about at my place and hadn't gone, when that Jiamen came. It was a careless slip of mine — I never reckoned that Xu Manli knew a few words of English. ..."

"Women are a curse from the first. And you're far too fond of your pleasures — two of them at hand right now!"

This was the old fellow Shang's voice. Hearing it, Liu Yuying cursed him in her heart: "Old wretch who won't die! You that deserve a thousand cuts!" Then she heard Zhao Botao laugh loudly.

"It seems that Jiamen isn't to be trusted either. Maybe he's playing both sides for his own gain. He took fifty thousand dollars from us in lobbying-money, and then went and dropped hints over at Wu Sunfu's place."

"Damn it all! But, Old Zhong, that fifty thousand's no great matter; we've ways of clawing it back. It's our credit that counts most! If this business fails, folk won't be able to trust us in anything else hereafter! We must find some way, whatever it takes, to keep that lot of arms from falling into their hands!"

"Suppose we go back to the original middleman and open negotiations again — how would that do? ..."

Suddenly the plane tree close by the balcony gave a rustling sound, and it disturbed the sound-waves of the two men's talk within. The wind, which had eased off considerably for some while, abruptly turned strong again. Liu Yuying happened to be facing east, and the wind, blowing full in her face, kept her from opening her eyes. Bang! The French window on the balcony shut of itself. Liu Yuying got a fright. At once the long window swung open of itself again, and Liu Yuying saw Feng Meiqing lift her head, her eyes opened wide in astonishment. The two women's glances met for an instant and then parted; Feng Meiqing's face reddened, but Liu Yuying only smiled and bit her lip.

"How is it that you're here too, Yuying!"

Feng Meiqing said it in embarrassment, and climbed down from the bed, giving her night-clothes a shake. She came out onto the balcony. The wind teased her loose night-clothes, now puffing them out fat, now curling them back to bare her plump white bottom. Liu Yuying giggled and said:

"Mei! There are people down on the road looking at you!"

"Where's the big fellow? — Ugh, this hateful wind! It's going to rain. Yuying, have you been to my house? How did you come to be here?"

Feng Meiqing held her night-clothes together with one hand and rattled on in a muddled, disjointed way, her eyes stealing all the while to Liu Yuying's face. That look was a complicated one: loathing, alarm and doubt, shame, jealousy — everything was in it. But Liu Yuying minded none of it. Her whole heart was set on eavesdropping on the talk of the two men within. Those few sentences she had picked up just now, by chance, had roused her curiosity, and had made her realize with a start why Old Zhao dared not ignore Xu Manli.

"What a hateful wind, truly!"

Liu Yuying knitted her brows, seeming to speak to herself, and did not answer Feng Meiqing's string of questions; she pricked up her ears and listened again, but could catch only a few blurred words that would not piece together into any sense. The wind confounded every sound; perhaps it had blown the two men's talk away to some other place! Liu Yuying sighed in disappointment.

"Yuying, who are you angry with? I've done nothing to offend you —"

Feng Meiqing could bear it no longer; her face went livid, and her eyes looked as though they could pin a person dead. This Liu Yuying had not foreseen, and a scorching hot ball of temper rose from her own heart and rushed up to the roots of her ears. But on second thought she mastered her temper, gently took Feng Meiqing's hand, and said with a smile:

"Tut, tut! Only a few days apart, and you've become a new person already — and grander in your manner too by far! You're not what you were, as anyone can see. Today I came to wish you joy — how should I dare be angry!"

Hearing those last two sentences, Feng Meiqing flushed crimson; she gave a sudden start, wrenched her hand free with force, and, without half a word, turned and ran back into the room, throwing herself down on the bed. Liu Yuying smiled with satisfaction and was on the point of going in herself when all at once Zhao Botao's voice came again, very loud and urgent, full of a strong note of optimism and self-assurance:

"You'll see: the bigger the show Wu Sunfu puts on, the more troubles he'll have! When Chinese run industry without foreign help, it's always a tiger's head and a snake's tail. And now he wants to play the bonds too — hah! This past month he was a 'bear' first, and then, seeing that nothing came of Changsha, he turned 'bull,' and now he has some six or seven million in hand. But I'd guess he's surely sold off a good deal on next month's futures. He reckons the Shanxi army will move and there'll be a great battle on the Tianjin–Pukou line, at the very earliest around the tenth of next month. Ha, ha! Wu Sunfu can calculate — the pity is that there's still Zhao Botao here to play a little joke on him on purpose, to grab him by the leg just when he's climbed halfway up!"

Then there was silence for a while. After that came a rapid question and answer, the two men's voices mingling into one, so that the words could not be made out. Liu Yuying stood lost in a daze, not quite understanding how Old Zhao would "grab" Wu Sunfu by the "leg"; and in any case these matters did not interest her — all she hoped was to hear something more of Xu Manli's tricks. But Feng Meiqing on the bed within was watching Liu Yuying with venomous eyes, biting the corner of her handkerchief between her teeth and breathing hard. Liu Yuying smiled, and, as if in deliberate pique, turned round with her back to Meiqing. Just then she heard Shang Zhongli's voice again:

"So you mean to fight it out with them, come what may ... How much do you plan to throw out?"

"That there's no telling. As it rises I'll throw out, pressing on till Wu the Third's whole game collapses and the Yizhong Company goes under! And there's another point, Zhongli: it's been said this long while that the northern section of the Tianjin–Pukou line is to be given up on strategic grounds — it's only a question of sooner or later. Today is the seventeenth; there are some ten days yet to this month's settlement; and if, when that time comes, we really can't win, and Wu the Third stands to gain, we can still turn round the old trick of the end of last month and use it once more — can't we? —"

Then came a burst of laughter, and the laughter grew louder and nearer, until suddenly Zhao Botao's head poked out at the window over there — luckily, though, he was looking down at the road below. Liu Yuying started all over, drew back into the room quick as lightning, and with another spring settled herself down beside Feng Meiqing, a hand pressed to her breast.

Feng Meiqing, full of resentment, thrust out her two legs, turned over on the bed and rolled a good foot or more away, as though Liu Yuying had thorns on her.

"Look at this childish fit of yours! Why, whatever is the matter? We're the best of sisters — whatever's in the belly, let it out on the lips!"

Liu Yuying, having recovered herself, said it with a smile, her eyes on Feng Meiqing's turned back, while in her heart she turned over and over the words she had just overheard. She knew well enough the reason for Feng Meiqing's sulks, but she had no idle mood at all to spare on such nameless jealousy. Because her own "venture" had met with unlooked-for success, she was setting her whole mind on how she too might become "a second Xu Manli," and thought to "hold" Old Zhao wholly in her grip, more cunningly even than Xu Manli did. So thinking, she reached out and turned Feng Meiqing's body round, saying aloud once more:

"Little sister, you must believe me! Mei! I did not come today, first, to pick a quarrel with you, nor, second, to have words with Old Zhao. I only came in on my way past to see you. You surely ought to know my nature: ever since he died, I have lost heart in everything; now I live for the pleasure of each day as it comes; I contend with no one for anything! We're the best of sisters — my one wish is to lend you a hand; how is it you suspect me of coming to cut the ground from under you?"

"Then tell me honestly — was it the big fellow who told you to come?"

"No! I have some other business of my own."

Liu Yuying answered off-hand with a smile, while in her heart she was reckoning whether to leave now, or to watch for a chance to spin Old Zhao a few more lies to his face.

"Is the big fellow in the outer room?"

Feng Meiqing smiled too, fixed her gaze on Liu Yuying's face, and waited for an answer, with a look so childish it made one want to laugh.

"There's a guest in there. — Can it be you don't know?"

Liu Yuying said it softly, leaning her face against Feng Meiqing's shoulder, the question in her heart still undecided. Feng Meiqing shook her head, said nothing, and pursed her lips in a languid smile. Now that her breast-full of jealousy had dispersed, she gradually felt her head grow heavy and her body limp. She had truly overtaxed herself a little in the night.

A silence for a time. Only the wind outside the window went on with its long, howling shriek.

"Mei! I'll be going now. The big fellow has a guest! Tomorrow I'll take you to the pictures."

So saying, Liu Yuying opened the door and sprang out. Her mind was made up! But, quite unexpectedly, there sat only the old fellow Shang alone, a cigar in his mouth, lost in thought. The two of them looked at each other, and Shang Zhongli, as though he could take her or leave her, stroked his beard and smiled. At once Liu Yuying changed her mind again. She shot a glance at Shang Zhongli, jerked a thumb back at the bedroom door, and, giggling with a coquettish, alluring laugh, went out.

When she reached the road, she ran into a shop to borrow their telephone and call a motor-car. She meant to go and find Han Mengxiang — "first to get a hold on this young fellow." The wind still roared madly; the car drove on against it. She, Liu Yuying, sat within, and her thoughts ran faster than the car, faster than the wind; biting her lip, she thought with a smile: "Old Zhao, Old Zhao — if you won't agree to my terms, well, we'll call it off! This little secret of yours — Wu Sunfu, I daresay, would be willing to pay a price for it! Whoever pays the higher price, to him I'll sell!"

Liu Yuying was a clever woman. Before she was seventeen she had had a few years' schooling, and was rather better lettered in Chinese than her friend Feng Meiqing. As for the inner workings of the Exchange and the securities market, there she came, moreover, "of a line with sources of its own." Her father had gone bankrupt and killed himself in the "Exchange panic" of some ten years and more ago; her elder brother too was a "speculator," who had spent half his life running the lantern-wheel round of "striking a windfall" and "absconding under debt," until last year, when his great failure at "playing gold" — he had embezzled a huge sum and stood trial — left him imprisoned in the Western Gaol to this day; and her father-in-law Lu Kuangshi, and her late husband, had "standard gold" forever on their lips and "government bonds" for their every other word. Of late she herself had come to treat the Exchange as her daytime "home," buying in ten thousand or selling out five thousand, again and again, in the spirit of "laying a bet at fantan"; — in this she was quite even-tempered, for, warned by the ruin of her father, her brother, even her husband, she was very steady, and was content if a deal of ten thousand in bonds could earn her fifty or sixty dollars.

She was a woman, and she knew that a woman's way of making money is not a man's: a man uses the capital outside his person, but a woman uses the capital of her person. And so, even-tempered though she was in dealing bonds, upon this connection with Old Zhao she set extravagant hopes. A month ago, when she had suddenly come to know Old Zhao through Han Mengxiang's lead, she had decided that this too was a kind of "speculation." On this "speculation" she meant to rake in one whole big stake!

Now was just the season of her "harvest" come round. Every nerve-fibre in her body was quivering; in her mind schemes without number were piled up, steps without number for carrying them out. When she reached the Exchange, she promised herself once more: "This ware of mine can be sold off piecemeal too, and why not! This month past, which of the bond-dealers has not been there boring holes and hunting cracks to spy out Old Zhao's methods!" Clever as she was, she had already sorted out and analysed the matter she had overheard, and her conclusion was: as for the "arms," the "Jiamen" and the rest, no one would care to hear of that but Wu Sunfu; and as for the bonds, Old Zhao meant not only to go "short," but had besides some "old trick" that would surely keep him from any loss. What that old trick was she did not quite understand, but she believed with all her heart that Old Zhao had plenty of cunning tricks he could both talk of and carry out.

Inside the Exchange it was more clamorous even than a vegetable market. People stood banked in layers, in a stifling reek of sweat. Liu Yuying could not push her way up. Through the chinks between people's heads she caught sight of Han Mengxiang's glossy black hair, but it was too far off for her to hail him. On the rostrum the men calling the deals and the men holding telephone receivers had all flushed red in the face, waving their hands and opening their mouths in a great bawling; yet not a word of their voices could be heard. The seventy or eighty broker-firms, with over a hundred assistants of their own and speculators beyond count, made up a thunderous din of figures, before which every ear alike lost its use.

On the rostrum the board for "Reorganization, current month" was swung out! And thereupon a louder, longer-lasting "thunder" of figures, a more excited "sea of faces," pressed forward as in a charge, pressing to the left, pressing to the right. Liu Yuying could not even hold the place she had had, and had to fall back to the door of the "market." Having got her breath, she advanced again, and with great difficulty cut a path through, taking a seat on one of a row of long wooden benches before the partition-wall — the wall midway between the market's entrance and exit, on which hung the brokers' firm-boards and the "Notices of this Exchange." This place was like a "rear-area field hospital," where only those beaten back from the battle-line sat gasping for breath. From here one could not so much as see the face of the man calling the deals on the rostrum; one could only make out, far off, that one upraised hand of his.

Glancing down, Liu Yuying saw that her pale-blue gauze gown was already soaked through with sweat, and that two red flushes had come out on her breast where the nipples were, and she could not help a smile. Here, she thought, was the "market" in its raving frenzy, while over yonder Zhao Botao or Wu Sunfu, the pullers of the "market's" strings, sat quietly in their sofas smoking cigars — how "comic" that was; and as for herself, she now held the great secret of both string-pullers in the palm of her hand; all those people before her were in the dark, and she alone in the light — and how "comic" that was too!

She twisted her waist aslant and smiled with pursed lips. Not one of the people sitting there with her had noticed this rare piece of goods! They had flushed red in the face, their bloodshot eyes bulging, wrangling with one another in a chatter. On their temples stood out green veins as thick as earthworms. Here and there sat one alone with bowed head, saying nothing — and that one was surely a loser: before his dead, staring eyes there was being played out, then and there, a ghastly, terrifying vision of selling fields, selling land, defaulting on debts, fleeing away.

In the chairs in front were two little-moustached men, talking head to head, deep in their talk. Looking over, Liu Yuying knew the man with the crescent-moon whiskers for Feng Meiqing's father, Yunqing. This old fellow kept his dark, blackish-green face lowered, letting his companion mutter and murmur on, and never opened his mouth. All at once a round-faced man of forty and more squeezed out from the cloud-like press of speculators in front, stumbling and jostling his way into this "rear-area field hospital" region, and, rushing up before Feng Yunqing, bawled at the top of his voice:

"Yunqing, Yunqing! It's up! A dime, a dime and a half, two dimes! Rising step by step! What do you say? Shall we rake in ten thousand this very moment?"

"Ha, ha, ha! Rake in! But I still hold we should throw out twenty or thirty thousand!"

Feng Yunqing's companion got in first, and stood up, meaning to squeeze out — to go up to that "front line" again. Liu Yuying saw that this man was no more than thirty-odd, with a fashionable toothbrush moustache, another familiar face often seen there. Feng Yunqing was still hesitating, undecided, when the round-faced man squeezed back in and lifted his face to watch the "boards" that were hung out in an unceasing stream. Here the toothbrush-moustached man urged Feng Yunqing once more: "Well, then? Throw out twenty thousand! It's risen three days running — it's bound to fall back!"

"Ai, ai! You keep saying it'll fall back, and Shen'an keeps saying it'll rise higher! I mean to watch which way the wind blows for a day before I decide!"

Feng Yunqing said it in a rush, his face flushed red. But the round-faced man, his mouth all askew, squeezed in again and cried out loudly:

"It's fallen back! Fallen back! Back to the opening price!"

At once the toothbrush-moustached man gave a resentful snort, stood up, and pushed his way forward as if gone mad. Feng Yunqing stared, unable to utter a sound. The round-faced man squeezed to Feng Yunqing's side and, panting, said:

"There's something queer about these bonds! Yunqing, I fancy it's the big operators on both sides, 'bull' and 'bear,' fighting it out there!"

"Just so! That's why I hold we should watch the wind another day. But, Shen'an — just now Zhuangfei was grumbling the whole way that I hadn't the nerve to sell short around the fourth of this month, and now that I won't let go either; he says it's all my fault that things have gone wrong — well, in truth, since the three of us run the company together, I can only bow to the majority. If you and Zhuangfei are of one mind, I've nothing to say!"

"Not at all, not at all! Now that the price has settled into a see-saw, we can well watch it a day!"

The man called Shen'an answered with a frown, and sat down in the empty place beside Feng Yunqing.

Liu Yuying, who had seen all this clearly and heard all this plainly, could not help smiling again. She looked at the palm of her hand, as though the fate of this band of three — three men of three minds, and yet "partners" in one venture — lay there in her palm. No, and not these three alone! The fate of the whole floor-full of people sweating and fighting bitterly over those Reorganization bonds lay too in the palm of her hand! She rose abruptly, and, as though no one else were there, squeezed over to Feng Yunqing and the others, calling out in a ringing, crystal voice:

"Uncle Feng! It's been an age — is the dealing going smoothly for you?"

"Ah! Miss Liu! — Oh, now I remember: Miss Liu, have you seen Ah-Mei? The day before yesterday she —"

"Oh, that I'll tell you of later; the Exchange today is truly uncanny — you mustn't let the chance to make your fortune slip, Uncle!"

Liu Yuying said it with a coquettish smile, and by the way shot a glance as well at He Shen'an's face. Suddenly, from the centre of the "cloud of battle" in front, there rose a shout — not a shout made up of figures, and one that rose above all the deafening uproar of the whole floor; at once many on the "front line" surged back like a tide, while in this tight-packed "rear-area field hospital" many too sprang up to try to squeeze forward, some standing right up on the chairs. Feng Yunqing and the rest were frightened to the colour of clay.

"The railing's been shoved down! It's nothing, don't panic! It's only the railing that's been shoved down!"

From the "board-hanging" place upstairs, someone leaned half his body out and, cupping his two hands to his mouth for a speaking-trumpet, bawled this out at the top of his voice.

"Tut, tut! They really don't care for their lives — it beats going to war!"

So saying, Liu Yuying let out a breath and patted her own bosom lightly with her hand; her gauze gown, already some six-tenths dry, was now soaked through again with a sudden sweat all over her. In a moment the alarm too had passed, and the "market" went on struggling, see-sawing on; people spent their last strength to fight for victory at the "close." He Shen'an turned his face round to Liu Yuying and said with a smile:

"Miss Liu, your face is very familiar — you come here often too, I suppose? Are you looking for a rise or a fall? I'm looking for a rise!"

"There are those who look for a fall, too! But, Uncle Feng, how much have you done? Are you pleased with it?"

"Not much, not much! The three of us together have done some two hundred thousand; for the moment we're neither buying in nor selling out — it all turns on how we do within these ten days!"

"Would that be going bull?"

"Just so! By old Yun's reckoning, those who went 'bear' these six months have had no good of it at all; and I'm of the same mind. Last month, around the 15th, there was so fierce a falling wind that everyone thought it must surely pour away a thousand li in one rush — and who'd have thought that at the month's end it leapt back up again! Miss Liu, have you heard of this Zhao Botao's doings? There's never a time he doesn't guess right! This time, the outside talk says he's still a bull!"

As He Shen'an came to those last few sentences his voice dropped very low, and he craned out his neck till he brought his lips right up to Liu Yuying's ear — perhaps because those few words truly had to be kept secret, but perhaps too because there was an attraction in all Liu Yuying's charm and prettiness. But Liu Yuying gave it no heed; she smiled with a sidelong look, and suddenly thought of her plan to "sell off piecemeal." Here was the chance before her — why not make a trial of it, the more so as she and Feng Yunqing were acquainted? So thinking, Liu Yuying seized the moment and led off first with a teasing word:

"Mm, so that's how it is! But I've heard a thing or two of it myself —"

"Ah, Miss Liu — what of Ah-Mei, you were saying?"

Feng Yunqing broke in on Liu Yuying's words very rashly, and his dark, blackish old face suddenly reddened a little. Liu Yuying saw it very clearly. At once she hit upon an idea: giving the corner of Feng Yunqing's coat a tug, she brought herself close to his ear and said softly:

"Don't you know, Uncle? Little sister has a little game of her own on! I saw her over at Old Zhao's place. It looks as if Old Zhao is to strike another windfall of some hundreds of thousands this month! I know him, he — mm, but have you been going 'bull' of late, Uncle? That is —"

She stopped short suddenly, and Liu Yuying turned her face round to look at Feng Yunqing with a smile. She could tease no further than this — and indeed nothing could be plainer — but Feng Yunqing, his face red, said not a word after all. Nor was there any "speech" in that look of his. At the moment of hearing that Meiqing was indeed over at Old Zhao's, his heart had fallen into unbearable confusion; his hopes, his not-yet-extinguished sense of shame, and that inborn nature of his that fretted over gain and loss alike, all burst forth in this one instant; the words that followed from Liu Yuying he simply heard and heard not!

"You're a man of understanding, Uncle; I, Yuying, have never played tricks, and I don't want much either — a little winning-share will do!"

Liu Yuying spoke again at Feng Yunqing's ear, dropping altogether now that halting, roundabout way of putting things. This time Feng Yunqing heard her quite plainly, yet, because it did not join up with what had gone before, he did not grasp Liu Yuying's meaning at all, and stared wide-eyed and blank. Their talk broke off at this point.

Just then a change came over the "market" too. That clamour of the trade — that thunderous sound made up almost wholly of figures, of five thousand, ten thousand, fifty thousand, one hundred thousand, two hundred thousand, and of one dime, one dime five, one dollar, and the like — turned suddenly into the hubbub of human voices one hears in a theatre, mingled with guffaws and sighs! The people on the "front line" fell back one after another, and some went right out of the "market." The Reorganization bonds were at last knocked down at a closing price that had leapt up half a dollar!

On the rostrum the display-board swung out "Seven-Year Long-Term Bonds, current month." These were old bonds, and below them came the trading of all the old bonds issued in the hands of the Beiyang government; none of these were the central target of "speculation," nor the Exchange's main business. There was no longer that battle-like "thunder of figures" as before, and something under half the people on the floor had dispersed. Just then Li Zhuangfei of the toothbrush moustache came running back in high spirits, his face all grimed with sweat. He shot a glance at He Shen'an, clapped Feng Yunqing on the shoulder, and shouted loudly:

"The close leapt up half a dollar! No matter how you two reckon it, I've thrown out ten thousand!"

"That — what a pity, what a pity! Zhuangfei, you!"

He Shen'an sprang up crying out, as though a piece of flesh had been cut from him. Feng Yunqing said nothing, still staring wide-eyed and blank.

"What's to pity! Shen'an, I, surnamed Li, do things straight and hard: if it rises further, I'll make up the difference out of my own pocket; and if it falls back? Will you make up the difference?"

"Very well! But is the standard to be tomorrow's close? Or the last round before settlement?"

He Shen'an and Li Zhuangfei fell to quarrelling, one sharp sentence hard upon another, while Feng Yunqing still stared blankly, heavy with care. He had his own calculations. He had resolved to ask his daughter whether she had after all sounded out Old Zhao's secret, and only then settle on a course. At that time, over and above the present two hundred thousand, he meant to do a stroke on his own, keeping it from his two partners.

Liu Yuying, watching the two, He and Li, from the side, thought it a comical sight.

"Zhuangfei! Do you believe those bulletins outside? They're rumours! Those section-chiefs and section-clerks you keep about you, putting up at the hotel — aren't they too getting out bulletins there? I ask you, which of those telegrams of theirs isn't made up out of their own bellies! How can you be so sure it'll fall?"

"I'll not argue much with you — the facts will show in time; but how is it to be reckoned, in the end?"

Li Zhuangfei's tone had softened somewhat. He Shen'an, seizing the moment, thought to press a step further, but just then someone squeezed over and cut in, calling out:

"Are you two like the old and new district magistrates holding a joint court of trial?"

This man was none other than Han Mengxiang, the very object of Liu Yuying's coming here; perhaps Han Mengxiang had spied Liu Yuying from afar, and that was why he had come.

On the rostrum they had come to trading the "Ninety-Six Bonds." This thing, all but turned to waste paper now, still found people to buy and sell it, after all — though the trade was thinner even than before.

"Ah! Yuying! How come you're here? Have you looked for the big fellow? You — really! —"

Han Mengxiang turned his face to Liu Yuying again, and swaggered over to her side. At once Liu Yuying shot him a glance, and secretly pushed out her lips toward Feng Yunqing and the others. Han Mengxiang smiled. And Liu Yuying then strolled languidly off to the front.

"How much was traded in this round — have you any figure on it?"

Li Zhuangfei leaned to Han Mengxiang's side and asked softly. And so the two of them sidled off to a spot two or three steps to the right, and stood there talking low. Here Feng Yunqing and He Shen'an put their heads together too, for a good long while. All at once Li Zhuangfei over there laughed out loud, hastily left Han Mengxiang, and walked straight to the foot of the calling-rostrum in front, to put his head together with yet another man.

By now the Exchange's morning session was over. Only some ten people were left within the market, brokers and clients alike, chatting idly there in twos and threes. The tea-boys were sweeping up the cigarette-ends on the floor and had sprinkled a good deal of water about. In the two rows of brokers' rooms telephones jangled from time to time. Some, holding little notebooks and pencils, lifted their faces to copy down the record of the rise and fall of quotations on the "boards." These "boards," white powder-letters on black, stood in trim, even ranks, filling the box-shaped railing all along upstairs. Everything was calm, everything slackened off; yet within, people's hearts were tense as ever. It was like the brief silence after a bitter fight, when all are making ready for the next hard battle to come!

"Heh, heh! Just now Lu Kuangshi said Wu Sunfu is going 'bull'! That's strange, surely! How can it be?"

Li Zhuangfei suddenly came running over to say this low to Feng Yunqing and the others, the pleased flush on his face now turned to the ashen grey of vexation.

Feng Yunqing and He Shen'an exchanged a glance, but did not answer. After a while there broke out among the three of them a brief, low-voiced, yet violent dispute. Li Zhuangfei, as if in a huff, went off first. Then He Shen'an and Feng Yunqing, one after the other, left the "market" too. At the great door of the Exchange, Feng Yunqing saw once more Liu Yuying and Han Mengxiang standing there talking. And thereupon the fair image of his daughter Meiqing flashed suddenly across Feng Yunqing's heart again. This was his "light of hope," his one "lighthouse" amid his wavering and confusion! He could not help a smile.

Liu Yuying, watching Feng Yunqing's retreating back, curled her lips in disdain.

Feng Yunqing went home in the teeth of the great wind. Sitting in the rickshaw, he dared not open his eyes. The wind was fiercer even than in the morning. The trees the whole way along shouted their war-cries to spur it on. Sitting in the car, Feng Yunqing felt as though he were still inside the Exchange, hearing the "thunder of figures." As he neared home, his heart, strangely, would not settle, and he asked himself: if that child Ah-Mei had not got it clear, then what was to be done? If she had heard it wrong, then what was to be done? This was a matter that bore on his family and his very life!

But by the time he reached home, Feng Yunqing's heart was set at ease after all. He put his trust in his own daughter, and put his trust too in that whole-hearted sincerity of his own when he had prayed the night before last for his ancestors' protection.

The first thing he said on coming in the door was, "Has the young mistress come back yet?" Before he asked it, he had drawn a lot in his heart: if she were already back, then his luck stood eight or nine in ten. And sure enough, Heaven does not fail the man who takes pains! His daughter too had only just come back, and was asleep in her room. On the instant, Feng Yunqing's ashen face was all wreathed in joy, and, forgetting his weariness, forgetting even the hunger in his belly, he hurried up the stairs.

His daughter's door was shut, and Feng Yunqing hesitated once more, all of a sudden; he could not decide whether he ought to knock and go in, or wait a while and let his daughter come out of herself. To be sure, he longed to hear that news, precious as gold, a moment the sooner, so as to make his arrangements at leisure; yet he was afraid, too, that his daughter, just come back, had shut her door because — as girls will — she had some private thing to do behind it, changing her underclothes, say, or washing her lower parts — and then, were he to burst in at so unclean a moment, would he not affront the god of joy, and turn his good luck to bad!

As he stood there wavering, undecided, all at once the concubine Old Ninth came toward him, holding in her hands a very well-stuffed wallet, with the look of one about to go out.

"Ah! You've come at just the right time — I've a word to ask you!"

The concubine Old Ninth cried out shrilly, caught hold of Feng Yunqing's ear, and dragged him off into the room.

A sheaf of bills was thrust into Feng Yunqing's hands; they were things from half a month back — a rice bill, a coal bill, a tailor's bill, a motor-car bill, bills from the Changfeng Fruit Shop and the Laodafang confectioner's; and besides these, two new ones, the one an electricity receipt from the power company, the other the rent-note for last month. Feng Yunqing stared, and turned all these shop-bills over one by one, working the abacus in his mind — and it came, as it turned out, to some four hundred dollars.

"Old Ninth — the rice shop, the coal shop, the motor-car firm — didn't we tell them it would all be settled by the Mid-Autumn, the middle of the eighth month?"

"Hmph! You've the face to say that to me! — I hadn't the face to say it to them! I'll tell you plainly: I've paid every last one of them off! It came to 431 dollars and some odd cents in all — and you'll pay me back this very day — for I too borrowed it from my sisters' set!"

"Now, now! Old Ninth, can it wait a few more days? If I've a hundred dollars on me today, then I'm a rank old cuckold!"

Feng Yunqing said it with an ingratiating smile, and gathered up the bills.

"No ready cash — that's no matter. Just give me the passbook for the ten thousand in silver at the Yuanfeng native bank, and we'll leave it at that. Put it in pawn!"

"That won't do, no — Old Ninth. That really won't do! Besides, it's only four hundred-odd dollars — how can you want a passbook for ten thousand in silver as security for it —"

"Pah! Only four hundred dollars! Have you lost your wits? The five thousand over at Fifth Sister's — wasn't that too passed through my hands? And still you say it's only four hundred-odd! That was a courtesy loan; when they lent it out, it was because they trusted me — they wouldn't even take security; and it falls due in a month now — have you the face to keep them waiting on it?"

The concubine arched her two long, fine, painted eyebrows, and the more she said the angrier she grew, and the more fearsome.

Feng Yunqing only smiled with a fawning face. At the mention of that five thousand dollars, he had some inkling in his heart: all that about borrowing it from Fifth Sister was false, and it was, most likely, the concubine Old Ninth's own private savings. But not for anything did he dare to bring the matter out into the open.

The concubine cursed him a few times more, then suddenly, remembering it was getting late, went off.

Feng Yunqing, as though a great amnesty had fallen to him, sprang up and stretched himself lazily, thought a moment more, then paced out to his daughter's room. The door stood ajar. Feng Yunqing first cleared his throat with a cough, and then pushed the door open and went in. Meiqing sat before the dressing-table by the window, gazing entranced into the mirror. She turned her face, and, seeing it was her father, gave a giggling laugh and at once bent down over the dressing-table, hiding her face.

The wind whistled outside the window. The wind blew the bamboo blind before the window too, slapping and slapping against the pane.

Feng Yunqing stood by his daughter's side, looking at her head of black hair, looking at her snow-white nape, looking at her slender waist half turned aside, and looking too at the pair of rounded legs she had stretched aslant to the foot of the dressing-table; and at last, as if satisfied, he let out a breath and asked softly:

"Ah-Mei! That matter — have you found it out clearly?"

"What!"

Meiqing suddenly lifted her head to speak, giving a start of the whole body as if in fright; no, she really was frightened in earnest, for it was only now, on her father's asking her so, that she at last recalled that matter he had charged her over and over to watch for a chance to find out — and which all this while she had forgotten clean away.

"Ai! Ah-Mei, it's about the bonds! Is he, after all, going 'bull,' or is he 'bear'? —"

"Oh! That! But, Papa, there's a bit of your talk I don't understand."

Meiqing looked at her father's face and said it hesitantly; but within that little heart of hers there was a rare confusion: should she say straight out that she had not yet found it out, or put him off any old how with some careless answer, or even trump up a few words to deceive him? She decided on the way of the careless put-off.

"My talk? Which words of mine don't you understand?"

"It's what you were just saying, 'bull' and 'bear' and all that — I'm always hearing people say them, but I don't quite understand."

"Ha, ha, so you did find it out. Silly child! 'Bull' means buying government bonds in, 'bear' means selling out."

"Then he must be a 'bull'!"

Meiqing suddenly blurted out this one sentence, and then giggled. She did not herself feel that this was a lie: wasn't Old Zhao very rich? A rich man surely buys in, and only one without money need sell out! As it looked to Meiqing's little-girl heart, if Old Zhao were brought to selling anything, then he would be no Old Zhao at all — no longer the Old Zhao that women loved!

"Ah, ah, is it true? He's a 'bull'?"

Feng Yunqing asked once more, as though afraid of having heard wrong, and at the same time his dark, blackish old face was already all wreathed in smiles, and his heart went pit-a-pat.

"It's true!"

Meiqing said it after a moment's thought, and could not help giggling again; then, as if abashed, she buried her face in her hands and bent down over the dressing-table.

Just then a gust of wind outside the window suddenly caught up the bamboo blind and, with a slap, flung it right up to the eaves. Then came the howling of a fiercer wind, and the windows all rattled and shook.

Feng Yunqing was a little startled, but at once took it for a lucky omen; there were, as it seemed, these two lines that ran: "When the bamboo blind flies up to the roof, the master of the house shall make his fortune!" He resolved to stake his whole fortune on one throw, to go "bull"; he resolved to lay hands on that "sacred" ten thousand in silver at the Yuanfeng native bank — Meiqing's "bottom-of-the-chest money"; and, running out of his daughter's room, he went straight out of doors again.
