### Afterword
Chen Yangshan was a professional revolutionary wrapped in mystery, a legendary figure of the Chinese Communist Party's hidden front, one of the founders of our Party's intelligence and security work. He was among the finest of the Party's Special Branch, a hero of the secret war. He built our Party's first important counter-espionage connection and drew from it a great store of secret intelligence; for sixteen years he worked deep in the tiger's den, and the enemy's agents never once found him out. "A plain life of hard roads, a hidden career of danger and daring": that was the true likeness of his life.
I worked in the spirit of a letter from the responsible state department, "On Assistance with the Writing of *A Biography of Chen Yangshan*," which laid down that "the task of writing the biography is to be borne by your bureau's Comrade Yao Huafei." At the time I spent more than two years turning over a great mass of books and materials: works on the history of Chinese intelligence, on the Central Special Branch, and on Chen Yangshan himself. I traveled to Beijing, Nanjing, and other cities to interview Cheng Jianyu, Qin Jie, Jin Chuxuan, and others with direct knowledge of the story, and with Chen Yangshan's son, Cheng Jianyu, I talked the manuscript over and checked it against the record many times.
When the first draft was done, I sought the views of the leaders and comrades of the relevant departments, revised it again, and, after their review, completed the manuscript. October 7, 2006 was the hundredth anniversary of Chen Yangshan's birth, and that September the China Friendship Publishing Company brought out *The Legend of Chen Yangshan, Lucky General of the Hidden Front*.
Chen Yangshan spent his working life on the hidden front, and because that struggle was by its nature secret and closed, his deeds were little known in the past. Even now much of what he did cannot be fully disclosed, and the sources are limited. What this book records of his fighting years can therefore be no more than a "brief account."
To spread the story of the hidden front's heroes further, I went on for more than ten years afterward collecting materials and episodes about Chen Yangshan and others like him, and above all the now-declassified records of the Central Special Branch and the Jin-Sui Border Region. As meetings and business trips allowed, I looked into Chen Yangshan's record with people in the same line of work in the places he had fought, in Shaanxi, Shanxi, Gansu, and Ningxia, and in Shanghai and Xi'an, or went to see for myself. Six times I rewrote the manuscript before it reached its present form.
This is a work of history and documentary record, and I have tried to make every case it sets down true and exact. So I did not follow the usual pattern of the literary biography. Instead I took his fighting years, his achievements, his character, and his way of working, and set them down as they were, without invention or embellishment, in the plain manner of the documentary record.
Here I owe particular thanks to Chen Yangshan's son, Cheng Jianyu, who even in grave illness went on supplying me with precious materials: information about Chen Yangshan's home district, anecdotes from his life, the handwritten precepts he left his children, photographs, and more.
In the writing of this book I had the support and guidance of the leading comrades of the Ministry of State Security and the Shanghai Municipal State Security Bureau, and many of Chen Yangshan's old comrades-in-arms, colleagues, and family warmly provided materials. Ling Yun, Liu Fuzhi, and other comrades wrote inscriptions for it. As I worked I had the care and guidance of the leaders of my own unit and the comrades of its departments, the support of the leaders above them, and help too from the staff of the *Espionage Watch* editorial office and from colleagues who had served alongside me. Dong Huying, Jiang An, and others went with me to Beijing, Nanjing, and elsewhere to conduct interviews; He Yuejun did my typing, corrected the text, and worked it into shape; and my good friend Guan Zhihua, a senior editor formerly with the *People's Daily* and a writer himself, helped me revise and polish the manuscript though his own editing and writing left him hard-pressed. I should add, too, that I owe thanks to my wife, Chen Dawen, and to my daughter and son-in-law, who shared the risks with me, offered ideas, read and corrected drafts, printed materials, and took the housework off my hands; even my thirteen-year-old twin granddaughters, Niuniu and Nini, would often bring me tea while I wrote.
Naturally, for whatever gaps and faults remain in the manuscript, I look to my elders, to specialists, to fellow workers, and to my readers to set me right without stint.
Yao Huafei
June 18, 2017
