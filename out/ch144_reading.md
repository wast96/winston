### Dredging for Tinfoil Ash

The four words "dredging for tinfoil ash" are, in Shanghai speech, an epithet of abuse for a man who grabs at money by crooked means—as though anyone really went and dredged for tinfoil ash! Yet nowadays the thing has become a plain and literal fact. The Shanghai people have always been given to superstition, and on the nights of the fourteenth and the thirtieth of the old calendar most of them buy a few strings of long ingots and burn them at their doors, by way of flattering the ghosts and praying for gain. On just those two nights there is sure to be someone abroad with a little dustpan and a little brush in hand, running helter-skelter from door to door; and the moment he spies a burnt heap of long ingots with the flames not yet out, he coolly and unhurriedly sweeps the whole lot into his pan. When the work is done, he gathers it up and carries it off to change for money. The tinfoil ash that was meant for the ghosts is turned into money for the use of the living: and this is their peculiar road to a fortune, this "dredging for tinfoil ash."
