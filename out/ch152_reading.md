### Skimming Oil

The practice they call "skimming the oil," improper act though it be, has by now become a universal thing in society. And what is meant by "skimming the oil"? Set out separately, there are about three kinds. The first: suppose you produce a dollar and send a servant out to buy something, and he buys only nine mao's worth and quietly makes off with the odd mao—this is what is called skimming the oil. The second: suppose a man, in the middle of his work, slips off on the sly to amuse himself a while or take a rest—this too is skimming the oil. The third: suppose one comes by a free ticket, or some article, without paying for it—this also is called skimming the oil. In a word, the skimmer of oil takes in the whole of those six words: sharp practice, small greed, and idleness.

This improper practice has been handed down and has a long history behind it, and one could not put it right now if one tried. Take the first kind, the servant's skim: whether it be the old manservant, the amah, or the tea-boys and the attendants, they will every one of them, when they go out to make a purchase, quite frankly and unblushingly take their little skim before they feel satisfied. Only, the mild-hearted skim little and the hard-hearted skim much. If you would forbid them to skim, you must go out and see to every single thing yourself before you can root it out; short of that there is no way to stop it, and you must just wink at it, one eye open and one eye shut, and let them skim away.
