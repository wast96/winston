## Chapter 12. Coda

The door on the stone steps stood open; it was Zhu Ting who had opened it. Where there is a man who can make a door not meant to be opened, there is always a man who can open it again.

So it is with a great many things in this world. Make a shield that no spear on earth can pierce, and someone is sure to forge a spear that will pierce your shield. In this world there is no such thing as a true "absolute."

Lu Xiaofeng sat on the stone steps, watching Huo Xiu in his cage; and it struck him of a sudden that the cage was for all the world like a prison cell.

*Whoever does wrong must be punished for it, and there is no escaping it.* Lu Xiaofeng sighed; that the affair could end like this contented him well enough. *And how, after all, had it come to end so?*

The Boss was measuring the height of the cavern with a wooden tripod. The Boss's Wife stood by, watching. She knew he must have hit on some new notion again, but she had no wish to ask; she knew that no man, deep in his thinking, cares to have a woman chattering at his elbow.

Then Zhu Ting asked her, of a sudden, "Is that man about to leave, then?"

"Mm," said the Boss's Wife.

"You won't go and see him off?" said Zhu Ting.

"If you go, I'll go," said the Boss's Wife.

"He doesn't seem to want me there," said Zhu Ting coldly.

"And you don't want to go either?" said the Boss's Wife. Zhu Ting owned it.

"Yet let him have some need of you," said the Boss's Wife, "and send any man at all to bring you word of it, and you'd be off to him that instant."

"Only because I know," said Zhu Ting, "that if ever I had need of him, he'd come to me just the same."

"Come, and never hail you, never say a word?" said the Boss's Wife.

"Coming is one thing," said Zhu Ting, "and talking's another thing altogether!"

The Boss's Wife sighed. "Friends the like of you two, I doubt you'd find a second pair in all the world."

Zhu Ting set down the tripod and gazed at her; then, of a sudden: "I've made up my mind. I'm staying on here."

"I know," said the Boss's Wife.

"And you can bear to bide in a place like this?" said Zhu Ting.

"So long as you can bear it, I can," said the Boss's Wife.

"If you'd sooner not stay," said Zhu Ting, "I shan't blame you for it."

The Boss's Wife's eyes flew wide. "You'd pack me off, would you, so that little fox-spirit can keep you company?"

Zhu Ting laughed. "And when did you learn to be jealous?"

"Just now," said the Boss's Wife.

"Just now?" said Zhu Ting.

"That little fox-spirit, whispering to you just now on the sly, what was she saying?" said the Boss's Wife.

Zhu Ting smiled. "A secret, to be sure."

The Boss's Wife's eyes went wide again. "What secret?"

"I'll tell you by and by," said Zhu Ting easily. "For now... for now you may go and see him off."

"I won't," said the Boss's Wife.

"Why not?" said Zhu Ting.

The Boss's Wife bit her lip. "From this day on, I'll keep at your side and never stir a step from you. Nowhere at all will I go, because—"

"Because what?" said Zhu Ting.

The Boss's Wife looked at him, her lovely eyes brimming with love, and said softly, "Because only now do I see what a wonderful man you are — and I'm afraid someone will steal you away from me."

Lu Xiaofeng, watching the two of them from far off, sighed of a sudden. "It seems the danger to those two has passed."

"What danger were they in?" said Hua Manlou.

"These two years past," said Lu Xiaofeng, "the Boss's Wife has seemed a little let down by the Boss, and I always feared the pair of them would come to be an ill-matched, unhappy couple."

"Was it that she thought the Boss too idle," said Hua Manlou, "too good for nothing?"

Lu Xiaofeng smiled. "But now, surely, she knows what a marvel of a genius her husband is."

Hua Manlou owned it. "Had it not been for the Boss, we might truly have been walled up to die in here."

Every woman longs to be able to feel proud of her husband.

Lu Xiaofeng sighed again. "The rest I could have borne; but the taste of going hungry, it seems, is a hard thing to bear indeed."

He was looking at Huo Xiu in the cage; but Huo Xiu, his eyes wide, was staring at Shangguan Xue'er beyond the bars.

In Xue'er's hands were a sausage and two flatcakes, and she was murmuring away to Huo Xiu, though what she said no one could tell.

Huo Xiu had gone red in the face and thick in the neck with rage. Of a sudden he sprang up and flung himself hard against the cage. Break it open he could not, of course; the cage was one he had built to his own design, and no man alive could break it.

Xue'er watched him coldly from outside, and made as though to go; but Huo Xiu called her back, and the two exchanged a few more words. Then Huo Xiu heaved a long, long sigh, drew his signature-mark on a slip of paper, and with that slip bought Xue'er's sausage and cakes. Down he sat on the ground at once, and fell to wolfing them down.

Hua Manlou asked of a sudden, "He'd still sooner die than tell where he's hidden that hoard of jewels?"

"He isn't afraid to die," said Lu Xiaofeng.

Hua Manlou smiled wryly. "Does he truly hold poverty more fearful than death?"

Lu Xiaofeng smiled. "But by now, maybe, he's found there's something more fearful yet than being poor."

"Hunger?" said Hua Manlou.

Before Lu Xiaofeng could speak, Xue'er came bounding up, her eyes ablaze. "I've sold him that sausage and the two cakes," she said, laughing. "Guess how much silver I got for them!" Guess they could not.

Xue'er brandished the slip of paper. "Fifty thousand tael I got for them," she said, "a full fifty thousand; and whenever I please I can take this note, written in his own hand, to his bank and draw the silver out."

Lu Xiaofeng could not help laughing. "You've a black heart on you."

Hua Manlou laughed. "There's not a dearer sausage to be found in all the world, I'll warrant."

"And so that old fox is fit to go mad with rage," said Xue'er, "and yet buy them he must, worse luck for him."

Hua Manlou sighed. "The taste of hunger's a bitter thing, sure enough."

"Do you mean to knock him clean out of everything he owns?" said Lu Xiaofeng.

"That wealth was ours to begin with," said Xue'er. "Don't forget I'm a Shangguan too."

Lu Xiaofeng smiled. "Even were you to squeeze fifty thousand tael of silver out of him every day, I doubt you'd have him emptied inside a year."

"Then I'll stop here and squeeze him three years, till he's emptied dry," said Xue'er. "There's someone here to keep me company, after all."

"Has the Boss truly made up his mind to stay on here?" said Lu Xiaofeng.

Xue'er nodded, and of a sudden a very mysterious smile crossed her face. "He told the Boss's Wife he means to stay so as to make a few marvellous things here in this place; but I know the real reason he's staying."

"And what is it?" said Lu Xiaofeng.

Xue'er blinked, her smile more mysterious still. "That's a secret."

"What secret?" said Lu Xiaofeng.

"If it's a secret," said Xue'er, "how could I tell you?"

Lu Xiaofeng stared at her a good while, then smiled again of a sudden. "Your secret I never wished to know to begin with; I'm only a little uneasy."

"Uneasy about what?" said Xue'er.

"When you take this note to draw the silver," said Lu Xiaofeng, "what if someone presses you as to where it came from?"

"No one ever will," said Xue'er.

"Oh?" said Lu Xiaofeng.

Xue'er laughed. "Don't forget he's a strange, secretive old man to start with. Even his most trusted men never knew his comings and goings; this was always his way of doing things."

Lu Xiaofeng sighed. "It seems this too is something he's brought upon himself."

"Just so," said Xue'er, laughing. "Had he not brought it on himself, squeezing his silver out of him wouldn't be nearly so easy."

A man's fate is his own making, first and last; and so the truly diligent are always blessed with the best of luck.

Lu Xiaofeng rose with a smile. "Then stay and squeeze him at your leisure; and while you're about it, squeeze me out a few jars of good wine as well."

Xue'er gazed at him. "You... you're leaving now?"

"If I stopped three days in a place like this," said Lu Xiaofeng, "it would be a wonder I weren't bored to death."

"And you'll not even ask after my secret?" said Xue'er.

"I won't," said Lu Xiaofeng.

Xue'er's eyes rolled round, and of a sudden she laughed again. "It's no matter, really, if I tell you; you're bound to know it sooner or later in any case."

Lu Xiaofeng did not gainsay her.

"He's staying here," said Xue'er, "for no other reason than that I've fallen in love with him, and he with me."

Lu Xiaofeng laughed.

"I know you don't believe me," said Xue'er lightly; "but once I've married him, you'll not be able to help it."

Lu Xiaofeng could not help himself. "You, marry him — and what of the Boss's Wife?"

"A Boss needn't have only the one Boss's Wife," said Xue'er easily. "You can have four eyebrows; why shouldn't the Boss have two Boss's Wives?"

The hillside lay under the setting sun, and Lu Xiaofeng walked upon it. He had not said a word in a great while; then of a sudden: "That little fox is at her lying again, for certain."

"Mm," said Hua Manlou.

"The Boss is no madman, after all," said Lu Xiaofeng. "Why would he wed a little imp like her for a second Boss's Wife?"

"Of course he wouldn't," said Hua Manlou.

Lu Xiaofeng walked on a way with his mouth shut, then said of a sudden, "But the Boss is a scoundrel, and a scoundrel takes leave of his wits often enough."

"And a second Boss's Wife is commonly a little fox-spirit," said Hua Manlou.

"So you'd best hurry back and talk the scoundrel round," said Lu Xiaofeng, "and tell him on no account to do so scoundrelly a thing."

"Why not go yourself?" said Hua Manlou.

"You know I don't speak to him," said Lu Xiaofeng.

"And if there's nothing in it at all?" said Hua Manlou. "Won't the Boss take the two of us for a pair of madmen?"

"And where's the harm in playing the madman once in a while?" said Lu Xiaofeng.

Hua Manlou sighed. "It seems that whoever makes a friend of you is bound, soon or late, to catch a touch of your madness."

But go he did. He had no way not to.

Lu Xiaofeng sat waiting by the roadside like a very fool. Luckily it was a lonely mountain path; save for an old woman out gathering wild herbs, not another soul came by. He had not long to wait before Hua Manlou was back.

"Well?" Lu Xiaofeng asked at once.

Hua Manlou kept a straight face. "You're a madman, and so am I."

"There was nothing in it at all?" said Lu Xiaofeng.

"They do have a secret, though," said Hua Manlou. "The Boss has taken Xue'er for his goddaughter."

Lu Xiaofeng was struck dumb.

Hua Manlou sighed again, and smiled wryly. "You knew well enough the little imp was lying; why must you go and let her take you in?"

Lu Xiaofeng sighed too, and smiled wryly. "Because I'm not only a scoundrel — I'm a blockhead into the bargain."

He raised his head, and of a sudden saw Xue'er come hurrying up at a run and a skip. "Did you two see someone pass by just now?" she panted.

"Only an old woman gathering wild herbs," said Lu Xiaofeng.

Xue'er sprang up. "That old woman must have been my sister!"

"Your sister?" said Lu Xiaofeng. "Shangguan Feiyan?"

Xue'er nodded, her eyes ablaze. "I've only just found out she isn't dead. She was always a great one for playing dead; a moment ago, once you'd gone, I went down below, and—"

Lu Xiaofeng did not wait for her to finish. Of a sudden he turned on his heel and walked off, hauling Hua Manlou along with him. "Whatever you say this time, I'll not be taken in; I won't so much as listen."

His mind, it seemed, was truly made up; he walked very fast indeed.

Xue'er gazed after them, lost in a daze, until they were far off; then she sighed softly and murmured, "Why is it that when I tell the truth, people won't believe me...?"
