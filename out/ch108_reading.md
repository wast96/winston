### Watching for the Ghost Face

The shop clerks of today, apart from a very few who have had some training and receive the customer with a pleasant face, mostly pull a scowling ghost-face at the customer (the author has treated of this before, in his essay "The Shop Clerk's Three Faces," to which the reader may refer). Go into their shops to do business, and they wear an air of half-heeding you and half-not, of half-noticing and half-ignoring, so that to go and buy a thing is simply to go and look at their ghost-faces.

A word of warning to the proprietor and the manager of a shop: hereafter you must in good earnest train and school your clerks before you entrust them with a clerk's duties. Bear in mind that the rise or ruin of a shop rests wholly upon its clerks—can this be a thing to slight? Can it be muddled through, letting them pull their ghost-faces as they please? For in truth, in this cold and self-seeking society of ours, let a man have some favour to ask, or be dressed a little too plainly, and I warrant that everywhere he goes he will meet with ghost-faces past all bearing to look upon.
