### Beggars Who Work Only the Foreign Trade

A beggar, in Shanghai proverb, is called a biesan. Now these biesan, too, divide into gangs in their begging: soliciting alms along the streets, say; giving carts a pull on the bridges; dogging people's heels for coppers; petitioning on the ground—all sorts and kinds, to be seen everywhere. There is besides one sort of beggar who neither solicits along the streets nor pulls carts on the bridges, but plants himself expressly at the doors of the great department stores and the banks; and the moment he sees a foreign lady come out he snaps to attention and makes her a deep bow, with a stream of "Missee" this and "Missee" that, and only after he has got his money does he saunter off. That such a beggar can actually speak a few words of pidgin, and put in a little effort in front of foreigners—why, he is a diplomat of the future! Ha, ha!
