## H2 Chapter 3. Birds of the Same Wood Each Take Flight

> 门开了。阿初一脸憔悴的站在门口。湿漉漉的头发撂在他笔直的鼻尖上，手里拎着一盏半明不灭的马灯，裤筒里浸泡的雨往鞋底里灌，鞋底里积存的水往外冒，浑身上下没有一处是干的。
The door opened. A-Chu stood on the threshold, his face haggard. His sodden hair hung down over the straight tip of his nose; in his hand he carried a storm lantern guttering between light and dark; the rain soaked into his trouser legs ran down into his shoes, and the water pooled in his shoes welled back out again. There was not a dry spot on him anywhere.

> 本来疲惫不堪的丽水，一看见阿初，就像看见了五百年前的冤家，郁积在她胸中的火星团子一下子被点燃了，她“噌”的一声窜起来，冲到门口，对准阿初的面孔扬手就是一记耳光，打得水花四溅。
Lishui, already worn to exhaustion, no sooner saw A-Chu than she saw in him an enemy from five hundred years ago, and the ball of sparks smoldering in her breast was kindled all at once. She sprang up with a start, rushed to the door, and swung her open hand full at A-Chu’s face in a slap that sent the water flying.

> 阿初一动不动，连最基本的本能反应都没有，只是两眼直勾勾地盯着丽水，眼光里闪动着与生俱来得倔强，以至于丽水不得不心怯。阿初面无表情地径直从丽水身边走过去，等丽水反应过来，他已经走到屋子中间，丽水紧跟着他身后。
A-Chu did not stir—not even the most basic flinch of instinct—but only stared fixedly at Lishui, an inborn stubbornness glinting in his eyes, so that Lishui could not help quailing. Expressionless, A-Chu walked straight past her; by the time Lishui had gathered herself he had already reached the middle of the room, and she followed close at his heels.

> “你知道回来了？你怎么不死在外面？”
“So you knew enough to come back? Why didn’t you die out there?”

> 阿初毫不理会地扯开了拖泥带水的外套扣子，把脱下来得外套扔在脚下。
Paying her no heed at all, A-Chu unfastened the buttons of his sodden, mud-dragged coat and threw it down at his feet.

> “他今天晚上要死了，你怎么办？”
“He is going to die tonight—what will you do then?”

> 阿初毫不理睬，继续解开黑色绒衣领扣。
A-Chu took no notice, and went on undoing the collar button of his black woolen shirt.

> “你回答我！”丽水一把拽住阿初的衣领。
“Answer me!” Lishui seized A-Chu by the collar.

> “放手。”阿初冷冰冰地说。
“Let go,” A-Chu said icily.

> 丽水不放。
Lishui did not let go.

> “放手啊！”阿初粗暴地大吼。
“Let go!” A-Chu roared roughly.

> 丽水的手不由自主地松开，由于过度气愤，丽水的脸庞变得青紫。阿初却突然之间想到自己留给少爷的那封信，心想：“糟糕！”不假思索，飞快地向书房跑去。
Lishui’s hand loosened in spite of her, and in her excess of fury her face turned livid. But all at once A-Chu thought of the letter he had left for the young master. “Damn!” he thought, and without a second’s reflection he bolted for the study.

> 丽水瞬间回过神来，追着他，两个人几乎同时闯进书房。阿初迅速地打开抽屉，脸色陡变，回过头来质问丽水。“你拿了我东西？”
Lishui came to herself in an instant and chased after him, so that the two of them burst into the study almost together. A-Chu swiftly pulled open the drawer; his face changed abruptly, and he wheeled on Lishui. “You took my things?”

> 丽水汽得瞠目结舌。“你混账！”
Lishui was so angry she could only gape and stammer. “You scoundrel!”

> “你把东西还我。”阿初的口气强硬。
“Give me back my things,” A-Chu said, his tone hard.

> 丽水汽得说不出话，两只手捂着胸喘气。
Lishui was too angry to speak, and stood pressing both hands to her chest, gasping.

> “把东西还我。”阿初说。
“Give me back my things,” A-Chu said.

> “你说我偷你东西?”丽水的自尊心受到有生以来最大的打击，而施行这种打击的仅仅是荣家的一个家奴，这是丽水最不能容忍的事。丽水勃然大怒。“混账奴才！你给我跪下！跪下！”
“You say I stole your things?” Lishui’s pride had taken the greatest blow of her life, and the one dealing it was a mere house servant of the Rong family—the thing Lishui could least of all endure. She flew into a rage. “You scoundrel of a slave! Down on your knees! Kneel!”

> 阿初冷笑，转身就走。
A-Chu gave a cold laugh and turned to go.

> “你给我站住！”丽水直冲过来。“你以为现在你身份不同了，就可以肆无忌惮地欺负主子了！”丽水扬起手来就要打，阿初一伸手捏紧她手腕，对着她的脸，咬金嚼铁地说：“你再打我，我就要还手了。”
“Stop right there!” Lishui charged straight at him. “You think that now your station has changed you can bully your masters without any restraint!” Lishui raised her hand to strike, but A-Chu shot out his own and gripped her wrist, and his jaw set like iron, he said to her face, “Strike me again, and I will strike back.”

> “你敢！”
“You wouldn’t dare!”

> “你看我敢不敢！”阿初猛地一松手，把丽水闪了一个踉跄。
“Watch and see whether I dare!” A-Chu let go all at once, and Lishui staggered back.

> “把东西还我。”阿初还是那句话。
“Give me back my things.” A-Chu said it once again.

> 丽水蔑视地看了他一眼。“你说，这家里哪一样东西是你的？你说！”
Lishui gave him a look of contempt. “Tell me—which single thing in this house is yours? Tell me!”

> “这里哪一样东西是你的？你以为你来度假？你从伦敦到卡迪芙，连车马费都没有了。到了这，你吃的、穿的、用的，哪一样是你自己掏的钱？少爷的情形你也看到了，坐吃山空。我的出诊费、代课费还不够这的房钱、饭钱、你的衣服钱、少爷的药钱……”
“And which single thing here is yours? Do you imagine you came here on holiday? From London to Cardiff you hadn’t even the fare for the journey. Here—the food you eat, the clothes you wear, the things you use—which of them did you pay for out of your own pocket? You have seen the young master’s state for yourself: sitting idle and eating a mountain hollow. My consultation fees and my supply-teaching fees are barely enough for the rent here, the food, your clothes, the young master’s medicine…”

> “原来我们姐弟一直靠初先生养活。”一句冷冰冰地话直直抛过来，荣升咳嗽了两声扶着扶梯站在楼梯口。丽水“哎呀”了一声，顾不得和阿初恶吵，慌不迭地上去扶他下楼。阿初没敢抬头，往后退了几步，虽然隔着楼梯，阿初低着头也能看见少爷手中拿着那沉甸甸的信。荣升走下楼，回头看了阿初一眼，说：“跪下。”
“So it seems the two of us have been kept alive all this while by Mr. Chu.” The words came flung out cold and straight: Rong Sheng, with two coughs, stood holding the banister at the top of the stairs. Lishui gave a little “Aiya,” and, forgetting her ugly quarrel with A-Chu, hurried up in a fluster to help him down. A-Chu did not dare raise his head and drew back a few steps; but even with lowered head, and the staircase between them, he could see the heavy letter in the young master’s hand. Rong Sheng came down, turned to glance at A-Chu, and said, “Kneel.”

> 阿初跪下了。
A-Chu knelt.

> 荣升由于身体虚弱，扶着椅子坐下，轻言细语地对丽水说：“表姐，你大呼小叫的，不怕人笑话。”丽水不吭声了。“表姐，我想喝杯咖啡。麻烦你。”
Rong Sheng, weak as he was, steadied himself against a chair and sat, then said softly and gently to Lishui, “Cousin, all this shouting and crying out—are you not afraid people will laugh at us?” Lishui fell silent. “Cousin, I should like a cup of coffee. If you would be so good.”

> 丽水赶紧地说好，端着咖啡器具到外面厨房去了。支开丽水，荣升的态度开始缓和。
Lishui at once said yes and carried the coffee things out to the kitchen. With Lishui sent off, Rong Sheng’s manner began to soften.

> “知道为什么要你跪？”
“Do you know why I had you kneel?”

> “是我说错话。”
“Because I spoke wrongly.”

> “不，你没说错话，你说的都是事实。你不满意、不开心，可以跟我讲。丽水到底是姑娘家，远来是客。你明不明白？”
“No, you did not speak wrongly. Everything you said is fact. If you are dissatisfied, if you are unhappy, you may tell me. But Lishui is, after all, a young woman, and one who has come from far away is a guest. Do you understand me?”

> 阿初点头。“你明白就好。”荣升向阿初指了指紧闭的落地窗帘。阿初立即去拉开窗帘，清晨的阳光照射进来，窗外的花枝在阳光的浸润下，显得生机盎然。
A-Chu nodded. “So long as you understand.” Rong Sheng pointed A-Chu toward the drawn curtains of the French window. A-Chu went at once and pulled them open; the early morning sun poured in, and the flowering branches beyond the window, steeped in the light, seemed to brim with life.

> 荣升不说话，靠在椅子上，感觉到惬意。阿初了解荣升，彼此之间默契很深。他知道荣升等他开口解释，可是这一次自己没法开口辩解，因为自己抛下病中的荣升，总觉得自己理亏。
Rong Sheng said nothing, but leaned back in the chair and felt at ease. A-Chu understood Rong Sheng; the tacit understanding between them ran deep. He knew Rong Sheng was waiting for him to speak and explain, but this time he could not open his mouth to defend himself, for having abandoned Rong Sheng in his illness he felt himself in the wrong.

> “到底什么事？你不想解释？那好，也许我看了这封信，就用不着听你解释了。”荣升动手去拆信。
“What was it, in the end? You don’t care to explain? Very well—perhaps once I have read this letter I shall not need to hear your explanation.” And Rong Sheng set his hand to opening it.

> “Please trust me！”阿初情急。
“Please trust me!” A-Chu cried in his alarm.

> 荣升隐隐约约的猜到这封信里装的是什么了。
Rong Sheng had a dim, half-formed guess at what the letter held.

> “少爷，请你相信我。如果我们之间的信任还在，请你把信还给我。”阿初走近荣升，恳切地说：“我现在站在这里，这封信就失去了存在的意义，请相信我！”阿初伸出手去。
“Young master, please believe me. If the trust between us still stands, then give me back the letter.” A-Chu came near to Rong Sheng and spoke earnestly. “I am standing here now; the letter has lost the meaning of its existence. Please believe me!” And A-Chu put out his hand.

> 荣升淡淡一笑，握着信的手舒展开来。
Rong Sheng gave a faint smile, and the hand that held the letter began to open.

> “别信他！”门“砰”的一声被撞开，丽水费劲地拖着一口打开的黑皮箱进来，双手一放一掀，皮箱里装的阿初的随身衣物、医疗器械、书本等东西杂乱无章地洒了一地。荣升看了一眼，就知道发生了什么事。
“Don’t believe him!” The door was flung open with a bang, and Lishui came hauling in, with an effort, an opened black suitcase. She set it down and tipped it over, and A-Chu’s personal clothes, medical instruments, books, and the rest spilled out in a heap across the floor. Rong Sheng took one look and knew what had happened.

> “他想逃！”丽水大声地吼。“要不是房东太太把他的箱子还回来，我们还被他蒙在鼓里。”
“He meant to run!” Lishui shouted. “If the landlady hadn’t brought his case back, we’d still have been kept in the dark by him.”

> “表姐，你出去。”
“Cousin, go out.”

> “表弟……”
“Cousin—”

> “这是我和他之间的事，你先出去。”荣升坚持。
“This is a matter between him and me. Go out for now.” Rong Sheng was firm.

> 丽水出是出去了，不过踩得地板震天响。
Out Lishui went—but she stamped the floorboards so that they shook the heavens.

> “什么时候的事？”荣升问。
“When was this decided?” Rong Sheng asked.

> 阿初话到嘴边，又咽回去。
The words rose to A-Chu’s lips, and he swallowed them again.

> “说话呀--”
“Speak—”

> 阿初踌躇地：“昨天晚上。”
A-Chu, hesitating: “Last night.”

> “是她辜负了你？”
“Did she fail you?”

> “不，是我辜负了她。”
“No—it was I who failed her.”

> 荣升颇感意外。这是他事先没有想到的。“为什么？”他问。阿初很痛苦，不知道如何讲清楚这一夜之间的逆转。荣升却突然想起昨夜自己恍恍惚惚听见的婴儿啼哭声。“你？为了那孩子？为了玛丽亚？”
Rong Sheng was quite taken aback; this he had not foreseen. “Why?” he asked. A-Chu was in anguish, not knowing how to make plain this reversal of a single night. But Rong Sheng suddenly recalled the infant’s cry he had heard, dim and dreamlike, the night before. “You? For the child’s sake? For Maria?”

> “是。”阿初答。
“Yes,” A-Chu answered.

> 荣升突然感到遗憾。但是，说出来得话却是另一种。“你知不知道，在上海，'私奔'是一件十分可耻的事。要是在乡下，'私奔'就是犯罪。罪犯是要被沉塘的。”
Rong Sheng felt a sudden regret. But what he said came out otherwise. “Do you know that in Shanghai, to ‘elope’ is a most shameful thing? Out in the countryside, to ‘elope’ is a crime—and the criminal is sunk in the pond to drown.”

> “这里不是乡下，这里是英国。少爷也不是封建家长，所以，阿初不会死。”
“This is not the countryside; this is England. And the young master is no feudal patriarch—so A-Chu will not die.”

> “这么肯定？”
“So certain of it?”

> “是。”阿初十分肯定。
“Yes.” A-Chu was quite certain.

> “我曾经为了'爱情'一度想放弃自己的生命。想不到，你却为了一条'生命'而放弃'爱情'，值得吗？”
“There was a time I once thought to give up my own life for the sake of ‘love.’ Who would have thought that you, for the sake of a single ‘life,’ would give up ‘love’? Was it worth it?”

> “值得。”
“It was worth it.”

> “为什么？”
“Why?”

> “'爱情'是生命中的点缀。”
“‘Love’ is an ornament upon life.”

> “是真的吗？”
“Is that true?”

> 阿初点头。
A-Chu nodded.

> “你真的是这样认为的？”
“Is that truly what you believe?”

> “是。”
“Yes.”

> “也许，这是你我最大的不同，我以为'爱情'是'生命'的全部。”
“Perhaps that is the greatest difference between you and me. I took ‘love’ to be the whole of ‘life.’”

> “少爷你失去了'爱情'，但是，你还活着。人活着，就有希望。包括'爱情'。也许，在不久的将来，在不远的地方，就有一位纯洁的女子，踏着月光，踩着露水，吹着哀伤的箫，等待你去唤醒她的心灵。'生命'对人来说，只有一次。珍惜'生命'就是珍惜'爱'。”
“Young master, you have lost your ‘love’—but you are still alive. And so long as a man lives, there is hope, ‘love’ among the rest. Perhaps in the not-distant future, in some not-distant place, there is a pure-hearted woman treading the moonlight, treading the dew, playing a mournful xiao, waiting for you to come and wake her soul. ‘Life’ comes to a man only once. To cherish ‘life’ is to cherish ‘love.’”

> 荣升感慨地说：“八年来，我一直为了失去的'爱'而困扰，以至于不能自拔。今天恍恍惚惚地又觉得自己还有希望。”
Rong Sheng said, moved, “For eight years I have been tormented by the ‘love’ I lost, so that I could not pull myself free of it. Today, dim and dreamlike, I feel once more that there may yet be hope for me.”

> “少爷你这八年来并没有病。”阿初说。
“Young master, these eight years you have not been ill at all,” A-Chu said.

> “你说什么？”荣升瞪大了眼睛。
“What did you say?” Rong Sheng’s eyes went wide.

> 阿初迎着少爷的目光说：“你没病！”
A-Chu met the young master’s gaze. “You are not ill!”

> 荣升瘫软地倒在椅子上，眼里有泪。
Rong Sheng sank limp into the chair, tears in his eyes.

> “自从少奶奶死了之后，你就把自己的心和她一起埋葬了。你埋葬了自己的心还不算，你连自己的身子也想毁掉，你不够勇敢，你没勇气杀死自己，你就病。你身子弱，全家上下都知道，要说大少爷装病，全家人没有人会相信。你明知道：虚不受补，越补越虚。你就不停地给自己灌补药，灌到自己吐血不止。”
“Ever since your wife died, you buried your own heart along with her. And burying your heart was not enough—you wanted to destroy your very body as well; but you were not brave enough, you had not the courage to kill yourself, and so you fell ill. You were frail of body, as the whole household knew; and if it were said that the eldest young master was feigning illness, not a soul in the family would have believed it. You knew perfectly well that a weakened body cannot take tonics—that the more you dose it, the weaker it grows—and so you poured tonic after tonic into yourself, until you were coughing up blood without end.”

> 荣升开始剧烈咳嗽。
Rong Sheng fell into a violent fit of coughing.

> “到了英国，我以为时过境迁，你会停止对自己的折磨。可是我错了，少爷你不但不想重新开始新生活，反而变本加厉。你吸鸦片，吸上了瘾。”
“When we came to England, I thought that with the change of time and place you would stop torturing yourself. But I was wrong. Far from wishing to begin a new life, young master, you only made it worse. You smoked opium, and smoked your way into an addiction.”

> “够了！”荣升大声断喝。“够了……已经太晚了……”
“Enough!” Rong Sheng cut him off with a shout. “Enough… it is already too late…”

> “不晚。少爷你还可以回头。”阿初平静地说。
“It is not too late. You can still turn back, young master,” A-Chu said calmly.

> “你说什么？”荣升霍地站起来。
“What did you say?” Rong Sheng started to his feet.

> 阿初指着衣柜上镶嵌的大镜子，说：“少爷你看，你目光清澈如水，身子虽然虚弱，但是精神状态良好。其实你已经在戒毒了。”
A-Chu pointed to the large mirror set into the wardrobe. “Look at yourself, young master. Your eyes are clear as water, and though your body is weak, your spirits are good. The truth is you have already been breaking the habit.”

> “从什么时候起？”
“Since when?”

> “三年前。我就开始让你戒毒了。我先试着减少你鸦片的用量，然后我用在医学院研制的戒毒膏化成水给你用药。我给你用了适当的镇静剂，让你睡眠多一些。”
“Three years ago I began weaning you off it. First I tried reducing your dose of opium; then I dissolved in water a cessation paste I had developed at the college and gave it to you as medicine. I gave you the right measure of sedative, so that you would sleep a little more.”

> 荣升恍然大悟。“怪不得这几年我老是睡不醒。”
Light dawned on Rong Sheng. “No wonder these last few years I could never wake up properly.”

> “但是你对鸦片的心理依赖依然故我，于是，我就……”阿初不知道该不该让他知道真相。
“But your psychological dependence on the opium was just as it had always been, and so I…” A-Chu did not know whether or not he ought to let him know the truth.

> “说下去。”荣升在鼓励他。
“Go on,” Rong Sheng urged him.

> “于是，我就用罌粟壳熬成水冒充鸦片汁给你用。在你不知不觉中，把你染上的毒瘾降到最低限度。还记得你的金烟枪吗？”
“And so I boiled poppy husks into a liquid and passed it off to you as opium juice. Without your ever knowing it, I brought the addiction you had taken on down to the very least. Do you remember your gold opium pipe?”

> “不是不翼而飞了吗？”
“Didn’t it sprout wings and vanish?”

> “我拿去卖了。”阿初说。
“I took it and sold it,” A-Chu said.

> “你当自己是什么？”荣升板着脸。
“And who do you take yourself for?” Rong Sheng said, his face stern.

> “我当自己是医生。”阿初坦然自若地说。
“I take myself for a doctor,” A-Chu said, calm and unruffled.

> 二人对着镜子都不禁莞尔一笑。
Facing the mirror, the two of them could not help a slight smile.

> “少爷，我们回国吧。”阿初认真地说。
“Young master, let us go home,” A-Chu said in earnest.

> “回国？你以为我没想过吗？路费呢？难道我们插翅飞过海去？”
“Go home? Do you think I haven’t thought of it? And the fare? Are we to grow wings and fly across the sea?”

> “少爷手上不就拿着路费吗？”阿初的眼光指向荣升手中的信。
“Isn’t the fare there in the young master’s own hand?” A-Chu’s eyes went to the letter Rong Sheng held.

> “你是预谋已久。”荣升说。
“So you have been plotting this a long while,” Rong Sheng said.

> 门外边稀里哗啦地一片响，阿初推开门，看见丽水把煮好的咖啡洒了一地。丽水汽得一边跺脚，一边躬下身去用抹布擦拭地板。
There was a great clatter beyond the door; A-Chu pushed it open to find that Lishui had spilled the coffee she had made all over the floor. In her fury she stamped her foot even as she bent to wipe the boards with a cloth.

> “我来吧。”阿初从丽水手中接过抹布。丽水端着咖啡，乜斜着眼在他身上晃了晃，看见荣升悠闲地往楼上走，丽水喊了一嗓子：“表弟，你就这样算了？”
“Let me.” A-Chu took the cloth from Lishui’s hand. Lishui, holding the coffee, cast him a sidelong look up and down, and, seeing Rong Sheng stroll leisurely up the stairs, called out, “Cousin, are you going to let this go, just like that?”

> 荣升回过头来看了他们一下，说：“他不好好地在这吗？你们好好相处吧，就快回国了。”
Rong Sheng turned to look at the pair of them. “Isn’t he here, safe and sound? The two of you get along well—we’ll be going home soon.”

> “回国？”丽水端着咖啡欢天喜地跟过去：“真的吗？”
“Going home?” Lishui, coffee in hand, followed after in high delight. “Truly?”

> “真的……”姐弟二人有说有笑地上楼去了。擦拭地板的阿初把抹布扔掉，接着，仰面朝天的躺在地板上，心里想着：“丛惠，去了哪里？你回国了吗？”
“Truly…” And cousin and cousin went up the stairs talking and laughing. A-Chu, wiping the floor, tossed the cloth aside, then lay back flat upon the boards, his face to the ceiling, thinking: “Cong Hui—where have you gone? Have you gone home?”

> 这一夜没有了箫声。
That night there was no sound of the xiao.

> 一个月后，荣升和阿初结束了在威尔逊卡迪芙的客居生活，准备回国。
A month later, Rong Sheng and A-Chu brought their sojourn in Cardiff to an end and made ready to return home.

> 启程的那一天，阿初早上依旧去出诊，在中午回来得路上，依旧绕道去了一趟卡迪芙邮电局，依然是一无所获。阿初在邮电局给上海的荣家发了封即将回国的电报，然后他在镇上要了一辆四人乘坐的马车，坐着马车赶回旅店。
On the day of departure A-Chu still went out on his morning rounds, and on the way back at noon he still made his detour to the Cardiff post and telegraph office, and still came away with nothing. At the office A-Chu sent a telegram to the Rong family in Shanghai to say they were soon coming home, and then hired in the town a carriage that seated four and rode it back to the inn.

> 丽水把整理好的行李堆放在门口，等马车一到，就招呼荣升出门。阿初从马车上跳下来，先服侍荣升、丽水上了马车，然后把行李一件件搬上去，等他搬完最后一个旅行包，回头的一瞬间，他看见全院的人都出来了，房东太太噙着泪朝自己招手，玛丽亚抱着刚满月的婴儿站在风口上，大家纷纷走过来和阿初拥抱。
Lishui had piled the packed luggage at the door, and when the carriage arrived she called Rong Sheng out. A-Chu sprang down from it, helped Rong Sheng and Lishui aboard first, then loaded the luggage on piece by piece; and just as he had carried up the last travelling bag, in the instant he turned his head he saw that everyone in the yard had come out. The landlady, tears in her eyes, waved to him; Maria, her month-old infant in her arms, stood in the mouth of the wind; and one after another they came over to embrace A-Chu.

> “Have a good journey！”
“Have a good journey!”

> “Take care！”在祝福和保重声中，阿初的眼睛渐渐模糊。
“Take care!” Amid the blessings and the words of care, A-Chu’s eyes gradually blurred.

> “他在磨蹭什么？”丽水在马车上嘀咕了一句。
“What is he dawdling at?” Lishui muttered from inside the carriage.

> “他赢得了人们的尊重。”荣升悄然地放下车帘。
“He has won people’s respect,” Rong Sheng said quietly, and let the carriage curtain fall.

> 阿初上了车，马车开始向前奔驰。玛丽亚把孩子交给木匠约翰，沿着马车奔跑……
A-Chu boarded, and the carriage began to speed forward. Maria handed her child to John the carpenter and ran alongside the carriage…

> 阿初发觉后，朝玛丽亚喊：“Go back！”
When A-Chu saw it, he called out to Maria, “Go back!”

> 只听得玛丽亚那嘶哑的声音：“Have a good journey……”那声音在马蹄声中渐渐逝去。
He heard only Maria’s hoarse voice: “Have a good journey…”, and the sound faded away little by little into the beat of the horses’ hooves.

> 下午三点三十分，离开船还有十五分钟，马车停在了一家钟表店的门口。
At three thirty in the afternoon, with fifteen minutes still to go before the ship sailed, the carriage stopped at the door of a watch-and-clock shop.

> “在车上等我。”荣升单独下了车，走进钟表店。
“Wait for me in the carriage.” Rong Sheng got down alone and went into the shop.

> “Welcome！”钟表店老板从柜台里站起来。
“Welcome!” The shopkeeper rose from behind the counter.

> “Afternoon！”荣升走近柜台。他记得几年前，自己刚到卡迪芙的时候，曾经光顾过这家钟表店，当时阿初极力怂恿他买一块古典的怀表，自己没有答应。几年来，这家钟表店没有任何变化，只是当年的古式的怀表已经没有了。
“Afternoon!” Rong Sheng came up to the counter. He remembered that some years before, when he had first arrived in Cardiff, he had patronized this shop, and that A-Chu had done all he could to press him to buy an old-fashioned pocket watch, which he had refused. In all those years the shop had not changed at all—only the antique pocket watch of that time was gone.

> “What can I do for you？”老板眯着眼睛揣摩着顾客的心思。
“What can I do for you?” The shopkeeper narrowed his eyes, taking the measure of his customer’s mind.

> 荣升隔着玻璃看中了一块雅致的金表，他用手指隔着玻璃轻轻叩击了那块表。“Can I have a look at this watch？”
Through the glass Rong Sheng’s eye fell on an elegant gold watch, and he tapped lightly at it through the glass with his finger. “Can I have a look at this watch?”

> “Well，there are only two watche soft hisstyle left，it's really good。”老板从柜台里取出金表。
“Well, there are only two watches of this style left; it’s really good.” The shopkeeper took the gold watch out from the case.

> 荣升把表搁在耳边，听了听。又把它放在手心上，表壳十分的精致，表链泛着金光。荣升非常满意地示意老板把表包装起来。
Rong Sheng held the watch to his ear and listened. Then he laid it in his palm; the case was exquisitely fine, the chain shining with a golden light. Well pleased, Rong Sheng signaled the shopkeeper to wrap it up.

> 马车上，丽水开始烦躁起来：“就快开船了，他不会又变卦了吧？”又催着阿初下去看看。阿初掀起车帘，正看见少爷从钟表店里走出来。“Thank you，see you。”钟表店老板谦恭地送客。
In the carriage Lishui was growing agitated. “The ship’s about to sail—he hasn’t changed his mind again, has he?” And she urged A-Chu to get down and take a look. A-Chu lifted the curtain just in time to see the young master come out of the shop. “Thank you, see you.” The shopkeeper saw his customer off with a bow.

> 荣升登车，三个人重新坐好。
Rong Sheng boarded, and the three of them settled themselves again.

> 荣升从口袋里摸出包装好的金表，递给阿初，说：“Happy Birthday！”
Rong Sheng fished the wrapped gold watch from his pocket, handed it to A-Chu, and said, “Happy Birthday!”

> “谢谢少爷。”阿初接过包装盒，小心翼翼地打开盒子，一块色泽明亮的金表呈现在阿初面前。“谢谢少爷！”阿初把表戴在手腕上，金光闪闪。
“Thank you, young master.” A-Chu took the box, opened it with great care, and there before him lay a bright-gleaming gold watch. “Thank you, young master!” A-Chu fastened it on his wrist, where it shone with a golden glitter.

> “哇！好漂亮的表！”丽水由衷地发出惊叹声，“表弟，你偏心。”丽水和荣升闹。
“Ah! What a lovely watch!” Lishui exclaimed from the heart. “Cousin, you play favorites.” And Lishui made a playful fuss at Rong Sheng.

> “等你过生日的时候再说。”荣升笑着对付丽水的胡搅蛮缠。
“We’ll see when your own birthday comes,” Rong Sheng said with a laugh, fending off Lishui’s willful teasing.

> 马车继续前进。
The carriage rolled on.

> 另一辆马车驶来，与他们的马车擦肩而过。那辆马车停在钟表店门口，披着披风的丛惠走下了车。
Another carriage came driving up and passed theirs shoulder to shoulder. That carriage stopped at the door of the watch shop, and Cong Hui, wrapped in a cloak, stepped down.

> “Welcome！”钟表店老板热情接待。
“Welcome!” The shopkeeper received her warmly.

> “Afternoon！”丛惠漫不经心地答应着，隔着玻璃看中了一块金表，恰恰和荣升看中的是同一款式。她用手指了指表，老板立即替她取出来。
“Afternoon!” Cong Hui answered absently, and through the glass her eye fell on a gold watch—the very same style Rong Sheng had chosen. She pointed at it, and the shopkeeper took it out for her at once.

> “不知道他喜不喜欢。”她喃喃自语。
“I wonder whether he’ll like it,” she murmured to herself.

> 丛惠买下了表，用光了身上所有的钱。当她疲惫地拖着一口皮箱出现在阿初住过的旅店时，才知道一切的一切都晚了。
Cong Hui bought the watch, spending every penny she had. And when, wearily dragging a suitcase, she appeared at the inn where A-Chu had lodged, she learned that all of it, every bit, had come too late.

> 也许，今生已经错过了。丛惠，这样想。
Perhaps, in this life, it was already missed. So Cong Hui thought.

> 那一刻，是1931年3月16日下午三点四十五分。
That moment was three forty-five in the afternoon, the sixteenth of March, 1931.
