### Lending a Hand

When people ride a rickshaw across the bridges along Suzhou Creek—the Pentang Lane Bridge, the Queen-of-Heaven Temple Bridge, the Old Lock Bridge, the Rubbish Bridge—no sooner does the rickshaw start up the rise of a bridge than some beggar, hair matted and face filthy, will grip the shaft with one hand and bawl out: "Give it a pull!" And once he has hauled it up onto the deck, he spreads his five fingers and demands money, saying: "Boss, one copper, just a trifle." On and on he nags without a stop, and if you give him no copper he goes off pouting and grumbling; and should the passenger be a woman, then, given nothing, he will often curse foully and insult her. There are strong beggars among them, small beggars, and women beggars too, and from seven in the morning until one at night are the working hours laid down for them.
