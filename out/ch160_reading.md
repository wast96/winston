### The Frisk

The police authorities of both the Chinese and the foreign concessions, mindful of the rampancy of the criminal element and the spread of the opium plague, and with a view to safeguarding the peace within their bounds and rooting out the poison, frequently mount searches of passersby—what is popularly called "the frisk." Anyone found carrying unlicensed firearms, or such contraband as raw opium, opium pellets, morphine, or huahui lottery slips, is straightway hauled off to court and punished under the law. When the searches were first carried out, those riding in motorcars, and women, were exempt from being frisked. But afterward, since plenty of criminals had slipped in among the wealthy motorcar passengers and the women too, the searches came in time to be applied to high and low alike, without distinction. Women had never been searched; but once it was found that they were hiding contraband on their persons, a squad of female detectives was specially hired to search them as well.

When a passerby or a rider is halted by a constable to be searched, he ought simply to let it be done; this is the officer's duty, carried out under the law. To take fright—to bolt, or to resist the search—is the very mark of a guilty conscience, and is besides against the law and not to be done. I recall that two years ago, during a search of pedestrians between Ningbo Road and Tianjin Road, the young proprietor of a certain shop, seeing the constables come swarming up, was seized with terror and all at once took to his heels. The constables mistook him for a bandit, pulled the trigger, and a single shot ran him clean through, so that he dropped stiff and did not rise again. He had brought it entirely upon himself, and threw his life away for nothing.
