### Avenue Joffre Turns Russian

On the French Concession there is a very long, very broad road called Avenue Joffre; only, before the European War it was not called Avenue Joffre but Avenue Paul Brunat. Once the European War had ended, the French residents, wishing to commemorate the deeds of their General Joffre, had the whole road renamed Avenue Joffre, to show they would never forget.

Because this road lies in the middle reach of the French Concession, with easy traffic and, along its sides, trees in leafy shade and a very fine prospect, the moneyed classes rent and settle here; and the Russian émigrés too are peculiarly thick upon it (just as the Japanese are peculiarly thick on North Sichuan Road). The various businesses they run — pharmacies, general stores, bars, late-supper rooms, dance-halls, massage parlors, barbershops, apartment houses — are all to be had, while the Russian beggars pacing to and fro along the road and chasing after alms are also not few. And there are stout, well-fed Russian women, thickly powdered and rouged a lurid red, wandering back and forth in the gathering dusk, positively everywhere the eye turns. Such being the scene, has it not become the Russianizing of Avenue Joffre?
