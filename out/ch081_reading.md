### Salable Looks

"So-and-so has good salable looks; such-a-one has poor salable looks"—talk of this kind is forever to be heard. But how are these two words, "salable looks," to be understood? Simply this: for the man scraping a living in society, the first requisite is salable looks, and only so can he win the advantage everywhere and be held in esteem; lacking them, he can only lose out at every turn and meet with cold contempt.

The meaning of "salable looks" is this: a man must be tall of build and his five features well-set, clear and quick of speech, fluent in his repartee—talking ghost-talk to a ghost and man-talk to a man—and his clothes and hat and shoes must be in the latest fashion; only then can he be hailed as "a man of good salable looks." Whether his belly be stuffed with straw, or he understands not a thing, matters not at all. Put so, the eyes of society are wholly a case of "judging a man by his looks, and so being wrong about Ziyu." Turn it about: let a man carry within him a bellyful of learning and a pen that could dash off a thousand words leaning against a horse, and yet, if he has no salable looks, be everywhere cheated and everywhere run up against a wall—this is the very token of how shallow people's eyes are and how deep their snobbery runs.

The rest—those who eat organization rice, or charity rice, or who run errands in the various trades (also called "brokers," the middlemen of buying and selling)—these too must have salable looks first of all; and still more do the road-politicians and the speculators, who go rushing east and west with a great briefcase in hand. With salable looks a man may hope, in time, to cut a great figure, rise in office, and grow rich.
