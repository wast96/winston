## Nineteen

The great clock struck nine, clang upon clang. The clear, slow, quivering sound of the metal wire carried into the ears of Wu Sunfu on the bed in the next room, and the closed eyelids seemed to twitch faintly. Yet the black tide of dream still lay heavy on his nerves. In his dream too he heard the clear notes of a bell; but that was a hurried bell, the bell on the striking-board of the exchange, the bell that proclaimed the "opening," the signal-cannon that began the "decisive battle" of Wu Sunfu and his fellows!

It was for this bell in his dream that the sleeping Wu Sunfu's eyelids twitched faintly. The bonds' "settlement day" fell the day after tomorrow but one; up to yesterday, Wu Sunfu and his fellows had thrown every scrap of the painfully scraped-together "reserve funds" onto the "front," and had opened a fierce assault along the whole line; and yet the bulls' array showed no great sign of wavering! Their one hope now was that the friendly forces of Du Zhuzhai would speedily take the field. Last night Wu Sunfu had wrangled over it with Du Zhuzhai again till deep in the night. This was already the fourth round of "diplomacy toward Du"! Du Zhuzhai's showing was not yet such as to leave Wu Sunfu and his fellows in despair. And yet, when all was said, this was a perilous pass!

All at once the sleeping, dreaming Wu Sunfu gave a savage laugh, then knitted his brows, clenched his teeth, and his whole body gave a jerk. Abruptly he opened his eyes; his blood-red eyeballs stared fixedly in a daze, and a fine sweat gradually spread over his brow. The thing in his dream had frightened his heart too much. A wan yellow sun played its shadows at the window, and from far off a light breeze bore in the muddy noise of the city.

"Luckily it was a dream! No more than a dream!" — Wu Sunfu thought over and over, as he rose hurriedly and left the bed. Yet as he washed his face, he saw again Zhao Botao's face from the dream come swimming into the basin; a face all sly smiles, the smile of victory! And passing carelessly before the tall dressing-mirror, he turned his head and saw again that his own face was plainly set in the look of a beaten man. In the great drawing-room and the great dining-room the servants were in a bustle changing the sofa-covers and carrying out the carpets to beat them; catching sight of it, Wu Sunfu thought all at once that the house was already mortgaged out, and that if by the due date he could not clear the mortgage loan, there would be no avoiding just such a bustle of moving out and giving up the place.

He felt that everywhere in the house there were gloating eyes mocking him. He felt that to sit in the "rear" waiting for news was ten times, twenty times harder to bear than to go up to the front in person! He gave no more thought to having arranged with Sun Jiren to meet at ten o'clock the day before, but took his car and went out.

Still at the record-breaking speed of 1930, the car flew along the not-very-busy road; yet Wu Sunfu inside it felt that the car too was making trouble for him, simply refusing to run fast. And all at once he found that at some point even that listless wan-yellow sun had gone into hiding, and now there was a fine rain, like smoke, like mist. And this dismal scene was very familiar too. Just so! It had been on just such a morning of fine, fog-like rain, when everything had likewise lost its clear outline and its air of might, that he too had sat in a car racing toward a dim, uncertain road ahead. Abruptly, out of the dust-buried past, a memory sprang up: two months before, on the very morning of the "decisive battle" when he and Zhao Botao had played the bulls together, it had been just such a dismal, rainy day as this! Yet now the scene was unchanged and the men were changed! Now he and Zhao Botao stood in opposing camps! And the weighty, decisive Du Zhuzhai — his attitude was past all guessing!

Alone in the car, Wu Sunfu bared his teeth in a dry laugh. He asked himself: even were he to hurry to the exchange and "come up to the front in person," what use would it be after all? The turn of victory or defeat should long since have been decided yesterday, the day before, the day before that; yet yesterday, the day before, the day before that, had long gone by, and every one of them had been met and laid out with the last drop of his money-power spent; so that today's victory or defeat in these last five minutes did not, it seemed, rest wholly on human strength either. Just so! Today they were still to fire their last cannon. It was exactly as if the commander-in-chief in a decisive battle were sending even his own bodyguard brigade up to the front to join the firing-line, to launch the last assault on the enemy. But to give the order to the front-line commander was enough — what need to come up to the front in person? — Wu Sunfu knitted his brows and laughed his savage laugh, and in his heart there was one thought: "Go home and wait for news!" And yet he could never get it past his lips. He had lost even this much of decision now! For all that he chid himself in his anxiety — "Be calm! Even in defeat, one must be calm!" — in plain fact he simply could not calm himself at all!

In just such wavering and anxiety the car bore Wu Sunfu to the door of the exchange and stopped. As in a dream, Wu Sunfu squeezed in through the great gate of the exchange and made straight for the "number-stall" of the broker Lu Kuangshi. It seemed the market was not yet open; the whole floor was a din of clamouring voices. But Wu Sunfu seemed to see none of it, to hear none of it; before his eyes there rose up only Zhao Botao's face, filling all space, from the sky above to the earth below.

In the broker's stall, not much bigger than a policeman's sentry-box, a stout gentleman was already crammed, telephoning away. This was none other than Wang Hefu. The broker Lu Kuangshi stood outside the "sentry-box" talking with his assistant. Wu Sunfu's coming stirred not a soul to notice; only when he was standing at Wang Hefu's side did Lu Kuangshi turn his head with a start and see him, and just then Wang Hefu too hung up the telephone.

"Ah, Sunfu! I was just looking for you! You've come at the right moment!"

Wang Hefu sprang up as he spoke, seized Wu Sunfu, dragged him into the "sentry-box," and stuffed him into the little corner beside the telephone, as if in dread of anyone's seeing him. Wu Sunfu smiled bitterly and wanted to speak, but in his haste could find no opening. But Wang Hefu, bending down, first asked in a low voice:

"You've not seen Jiren? — He'll be coming here too in a while. How stands it with Zhuzhai after all? Has he made up his mind?"

"There's an eight-tenths certainty of it. But he'll hardly be willing to go in for it in a big way. At most a matter of one million."

Wu Sunfu, the moment he opened his mouth, was optimistic again, and in truth he gradually grew calm. Wang Hefu stroked his beard and smiled.

"He can throw out one million? Splendid! But Sunfu, we ourselves are cleaned out dry today; your silk-mill mortgage loan can't be brought off after all — Jiren and I schemed out no end of ways yesterday, and not one of them came off anywhere. Today we can only — "

"Can only what? Surely the one hundred thousand dollars settled on the day before yesterday hasn't fallen through too?"

"That — luckily that hasn't fallen through! Today we can only hold to that little sum and work with it."

"Then we throw it out the moment the market opens? Have you given Mengxiang his instructions?"

"Ah, ah! Don't so much as mention any Mengxiang to me again! Only last night did we learn that this man too is not to be trusted! We had meant to use him for a blind, so that every short sale went through his hands — and who'd have thought he was secretly reporting it all to Zhao Botao! Isn't that the very worst of it?"

As Wang Hefu said this, his voice was thin as the whine of a mosquito. Wu Sunfu did not hear it all, but he understood all of it; his face changed of a sudden, there was a buzzing in his ears, and black stars leapt wildly before his eyes. Betrayed by his own subordinates again! This was sharper than any blow! After a while Wu Sunfu ground out one sentence between his teeth:

"The heart of man is past all fathoming indeed! — Then, Hefu, today when we sell short, we must have Lu Kuangshi pass it through?"

"No! We've found another broker, everything's already arranged. The moment the market opens, we sell!"

The words were scarcely out when the bell crashed out outside — the market was open! Then came the thunder of dealing, rolling and roaring, till the very building seemed to shake. And Wang Hefu ran out. But Wu Sunfu sat without stirring. He could not stir; he felt his two legs would no longer obey him, and there was a buzzing in his ears again. The black stars leapt wildly before his eyes once more. Never had he been so weak — he was truly changed!

All at once Wang Hefu came running back, out of breath and crestfallen, and, wringing his hands, cried out to Wu Sunfu:

"Ai, ai! The opening's come out risen again! Up half a dollar!"

"Ah — throw it out quick! Take that one hundred thousand you're holding and throw it all out!"

Wu Sunfu started up and cried it aloud, but of a sudden a fit of giddiness took him, and with it a nausea at the pit of his stomach; his two legs went soft and he toppled over, his eyes staring wide, his face deathly white. Wang Hefu, frightened till his fingertips went cold, sprang forward, pinched the philtrum above Wu Sunfu's lip with one hand and pulled at his hair with the other. In the urgency of the moment there was no one to lend a hand. Just as they were all in a flurried heap, Sun Jiren luckily arrived; Sun Jiren was cooler, and quick-witted besides, and seeing a cup of cold water at hand, he took a mouthful and sprayed it in Wu Sunfu's face. Wu Sunfu's eyeballs stirred, and with a gurgle he spat out a mass of thick phlegm.

"Throw it out, quick — "

Wu Sunfu opened his eyes wide — still the same one sentence. Sun Jiren and Wang Hefu exchanged a glance. Then Sun Jiren, patting Wu Sunfu on the shoulder, said:

"Set your mind at rest, Sunfu! We'll see to it here; you go home! It's crowded and close here, you can't stay!"

"It's nothing! That was only a rush of phlegm for the moment, it's passed now! — But — is it thrown out?"

Wu Sunfu stood up all at once as he spoke; his colour and the look in his eyes were indeed much better, but his brow was red as fire. This was no healthy red, as Sun Jiren saw very clearly; so, heedless of how Wu Sunfu insisted he would not go, he dragged him out by main force and put him into the car.

Just at this time there was breaking out in the market a duel between "bulls" and "bears" such as had never been before! Wu Sunfu and his fellows had fired their last cannon! One million five hundred thousand in Troop-Disbandment bonds was flung on the market at a single throw, and the board that was hung out showed the price falling step by step!

Had the friendly force of Wu Sunfu and his fellows, Du Zhuzhai, joined the firing-line at this juncture, the "bears" would have won an utter victory. But just as Wu Sunfu's car was pulling away from the door of the exchange, Du Zhuzhai came driving up in his. The chauffeurs on either side squeezed their horns in greeting, but the masters in the two cars neither of them noticed. Zhuzhai's car stopped with a screech; Sunfu's car flew back to the mansion as if on wings.

Perhaps it was just the din of voices and the reek of sweat in the exchange that had made Wu Sunfu faint for the moment; in the car he was already much better, and the unnatural fire at his brow gradually withdrew, so that he could think a little "rationally." But this "rational" thinking turned his colour bit by bit to a pallor; his heart settled heavy as lead at the pit of his chest, pressing on his breath.

The misty fine rain had now turned to a downpour pelting straight down. The wind, too, had an edge that pierced. When he got out of the car at home, Wu Sunfu gave a sudden shiver, and the hairs stood up all over his body. Ah-xuan and Lin Peishan were shouting and laughing loudly in the great dining-room, and just as Wu Sunfu was passing, Ah-xuan came dashing out with some book or other in his hand, Lin Peishan chasing behind. Wu Sunfu knitted his brows, turned his face aside, and walked past. Of late he had had no spirit to spare for such small matters, and the Fourth Young Lady's revolt too had insensibly shrunk his authority within the household — at the least, Ah-xuan had grown a shade more unbridled than before.

When he had settled himself in the study, the first order Wu Sunfu gave the servant was "Fetch Dr. Ding," and the second order was "Any stranger who calls, turn away without exception!" He had still a third order about to be issued, when suddenly a telegram on the desk drew off his attention; and so with a wave of his hand he dismissed the servant and looked at the telegram.

It was a telegram Tang Yunshan had sent from Hong Kong, some thirty or fifty characters, not yet deciphered. Wu Sunfu took up the telegraph code-book and had rendered out seven or eight characters, when he forgot clean away the third order he had not yet issued. But all at once he thought of another matter, tossed the telegram aside, and snatched up the telephone. He hesitated a moment, and in the end called the number of Du Zhuzhai's mansion. When he had made sure of Zhuzhai's whereabouts, a little smile came to Wu Sunfu's face. The one chance in ten thousand of hope swelled again in his heart until it became ten per cent, twenty per cent, thirty!

And what added a ladle of oil to this rekindled hope was that Tang Yunshan's telegram turned out to be good news after all: he reported that his affairs went smoothly, that the times showed a turn for the better, and that he had already, in Hong Kong, come to terms with a number of powerful men of the industrial and commercial world, so that the Yizhong company might yet stage a comeback; and last, he said he would return to Shanghai within the day.

Wu Sunfu could not help laughing aloud to himself. Was it not true that Heaven does not fail the man who takes pains!

But this burst of gladness cooled again in a twinkling. Though the shadow of a smile still hung at the corner of Wu Sunfu's mouth, it was a bitter smile now. This coming to terms with powerful men of the Hong Kong industrial and commercial world — might it not be a hollow dumpling? And of such "hollow dumplings" Tang Yunshan had served up more than once! Moreover, even if this time's "dumpling" was not, after all, "hollow," could distant water quench a near fire? The decisive battle here on the bond market must, at the latest, be won or lost tomorrow! What Wu Sunfu and his fellows were contending for was just the "now";

the "now" was everything, the "now" was the "reality"!

And even if this time it was not a "hollow dumpling," Wu Sunfu could not but blame Tang Yunshan for being too muddle-headed. Had he not wired him again and again: the moment you raise the money, wire it up at once? And now here it was, still only an empty telegram! To return to Shanghai within the day, was it? As though Hong Kong were still the eighteenth century, where great silver ingots passed current and a man must fetch them himself! Others were in the fire, and he in the water!

Thinking so, Wu Sunfu lost from his face even the shadow of that bitter smile. The misery after a bout of empty rejoicing is worse than never having had the rejoicing at all. He had just meant, on finishing the telegram, to ring up Sun Jiren and the rest and report the glad news, but now he had not the spirit for it. He sat in his chair with his head in his hands, and felt his head afire; he stood and paced a few steps, and then it was a shiver at every step, cold water sluicing down his spine. He sat, and stood again, and stood, and sat, as though flung now into a fire-heap, now rolled into an ice-cellar.

He had to own that he had fallen ill. Just so! Ever since the strike at his mill last time, he had got this queer malady, and it often came on him. And just now, in the exchange, he had actually fainted away! Could this be the first stage of a stroke of the brain? The old Master had died of a stroke of the brain! "How is it Dr. Ding is still not here? Curse it! At the pinch of need, there's not a soul to be relied on!" — and for no reason Wu Sunfu vented his anger on an irrelevant third party!

Suddenly the telephone bell rang. How anxious that jangling sound was to the ear!

The flesh leapt on Wu Sunfu's whole body. He knew this must be Sun Jiren and the others come to report the state of the market; as he took up the receiver, his hand shook too; he clenched his teeth and called "Hello" twice, feebly, then held his breath to listen to the report on which life and death hung. But unexpectedly his brows lifted, there was light again in his eyes, and then he actually smiled.

"Oh — risen and then fallen, has it! — Oh! Fallen down to thirty-three? — Ai, ai! — A pity! — It looks as if the bulls' appetite has weakened already? Ha — the Reorganization bonds have only just opened? — What? — You mean to throw out another two million? — Margin on account? — I'm for it! — Yunshan's wire came just now, there's a sure thing over there. — Just so, we may as well go at it with a free hand! — The money's not wired up yet, but we go at it with a free hand! — Oh, so Old Zhao too is staking all on a single throw; the two of them are a match. — Oh, so it's clear Han Mengxiang deserves to die indeed! Without his going and reporting our position, Old Zhao would have lost his nerve yesterday! — Just so! Later on we must give the fellow something to remember us by! — Zhuzhai? He got to the exchange long ago! — You've not seen him? Go and look for him! — Oh … "

Wu Sunfu hung up the receiver, and his face sank of a sudden again. This was not gloom; this was fury. That Han Mengxiang should be so untrustworthy — most unpardonable! And there was Liu Yuying besides! The shameless creature, playing informer at both ends! How many great matters are ruined by such conscienceless, faithless "subordinates"! Not to speak of the ones surnamed Han and Liu — even his own true-born younger sister, the Fourth Young Lady, would not make allowances for him, but took him for a tiger, and even ran away and would not come home!

A gust of fury shot straight at his heart like a flight of wild arrows, and Wu Sunfu trembled all over. His face iron-grey, his teeth clenched, he strode swiftly about the room. Of late his authority had been undermined past all recognition! He must rouse himself and make a stand! Once this pass on the exchange, this bond crisis, was over, he must rebuild his old authority! In society, and in the household, he must once more be the incarnation of a dread and sacred majesty! As he walked, as he thought, he promised himself many hopes, many plans for the future! Only let this present struggle on the bond market reach a favourable stage, and he would set them all going one by one!

The telephone bell rang again all at once, as urgent as ever!

This time, having first swallowed his "heart-settling pill," Wu Sunfu was not so flustered as before; his hand took up the receiver, firm and quick. The moment he caught the voice, he called back:

"Is that you, Hefu? — Oh, oh, speak! It's all right! Speak!"

Outside the window a wild wind sprang up of a sudden, and in the garden the trees roared in fury. Listening at the telephone, Wu Sunfu changed colour all at once, and cried out shrilly:

"What! Risen, has it? — Someone's scooping it up on the low price we've pressed it to! — Oh! Not Old Zhao, but a new account? Who, who is it? — Ah! Is it Zhuzhai? — Ai, ai! — The whole tide has turned against us! … "

Bang! Wu Sunfu flung the receiver on the desk, stepped back, and collapsed into the sofa, his eyes staring straight ahead, doing nothing but pant. Who would have thought that Zhuzhai would play just this stroke! The great matter was ruined in his hands! Then that frank and open talk with him last night, that talk in which he had told him all the ins and outs, the true and the false, of the market — had it not become throwing the door open and bowing the robber in? — "Ai! Every one has turned against me, deserted me! I, Wu Sunfu, in what have I ever done a man wrong!" Only this one thought pounded hard at Wu Sunfu's heart. Of a sudden he gave a savage laugh, sprang up and darted to the desk, pulled open a drawer with one hand, snatched out a pistol, and levelled the muzzle at his own breast. His face was black shot through with purple, his eyeballs starting as if they would burst out.

Outside the window the wild wind roared in fury, and the slanting rain beat rat-tat-tat on the glass. But the pistol was not fired. Wu Sunfu heaved a long sigh, his body sank into the swivel-chair, and the pistol dropped to the floor. Just at this moment the servant Li Gui showed Dr. Ding in.

Wu Sunfu started up and, with a savage laugh, cried out to Dr. Ding:

"Just now a certain thing all but happened that would have cost you some trouble; but now it's over. Since you're here, pray sit down a while!"

Dr. Ding shrugged his shoulders in astonishment, and before he could open his mouth, Wu Sunfu had already turned away and snatched up the telephone to make another call. This time it was to his mill. When he had made sure it was Tu Weiyue, he only gave one order in a stern voice: "Shut the whole mill down tomorrow!" Then, paying no more heed to the squeaking in the receiver, he hung up with one hand, turned his face to Dr. Ding, and said with a faint smile:

"Dr. Ding, where would you say is the better place to go to escape the heat? I've a mind for a bit of sea air!"

"Then that would be Qingdao! Or else, farther off, Qinhuangdao would do!"

"And what of Guling?"

"Guling is good too, but there's no sea air there; and besides, these few days they say the Red Army is attacking Ji'an, Changsha is under siege, and Nanchang and Jiujiang are both in a hard press! — "

"Ha-ha-ha, that's no matter! I've just a mind to go and see what a wonder this Red Army is, with its three heads and six arms! Likely they're no more than bandits! It's been that all along nobody paid them any heed, and so indulged them into what they are! But, Dr. Ding, pray sit a while; I'll go and give a few orders and be back."

Laughing wildly and strangely, Wu Sunfu stood up and walked out of the study, and ran straight upstairs. Now that he knew all was over, he had grown calm again; he stepped softly into his own room, and saw the young mistress leaning wearily on the sofa by the window, reading a book.

"Peiyao! Quick, have them pack up; this very night we're to board a steamer and leave the wharf. Off to escape the heat!"

The young mistress started violently and stood up abruptly; the book on her knees dropped to the floor, and out from its pages flew a withered white rose. This book, this dried flower, Wu Sunfu was seeing now for the third time, but as on the two times before, this time again his mind was caught in a thousand cares, and it slipped past his notice. The young mistress reddened, threw a glance at the floor, and answered vaguely:

"Wouldn't that be too cramped and hurried? But — as you will."
