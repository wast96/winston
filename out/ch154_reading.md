### Hatching Bean Sprouts

The three words "hatching bean sprouts" are a figure for a man who has no clothes and burrows under the quilt, unable to go out—like a bean hidden away in the straw that has not yet put forth its sprout. But hatching bean sprouts is of two kinds, the temporary and the permanent. The temporary is your band of dissolute young men, who go out of doors got up in the very pink of fashion, for all the world like some fine young gallant of a corrupt age; but once abroad, they eat it all up, gamble it all away, and spend it all clean, until they are down to rags and dare not show their faces for shame, and put up for the while at some inn, waiting to be relieved. The permanent kind is your bottommost vagrant, who eats the sun by day and the dew by night: three or four of them will club together and make a household of a little flophouse. Not only are their clothes incomplete—they will time and again pawn their very trousers, so that two or three go shares in a single pair; and while those without trousers lie snug asleep in bed, the one with the trousers goes out to contrive something, and only when he has raised a little money does he come back to put it to use and let the next man take his turn in the trousers.
