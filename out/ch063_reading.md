### Tiger Stoves

The tiger stove is a little shop that sells boiled water, so called because the brick range on which it boils the water is shaped somewhat like a tiger. Their price for water, in the old days, when rents were low and coal was cheap, was very low indeed: a single big cash would buy a ladle of boiling water; but by now the ladle that went for one cash has risen to five. Their shops are mostly set up at the mouth of a lane or within it, that those who live in the lane may come to buy water; a few are opened along the quieter streets besides, but only a few. Their hours of business run from six in the early morning right through to twelve at night, when they close up (that is, shut the shop door); and there are tiger stoves near the low brothels, moreover, that never close the whole night through. Some have a tiger stove below selling water, with tea sold within and on the floor above — shops of that kind are mostly opened along the streets. The tiger stoves inside the lanes will further set out a cupboard and a counter and sell cigarettes, sweets and sundries on the side. The trade even has a guild of its own, called the Water-Stove Guild, and whenever some great matter comes up they all go there to meet and thrash it out. Do not, I beg you, sneer at the keeping of a tiger stove for a mean sort of business: the truth is that plenty who follow it have made their fortunes!
