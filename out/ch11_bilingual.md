## H2 Chapter 11. A Whole Life’s Fortunes Drift Like Duckweed

> 雅淑以为天上的云彩是瞬息万变的，想不到人世间的情爱也是瞬息万变的。雅淑觉得这只碧绿的镯子还从来没有如此刺眼过，简直令人芒刺在背。
Yashu had always thought it was the clouds in the sky that changed from one breath to the next; it had never dawned on her that the loves of this human world could change just as fast. Never before had this emerald bracelet looked so hateful to her eyes—the mere sight of it was like a thorn pricking at her back.

> 阿初把自己送他的玉镯转瞬之间给了荣升，为什么？他完全可以用另一种委婉的方式退还给她，为什么要选择“出卖”她？自己爱他，他却不珍惜自己。
In the space of an instant A-Chu had handed the jade bracelet she had given him over to Rong Sheng. Why? He could easily have found some gentler, more roundabout way to return it to her; why had he chosen instead to “sell her out”? She loved him, and he set no store by her at all.

> 这只镯子色泽圆润，光华柔媚，像是在嘲讽自己，抑或是威胁？是取笑？还是鞭挞？其实这些想法都不重要，重要的是荣升的心里怎么想她，荣升的眼里怎么看她？和雅淑决定以不变应万变。
The bracelet’s colour was round and mellow, its glow soft and beguiling, as though it were mocking her—or was it a threat? A jeer, or a lash? In truth none of these thoughts mattered. What mattered was what Rong Sheng made of her in his heart, how Rong Sheng looked upon her with his eyes. He Yashu made up her mind to meet every change with changelessness.

> “这只镯子你从哪里得来得？”雅淑气定神闲地问。
“Where did you come by this bracelet?” Yashu asked, cool and unruffled.

> “在我书房里。”
“It was in my study.”

> “可是，如果我没有记错的话，你从来没有邀请我去过你家。”
“But, if my memory serves me, you have never once invited me to your home.”

> “我也很奇怪。”荣升想起来，原本在来得路上他想询问阿初的，可是他忘记问了。他笑了笑说：“阿初……也许知道……”
“That puzzles me too.” Rong Sheng remembered now that on the way over he had meant to ask A-Chu about it, and had forgotten. He gave a little smile. “A-Chu . . . might know . . .”

> 雅淑的心被尖锐的刺扎了一下，牵动肠胃也开始痉挛。她果断地截断了荣升的话。“他是一个没有自知之明的人。”
A sharp barb pricked Yashu’s heart, and her stomach began to cramp with it. Resolutely she cut Rong Sheng short. “He is a man with no sense of his own place.”

> “谁？你说谁？阿初？”荣升十分意外，因为雅淑是一个从不在背后议论和批评旁人的贤丛惠女人。
“Who? Whom do you mean? A-Chu?” Rong Sheng was taken aback, for Yashu was a gracious, gentle woman who never gossiped about others or found fault with them behind their backs.

> “关于这只镯子……我想没有比这更严重、更糟糕的事了，事关我名誉。”雅淑说的异常焦虑和诚恳。
“About this bracelet . . . I cannot think of anything more serious, more wretched than this—it touches my very name.” Yashu spoke with an uncommon anxiety and earnestness.

> “什么意思？难道一只镯子还代表着什么企图？”
“What do you mean? Surely a bracelet doesn’t stand for some scheme?”

> “你说企图？啊，是了。其实，我早就该告诉你一些真相。”
“A scheme, you say? Ah—yes. The truth is, I ought to have told you certain things long ago.”

> 荣升开始迷惑了，有什么事情如此严重？严重到她急于表白，急于撇清自己？她做了什么？
Rong Sheng began to be bewildered. What could be so grave? So grave that she was in such haste to declare herself, in such haste to clear her name? What had she done?

> “你推荐我到同济医院看病，你告诉我初医生的医德很好，医术也是一流的。所以，我去他的诊室看过病。这个人表面纯良，热情周到，对于我更是殷勤倍至，体贴入微。说老实话，有一段时间，我几乎认为他是天底下最好的医生。”
“You recommended that I see a doctor at the Tongji Hospital; you told me that Doctor Chu had a fine bedside manner and skill of the first rank. And so I went to his consulting room. On the surface the man is all goodness—warm, attentive, thoughtful, and to me especially so, solicitous and tender in every particular. To tell the truth, for a time I very nearly took him for the best doctor under heaven.”

> “其实呢？”
“And in truth?”

> “其实他居心不良。他是一个极不道德的人。请原谅我的直言不讳。他的行为真是伪善极了。他总是借故让我去他的诊室，单独和我相处，说一些不着边际的话语，试图打动我的心。他每次都替我叫车，付车钱，处处都赔着小心，讨我的欢心，他还曾经冲动地提出一些非分的要求。”
“In truth his intentions were vile. He is a thoroughly immoral man. Forgive me for speaking so bluntly. His conduct was the very picture of hypocrisy. He was forever finding pretexts to have me come to his consulting room, to be alone with me, saying all manner of aimless things in an effort to move my heart. Each time he would call a cab for me and pay the fare, careful of me at every turn, courting my favour; and once, on an impulse, he even made certain improper demands.”

> “也许你言过其实了。”荣升在努力克制自己狂躁的情绪。“刻意讨好你，我相信，其他的，我不信。”
“Perhaps you exaggerate.” Rong Sheng was struggling to master his own agitation. “That he deliberately courted your favour, I believe; the rest, I do not.”

> “你应该相信我，而不是相信他。那只镯子就是他偷去的。”
“You should believe me, not him. That bracelet is the very thing he stole.”

> “他偷去的？”
“He stole it?”

> “是的。就在上星期，我去他诊室复查身体，他借口诊脉，叫我把玉镯抹去，放到皮包里。可是，我回家的时候，才发现，那玉镯不见了。现在看起来，分明就是他窃取的，他想以此要挟我。”
“Yes. Only last week, when I went to his consulting room for a follow-up, he made feeling my pulse his pretext and had me slip off the jade bracelet and put it in my handbag. But when I reached home, I found the bracelet gone. Looking back now, it is plain that he took it—he means to use it to coerce me.”

> “他要挟你什么？”
“Coerce you into what?”

> “放弃你，而跟他苟合。”雅淑用手紧紧按住自己的心房，说：“这种事情，提及不堪，令人汗颜。”
“Into giving you up, and giving myself to him instead.” Yashu pressed her hand hard against her heart. “It is a thing too shameful to speak of; it makes one burn with disgrace.”

> “你是说，他一直主动追求你？”
“You mean he has been pursuing you all along?”

> “是的。可是我早已明确拒绝他了。你知道吗，我的内心是如此眷念着你，根本无法兼容他所谓的'热情'。”
“Yes. But I refused him plainly, long ago. Do you know, my heart is so bound up in longing for you that there is simply no room in it for his so-called ‘warmth.’”

> “他为什么要这样做？”
“Why would he do such a thing?”

> “可能是因为我对他的深情表白无动于衷，漠然置之。他把我对他的体谅和'宽容'当成了默许。于是，生出许多欲念来。可是，这是我无法控制的，我不能限制他的行动和改变他的想法。”
“Perhaps because I met his fervent declarations with indifference and passed them by coldly. He took my forbearance and my ‘tolerance’ of him for a tacit yes, and out of that grew all manner of cravings. But this is beyond my power to control; I cannot govern his actions or change his mind.”

> 尽管雅淑的“自白”杂乱无序，但是，荣升轻而易举地从她词不达意的话语中识破了雅淑内心的隐秘。
Disordered and rambling as Yashu’s “confession” was, Rong Sheng saw through her clumsy, ill-fitting words with the greatest ease to the secret hidden in her heart.

> “可是他，明明知道我们在交往。”荣升说。
“But he,” said Rong Sheng, “knew perfectly well that you and I were courting.”

> “他一定错以为我是个用情不专的女子，又或许是他想挑战你在大家庭里的权威？”
“He must have mistaken me for a fickle woman—or perhaps he wished to challenge your authority in this great household.”

> “雅淑，我今天很痛心。本来我准备今天正式向你求婚的。”荣升自嘲地笑了。“你知道吗，雅淑，有时候颠倒乾坤，不一定就会混淆视听。”
“Yashu, my heart aches today. I had meant, this very day, to formally ask for your hand.” Rong Sheng gave a self-mocking laugh. “You know, Yashu, sometimes, though you turn heaven and earth on their heads, you cannot muddle what a man sees and hears.”

> “阿升！我是爱你的！”雅淑脸色惨白，她不知道以自己的聪明机智，怎么会牵制不住一个养在深宅大院的少爷。
“A-Sheng! I love you!” Yashu’s face went deathly pale; she could not understand how, with all her cleverness and quick wit, she had failed to keep her hold on a young master reared deep within a great sheltered house.

> “你知道自己错在哪里吗？你太不了解阿初，你也不了解我！真的非常遗憾。”荣升“腾”地站起来。“我们完了。”
“Do you know where you went wrong? You understand A-Chu far too little, and you do not understand me either! It is a great pity, truly.” Rong Sheng shot to his feet. “We are finished.”

> “为什么？”雅淑惊慌失措，完全失去了应有的仪态。“为什么？你告诉我，阿初他到底跟你讲了些什么？他的话，你不能相信，他造谣。你告诉我，告诉我他说了我什么，我可以做出必要的解释。”
“Why?” Yashu was thrown into a panic, losing every trace of the composure she should have kept. “Why? Tell me—what on earth did A-Chu say to you? You cannot believe his words; he is spreading slander. Tell me, tell me what he said about me, and I can make whatever explanation is necessary.”

> “他一个字也没说。”荣升突然发现雅淑很可怜。“所有的话都是你一个人说的。”
“He did not say a single word.” Rong Sheng found, all at once, that Yashu was pitiable. “Every word has been yours alone.”

> 和雅淑茫然无助地看着荣升，凄恻逼人地说：“你居然要抛弃我？”
He Yashu looked at Rong Sheng, dazed and helpless, and said, in a voice piercing with grief: “So you really mean to cast me off?”

> “爱情需要真诚，投机的人往往与'真爱'失之交臂。为什么当你生命之火即将熄灭的瞬间，你是如此的美丽动人？为什么当所有的困难都逐渐克服，乃至消失的时候，你却变得如此俗不可耐。我原以为，你会从我所有的幻像中脱颖而出，我错了。雅淑，人生苦短，浮云朝露而已，善待自己，保重自己。”
“Love needs sincerity; the opportunist, more often than not, lets ‘true love’ slip through her fingers. Why is it that in the instant the flame of your life was about to go out, you were so beautiful, so moving? Why is it that now, when every difficulty has been gradually overcome, has all but vanished, you have become so unbearably vulgar? I had thought you would rise clear above all my illusions; I was wrong. Yashu, life is short and bitter, no more than a floating cloud, than the morning dew—be good to yourself, take care of yourself.”

> 当雅淑看到荣升决然而去的瞬间，她晕倒了。
At the moment she saw Rong Sheng turn and go, resolute, Yashu fainted dead away.

> 仿佛只是一瞬之间，一瞬之间自己所营造出来得美丽新世界，化做了五彩缤纷的泡沫。荣升和雅淑的希望都彻底幻灭了。
It seemed the work of a single instant—in a single instant the beautiful new world she had built for herself dissolved into gaudy, many-coloured bubbles. Every hope of Rong Sheng’s and of Yashu’s alike was utterly shattered.

> 夕阳灿烂，美丽光华的色彩均匀洒在“墨菊斋”的书桌上。杏儿、蝉儿、红儿、云儿等丫鬟们聚集在“墨菊斋”，吵着要阿初教国画，阿初说自己都是个门外汉，跟少爷学了点中国画的皮毛而已，不敢胜任“老师”一职。但是，双拳难抵四手，终究拗不过丫鬟们的热情怂恿，于是，他从国画的“散点取景、平面造型”讲起，一直谈到荣升的画中的贤愚冷暖，以及荣升心中的幽怨累积。他说：“少爷做事，中规中矩，以至于构图僵硬；他胸中大千世界，过于黯淡忧郁，所以他画的瘦石寒山冷得没有生气。”
The setting sun was glorious; its lovely radiant colours fell evenly across the writing-desk of the Moju Studio. The maidservants—Xing’er, Chan’er, Hong’er, Yun’er and the rest—had gathered in the Moju Studio, clamouring for A-Chu to teach them Chinese painting. A-Chu said he was a mere amateur himself, that he had only picked up a smattering of Chinese painting from the young master and dared not take up the post of “teacher.” But two fists are no match for eight hands, and in the end he could not hold out against the maids’ eager coaxing. So he began with the “scattered-point framing and flat modelling” of Chinese painting, and went on to speak of the wise and the foolish, the warmth and the chill, in Rong Sheng’s paintings, and of the accumulated melancholy in Rong Sheng’s heart. He said: “The young master does everything by rule and measure, so much so that his compositions come out stiff; the whole wide world within his breast is too dim, too sunk in gloom, and so the gaunt rocks and cold hills he paints are chill and without life.”

> “阿初少爷，反正少爷的画我们都看不懂，你画几张我们一眼就能看懂的画好吗？”蝉儿说。
“Master A-Chu, we can’t make head or tail of the young master’s paintings anyway—won’t you paint us a few we can understand at a glance?” said Chan’er.

> “好啊，我就画你们。就画一样，看看，你们认不认得？”阿初从笔架上取下一支短而细的羊毫，笔尖饱蘸了红色的染料，滴在雪白的宣纸上，勾画出一张微微上翘，“桀骜不驯”的红色嘴唇。
“Very well, I’ll paint you. I’ll paint just one thing—see whether you can tell what it is.” From the brush-rest A-Chu took a short, fine goat’s-hair brush, dipped its tip full of red pigment, and let it fall on the snow-white xuan paper, drawing out a pair of slightly upturned, “unruly” red lips.

> “这是杏儿。”丫鬟们异口同声地指认。
“That’s Xing’er,” the maids pointed out with one voice.

> “嗬，这样都看得出来啊。”阿初笑盈盈地把宣纸递给杏儿。“送给你。”
“Oho, so you can tell even from that.” A-Chu, all smiles, handed the sheet of xuan paper to Xing’er. “It’s for you.”

> “谢谢阿初少爷。”杏儿乐滋滋地接了过来。
“Thank you, Master A-Chu.” Xing’er took it, delighted.

> 阿初又画了一双灵巧活泼的手。问：“这是谁？”没等丫鬟们讲话，蝉儿满脸绯红地抢了画，说：“阿初少爷，你什么时候盯着人家的手看，没正经。”
A-Chu next painted a pair of deft, lively hands. He asked, “Whose are these?” Before the maids could speak, Chan’er, her face crimson, snatched the picture away and said, “Master A-Chu, when did you ever sit staring at a girl’s hands? For shame.”

> 丫鬟们哄笑起来。
The maids burst out laughing.

> “再画一个。”阿初画上了瘾，他换了支又长又粗的毛笔，画了一条油松松的麻花辫子，在辫梢上，系了一条蝴蝶丝带。
“Let me do one more.” A-Chu had caught the habit of it now. He changed to a long, thick brush and painted a glossy plaited braid, with a butterfly ribbon tied at its tip.

> “这是谁啊？”丫鬟们开始猜。
“And whose is this?” The maids began to guess.

> 阿初笑而不答。
A-Chu only smiled and said nothing.

> “是谁啊？”杏儿不依，要阿初说出来。
“Whose is it?” Xing’er would not let it drop, and pressed him to tell.

> “是不是阿初少爷的相好啊？”红儿促狭地问。
“Is it Master A-Chu’s sweetheart?” Hong’er asked mischievously.

> 阿初说：“猜不到吧，再添几样。”他又画了红色的指甲、涂了金粉的唇、蓝色的眼睫等等，各具姿态，异常招摇。
“Can’t guess, can you? I’ll add a few more things.” He painted red fingernails, lips brushed with gold powder, blue eyelashes and the like, each in its own attitude, all uncommonly showy.

> “到底是谁啊？”丫鬟们的好奇心全被勾上来，一起逼阿初讲出来。阿初忍着笑说：“这是大光明电影院门口招揽生意的姑娘。”一句话出口，险遭丫鬟们“群殴”。大家不依不饶，要他再正正经经画一张。
“Who is it, then?” The maids’ curiosity was thoroughly roused, and together they pressed A-Chu to tell. Holding back his laughter, he said, “This is one of the girls who tout for custom at the door of the Grand Theatre.” The words were scarcely out when he was nearly set upon by the whole company of maids. They would not let him off, and demanded that he paint a proper one.

> “画什么呢？”阿初广泛征求丫鬟们的意见，一副礼贤下士的诚恳样子。
“What shall I paint?” A-Chu canvassed the maids’ opinions at large, with the earnest air of a man deferring courteously to worthier heads.

> “画一下那位和小姐吧。”蝉儿说。“我们都还没见过这位未来得少奶奶呢。”
“Paint that Miss He,” said Chan’er. “None of us has so much as set eyes on our future young mistress.”

> “是呀。”杏儿附和。“人家说，看看眉眼就知道人怎么样了。”
“Yes,” Xing’er chimed in. “They say you can tell what a person is like just from the eyes and brows.”

> 阿初说，服从各位姐姐的命令，不过要保密，少爷最不喜欢别人谈论自己的私生活。他拿起笔，画了雅淑的眉毛和僵硬的鼻子、苍白无力的嘴唇。
A-Chu said he would obey the commands of his elder sisters, but that they must keep it secret, for the young master most disliked others discussing his private life. He took up the brush and painted Yashu’s eyebrows, her rigid nose, her pale and bloodless lips.

> “为什么没有眼睛啊？”杏儿问。
“Why are there no eyes?” asked Xing’er.

> “因为眼睛是心灵的窗户，没有读懂她心灵的人，是画不出她的眼睛的。”
“Because the eyes are the windows of the soul, and one who has not read a person’s soul cannot paint her eyes.”

> “少爷呢？”蝉儿说。“少爷应该读得懂她的心，应该留给少爷画。”
“What about the young master?” said Chan’er. “The young master should be able to read her heart—the eyes should be left for him to paint.”

> 大家一致叫好。
They all cried out in approval.

> 只有阿初淡然一笑，说：“那也未必。不识庐山真面目，只因身在此山中。”话音未落，“墨菊斋”书房的门被重重地撞开了。
A-Chu alone gave a faint smile and said, “Not necessarily. One cannot know the true face of Mount Lu, only because one is oneself within the mountain.” The words were hardly finished when the door of the Moju Studio’s study was flung violently open.

> 他们看见了冷脸寒颜的荣升。空气一下沉静了。
There before them stood Rong Sheng, his face cold and grim. The air went suddenly still.

> “少爷，您怎么回来了？我正打算九点钟去接你。”阿初替他接过礼帽。
“Young master, why have you come back? I was about to fetch you at nine o’clock.” A-Chu took his hat for him.

> “不必了。”荣升脱了外套，走到书桌前，看了看画。说：“画得不错。拿我的精神世界做故事背景，不错啊……不过，选题不佳！”他把宣纸抓起来揉成团，顺手丢进废纸篓。回头对丫鬟们说：“都出去。”
“No need.” Rong Sheng took off his coat, went up to the desk, looked at the picture, and said: “Well painted. Taking my inner world for the background of your tale—not bad at all . . . But the choice of subject is poor!” He snatched up the sheet of xuan paper, crushed it into a ball, and tossed it into the wastepaper basket. Turning back to the maids, he said, “All of you, out.”

> 丫鬟们屏声敛气纷纷退下。
The maids held their breath and withdrew one after another.

> 阿初察言观色，觉得少爷情绪异常。他想把话题岔开，故而他对少爷的冷漠，有意“视而不见”。
A-Chu read his master’s face and manner and saw that his mood was badly off. Wishing to turn the subject aside, he deliberately “saw nothing” of the young master’s coldness.

> “您吃晚饭了吗？”阿初问。“要不要我通知厨房……”
“Have you had your supper?” A-Chu asked. “Shall I let the kitchen know—”

> “不必了，我今天吃得很饱，估计一个星期都不想再吃。”
“No need. I have eaten very well today; I expect I shan’t want to eat again for a week.”

> “你和雅淑小姐，没什么吧？”
“You and Miss Yashu—all is well, I trust?”

> “我们会有什么？哦，我们去看了一部电影，故事很精彩，大家都看得很投入。”荣升说。
“What could be the matter with us? Oh—we went to see a film; the story was splendid, everyone was quite caught up in it,” said Rong Sheng.

> “什么电影？”
“What film?”

> “片名不记得了。不过，都是一些看客和记者们'喜闻乐见'的场面，富有创意的台词，自作多情的表演。爱情、阴谋、中伤、谣言。”荣升一口气说下来，显然有些力不从心，他长舒了一口憋在胸中的闷气。说：“她真是太傻了，傻得令人难过，不，不是难过，是好笑，真好笑。一个恋爱中的女人傻到要把自己的灵魂、内心深处的隐私，都全部裸露在一个历尽沧桑的男人眼里。这个男人虚伪、自私、阴险，这个男人其实不爱她，只是想解脱，想用她的'爱'解脱自己的'痛'。所以这个男人注定得不到女人的'爱'，不过，这个女人不仅傻、而且蠢，她依然想留住这个男人的眷恋。”
“I don’t recall the title. But it was all the sort of thing that spectators and reporters ‘love to see and hear’: lines full of invention, performances full of self-flattering sentiment. Love, intrigue, calumny, rumour.” Rong Sheng poured it all out in one breath, plainly finding it something of a strain, and let out a long sigh, releasing the pent-up, stifling air in his chest. He said: “She really is too foolish—foolish to the point of grief; no, not grief, it is laughable, truly laughable. A woman in love, foolish enough to lay bare her whole soul, the secrets in the depths of her heart, before the eyes of a man who has been through every vicissitude of the world. This man is false, selfish, sinister; this man does not, in fact, love her—he only wants to free himself, to use her ‘love’ to free himself from his own ‘pain.’ And so this man is fated never to win the woman’s ‘love’; and yet this woman is not only foolish but stupid, for still she wants to keep this man’s affection.”

> 阿初很紧张，很久没有看到荣升这样狂躁了。
A-Chu was very tense; it had been a long while since he had seen Rong Sheng so wrought up.

> “结局呢？”
“And the ending?”

> “结局通常都是悲剧。往往只有悲剧才能打动人的心灵，引发人们的共鸣。'但愿墓门旁边，活跃青春的生命。'”荣升喃喃吟诵着普希金的名句。“本来以为自己找到了一根救命稻草……”
“The ending is usually a tragedy. It is often only tragedy that can move the human soul, that can stir men to a common feeling. ‘Let there be, beside the gate of the grave, young life at play.’” Rong Sheng murmured the famous lines of Pushkin. “I had thought I’d found myself a straw to clutch at . . .”

> “少爷，你？你和雅淑小姐到底怎么了？”阿初问。
“Young master, you—what has really happened between you and Miss Yashu?” A-Chu asked.

> “你问我们怎么了？谢幕了。”荣升笑起来，笑得有几许无奈和苍凉。“我很投入地演出，是因为我原以为自己是一个大主角，一个多情才子。演到中途，突然发现自己是一个与剧情毫不相关的小配角，一个跳梁小丑。我这个人不喜欢做配角，不喜欢被别人嘲笑，所以，我提前谢幕了。”
“You ask what has happened to us? The curtain has come down.” Rong Sheng laughed, a laugh with more than a little helplessness and desolation in it. “I threw myself into the performance because I had thought myself the leading man, a gifted, passionate hero. Halfway through the play I suddenly found I was a bit-part player with nothing to do with the plot, a clown capering on the sidelines. I am not a man who likes to play the supporting role, who likes to be laughed at by others; and so I have taken my bow early.”

> “雅淑小姐一定很难过。”阿初可以想像到雅淑的失望和伤心，当然，不是为了“爱情”，只是为了“生计”。
“Miss Yashu must be very distressed.” A-Chu could imagine Yashu’s disappointment and heartbreak—not, of course, for the sake of “love,” but for the sake of a “livelihood.”

> “你好像很同情这位小姐？”荣升终于开始进攻主题了。
“You seem to pity this young lady?” Rong Sheng was at last coming round to the heart of the matter.

> “没有。”阿初答。
“No,” A-Chu answered.

> “没有？那么，你是很讨厌这位小姐？”
“No? Then you dislike her very much?”

> “我跟她没有任何关系。”阿初觉得自己必须分辩一句了。
“I have nothing whatever to do with her.” A-Chu felt he must put in a word to clear himself.

> “没有任何关系？她的镯子怎么会出现在我的书房？”荣升问。
“Nothing whatever to do with her? Then how did her bracelet come to be in my study?” Rong Sheng asked.

> “这无关紧要。”
“That is of no consequence.”

> “对我很重要。”
“It is of great consequence to me.”

> “和小姐怎么说？”
“What did Miss He say?”

> “我想听你怎么说？”
“I’d rather hear what you say.”

> “我没话说。”阿初不想在继续这种无谓的话题。“她怎么说怎么是。”
“I have nothing to say.” A-Chu had no wish to go on with this pointless subject. “Whatever she says, so be it.”

> “她说你偷的。”
“She says you stole it.”

> “如果她是这样认为的，我就承认。”阿初终于知道荣升为什么火药味十足了。
“If that is what she believes, then I admit it.” A-Chu understood at last why Rong Sheng was so charged with gunpowder.

> “你很喜欢替女孩子背黑锅吗？”
“Are you so fond of taking the blame for a girl?”

> “我以为少爷会对这只镯子感兴趣……”
“I thought the young master might take an interest in this bracelet . . .”

> “你为什么总是让我生活在谎言里？”荣升的气势咄咄逼人。“为什么？你看不起雅淑，对不对？你也看不起我。”
“Why must you always make me live in lies?” Rong Sheng’s manner bore down on him, overbearing. “Why? You look down on Yashu, don’t you? You look down on me too.”

> “少爷？”
“Young master?”

> “我们这些所谓的社会名流、绅士淑女，在你眼里一钱不值。俗不可耐？”
“In your eyes we so-called society notables, gentlemen and gentlewomen, aren’t worth a copper. Unbearably vulgar, are we?”

> “少爷，感情是不能勉强的，遭受失恋痛苦的不止你一人，也许雅淑小姐比您更痛苦。”
“Young master, the heart cannot be forced; you are not the only one to suffer the pain of a love gone wrong—perhaps Miss Yashu suffers more than you.”

> “你指责我？明知道是一场游戏，还要大惊小怪？”
“You reproach me? Knowing all along it was a game, must you make so great a fuss?”

> “不是一场游戏。”阿初说。“您爱上她了，少爷。不然，何必生气呢？你起初只是想捞一根救命稻草，可是，你不知道，感情是在不知不觉中培养起来得，你为此付出了时间、精力。爱情，不是游戏，在里面做游戏的人，很可能被游戏束缚。雅淑小姐很聪明，很实际，她知道一个女人应该怎样去面对残酷的生活。她无非是想多一些选择而已。无可厚非。”
“It was not a game,” said A-Chu. “You have fallen in love with her, young master. Otherwise, why be angry? At first you only wanted to clutch at a straw; but you did not know that feeling is nurtured before one is even aware of it, and you had spent time and heart upon it. Love is not a game, and one who plays a game inside it may well find himself bound by the game. Miss Yashu is very clever, very practical; she knows how a woman must face a cruel life. She wanted no more than a few more choices for herself. There is nothing to blame in that.”

> “你很得意是吧？他选择了你，而不是我。”
“You are very pleased, aren’t you? She chose you, and not me.”

> “她谁也没有选。少爷。你已经剥夺了她选择的权利。你有浓烈的怀旧情结，你允许你自己的心灵同时拥有两个女人的精神世界。但是，你不允许雅淑小姐的行为有任何偏差，这本身也是不公平的。”
“She chose no one, young master. You have already stripped her of the right to choose. You have a strong strain of nostalgia in you; you allow your own heart to hold the spiritual worlds of two women at once. But you allow Miss Yashu’s conduct not the smallest deviation, and that, in itself, is unfair.”

> “你暗示我歧视女性？”荣升忍无可忍地往前逼近了一步。
“You hint that I hold women in contempt?” Beyond all endurance, Rong Sheng took a step nearer.

> 阿初不自觉地往后退却，他低下头，说：“我对事不对人。”
A-Chu, without meaning to, drew back. He lowered his head and said, “I speak to the matter, not to the man.”

> “你教训我？”荣升冷笑。“我已经放弃了做一个寂寞的智者，选择做一个平凡的庸人。你却轻而易举地把我美好的梦想给打破了，当我变成一个歧路徘徊的懦夫时，你就来振振有词地教训我。你以为你是谁？荣家的主人？”荣升狂怒地砸翻了砚台和笔架，满地狼藉。
“You lecture me?” Rong Sheng sneered. “I have already given up being a lonely sage and chosen to be an ordinary, commonplace man. Yet you, with the greatest ease, have shattered my beautiful dream; and now that I have become a coward hesitating at the crossroads, you come and lecture me, full of high-sounding words. Who do you think you are? The master of the Rong house?” In a fury Rong Sheng overturned the inkstone and the brush-rest, and the floor was left in wild disorder.

> 荣升最后一句话严重的伤害到阿初的自尊。阿初很难过，他在不断克制自己的心绪，调整自己的心态，因为“争论”不能升级，他要顾及到荣家的颜面。
That last remark of Rong Sheng’s wounded A-Chu’s self-respect to the quick. A-Chu was deeply pained; he kept reining in his feelings, adjusting his frame of mind, because the “quarrel” must not be allowed to escalate—he had to consider the face of the Rong family.

> 他选择“沉默”。“沉默”代表无声的抗议。
He chose “silence.” “Silence” was a voiceless protest.

> 殊不知这种简单而又直接的防御手段，像一根尖锐的刺扎在荣升眼睛里，有一种不除不快的感觉。
Little did he know that this simple and direct means of defence was like a sharp thorn stuck in Rong Sheng’s eye, rousing in him a feeling that would not be eased until it was plucked out.

> “你怎么不说话了？你应该继续发表你的高论啊。你不是字玑句珠吗？你的浅德幽光足可以照亮整个荣家大院了。你不屑跟我讲话是吧？巧得很，我也不想再聆听你的'教诲'。”荣升转过头去，指了指自己的心口，突然又指向阿初说：“掌嘴。”
“Why have you stopped speaking? You ought to go on delivering your fine opinions. Are you not the man of jewelled phrase and pearled sentence? Your slender virtue and hidden light are quite enough to light up the whole Rong compound. You disdain to speak with me, is that it? How very apt—I too have no wish to go on hearing your ‘instruction.’” Rong Sheng turned his head away, pointed at his own heart, then suddenly pointed at A-Chu and said: “Slap yourself.”

> 荣升发难了。
Rong Sheng had thrown down the gauntlet.

> “少爷？”阿初以为自己听错了。
“Young master?” A-Chu thought he had misheard.

> “你要跟我讲平等、自由是吧？我不跟你讲。我跟你讲专制、讲身份。”荣升的话异常刻薄起来。“我歧视雅淑这类女人，我讨厌你虚伪的宽容和忍让，我憎恨情感，厌恶你这种看上去委屈，实际上张狂的眼光。你无非就是用'沉默'来告诉我……你很阳光，我很阴暗。”
“You want to talk to me of equality and freedom, is that it? I won’t talk of those. I’ll talk to you of autocracy, of rank and station.” Rong Sheng’s words turned uncommonly cutting. “I hold women of Yashu’s kind in contempt; I loathe your false tolerance and forbearance; I detest sentiment, and I abhor that look of yours—wronged on the surface, insolent underneath. You use your ‘silence’ for nothing but to tell me . . . that you stand in the sunlight, and I in the dark.”

> 阿初对荣升如此大的情绪波动，始料不及。
The violence of Rong Sheng’s swings of feeling was more than A-Chu had ever foreseen.

> “我叫你掌嘴！你没听见吗？打呀！”荣升像一头受了伤的猎豹，他想撕裂一切他可以撕裂的面具。
“I told you to slap yourself! Did you not hear? Strike!” Rong Sheng was like a wounded leopard; he wanted to tear apart every mask he could lay hold of.

> 不是第一次，忍受“家法”；但是，阿初第一次感到难过和难堪。他们之间永远不可能有平等。一个是高高在上施恩的人，一个是感激涕零受人恩丛惠的人，怎么可能平等？平等只是偶然的，不平等是必然的。
It was not the first time he had endured the “family discipline”; but it was the first time A-Chu felt grief and humiliation. Between the two of them there could never be equality. One was the man on high who bestowed his favour, the other the man who received that favour and wept with gratitude; how could they ever be equals? Equality was a thing of chance; inequality was the necessary rule.

> 阿初仿佛回到了一个自己完全陌生的国度。荣升的面庞此刻变得十分陌生，不，不是陌生，而是逐渐清晰，逐渐熟悉。这八年来，荣升并没有丝毫的改变。改变的是自己！自己的思维和心灵已经改变，这种改变促使他不愿意回到从前。像少爷手中的标尺一样，任意由人调整刻度、拉伸卷曲。
It was as though A-Chu had returned to a country wholly strange to him. Rong Sheng’s face had become, at this moment, very strange—no, not strange, but growing clearer, growing more familiar. In these eight years Rong Sheng had not changed in the slightest. What had changed was himself! His own mind and heart had changed, and that change made him unwilling to go back to what he had been—like the graduated rule in the young master’s hand, its marks reset at will by another, stretched and curled at another’s pleasure.

> 如果大家不能安然共处，那么，夺门而去，拂袖就走，并非难事。
If they could not dwell together in peace, then to burst out of the door, to shake out his sleeves and go, was no hard thing.

> 可是，四太太怎么办呢？自己走得爽快，要回头也就难了。四太太的家庭地位，二十年来得殷殷期盼，化为乌有。自己在大太太面前不是信誓旦旦的要报荣家的栽培之恩吗？怎么能出尔反尔呢？
But what of Fourth Madam? If he left with a light heart, it would be hard indeed to turn back afterwards. Fourth Madam’s standing in the family, the earnest hopes of twenty-odd years, would come to nothing. Had he not sworn solemnly, before First Madam, to repay the Rong family’s care in raising him? How could he go back on his word?

> 荣升的居高临下，是因为他坚实的家长地位。就算他自己放弃荣氏家族的权利，他也不会丧失家人的尊重。他的只言片语，也同样可以撼动荣氏家族的地基。而自己只是一个赝品，就算自己拥有了社会地位、金钱、名誉，在荣家他依然没有自我。表面上自己是驶在海上的一艘豪华游艇，实际上这只是从水中看到的“倒影”罢了，自己的人生犹如水中一叶浮萍。阿初强迫自己用现实地位和感恩的情感去遮蔽住自由的思想，平等的观念，尽量减低自己内心所承受的被奴役的痛苦感觉。
Rong Sheng’s air of looking down from on high rested on his solid position as head of the family. Even if he himself renounced his rights in the Rong clan, he would not forfeit the family’s respect. A word or two from him could shake the very foundations of the Rong house. But he himself was only a counterfeit; even were he to gain social standing, money, reputation, in the Rong house he would still have no self. On the surface he was a luxurious yacht cruising the sea; in truth this was no more than a “reflection” seen from within the water—his own life like a single leaf of duckweed adrift upon it. A-Chu forced himself to use his real position and his feeling of gratitude to veil his thoughts of freedom, his notion of equality, to lessen as far as he could the painful sense of bondage he bore within.

> 想着雅淑的眼泪、四太太的恩情……他扬起手狠狠地打了自己。压抑已久的情绪，却在近乎自虐中释放出来。他打的极重，没有停手，他想着自己平生的际遇，犹似萍飘，眼前甚至出现父母双亲的幻影，这来自天外的模糊幻影，不断地重叠放映。他流泪了，血从嘴角处缓缓渗出。
Thinking of Yashu’s tears, of Fourth Madam’s kindness . . . he raised his hand and struck himself, hard. Feelings long suppressed found release in what was almost self-torture. He struck himself very heavily and did not stop; he thought of the fortunes of his life, adrift like duckweed, and before his eyes there rose even the phantom of his own father and mother—this blurred vision come from beyond the world played over and over, layer upon layer. He wept, and blood seeped slowly from the corner of his mouth.

> 阿初听见了哭声。不是幻觉，真的有人在哭泣。是为我哭吗？阿初想。
A-Chu heard the sound of weeping. It was no hallucination; someone truly was crying. Are they weeping for me? A-Chu wondered.

> 的确不是阿初的幻觉，荣升也听见了哭声。
It was indeed no hallucination of A-Chu’s; Rong Sheng heard the weeping too.

> “呜呜咽咽”的声音是从窗外传来得，是杏儿和蝉儿等人在用她们特殊的方式为阿初抱屈，她们觉得大少爷太过无情，“量刑过重”了。
The choked, sobbing sound came from beyond the window: Xing’er, Chan’er and the others, protesting in their own way on A-Chu’s behalf, feeling that the eldest young master had been too heartless, that the “sentence” was “too harsh by far.”

> 她们的哭声削弱了荣升强硬的态度和“病态”的心理。同时，也减轻了阿初心中的愤怨，他感到了人与人之间平等的关怀，所谓贤愚冷暖，尽在这哭声中融化了。
Their weeping weakened Rong Sheng’s hard-set manner and “morbid” state of mind. At the same time it eased the resentment in A-Chu’s heart; he felt the equal care of one human being for another, and all the so-called wisdom and folly, warmth and chill, melted away in that sound of weeping.

> “够了！”荣升喝住阿初的同时，也给了自己台阶下。“以后做人做事，中规中矩。不要再给我擅作威福的借口。”荣升说完，摔门而去。
“Enough!” With the word Rong Sheng both halted A-Chu and gave himself a way down. “From now on, conduct yourself by rule and measure. Give me no more pretexts to wield my power over you.” So saying, he slammed the door and left.

> 丫鬟们不提防他突然冲出来，怯怯然纷纷后退。
The maids, not expecting him to come rushing out, drew back in timid alarm.

> “哭什么？”荣升冷若冰霜地说。“该怜悯的人，得不到怜悯！珍贵的眼泪，应该留给你们将来所爱的人。而不是轻狂地、廉价地、抛售给一个在你们爱情旅程里毫不相关的路人。”
“What are you crying for?” said Rong Sheng, cold as ice and frost. “The one who deserves pity gets none! Precious tears should be saved for those you will one day love—not flung out, wanton and cheap, upon a passerby who has nothing to do with the journey of your love.”

> 丫鬟们听不懂。一味地低头退让少爷。
The maids did not understand. They only bowed their heads and gave way before the young master.

> 阿初懂了。
A-Chu understood.

> 他可怜荣升对“爱”的狭隘和自私；他也怜悯荣升在爱情旅途里不幸的遭遇。他想到了丛惠，自己回国，对丛惠也许是一种伤害。
He pitied Rong Sheng for the narrowness and selfishness of his “love”; he pitied him too for the unhappy fortunes that had met him on the road of love. He thought of Cong Hui—perhaps his own return to China was, for Cong Hui, a kind of injury.

> 他听见荣升离去的脚步声和丫鬟们纷纷进屋的声音。
He heard Rong Sheng’s departing footsteps, and the sound of the maids coming into the room one after another.

> 她们谁都没有说话，她们替阿初倒水、擦洗嘴角上的血污，她们悄无声息地打扫房间，扶正笔架，铺好宣纸。
None of them said a word. They poured water for A-Chu, wiped the blood from the corner of his mouth; noiselessly they tidied the room, set the brush-rest upright, laid out fresh xuan paper.

> “阿初，阿初……我的初。你怎么样了？”闻讯而来得四太太在红儿的陪伴下，气喘吁吁地冲进来。阿初赶紧笑着迎过去，说：“这是做什么？好像我得了一场大病似的。”
“A-Chu, A-Chu . . . my Chu. How are you?” Fourth Madam, who had come at the news attended by Hong’er, rushed in gasping for breath. A-Chu hurried smiling to meet her and said, “What is all this? Anyone would think I’d fallen gravely ill.”

> “你还胡说。”四太太凑近了来看他。心疼地说：“你干吗要惹他？生出这无妄之灾。”
“Still talking nonsense.” Fourth Madam came close to look at him. Aching with tenderness she said, “Why did you have to provoke him, and bring this undeserved calamity on yourself?”

> “谁敢惹他，他不讲理罢了。”阿初说。“又不是第一次。”
“Who would dare provoke him? He simply won’t listen to reason,” said A-Chu. “And it isn’t the first time.”

> “我保证，阿初。”四太太含着眼泪说。“我保证，这是最后一次，以后，再也不会了，阿初，再也没有下一次了，绝对没有。”
“I promise you, A-Chu.” Fourth Madam spoke with tears in her eyes. “I promise you, this is the last time; from now on, never again—A-Chu, there will never be a next time, never again, absolutely never.”

> 阿初看着四太太，心生感动，他很想告诉四太太，她像极了自己幻境中的母亲。
A-Chu looked at Fourth Madam, moved to the heart. He longed to tell her that she was the very image of the mother in his dreams.

> 傍晚时分，荣华到“墨菊斋”来给老余拿消炎药。原来阿初事先跟她约好的，今天送药过去，偏偏阿初今天忘了这件事。所以，他一看见荣华就恍然有所悟地说：“该死，该死。我一点也记不起来了。害二小姐跑一趟。”
Towards evening Ronghua came to the Moju Studio to fetch anti-inflammatory medicine for Lao Yu. A-Chu had arranged it with her beforehand—that he would bring the medicine over today—but today, of all days, A-Chu had forgotten. And so, the moment he saw Ronghua, it came back to him in a flash and he said, “Curse it, curse it. I clean forgot. I’ve made the second young lady run all this way for nothing.”

> “那有什么，我跑你跑，还不都是一样。”
“What of it? Whether you run the errand or I do, it comes to the same thing.”

> “他还住您哪里吗？”
“Is he still staying at your place?”

> “我叫阿福给他找了个安静的地方。阿福一直以为是我开车撞了人，比我还担心呢。”荣华笑着说。
“I had A-Fu find him a quiet spot. A-Fu still thinks it was I who ran the man down in the car; he frets over it more than I do.” Ronghua said this with a smile.

> 阿初把药递给荣华，说：“他现在不发烧了吧？”
A-Chu handed the medicine to Ronghua and said, “He’s no longer running a fever, I hope?”

> “略有些低烧。你脸上怎么了？”荣华关心地问。
“A slight low fever still. What’s happened to your face?” Ronghua asked with concern.

> “不好意思。”阿初有些尴尬。
“It’s nothing.” A-Chu was a little embarrassed.

> “是我大哥吗？”荣华试探地说。“我一直听说他脾气不大好，有暴力倾向。”
“Was it my eldest brother?” Ronghua ventured. “I’ve always heard his temper isn’t good, that he has violent tendencies.”

> “没有这么严重。”阿初笑起来。“这件事说起来，也是我自作聪明，自作自受。”
“It’s not as bad as that.” A-Chu laughed. “As for this business, it was my own cleverness that brought it on me—I have only myself to blame.”

> “为什么呢？”
“How so?”

> 阿初不好明说其事，他想着替雅淑留点薄面，毕竟自己还要面对雅淑，当然，也许面对的是她的唾弃。
A-Chu could not speak of the matter plainly; he was minded to leave Yashu a shred of face, for he had still to face her—and to face, perhaps, her scorn.

> “权当是自己的错，该当家法吧。”阿初自言自语地笑笑。
“Let us just say the fault was my own, and I had the family discipline coming to me.” A-Chu smiled to himself, half in soliloquy.

> “家法？法字怎么写？”荣华问。
“The family discipline? How do you write the character fa, ‘law’?” Ronghua asked.

> 阿初提起笔来，在宣纸上写了一个大大的“法”字，示意荣华看。
A-Chu took up the brush, wrote a great character 法 on the xuan paper, and showed it to Ronghua.

> “古体怎么写？”荣华继续问。
“And how is it written in the archaic form?” Ronghua went on.

> “古体？”阿初想想，提起笔，写了更大的一个“灋”字。
“The archaic form?” A-Chu thought a moment, took up the brush, and wrote an even larger character: 灋.

> 灋古体的“法”字
灋—the ancient form of the character 法, “law.”

> “怎么解？”荣华的眼睛里不自觉地泛起一丝钦羡才华的光泽。
“How is it to be explained?” In Ronghua’s eyes there rose, unbidden, a gleam of admiration for his learning.

> “灋，刑也。水字旁寓意公平，平之如水嘛。”
“灋 means punishment, the penal law. The ‘water’ radical carries the sense of fairness—level as water, you see.”

> “那么廌呢？做何解？”荣华故意巧妙地提笔把“廌”字圈起来。
“And what of the zhi? How is that to be explained?” Ronghua deliberately, cleverly, took up the brush and circled the element 廌.

> 阿初没有荣华的机心，他老老实实地回答，说：“廌是中古代时期传说中的独角兽，生性正直勇猛，遇到不公平的事情，它会用角去顶，所以，它的下面是一个去字。”阿初接过荣华手中的笔，在“去”字头上画了一个向上顶的小箭头。
A-Chu had none of Ronghua’s guile; he answered plainly and honestly: “The zhi is a one-horned beast of legend from high antiquity, upright and fierce by nature; when it met with anything unjust, it would butt at it with its horn—and so beneath it stands the character 去, ‘to drive away.’” A-Chu took the brush from Ronghua’s hand and drew, above the character 去, a small arrow pointing upward, in the act of butting.

> “去顶！很形像。”荣华说。“可是，你为什么不顶？”
“Butting away! How vivid,” said Ronghua. “But why do you not butt?”

> “什么？”阿初冷不防被荣华射了一箭。
“What?” A-Chu was caught off guard by Ronghua’s arrow.

> “你也遇到了不公平的待遇，为什么不顶？”
“You too have met with unjust treatment—why do you not butt?”

> “他是少爷。”
“He is the young master.”

> “这不公平。”荣华严肃起来。
“That is not just,” said Ronghua, growing grave.

> “世上没有绝对的公平。”阿初把羊毫笔轻轻投掷到砚台上，溅起黑色的墨珠。
“There is no absolute justice in the world.” A-Chu tossed the goat’s-hair brush lightly onto the inkstone, and black beads of ink splashed up.

> “你看这些四溅的墨珠，本来它们在砚台里沉睡着，像一滩死水，你的笔无意中搅动了它们，墨水不平了，有了些许波澜。事物'不平则鸣'，所以它们肆意地飞溅，随意绽放在桌面。”荣华把羊毫笔挂上笔架。说：“墨珠尚且要争，你为什么不去争取你应有的合法权益和地位？你为荣家付出了很多辛劳，为什么从不想到索取应有的劳动报酬？你牺牲了很多属于自己的利益，甚至是自尊。你一味忍让我哥哥蛮横的行为，其实是'害'他。一个不出去工作，根本不知道辛劳为何事的人，本身就是社会的'沉渣'。”
“Look at these scattered beads of ink. They were sleeping in the inkstone, a pool of dead water; your brush stirred them without meaning to, and the ink was level no longer—it took on a few small waves. Where things are not level, they cry out; and so the beads fly wantonly, blossoming at random across the desktop.” Ronghua hung the goat’s-hair brush back on the rest. She said: “If even beads of ink must contend, why do you not contend for the rights and the standing that should be yours? You have given the Rong family a great deal of toil; why does it never occur to you to demand the wages your labour deserves? You have sacrificed a great many of your own interests, your very self-respect even. Your endless yielding to my brother’s high-handed conduct is in truth a way of ‘harming’ him. A man who never goes out to work, who has no notion of what toil even is, is himself the ‘dregs’ of society.”

> “二小姐！”阿初打断了荣华慷慨激昂的讲话。“二小姐，对不起，我知道您是哪一种人，我很敬佩您。不过，我的人生经历跟您相差太远。如果没有过世的老爷栽培，没有少爷在经济上给我的资助，我是无法顺利完成全部的学业，也不会有今天的成绩。我跟您不一样，我欠荣家的。”阿初的态度异常诚恳，反让荣华局促起来。
“Second young lady!” A-Chu broke in on Ronghua’s impassioned speech. “Second young lady, forgive me—I know what kind of person you are, and I admire you greatly. But the course of my life has been very different from yours. Without the late Master’s care in raising me, without the young master’s support in matters of money, I could never have completed all my studies, nor have what I have today. I am not like you; I am in the Rong family’s debt.” A-Chu’s manner was so earnest that it was Ronghua’s turn to grow uneasy.

> “你很宽容。”荣华说。
“You are very forgiving,” said Ronghua.

> “Toerrishuman，toforgivedivine.”阿初说。这句话引自蒲柏的诗歌，犯错人难免，宽恕最可贵。
“‘To err is human, to forgive divine,’” said A-Chu—a line from the poet Pope.

> “看来，我枉做小人了。”荣华说。
“It seems I have played the petty schemer for nothing,” said Ronghua.

> “您很关心我。”阿初立即把话拉回来。“我感激在心。”
“You care about me,” A-Chu at once drew the words back. “I am grateful in my heart.”

> “真的？”
“Truly?”

> “点点滴滴。”阿初指心。
“In every last drop.” A-Chu pointed to his heart.

> 荣华开心了。“你这张嘴，很会哄女人。”
Ronghua was pleased. “That mouth of yours—it knows well how to coax a woman.”

> “您这是褒还是贬啊？”
“Is that praise, or blame?”

> “自己猜。”
“Guess for yourself.”

> 两个人都会心的笑起来，美丽动人的剪影在粉红色的灯光照耀下，显得分外光明。此刻，蝉儿端着燕窝银耳羹敲响了书房的门。
The two of them smiled in mutual understanding, and their lovely silhouettes, lit by the pink lamplight, seemed the very picture of brightness. Just then Chan’er, carrying a bowl of bird’s-nest-and-white-fungus sweet soup, knocked at the study door.

> “阿初少爷，大少爷在大太太房里歇了，您不用替他等门了。还有啊，我到厨房替你熬了一小壶燕窝银耳羹，你趁热吃。”
“Master A-Chu, the eldest young master has retired for the night in First Madam’s rooms; you needn’t wait up to let him in. And here—I stewed you a little pot of bird’s-nest-and-white-fungus soup in the kitchen; eat it while it’s hot.”

> 阿初称谢，叫荣华一起吃了。
A-Chu thanked her, and asked Ronghua to share it with him.

> 又到清明了。
It was Qingming again.

> 四太太想着。今年的清明节应该不同往年了。
So Fourth Madam was thinking. This year’s Qingming should be unlike the years before.

> 她活着，没有爱情，只有亲情。
She lived without love, with only the ties of kin.

> “复仇”的使命感维系着她的生命，她一生中唯一地向往就是“回家”，堂堂正正的“回家”。她为此不断的透支着自己的青春年华，二十多年来，她“画地为牢”、“深居简出”，任由无情的岁月像流水一样从自己的身边匆匆划过，美丽的风华像自己手中的春沙，从白皙的指缝间慢慢渗漏。春红谢尽了，她依然在等待，她的生命在等待中延伸……
It was the sense of mission in “revenge” that held her life together; the one thing she had longed for all her life was to “go home”—to go home openly, with her head held high. For this she had drawn ceaselessly on the capital of her own youth. For twenty-odd years she had “drawn a prison on the ground and kept within it,” living secluded and seldom stirring abroad, letting the heartless years slip past her like flowing water, letting her lovely bloom trickle away like spring sand in her hand, slow through the gaps of her pale fingers. The spring red had all faded, and still she waited; her life stretched onward in the waiting . . .

> 父亲死了二十多年了，他的音容笑貌还停留在二十多年前那最后一餐的晚宴上。为了父亲的遗骸能早日迁葬，为了剥开隐瞒了二十年的血腥真相，她忍受了一生的“孤独”，耗去了毕生的“幸福”，她从来没有放弃过等待，等待揭开“真相”的那一刻，那一瞬间。
Her father had been dead more than twenty years, and his face and voice, his smile, still lingered at that last supper of more than twenty years before. That her father’s remains might be moved to their proper grave at last, that the bloody truth hidden for twenty years might be laid open, she had borne a lifetime of “loneliness” and spent a whole life’s “happiness”; never had she given up her waiting—waiting for the moment, the very instant, when the “truth” would be uncovered.

> 那一瞬间就快来临了。
That instant was almost here.

> 慈云寺的钟声响起来。
The bell of the Ciyun Temple began to sound.

> 阿初着装严谨，他专程陪着一身素服的四太太到慈云寺来焚香祭祖。
Dressed with strict propriety, A-Chu made the journey to escort Fourth Madam, all in plain mourning white, to the Ciyun Temple to burn incense and make offering to the ancestors.

> 阿初的情绪虽然不高，但也没有四太太那浓郁的愁结。他沿着弯曲的石阶向上走，看着到处用红漆涂写的“佛”字墙壁，感觉到空气中也泛起了藏香的气味。寺院里的佛钟敲响了，满地落红缤纷，阿初的魂魄里宛如行云流动，心境美好，有一种身在世外，清新宁静的感悟。
A-Chu’s spirits were not high, yet he did not carry Fourth Madam’s dense knot of sorrow. He climbed the winding stone steps, looking at the walls where the character 佛, “Buddha,” was written everywhere in red lacquer, and felt the scent of Tibetan incense rise in the air. The temple’s bronze bell rang out, fallen petals lay scattered red across the ground, and within A-Chu’s soul it was as though drifting clouds moved—his heart at ease, with the fresh, still awareness of one who stands beyond the world.

> 他们在佛前许过愿后，四太太叫阿初在佛前抽了一支签。此时，一个身披黑纱长相丑陋的老尼，主动来给阿初解签。
After they had made their vows before the Buddha, Fourth Madam had A-Chu draw a fortune-stick before the image. At this an old nun, draped in black gauze and ugly of face, came of her own accord to read the stick for A-Chu.

> 她递给阿初一张皱巴巴的小纸片，说上面写的都是解签的话。
She handed A-Chu a crumpled scrap of paper and said the words written on it were the reading of the stick.

> 阿初虽然不相信，还是展开来看，上面写了四句话：平生际遇似萍飘，荣华富贵烟云罩。错认他乡是故乡，何日归家洗客袍？
Unbelieving though he was, A-Chu unfolded it to look. On it were written four lines: A whole life’s fortunes drift like duckweed; glory and wealth lie wrapped in cloud and mist. By error he takes the strange land for home—what day shall he go home to wash the traveller’s robe?

> “何日归家洗客袍？”阿初不自觉地重复了一句，什么意思呢？
“What day shall he go home to wash the traveller’s robe?” A-Chu repeated the line without thinking. What could it mean?
