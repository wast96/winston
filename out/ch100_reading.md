### The Glass Boxes

The third-floor boxes of a theatre are priced very cheaply and have always been the place from which the common run of the lower orders watch; as for the great men, the gentry, the men of weight and the celebrities, they mostly disdain to go up into such boxes to hear the opera. In former days, when Xu Shaoqing ran the Dangui First Stage on Fuzhou Road, he had, for a certain period, glass fitted round the outside of the third floor and named it the "glass box." Proprietor Xu's notion was that in this way he might draw the higher class of patron too. But the Beiping-and-Tianjin people speak of "hearing" opera, while the Shanghai people speak of "watching" it; and since watching is the thing, the nearer one's seat to the stage the better. "Glass box" was a fine-sounding name, sure enough, but being up on the third floor it was very awkward for the line of sight, and once fitted out it still failed to draw the higher class of patron, so before long it was torn out and the name "glass box" done away with. (The old site of the Dangui First Stage was itself pulled down the year before last and rebuilt into shops; the Zhimei Lou restaurant now stands there.)
