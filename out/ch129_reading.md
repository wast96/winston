### Trading on a Big Name

A man who runs an unlawful business in this society—an opium-den, a gambling-house, or the like—must have someone to lend him a name and stand behind him (also called a "prop," meaning a bodyguard) before he can be left in peace. To be a prop, one must command a fair amount of hidden influence in society before one can fill the role. Otherwise, if the prop is not tough, then the day trouble comes there are plenty who end up losing face and left stinking like duck-piss.

One often hears two men, A and B, fall to cursing each other, and A demands: "Whose signboard are you leaning on, to throw your weight about like this?"—the phrase for "throwing your weight about" being, in Shanghai slang, a byword for the fierce and overbearing.
