### The Coolies at the Post Office Door

The General Post Office on North Suzhou Road, besides its fixed coolies, needs day after day a gang of coolies hired by the hour, which in the coolies' own words is "hunting pheasants." And so every day there are sure to be a crowd of ragged, unkempt, dirt-faced fellow-sufferers, their brows knit with care, standing before the post-office door, craning their necks and straining their eyes for the doorkeeper to toss out the numbered chits a little sooner. The moment they see the doorkeeper come out and raise his hand, this pack of poor wretches surge forward all together like starving tigers gulping down their prey, hemming the doorkeeper tightly round; and as the chits in his hand are flung up into the air and come scattering down, all one can see is the dust flying up, the shouting deafening, the snatching and trampling of each against each, a mass of black hands groping and grabbing over the ground — those who get one going in to work with a look of joy on their faces, and those who get none able only to stand there, unable to cry, unable to laugh, and slink back crestfallen to the riverside to wait for the second toss of the chits.

Alas! What is it all for? All of it is the bread question. An hour's hard toil, though it be worth but two or three mao, is for them enough to keep three meals free of worry and see a day muddled through.
