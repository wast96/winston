## 10

On 5 May 1946 the Nationalist government held a grand return-to-the-capital ceremony at the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum in Nanjing. Guo Bingyan, however, chose that day to go out beyond the city to Qixia Temple and keep his day of fasting. Toward evening, when the abbot walked him out to the mountain gate himself, there stood on the steps a thin, frail young novice, waiting stiffly upright, a tray held in both hands.

On the tray lay a single silver dollar.

In an instant Guo Bingyan was on his guard, and Adjutant Yan and the attendants tensed with him, reaching inside their coats—but all around them was only the wooded hills in the dusk.

"A lady donor left this, to be returned to its owner." The young novice spoke now, timidly. "She said it was the layman's old possession."

Guo Bingyan reached out and took up the silver dollar, gave it a twist between two fingers, and slid it open—and there inside was the white tablet sealed in thin wax. The shot rang out in that very instant; the bullet passed through Guo Bingyan's skull, and as it struck him down on the steps, the countless birds of the wooded hills burst up in fright and rushed, in a clatter of wings, into the sky.

Half a month later Tang Ya came to Jeju Island, bearing Jiang Yongnan's ashes. Crossing a great stretch of barley fields under harvest, she walked all the way to the sea, and as she entered a fishing village, Jiang Yongzhu, with his wife and daughter, was already waiting outside their old home, dressed in their traditional robes.

Jiang Yongnan was buried at last in his native place, at his parents' side.

When everyone had tactfully withdrawn, Tang Ya took from her bag a bottle of American vodka, and rice wine from Fuling, the shaker, the shot glass, and set them out one by one before the gravestone, and began to mix that cocktail called potassium cyanide. Sitting on the ground, shaking the drink, she said, "I saw that bartender again. It turned out he never went home—he'd opened a bar of his own in Shanghai. And at last I know why we could never taste that fiery flavor again. All along, we'd left one thing out of it: a pinch of salt."

As she spoke she poured the milky liquid into the shot glass, as though Jiang Yongnan sat cross-legged before her and the two of them were drinking together. In her round eyes was refracted a light as warm as the sun.

And she saw again the man who had turned, in the crowd, to look back at her.
