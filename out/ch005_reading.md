### Zhang Taiyan in the Western Gaol

In the Guangxu years of the fallen Qing, Cai Yuanpei, Wu Zhihui, Zhang Taiyan and others got up the Subao, naming Zhang and Zou Rong its chief and deputy editors. The Subao was an organ for the preaching of revolution, and it flayed the cruelty and misgovernment of the Qing court without stint, so that it was much welcomed by readers and, at the same time, marked down by the Qing government.

In the thirty-second year of Guangxu the Subao affair broke: the office was sealed up, and Cai Yuanpei and Wu Zhihui, catching the alarm, made off to Japan ahead of it and escaped the disaster; Zhang Taiyan and Zou Rong were both sentenced to prison and shut up in the Western Gaol. Zou Rong was a Sichuanese, styled Weidan, young and high-mettled, and his pen was of the keenest: he had written a book, The Revolutionary Army, that attacked the house of Aisin Gioro down to the last particular. In confinement he fell ill of grief and rage and died at last in his cell, to everyone's sorrow. Zhang, his term served, was let out, and went straight abroad to Japan; only when the Manchu Qing had been pulled down and the Republic founded did he come home.
