### The Seamstresses

The artisans of Shanghai who cut and make clothes fall, besides the ordinary needleworkers (the Su and Guang ready-made men, that is) and the Red Gang needleworkers (those who make only Western suits and overcoats), into one further kind: the woman tailor.

The woman tailor keeps no shop of her own; when people want her services she comes to the house to work, and provided she is given her meals, one dollar buys four days of her labor. But their skill of hand is always poorer than the men's, and so a woman tailor is set only to cloth garments and children's clothes, which she can just manage; put her to fine silks and satins or to furs, and she must beg to be excused as beyond her.
