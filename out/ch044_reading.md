### College Students

The three words "college student" — how noble a name they make; and society looks on the noble college student with the greatest esteem, much as in the examination days of the Qing it esteemed a juren, a jinshi, a Hanlin. For a child to read his way up from kindergarten all the way to college is truly no easy thing; the old man at home must shoulder a very, very great weight of school fees before a college student can be raised. The middling poor father, wanting his son to study only so far as middle school, finds himself already unequal to it; where is he to find the strength to nurture him up to a university! Reach college, and one has learning of a fair degree, and the old man at home must have a fair few dollars besides. For these two reasons people cluck the tongue in envy and hold the thing in the highest regard.

The college student studies the deep and lofty branches of learning, and sets himself up as a future master of the nation; how single-minded he ought to be, how diligent in his search, delving day and night, if he is one day to hope to turn his learning to use. But your common college student nowadays takes dissipation for his business: dancing, gambling, whoring, cutting a slice at the salt-meat houses, putting up at inns — every one of these tastes he has caught, and, hailing his friends and leading on his fellows, vies in the round of pleasures till it is second nature. What studying is, what learning is, they put clean out of mind, and when graduation comes they wheedle out a mortarboard and a diploma and reckon their duty done. The root of this disease is to be laid at the door of the pheasant universities, for a university of that kind has long embraced laissez-faire toward its students, and the rest of its instruction and discipline goes muddling anyhow along, until this unhappy state of things is brewed. But to speak of the good side, excellent and hard-toiling college students are not, after all, wanting; only these preposterous ones are, too, no small number!
