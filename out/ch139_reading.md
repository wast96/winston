### Selling Long Ingots

On the thirtieth and the fourteenth of every old-calendar month, of an evening, one hears through the streets and lanes the cry "Long ingots, who'll buy? Long ingots, who'll buy?"—shouted back and forth like the passing of a shuttle. Those who sell the long ingots are all women from the country villages near Shanghai; it is a sideline of theirs, and they make the things themselves and hawk them themselves. As for what a long ingot is: tinfoil and paper are worked up together, layer alternating with layer, into the shape of a silver ingot, and these are then strung on a thread into a long chain—whence the name "long ingot." And because the Shanghai folk are fond of their superstitions, on the nights of the thirtieth and the fourteenth they buy a string and burn it, and by this, they say, they may look forward to a half-month's good luck. And so the countrywomen, falling in with what people like, have made of it a fixed little sideline.
