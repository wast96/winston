### Tattooing

The "old brothers" of the town (as the hoodlums style themselves) have their chests and both arms all pricked over with tattoos—landscapes and figures, flowers and plants and fruit and trees, birds of the air and beasts of the field, every sort of thing. The design is blue and the flesh white, and a tattoo makes a man peculiarly conspicuous. And just as an old brother who bore no tattoo would seem to have forfeited his standing, so the hoodlums' missuses too will have a few pricked on, to mark their own dignity.

Some ten-odd years ago the Concession authorities once gave orders for a great round-up of the tattooed brethren: whether you had broken the law or not, let them but catch sight of a tattoo on your arm and off you were hauled to the police station. At this the old brothers hid where they could and dodged where they could, frightened out of their wits. Those who could not get out of the way in time went so far as to seek out a doctor and beg him to take the thing off; but a tattoo, once pricked in, is very hard to be rid of, and so they got themselves all mangled in flesh and blood, the skin horribly split.

In truth the business of tattooing began with the sailors aboard ship. For a man who lives by the sailor's trade, should he have the ill luck to meet with disaster and drown in the great ocean, is all rotted by the time he is fished up, and his true face not easily known; and so he has himself tattooed beforehand, as a token by which his family and friends may know him. How, in course of time, it came to spread across to the "old brothers" who eat the sun by day and the dew by night, there is no telling—which is really rather a puzzle.
