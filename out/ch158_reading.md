### Crying the Fire Watch

When the depth of winter comes, in the small hours of the night you will often see a man carrying a lantern in one hand and a length of bamboo clapper in the other, striking it as he goes and calling out: "Mind your fires! The winter days are dry, the rivers low and the water shallow—prop your front doors, latch your back doors." This cry can be heard all up and down the streets and lanes, and is called "crying the fire watch." On his lantern the character for "fire" is pasted upside down—no one knows the sense of it; some say it is to make people take notice. At the end of every month, long or short, these watchmen go from door to door collecting a few coppers as the price of their fire-crying—which is really just a genteel sort of begging, and no more.
