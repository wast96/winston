## H2 Chapter 9. The One Who Opens the Door Is the One Who Closed It

> 门被推开了。
The doors were pushed open.

> 奇怪的是佛堂里面没有供佛，供了一张发黄的大照片。照片上是一个美丽又可爱的小婴孩，手里举着摇铃，睁着一双清澈的大眼睛。香果和鲜花堆积在这里，一个黑色的灵牌竖在这婴儿照片的底下，提示着婴儿的不幸早夭。阿初不自觉地走近香案，仰起头凝视这婴儿，当他的目光从上到下扫视到灵牌时，他的心禁不住一阵紧缩。灵牌上赫然写着几个烫金字“杨慕初之灵位”。这奇异的照片和诡诈的灵牌使阿初莫名其妙地感到恐惧，仿佛自己就是那死去的婴儿，他的手情不自禁地想去触摸那婴儿平滑光洁的脸。
The strange thing was that the shrine hall held no image of the Buddha, but instead an enshrined, yellowed photograph, large in size. In it was a beautiful, endearing infant, a rattle raised in its hand, a pair of clear, wide eyes gazing out. Incense-fruit and fresh flowers were heaped here, and a black spirit-tablet stood beneath the baby’s photograph, telling of the child’s unlucky early death. A-Chu, without meaning to, drew near the incense table and lifted his head to gaze at the infant; and when his eyes traveled from top to bottom down to the spirit-tablet, his heart could not help contracting. There on the tablet, in glaring gilt characters, was written: “Spirit-Seat of Yang Muchu.” This uncanny photograph and sinister spirit-tablet filled A-Chu with an unaccountable dread, as though he himself were the dead infant; his hand, of its own accord, reached to touch the baby’s smooth, glossy face.

> “别碰他！”仿佛从地狱里传来一声女人地冷喝。阿初本能地打了一个冷战。阿初回过头去，看见一个黑衣裹身、黑纱披头的女人站在自己面前。那女人四十岁上下，一张冷冰冰的脸，叫人怎么看怎么不舒服。
“Don’t touch him!” A woman’s cold cry seemed to come up out of hell. A-Chu shuddered by instinct. He turned his head and saw a woman standing before him, her body wrapped in black, her head draped in black gauze. She was about forty, with a coldly frozen face that one found more disagreeable the longer one looked.

> “你是谁？”女人在看清阿初的容貌后，也不自觉地打了一个冷战。
“Who are you?” Having seen A-Chu’s features clearly, the woman too, without meaning to, gave a shudder.

> “对不起，我走错路了。”阿初尽量在脸上挤出一丝笑容，以示礼貌。
“I’m sorry—I took the wrong path.” A-Chu tried his best to squeeze out a trace of a smile, by way of courtesy.

> “我问你是谁？”
“I asked who you are.”

> “我是杨家的客人。”阿初解释道：“我是来参加杨小姐生日宴会的。我……我一时没注意，走岔了路，府上的确太大了……”那女人不说话，眼珠子一直围着阿初上下乱转，阿初觉得自己很尴尬，后悔自己不该凭着感觉走。“您？您是府上的……？”阿初希望她能主动作答。
“I’m a guest of the Yang family.” A-Chu explained: “I came to attend Miss Yang’s birthday banquet. I… I wasn’t paying attention for a moment and lost my way; the residence really is too vast…” The woman said nothing; her eyes kept roving up and down over A-Chu, and A-Chu felt himself very awkward, regretting that he had walked on by feeling alone. “You? You are the household’s…?” A-Chu hoped she might answer of her own accord.

> “我是杨太太。”
“I am Madam Yang.”

> “杨太太？”阿初以为自己听错了。下意识地又问了一句：“您是杨思桐小姐的母亲？”
“Madam Yang?” A-Chu thought he had heard wrong. Half-consciously he asked again: “You are Miss Yang Sitong’s mother?”

> “是。”
“I am.”

> “那……今天不是您女儿的生日吗？您怎么……穿成这样？”
“Then… isn’t today your daughter’s birthday? How is it that you… are dressed like this?”

> 杨太太沉默不语。
Madam Yang kept silent.

> 阿初觉得自己话多了，勉强笑着说：“对不起，我唐突了。”
A-Chu felt he had said too much, and with a forced smile said, “I’m sorry—I’ve been presumptuous.”

> “你一定很好奇吧，自己女儿的生日，母亲却穿得像个鬼。”杨太太从烟匣子里抽出一支烟来，问阿初：“你抽烟吗？抽就来一支。”
“You must be very curious—your own daughter’s birthday, and the mother dressed like a ghost.” Madam Yang drew a cigarette from a cigarette case and asked A-Chu, “Do you smoke? Have one if you do.”

> “不，谢谢，我不吸烟。”
“No, thank you—I don’t smoke.”

> 杨太太把烟衔在嘴上，正准备掏打火机，阿初习惯成自然地抢先从口袋里摸出打火机替杨太太点燃了烟。
Madam Yang set the cigarette between her lips and was just about to reach for a lighter when A-Chu, from long habit, forestalled her, fishing a lighter from his pocket to light it for her.

> 杨太太斜着眼看着打火机，说：“英国货。”
Madam Yang eyed the lighter sidelong and said, “British made.”

> “是。”阿初应声。
“Yes,” A-Chu answered.

> “你去过英国？”
“You’ve been to England?”

> “是，在英国待了八年。”
“Yes—I spent eight years in England.”

> “那你还回来？”
“And still you came back?”

> “家在上海。”阿初说到“家”的时候，杨太太抬了抬头。
“Home is in Shanghai.” When A-Chu said the word “home,” Madam Yang raised her head a little.

> “你不抽烟，却随身携带打火机？”
“You don’t smoke, yet you carry a lighter with you?”

> 阿初笑笑，不作回应。
A-Chu smiled and made no reply.

> 杨太太吸了口烟，幽幽地叹了口气，说：“今天是我儿子的祭日。”她说完这句话，突然笑起来，仰面看着婴儿的照片。“你看，他多漂亮。”
Madam Yang drew on her cigarette, let out a long, faint sigh, and said, “Today is my son’s death-day.” The words spoken, she suddenly began to laugh, looking up at the infant’s photograph. “See how beautiful he is.”

> 这是一个伤心的母亲，阿初想。女儿的生日居然是儿子的祭日，这种生日，不过也罢。偏偏杨家摆出天大的气势来替女儿过生日，难道就没有一个人顾虑母亲的感受吗？
This is a grieving mother, A-Chu thought. That a daughter’s birthday should be a son’s death-day—such a birthday might as well go unkept. Yet the Yang family had chosen to make so grand a show of celebrating the daughter’s birthday; was there not one soul who considered the mother’s feelings?

> “逝者已逝，您不要太难过。”
“The dead are gone; you mustn’t grieve too much.”

> “你是哪家府上的公子？思桐的朋友没有我不知道的，怎么以前从来没有见过你？”
“Which household’s young gentleman are you? There’s no friend of Sitong’s I don’t know—how is it I’ve never seen you before?”

> 阿初并不正面回答，他随手取出自己刚印的名片，双手奉上。并用他游刄有余的社交手段来迂回变幻。“我叫阿初，刚从英国回来。我是医生，在同济医院工作。是第一次到府上来，很高兴认识杨太太。”
A-Chu did not answer head-on; he simply took out one of his newly printed calling cards and presented it with both hands, deploying his consummate social craft to turn and shift the talk. “My name is A-Chu; I’ve just returned from England. I’m a physician, working at the Tongji Hospital. It’s my first time to your residence, and I’m very glad to make Madam Yang’s acquaintance.”

> 阿初和杨太太做了简短的交谈后，有礼貌地跟杨太太告辞。他离开阴森的佛堂后，俨如一个被缚多年的囚犯挣脱了身上枷锁，觉得异常轻松。
After a brief exchange with Madam Yang, A-Chu courteously took his leave. Once he had left the gloomy shrine hall, he was like a prisoner long bound who has thrown off the shackles from his body, feeling strangely light.

> 太不正常了。阿初在想。
It’s all too abnormal, A-Chu thought.

> 自己仿佛熟悉这里的一切，但是，一接近、一触摸，他就会有沉重感，自己的思想也呈迷失状，他并不想在黑夜中去寻觅“真相”，他害怕背负着漆黑的死亡。就像那照片上的婴儿。
He seemed to know everything here; yet the moment he drew near, the moment he touched, a heaviness would come over him, and his mind would fall into a lost, straying state. He had no wish to seek out the “truth” in the dark of night; he was afraid of shouldering a pitch-black death—like the infant in that photograph.

> 他要回到现实中去。真实的生活场景会使自己感到安全，因为那里洋溢着“生”的温暖。当阿初走着捷径，熟门熟路地走回灯火辉煌的大厅时，令他感到十分意外的事发生了。
He wanted to return to reality. Scenes of real, living life would make him feel safe, for there the warmth of the “living” overflowed. When A-Chu, taking a shortcut, made his familiar way back to the brilliantly lit hall, something happened that took him utterly by surprise.

> 一个浑身酒气的少爷强行拉着荣荣的手，满嘴的胡言乱语，荣荣在惊叫，大厅里的人在纷纷解劝，包括杨思桐也在气急败坏的喝止。
A young gentleman reeking of drink was forcibly gripping Rongrong’s hand and pouring out a stream of drunken nonsense; Rongrong was crying out in alarm, and the people in the hall were all trying to talk him down—Yang Sitong among them, sharply and furiously ordering him to stop.

> 原来，这借酒撒疯的主，不是别人，就是跟清朝遗老遗少和家的大小姐和雅姗订了亲事，又泡了汤的少爷，汤少棋的哥哥汤少礼。此人，原是个“五毒”俱全的花花公子，仗着父辈的福荫，靠几家古董铺子讨生活。是圈子里出了名的纨绔，他还以“怡红公子”自居，自作多情。
It turned out that this fellow raising a drunken ruckus was none other than the young gentleman who had been engaged to the eldest daughter of the He family—Manchu remnants of the fallen Qing—only for the match to fall through: Tang Shaoli, elder brother of Tang Shaoqi. This man was a dandy steeped in every one of the “five poisons,” living off the shade of his forefathers’ merit and scraping by on a few antique shops. He was a notorious wastrel in the smart set, and styled himself the “Gallant of the Crimson Bower,” fancying himself irresistible.

> 旗人和家原先也是高不可攀的皇室贵胄，可是，时过境迁，和家的经济地位受政治地位的直接冲击，整个成了一个破落户。还好，饿死的骆驼比马大，有一个“家族地位”保驾护航，又生得两个如花似玉的女儿，不愁不嫁个有钱人家。
The He family, Bannermen, had once been unapproachable royal kin; but times change, and the He family’s economic standing, struck directly by the blow to its political standing, had gone wholly to seed. Still, a starved camel is bigger than a horse: with a “family rank” to guard and guide them, and having borne two daughters fair as flowers, they need not fear failing to marry into some wealthy house.

> 所以，汤家去和家提亲，水到渠成。
And so, when the Tang family came to the He family to propose, it was as water flowing into its channel.

> 没曾想，大小姐和雅姗不同意，半夜里跟个穷学生私奔了。和家丢了个大活人，汤家丢了个大面子。
Who could have foreseen it—the eldest daughter, He Yashan, would not agree, and in the dead of night eloped with a poor student. The He family lost a living person, and the Tang family lost a great deal of face.

> 为了挽回两家的名声，和家决定由二小姐和雅淑代嫁，和家与汤家仍是亲家。可是，“好事多磨”，这二小姐死活不肯嫁到汤家去，说是：汤大少恶名远扬，风流成性。况且，他是和姐姐订的婚事，就是姐夫了。小姨子怎么能去嫁姐夫呢？乱了伦常。二小姐说的振振有词，堵的汤家哑口无言。本来，汤大少对这对木头姊妹花没什么大兴趣，可是，自从报纸上，大炒特炒药业首富公子荣升回国邂逅和雅淑一幕，写的活灵活现，什么地下情人，什么深情拥抱，还把汤少求亲失败拿来大肆渲染，弄得汤大少灰头土脸，发誓要把和家的丫头娶回来做老婆，不为别的，就咽不下这口气！
To retrieve the good name of both houses, the He family resolved that the second daughter, He Yashu, should marry in her sister’s stead, so that the He and Tang families remained in-laws all the same. But—good things are much thwarted—this second daughter would not, for the life of her, marry into the Tang house. She said: the eldest Tang was of ill fame far and wide, dissolute by nature; and besides, he had been betrothed to her elder sister, and so was her brother-in-law. How could a younger sister-in-law marry her own brother-in-law? It would throw the bonds of kinship into confusion. The second daughter spoke with such force of reason that she left the Tang family without a word to answer. In truth, the eldest Tang had had no great interest in this pair of wooden sister-flowers; but ever since the newspapers made such a sensational splash of the scene in which the pharmaceutical magnate’s son, Rong Sheng, home from abroad, chanced to meet He Yashu—painted to the life, all “secret lovers” and “fervent embraces,” with Tang’s failed suit dragged in and played up for all it was worth—the eldest Tang had been left crestfallen and had sworn to take the He family’s girl to wife, for no other reason than that he could not swallow the affront!

> 不巧，今天在这里看见荣荣，汤少礼就借酒滋事，汤少棋怕把事情闹大，于是首当其冲地拉架。
As ill luck would have it, seeing Rongrong here today, Tang Shaoli seized on drink to make trouble, and Tang Shaoqi, fearing the matter would blow up, bore the brunt of it and rushed in to break up the fight.

> “哥哥，你放手啊。”汤少棋死命地拽着汤少礼的领子。
“Brother, let go.” Tang Shaoqi tugged with all her might at Tang Shaoli’s collar.

> “放手？凭什么放手？应该叫她哥哥放手，叫荣大少放手，他凭什么霸占我的女人？他是比我有钱？还是比我有势？”汤少礼在吼。
“Let go? Why should I let go? It’s her brother who should let go, that Rong fellow who should let go—by what right does he seize my woman? Is he richer than I am? Is he mightier than I am?” Tang Shaoli was roaring.

> “你干什么！”阿初上前，护住荣荣。
“What are you doing!” A-Chu came forward and shielded Rongrong.

> “他喝醉了。”思桐在解释。
“He’s drunk,” Sitong explained.

> 荣荣总算盼到了救星，大声叫着阿初。
Rongrong had at last got her savior, and cried out A-Chu’s name aloud.

> “英雄救美？啊？英雄救美！阿初？我知道你是谁！我汤大少爷知道你的底细！”汤少礼讥笑地用手指戳了戳自己的脑门，又醉醺醺地指向阿初。“我知道你是谁？荣家小公子？我在报纸上看过你，看穿了你。要不要我在大家面前揭穿你的真面目？”
“A hero saving a beauty? Eh? A hero saving a beauty! A-Chu? I know who you are! I, the eldest Tang, know your true colors!” Tang Shaoli jabbed a mocking finger at his own forehead, then swung it drunkenly toward A-Chu. “I know who you are! The little Rong gentleman? I’ve seen you in the papers—I’ve seen through you. Shall I strip off your true face here in front of everyone?”

> 阿初冷笑。“你想说什么？尽管说。”
A-Chu laughed coldly. “What is it you want to say? Say it, by all means.”

> “好，这是你说的。大家都来看看，看呀，这个冒牌货！这个冒充贵族的下等人。他是荣家大少爷的听佣，一个冒充贵族的可怜虫，居然敢冠冕堂皇地走进来，不，是混进来，荣荣，你真会'玩'，玩得够出格。你哥哥抢我老婆，你呢，跟下人厮混……”话音未落，荣荣举手给了他一记耳光。“跟你这种粗浅鄙陋的人说话，简直就是对我的侮辱！我现在知道那和家两姊妹为什么死也不肯嫁你了，像你这种人渣，根本不配拥有家庭。”
“Good—those are your own words. Everyone, come and look, look here at this counterfeit! This low creature passing himself off as a noble. He’s the hired servant of the Rong family’s eldest Young Master, a pitiful wretch masquerading as gentility, who has dared to march in here bold as brass—no, to sneak in. Rongrong, you really know how to ‘play’—played it clear out of bounds. Your brother steals my wife, and you—you tumble about with a servant…” The words not yet out, Rongrong’s hand flew up and dealt him a slap. “To speak with a coarse, base creature like you is nothing but an insult to me! Now I see why those two He sisters would rather die than marry you. Scum like you doesn’t deserve a family at all.”

> “荣荣你太过分了。”汤少棋开始维护自己的哥哥了。“你带一个下等人来参加上流社会的晚会，本身就是对主人的不尊重，是对上流社会的集体污辱。你还口不择言……”
“Rongrong, you go too far.” Tang Shaoqi began to defend her brother. “Bringing a low creature to a high-society soirée is in itself a disrespect to the host, a collective insult to polite society. And you, running on with such reckless words…”

> “你住嘴！”阿初生平第一次在女人面前发火了。“小心你的假牙掉出来。”
“Hold your tongue!” For the first time in his life A-Chu lost his temper before a woman. “Mind your false teeth don’t fall out.”

> “你敢讽刺我，取笑我。”汤少棋尖叫起来。在一群女人面前讽刺一个女人的容貌，是及其刻薄的行为。“思桐，这个下等人居然敢当众侮辱我！”
“You dare to mock me, to make sport of me.” Tang Shaoqi shrieked. To mock a woman’s looks before a crowd of women is a most cutting act. “Sitong, this low creature has dared to insult me in public!”

> “先生，请注意你的言行。”杨思桐的心情十分恶劣，自己的生日宴会被这群疯子搞得一塌糊涂。
“Sir, mind your words and conduct.” Yang Sitong was in a foul temper; her birthday banquet had been thrown into utter chaos by this pack of madmen.

> “小姐，到现在为止，我还没有一句不敬之词奉上。不过，人的忍耐是有限度的。来参加今天的晚会，对我来说，并非什么殊荣，如果是由于我导致了今天的不愉快，我向您道歉，小姐，毕竟今天是您的生日。但是，对于这位先生种种可恶的言行，我觉得，他应该向荣小姐道歉。”阿初说。
“Miss, up to now I have not offered a single disrespectful word. But there is a limit to a man’s patience. To attend tonight’s soirée is no great honor to me, and if it is I who have caused today’s unpleasantness, I apologize to you, Miss—after all, today is your birthday. But as for this gentleman’s every hateful word and deed, I think he owes Miss Rong an apology.” So said A-Chu.

> “可是，汤少本身就是一个叛逆。对于一个叛逆者而言，他古怪的言行是可以原谅的。”杨思桐显然在偏袒汤家。
“But the young Tang is himself a rebel. And a rebel’s odd words and deeds may be forgiven.” Yang Sitong was plainly taking the Tang family’s part.

> “没有善恶观念的人，根本不配做个'叛逆'！”阿初轻蔑地说。“小姐，对于您的刻意偏袒，我感到非常遗憾。我们走吧，荣荣，不需要为了别人的庸俗和堕落而感到丝毫抱歉和内疚。”
“A man with no sense of good and evil is not fit to be a ‘rebel’ at all!” said A-Chu with contempt. “Miss, at your deliberate partiality I feel deep regret. Let us go, Rongrong—there’s no need to feel the least apology or guilt over the vulgarity and degeneracy of others.”

> “你们别想走！”汤少礼饿虎扑食般向荣荣扑过来。
“Don’t think you’re leaving!” Tang Shaoli lunged at Rongrong like a starving tiger pouncing on its prey.

> “放手！”阿初大声呵斥。
“Let go!” A-Chu shouted sternly.

> “欲望……不是善恶的问题。欲壑难填你没听过吗？”汤少礼不但没放手，反而全身压了上来。“欲望驱使人作恶。欲望没有错，为什么每个女人都妄想占有自己男人的全部灵魂，不，是肉体。自私，不肯分享爱情。于是，女人们得到了男人无情的背叛，抛弃。爱为什么不能有瑕疵呢？残缺的爱才是最美丽的。”
“Desire… is not a matter of good and evil. Haven’t you heard that the gulf of desire is never filled? Desire drives a man to do evil. There’s nothing wrong with desire—why must every woman fondly hope to possess the whole of her man’s soul—no, his body? Selfish, unwilling to share their love. And so women earn men’s heartless betrayal and abandonment. Why can’t love have its flaws? Flawed love is the most beautiful of all.” Tang Shaoli, far from letting go, pressed his whole weight upon her.

> “你神经病！”荣荣开始大骂起来。
“You madman!” Rongrong began to curse aloud.

> 阿初用力将汤少礼的手从荣荣身上拉开。汤少礼的酒色身子一软，被阿初摔倒在地。“太不文明了！”汤少礼就地坐直了身。“粗暴！下等人！不要以为我失去了和家两姊妹，我就会还原一步，降格以求。不，绝不可能。我汤少礼就是化了风，挫成了灰，长成青苔，变了种，那也是上等人，在你面前，那也是参天大树！”
A-Chu wrenched Tang Shaoli’s hand from Rongrong’s body by force. Tang Shaoli’s body, softened by drink and lust, gave way, and A-Chu threw him to the ground. “How uncivilized!” Tang Shaoli sat up where he had fallen. “Brutish! Low creature! Don’t imagine that because I lost the two He sisters, I’ll take a step back and stoop to lower my sights. No—never. Even if I, Tang Shaoli, were turned to wind, ground to ash, grown into moss, changed into another kind—I would still be a man of the upper sort; before you, I would still be a soaring, sky-high tree!”

> “什么是上等人？现在还有贵族吗？爱新觉罗也改姓金了。你算哪棵葱？”阿初的话很平和，但是很尖酸。“你汤少礼就是化了风，挫成了灰，长成青苔，变了种，那也是个暴发户，温室里的草，阳光尚且不能见，谈何参天大树？荣荣，我们走，再多待一分钟，我都觉得厌恶。诸位失陪。”阿初拉着荣荣径直向门外走去，他高昂着头，活像一个骑士带走了自己心爱的姑娘。
“What is a man of the upper sort? Are there still nobles now? Even the Aisin Gioro have changed their surname to Jin. What sort of nobody are you?” A-Chu’s words were mild, but very biting. “Even if you, Tang Shaoli, were turned to wind, ground to ash, grown into moss, changed into another kind, you’d still be an upstart, a grass in a hothouse that can’t so much as bear the sun—what talk is there of a sky-high tree? Rongrong, let’s go; one more minute here and I shall be sick with disgust. Ladies and gentlemen, I take my leave.” A-Chu drew Rongrong straight out the door, his head held high, for all the world like a knight bearing off the maiden he loved.

> “真是丢人丢到家了。”杨思桐气冲冲上楼去了。
“What a disgrace—shamed to the last degree.” Yang Sitong stormed off upstairs in a fury.

> “思桐，等等我。”汤少棋紧跟上去陪不是，华丽的大厅里，空留下一群扫兴无趣的宾客。
“Sitong, wait for me.” Tang Shaoqi hurried after to make her apologies, and in the splendid hall there remained only a crowd of guests, out of spirits and out of humor.

> 荣荣几乎是被阿初连拉带拖地走出来得，阿初还嫌她动作慢，索性将她抱起来，走到停车坪，侍应生替他打开车门，阿初直接把荣荣扔到副驾上，自己上车，发动了车子。荣荣看他脸若冰霜，也不敢搭腔讲笑话。一路上，两个人都不说话。等他们回到家，才发现荣荣脚上的水晶鞋少了一只。
Rongrong was all but hauled and dragged out by A-Chu; and finding even that too slow, he simply swept her up into his arms and carried her to the parking court, where an attendant opened the car door for him. A-Chu set Rongrong straight into the passenger seat, got in himself, and started the car. Seeing his face like frost, she did not dare venture a word or a joke. The whole way home the two of them said nothing. Only when they reached the house did they find that one of the crystal shoes was missing from Rongrong’s foot.

> “怎么办？”荣荣苦着脸说。“怎么跟四姨娘讲？她最喜欢这双鞋子了。”
“What am I to do?” Rongrong said with a woeful face. “How shall I explain it to Fourth Aunt? She loves that pair of shoes best of all.”

> “我去跟干娘说。”阿初说。
“I’ll go and tell my godmother,” said A-Chu.

> 阿初硬着头皮，拿了一只水晶鞋子去见四太太，他委婉地讲述了失鞋的过程。总之，是自己不小心，是自己不对，下次，他想办法把鞋子找回来，求四太太原谅等等。
A-Chu steeled himself, took the one crystal shoe, and went to see Fourth Madam, recounting in careful, roundabout terms how the shoe had come to be lost. In sum: it was his own carelessness, his own fault; next time he would find a way to recover the shoe; he begged Fourth Madam’s forgiveness, and so on.

> “当真是在杨家遗失的吗？”四太太反复地询问同一个问题，她似乎对鞋子的遗失并不在意，她关心的是鞋子所遗之处。
“Was it truly at the Yang house that it was lost?” Fourth Madam kept asking the same question over and over. She seemed not to mind the loss of the shoe at all; what she cared about was the place where the shoe had been lost.

> “是在杨家。”阿初肯定地说。
“It was at the Yang house,” A-Chu said with certainty.

> “你保证？”
“You’re sure?”

> “绝对是。”
“Absolutely.”

> “好极了。”四太太脸上绽放出一种令人难以捉摸的笑容。“比我想像的还要好。谢谢你，阿初。”
“Splendid.” A smile past all fathoming bloomed on Fourth Madam’s face. “Better even than I had hoped. Thank you, A-Chu.”

> 阿初觉得四太太的话，匪夷所思，令他入坠五里雾中……
A-Chu found Fourth Madam’s words beyond all reason, and felt himself lost in a five-mile fog…

> 杨羽柏，一个地地道道的冒险家，一个经历了晚清崩溃时代的商人，一个处于列强瓜分中国危险时代的银行家，一个极具深厚文化涵养的人。他自认能洞识世界经济的潮流，当这个国家陷入困境和衰弱，当日本人的经济和军事威胁迫于眉际时，他依然能从容不迫地应付自如，一跃而成为经济舞台上的台柱，这是他生平最得意的地方。
Yang Yubo—an adventurer through and through, a merchant who had lived through the age of the late Qing’s collapse, a banker in the perilous age when the great powers were carving up China, a man of deep cultivation. He held that he could read the tides of the world economy; and when the country sank into distress and decline, when the economic and military menace of the Japanese pressed hard upon his brow, he could still meet it all with unhurried composure, leaping at a bound to become a mainstay of the economic stage—this was the pride of his whole life.

> 他的卧房布置得古香古色，充满了与他年龄极不相仿的浪漫色彩。
His bedchamber was furnished in an antique, old-world taste, filled with a romantic color that ill accorded with his years.

> 他已经不习惯大厅里高朋满座，语喧声腾了。所以，他躲在自己狭小的私人空间里寻找一些缥缈的幻影，那是他喜欢的女人的影子。来自内心的敏感和虚弱，时时困扰着他幽密不宣的世界。
He was no longer used to a hall crowded with distinguished guests, loud with the clamor of voices. And so he hid himself in his own narrow private space, seeking after certain faint, elusive phantoms—the shadow of the woman he loved. A sensitivity and frailty that came from within tormented, again and again, his secret, unspoken world.

> 正在杨羽柏享受宁静的时刻，杨太太来了。她穿了套薄薄的春衫，脸上涂了厚厚的粉，脚下汲了两只木屐。
Just as Yang Yubo was enjoying this moment of quiet, Madam Yang came. She wore a thin spring blouse, her face thick with powder, a pair of wooden clogs on her feet.

> “先生，我来了。”她谦卑地九十度鞠躬，杨羽柏能清晰地看见她盘踞在头发上红色绒花的金丝线。那是二十多年前，他买给她的。
“Sir, I’ve come.” She bowed humbly, ninety degrees at the waist, and Yang Yubo could plainly see the gold thread on the red velvet flower nested in her hair. He had bought it for her more than twenty years before.

> “你不用这样卑躬屈膝。”杨羽柏说。
“You needn’t be so servile,” said Yang Yubo.

> “我想用我特殊的方式表达对先生的爱。”她的声音柔媚，不像年近五十的人。但是，杨羽柏听到耳里，很不舒服。
“I want to express my love for you in my own special way.” Her voice was soft and beguiling, not like a woman near fifty. But to Yang Yubo’s ear it was most unpleasant.

> “我讨厌你鞠躬的姿势。”杨羽柏很不客气。
“I hate the way you bow,” said Yang Yubo, without ceremony.

> “我以为你喜欢。”
“I thought you liked it.”

> “那是从前。英子。”
“That was long ago. Yingzi.”

> “终于肯叫我的名字了。”杨太太异常激动。“我等这一天，等得太久了。我们不要再相互折磨了，忘记吧，忘记所谓的怨恨，怨恨，会让你变得自私、狭隘、丑陋。”
“So you’re willing at last to call me by my name.” Madam Yang was strangely moved. “I’ve waited too long for this day. Let us stop tormenting each other. Forget it—forget the so-called rancor. Rancor will make you selfish, narrow, ugly.”

> “我还不够自私、狭隘和丑陋吗？二十年前我们做了什么？伤天害理啊！这二十几年来，我一直在痛苦的深渊里辗转，我，我连自己养了二十几年的儿子都不敢正面相对，我还能做什么？我还能为你做什么？”
“Am I not selfish, narrow, and ugly enough already? What did we do twenty years ago? An offense against heaven and reason! For twenty-odd years I have tossed and turned in a pit of anguish—I, I dare not even face the son I’ve raised these twenty-odd years. What else can I do? What else can I do for you?”

> “你可以做我的男人。”
“You can be my man.”

> “你的脸！你的脸一直在提醒我，告诉我，我是个作恶多端的罪人。”
“Your face! Your face has never stopped reminding me, telling me, that I am a sinner steeped in evil.”

> “我的脸，是为了你牺牲的。”她冲动地拉过杨羽柏的手，让他的手抚摸自己苍白的面颊。“我的脸，一直努力地在帮你掩盖事实的真相。不是吗？”
“My face—I sacrificed it for you.” On an impulse she seized Yang Yubo’s hand and made it stroke her own pale cheek. “My face has striven all this while to help you cover up the truth of the matter. Has it not?”

> “事实是无法掩盖的。”杨羽柏抽回了自己的手。
“The truth cannot be covered up.” Yang Yubo drew his hand back.

> “事实上，你已经十几年没有碰过我了，我是个女人！”她声嘶力竭地叫喊着。“我是你的女人！”
“In fact, you haven’t touched me in more than ten years—and I am a woman!” She screamed herself hoarse. “I am your woman!”

> “我从来没有否认过。”杨羽柏冷淡地回应。
“I have never denied it,” Yang Yubo answered coldly.

> “那你证明给我看！”杨太太猛地把睡衣脱掉，她虽然青春已逝，但是过度的保养，使她的皮肤依旧光滑细腻。可是，在杨羽柏眼里，白色毛孔里总会溢出猩红的血，很多年了，他从来没告诉过她，他现在已经不能碰女人了。
“Then prove it to me!” Madam Yang wrenched off her nightgown. Though her youth was gone, her excessive care of herself had kept her skin smooth and fine as ever. But in Yang Yubo’s eyes, crimson blood always seemed to seep from the white pores. For many years now he had never told her that he could no longer touch a woman.

> 他只要一看到女人的身体，他就会看见血，他唯恐自己会得神经分裂症。“我不需要用爱去证明对你的忠诚，我已经为你付出了人世间最惨痛的代价！你以为，我让你寂寞孤独的活着，是利用你的身体对你进行谴责和清算。你错了，我不碰你，是怕自己伤害你。”
He had only to look at a woman’s body and he would see blood, and he dreaded that he would fall into a splitting of the mind. “I don’t need to prove my faithfulness to you with love. I have already paid the most agonizing price this world can exact for your sake! You suppose that I have let you live on, lonely and forsaken, in order to use your body to reproach and settle accounts with you. You are wrong. I don’t touch you because I fear I would harm you.”

> “你说得很动听，可是我，不相信。我知道，你爱她！”
“Your words are very fine to hear; but I, I don’t believe them. I know—you love her!”

> “不！”
“No!”

> “你从来没有得到过她！是你杀了她！”
“You never once possessed her! It was you who killed her!”

> “不是的！”杨羽柏像困兽一样红了眼。
“It’s not so!” Yang Yubo’s eyes went red like a beast at bay.

> “我告诉你，告诉你一个秘密，一个你梦寐以求的好消息。她们没有死！她们一直都活着！”杨太太的脸仿佛刹那间被撕裂了，露出极不协调的狰狞面目。
“I’ll tell you—tell you a secret, a piece of good news you’ve dreamed of waking and sleeping. They are not dead! They have been alive all along!” Madam Yang’s face seemed torn open in an instant, baring a grotesque, ill-fitting savagery.

> “你胡说！”杨羽柏咆哮。
“You’re talking nonsense!” Yang Yubo roared.

> “我看见他了。”
“I saw him.”

> “他？他是谁？”
“Him? Who is he?”

> “你的另一个'儿子'。二十年来不断带给你梦魇的'儿子'，那个你曾经告诉我已经死了的孩子。我看见他了，亲眼目睹，我真不敢相信……”
“Your other ‘son.’ The ‘son’ who has brought you nightmares without end these twenty years—the child you once told me was dead. I saw him, saw him with my own eyes; I could scarcely believe it…”

> “不，不会的……”
“No—it can’t be…”

> “没有这样逼真的画面，活脱脱就是他父亲！”
“No image could be so lifelike—the very living likeness of his father!”

> 杨羽柏浑身瘫软地坐在了沙发上，他的额头在冒汗。
Yang Yubo sank limp and boneless onto the sofa, sweat starting from his brow.

> “他们都活着，他们像地沟里的老鼠，一直潜藏在阴暗的角落，等待时机，撕嚼我们的肉，痛饮我们的血，他们等了二十多年，你认为他们会善罢甘休吗？”
“They are all alive; like rats in a gutter they have been lurking all this while in the shadowed corners, biding their time, to tear our flesh and drink deep of our blood. They have waited twenty-odd years—do you think they will let the matter rest?”

> “你危言耸听。”
“You’re crying wolf.”

> “这个人必须死。”
“This man must die.”

> “我们还不知道他是谁。”
“We don’t even know who he is yet.”

> “我这里有他的名片，你要不相信我的话，自己亲自去看看病。也许，能把顽疾给根除了。”
“I have his calling card here. If you won’t believe what I say, go and see the doctor yourself. Perhaps you may root out the stubborn malady.”

> 杨羽柏没有了丝毫斗志，他接过了英子手上的名片。
Yang Yubo had not a shred of fight left in him; he took the calling card from Yingzi’s hand.

> “还有一件东西，我想你一定会感兴趣。”杨太太不知什么时候，手中拎起了一只鞋子，当杨羽柏看见这只鞋子的时候，脸色大变，仓皇至极，恐惧万分。
“There’s one thing more I think you’ll surely find of interest.” When, Madam Yang could not have said, a shoe had appeared in her hand; and when Yang Yubo saw the shoe his face changed utterly, panic-stricken to the last degree, terrified beyond measure.

> 那是一只漂亮的水晶鞋。
It was a beautiful crystal shoe.

> “你记性很好，还认得此物。”
“Your memory is very good—you know it still.”

> “你从哪里得来得？”
“Where did you get it?”

> “家里的草坪上。”
“On the lawn at home.”

> “鬼使神差，鬼使神差。”杨羽柏喃喃自语。
“By some devil’s contrivance, some devil’s contrivance,” Yang Yubo muttered to himself.

> “鬼蜮伎俩！是鬼蜮伎俩。”
“A ghost’s sly trick! It is a ghost’s sly trick.”

> “她来了？”杨羽柏的瞳孔几乎要鼓爆了。
“She has come?” Yang Yubo’s pupils all but burst from their sockets.

> “应该是，'鬼'来了。”杨太太说的阴森又暧昧，她充满鬼气的眼睛里闪着鬼火般的磷光。
“It should be so—the ‘ghost’ has come.” Madam Yang spoke it grim and ambiguous, and in her ghost-haunted eyes there glimmered a phosphor-light like a ghost-fire.
