### Counterfeit Silver Dollars

Counterfeit silver dollars, too, are the forger's work; and they fall roughly into four kinds: (1) copper-cored, (2) all-copper, (3) acid-treated, and (4) edge-filed. The copper-cored and the all-copper, they say, must be made in secret and on a large scale; the acid and the edge-filed are the work of idle hands from the little native banks and off-duty men from the silversmiths' shops. The acid dollar is made with a kind of silver-eating solution: a good dollar is dropped into the solution and taken out again after a few minutes, whereupon a layer has gone from the coin; the silver so shed sinks to the bottom, and by dropping the coins in one at a time and gathering little into much, the forgers get their wish and reach their unlawful end of gain—though the good dollars are all left the lighter for it. As for the edge-filing dodge, a single file is all it takes, ground along the rim of the dollar to shave off the silver. Only, once a good dollar has been filed, its weight is reduced, and in changing it one must lose the agio besides. But the forger thinks only of his own profit, and for the rest cares nothing at all. The copper-cored and the all-copper are called counterfeit dollars; the acid and the edge-filed can only be called debased coin, for they still pass for money—except that the master of the money-changing shop pockets a little extra by them.
