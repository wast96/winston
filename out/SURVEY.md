# Structure survey - Zhou Enlai: Commander of the Hidden Front

## What this book is, and what follows from it

*Zhou Enlai: Commander of the Hidden Front* (隐蔽战线统帅周恩来) is a 537-page
narrative history of the Chinese Communist Party's Central Special Section
(中央特科), the underground intelligence and security service Zhou Enlai built
and ran in Shanghai from 1927 into the early 1930s. Mu Xin (穆欣) wrote it in
2001 out of decades of interviews with the surviving agents; China Youth Press
issued it in January 2002. It is a large book, roughly twice the length of a
typical project here (377,000 Chinese characters), and its shape and its traps
follow from four facts about it.

1. **It is clean, modern, simplified-Chinese typesetting.** Horizontal text,
   a 2002 offset print, no vertical columns and no traditional characters. OCR
   should be far kinder here than on a Republican-era scan: the model is
   `chi_sim`, psm 6. The recurring risk is not gibberish but plausible name
   mangles, and this book is wall-to-wall names, so the targeted crop-verify
   pass is where the accuracy budget goes.

2. **It is a partisan history dense with real people.** Almost every section is
   built around a named agent or adversary: Zhou Enlai, Chen Geng, Li Kenong,
   Qian Zhuangfei, Hu Di, Gu Shunzhang, Yang Dengying, Xu Enzeng, Chiang
   Kai-shek, and dozens more, plus institutions (the Special Section's four
   branches, the concession police, the Songhu Garrison Command, the KMT
   Investigation Section) and set-piece events (the April 12 coup, the
   September 18 incident, Gu Shunzhang's 1931 defection, the forged Wu Hao
   Notice). Footnote density will run high and stay high: this is a book whose
   whole value to a Western reader is in the apparatus. It is also frankly
   written from inside the CCP tradition, so the fact-checking discipline
   matters. Several of its central episodes (the Gu Shunzhang defection, the
   Wu Hao Notice affair) are unusually well documented in independent
   scholarship and make good corroboration targets; verdicts go in the notes.

3. **The front matter is a long photo-plate gathering, and the body offset is
   constant.** PDF pages 3 to 34 are a run of about 32 numbered plate folios
   (portraits of the agents, Zhou's and Mao's handwriting, a captioned密信),
   on their own sequence. Then a 3-page 前言 (PDF 35 to 37), a 6-page 目录
   (PDF 38 to 43), and the body opens at PDF 44 = printed page 1. Because the
   plates are all up front, the body offset never drifts: printed = pdf minus
   43, verified at chapter 1 (p.1), the Wu Hao chapter (p.509) and the
   afterword (p.537). The plate captions name people who recur in the text, so
   the plates become figures with real alt text and cross-reference the
   footnotes. (Note: in the auto-outline below, the Preface shows as 9 pages
   because the span runs to the next opener; its real length is 3 pages, the
   other 6 being the table of contents.)

4. **The physical copy is a stamped library book.** It is Xidian University's
   copy: a red oval seal, a barcode, and a little handwriting sit on the cover
   and its verso. These are cosmetic and do not touch the body text. There are
   no embedded PDF bookmarks, so this structure was recovered from the printed
   目录 and verified folio by folio against the scan.

The structure has no numbered chapters: the book runs as 25 titled narrative
chapters, framed by a 前言, a 结束语 (Conclusion), and a 后记 (Afterword). The
proposed batching below is one to two chapters each, about 30 printed pages, so
each batch is one focused conversation; the final batch (the Wu Hao chapter plus
the two closing pieces) is kept light to carry the back matter, cover, and
whole-book reconciliation.
