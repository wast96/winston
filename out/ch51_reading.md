## 11

It was near dusk when Yanzhi came back to Fei Family Village; snow was falling from the sky, and the village lay as quiet as ever. This was the first spring festival since the founding of New China. Yanzhi's five mu of land had long since been parceled out to two households, and her courtyard had become the work team's office and canteen. The head of the work team read her paper through and said that once the new year had turned, he would have half a room cleared for her. Yanzhi said, "This is my home."

The team head said, "It's the world of the laboring people now."

Yanzhi argued no more; there was no longer any place in Fei Family Village for her to stand. Yanzhi wanted only to take away the portrait that had hung at the head of her bed, and so she begged the team head to let her look around one last time. He nodded and followed behind her as she went through every room. The painting was long gone without a trace. Growing anxious, Yanzhi searched the whole courtyard again along the foot of the wall. The team head asked her what on earth she was looking for. Yanzhi said, a painting. The team head said that a bourgeois thing like that had long since been buried along with the old society.

Yanzhi left the village the way she had come, walking on and on through the snow with no notion where she was bound. After dark the wind dropped and the snow stopped too, and between heaven and earth there was not a sound. Yanzhi thought she would freeze to death that night. She curled up in the thatched shelter at the ferry crossing, without so much as a single match to build a fire.

Some days later a woman, unkempt and grimy, crossed the empty streets of Xietang and appeared outside the Taishun tailor shop. She looked a long time at the low-hanging cotton door-curtain before she could bring herself, with an effort, to climb the steps. Yanzhi lifted the curtain, and a smell of paste met her full in the face. Baosheng was bent over the cutting board, sizing a length of cloth. Wind blew in from the street, and from the charcoal brazier in the middle of the room a string of sparks went drifting up.

Baosheng gazed at the woman in the doorway. There was a trace of mustache on his lip now, and glasses sat astride his nose. For a long while Baosheng came slowly forward, each step as though it spanned a century. He took Yanzhi's hand and drew her all the way to the brazier. "Warm your hands first," he said. "I'll go and make you something to eat."
