### Two and a Half Slickers

Everyone says that Shanghai is a world of slick operators. And true enough—look about you, and the whole of society is charged with an air of cunning: only a man with the slicker's gifts can bare his fangs and brandish his claws and swagger about in his might, only such a one can found a house and make a fortune, serving himself and harming others. So many are the advantages of being a slicker that it is small wonder the Shanghai people all put a little work into that word "slick." Ha, ha!

And who, then, are Shanghai's two most celebrated slickers—and the half? One, they say, is a certain Sun of a certain pharmacy, who by selling this "essence" and that "essence" opened himself a great source of wealth; one is the Daoist priest of a certain temple; and the half is a half-blind Mr. Kou-Tian, who day in and day out plays at conjuring spirits and makes a special trade of gulling women and children. (The "alas-and-alack" grass-head old boss, who lately came to grief in a great botch, is not counted among them.) The Daoist's slick arts and magic powers can bring pious men and pious women to believe in him with one accord, burning their incense and saying their prayers till the temple gate is thronged like a market; and on the days of the new moon and the full, above all, men and women crowd in pell-mell, shoulder rubbing shoulder, and in all these several decades his trade has never once fallen off. How mighty a spell it is! Truly the power of the Way is boundless, and worthy of all men's admiration.
