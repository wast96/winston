## About the Author

Xu Xu was born in 1908, a native of Cixi county in Zhejiang. In 1931 he graduated from the Department of Philosophy at Peking University; in 1933 he went to Shanghai to live by his pen; and in 1937 he published *Ghost Love*, the sensation that made his name.

After Pearl Harbor he moved from Shanghai to Chongqing, and in 1943 brought out there the representative novel of his early period, *The Whistling Wind*, which took the public by storm — so much so that the year was hailed as "the Year of Xu Xu." In 1944 he went to the United States; in 1946 he returned to the Chinese mainland; in 1950 he settled in Hong Kong. In 1956, in Hong Kong, he published the representative novel of his later period, *River of Fury*.

Xu Xu wrote copiously — poetry, plays, fiction, essays, sketches, literary criticism — but it was in fiction that his achievement was greatest. His work took love for its usual subject, handled with a strikingly fresh touch that broke free of the old conventions of the love story and made the reader see anew; he had a gift, too, for uncovering the intricate, deep-set folds of human nature, and the romantic air of his writing was strong. He made his name early, and for his literary attainment may be called a master of his generation. Writers of the new generation — in Hong Kong, in Taiwan, and among the émigrés in America, such as Yu Lihua, Ji Zheng and Nie Hualing — counted it an honour to obtain a preface from him for their own books, as though a single word of his appraisal raised their worth ten times over. In the 1970s a collected edition of his works came out in Taiwan volume by volume, close to forty in all, an imposing sight. In 1980 Xu Xu died of lung cancer in Hong Kong, aged seventy-two.
