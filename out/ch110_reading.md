### Missing Persons

Shanghai is a place of wide extent, where people from all quarters are mixed together and kidnappers and rogues mingle among them, so that lost children and vanished bondmaids are a thing that crops up again and again. When someone has the misfortune to meet with such a case, he either advertises in the papers to seek the lost, or pastes up a few missing-person notices on the telephone poles and along the foot of the walls. The wording runs, without fail: on such a day at such an hour a child was lost, wearing such-and-such clothes, the features of the face thus, the build thus, the shoes and cap thus, all set down one by one, and, should any kind and worthy gentleman know of his whereabouts, let him be brought to such-and-such house, at such-and-such number, in such-and-such lane, on such-and-such road, with a reward of so many dollars, and the like. Only, the character "person" in "seek the person" is always written upside-down; the notion, so they say, is that written thus the lost one cannot wander far and will be the more easily found. Who first devised this superstition there is now no telling. As for the causes of a person's going missing—apart from the greater half who are carried off by kidnappers and hidden away to be sold, the lesser half who lose their road and cannot find their way home is a common thing enough too.
