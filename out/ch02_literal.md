## 第二章 Chapter Two: The First Rehearsal of the Murder of Chiang Kai-shek

### 5. The bad blood between Wang Yaqiao and Chiang Kai-shek goes back a long way

March 1928, Hefei.

Toward evening time, one rickshaw, along a road sparse with walkers, toward the Tongmenghui dormitory quarter at the city district's center came driving. In the car sat a gentleman wearing a formal hat, wearing a gray cloth long gown. To prevent walkers discovering, this guest just out of the railway station purposely pulled the hat brim very low, covering that horse-shaped long face of his. He was Dai Chunfeng.

At this moment he sat in the flying rickshaw alone thinking his heart's affairs. Before his eyes constantly flashed a person of middle build, a character-国 square face, an 八-character mustache kept on the lip; this person was precisely the Wang Yaqiao who made Chiang Kai-shek feel greatly headache-ridden.

"Please send my card in to the Ninth Master; say there is a person called Dai Chunfeng who begs to see him!" Dai Chunfeng, born at Xiakou town in Jiangshan county, Zhejiang province, whenever he recalled the treatment he met in his teens knocking about the Shanghai waterfront, would think longingly of the Hatchet Gang chief Wang Yaqiao's recognition-kindness [知遇之恩]. In those years he ran out from the Jiangshan countryside; at first under the Zhejiang warlord Zhou Fengqi's command he served as an orderly-soldier; later, because he could not endure the strict army life, he fled alone to Shanghai and drifted days in the ten-li foreign strip. Dai Chunfeng even now clearly remembered: it was a spring; he was at the point of no-road-to-turn in Shanghai; one day he suddenly saw on Nanjing Road come walking a column holding snow-bright great axes, and people beside him sighed in admiration: "You boy, look — that Ninth Master Wang is a true hero. He too is a man who came from Anhui to Shanghai to storm the world, but he has made his climate in Shanghai. The Hatchet Gang — look how much awe-wind it has!" "Look at Wang Yaqiao — this is a real man. With one hatchet he stormed the world; now on the Shanghai waterfront even Huang Jinrong and Du Yuesheng yield him several parts!"

"Wang Yaqiao?!" Dai Chunfeng, hearing this name, secretly ate a start!

"Just so — you boy have truly lived a man's turn in vain [真空做一回人]; in Shanghai you actually do not know Ninth Master Wang's great name?" Dai Chunfeng stood at the road edge; someone, seeing him stand there dazed watching Wang Yaqiao stride past high-gazing and wide-stepping, mocked him saying: "You truly are a useless little biesan! Roll — quick, roll away from me. If you don't even know Ninth Master Wang, can you still count as a person?"

In truth, how could Dai Chunfeng not know Wang Yaqiao? In earlier years, when he was begging along the streets and was struck senseless by a motorcar and lay across the road, it was precisely Wang Yaqiao who saved his one life! At this moment the Hatchet Gang's awe-wind momentum deeply shook him, made him, at the point of no road, brighten at heart. He pondered several days, and at last resolved to entrust all hope of getting ahead onto that Anhui man. This day, Dai Chunfeng, swelling his gall, came to the Anhui Guildhall. He had no calling card; he could only overnight write a self-recommending document in the Mao Sui manner [毛遂自荐], and at the guildhall gate respectfully present it to the gate-keeping Hatchet Gang disciple. Dai Chunfeng waited carefully outside the gate, thinking that a great-name-tripod-ringing Hatchet Gang master like Wang Yaqiao fundamentally would not notice a down-and-out person like him. Who knew — his document introducing his own history had gone in not long when someone passed word out, saying: "Boy, the Ninth Master invites you in!" Dai Chunfeng simply dared not believe his own ears: "The Ninth Master… wants to see me?" That person, seeing him frozen there, said, "Why are you still standing stupefied? Or must the Ninth Master come out himself to invite you?"

Only now did Dai Chunfeng wake as from a dream; hurriedly following that person he turned past a screen wall, came to the main room; he tiptoe-entered, and only then discovered several people encircling one table of mahjong, in a great bamboo-grove battle [竹林之战, mahjong]. Dai Chunfeng carefully stood in attendance at one side, not daring to open his mouth. He waited about two clock-hours before someone at the Eight Immortals table lifted his head and asked: "Hey, young fellow — I hear your ancestral registration is also Anhui?"

Dai Chunfeng stole a look, and discovered the one questioning him was precisely the Wang Yaqiao seen once at the Nanjing Road corner the day before yesterday. The 八-character mustache on his lip was especially eye-catching; flustered, he bowed in salute and deferentially said: "Ninth Master above — yes, yes, my ancestors in early years indeed also sought their living on Anhui ground, and with the Ninth Master are exactly fellow-countrymen. Therefore I dared danger to hurry here and throw myself on the Ninth Master, so as to seek in Shanghai a place to set body and establish life!"

Wang Yaqiao saw that Dai Chunfeng's bearing, though coarse, held within the coarseness a kind of restrained capability [拘谨干练] ordinary people do not possess, and he looked at him with scraped eyes [刮目相看], saying: "Since you too are an Anhui man, why then does your self-recommendation call you a native of Jiangshan, Zhejiang? Is this not self-contradiction? You wouldn't be a person who impersonates and swindles, would you?"

Dai Chunfeng half-rose [LOW-CONF: the glyph reads 欠身 or similar] with an apologetic smile: "The Ninth Master misunderstands. I, Dai Chunfeng, am a down-and-out man, but I would not go so far as to recklessly change my ancestral registration for one bowl of rice, would I? My coming to throw myself on the Ninth Master is, first, not for office, second, not for wealth; chiefly I come admiring the Ninth Master's character and virtuous conduct. Above all the Ninth Master's lofty principle of upholding honor and scattering wealth [仗义疏财] is what I, Dai Chunfeng, revere. So I come specially to pay respects, and ask only that the Ninth Master give me, Dai Chunfeng, a bowl of rice to eat — that is all."

"Good. Good, good!" Wang Yaqiao, seeing Dai Chunfeng's talk was not vulgar, and his two eyes shone burning-bright, knew in his feelings that in time this man would certainly not stand beneath himself; so he hastily rose, came before Dai Chunfeng, and said: "Dai Chunfeng, today's Shanghai may be called a place where officials and bandits run rampant, with people of every color, inevitably fish and dragons mixed and muddled. That you are willing at this juncture to come specially and throw yourself on me shows you have an upright person's character. Since it is so, stay under my gate. Hear me well: so long as there is me, Wang Yaqiao, there is no fear you, Dai Chunfeng, will lack a bowl of rice."

It was from that day that Dai Chunfeng, in Wang Yaqiao's Anhui Guildhall, became a small disciple of the Hatchet Gang. Later, because Wang Yaqiao saw Dai Chunfeng not only had the skill of pleasing people but also possessed the shrewd coolness indispensable in gang figures, he regarded him with special weight. What especially made Wang Yaqiao feel startled was that Dai Chunfeng, though at that time only an insignificant small disciple in the Hatchet Gang, yet had extraordinary daring-insight. The two men were almost exactly alike even in the trait of loving women. Truly hero-recognizes-hero [惺惺相惜]; Wang Yaqiao later not only promoted Dai Chunfeng to important duty but also smeared blood in covenant with him, and they became sworn brothers of the exchanged-card, knocked-head kind. This was a thing Dai Chunfeng, when he first came to Shanghai's Anhui Guildhall to throw himself on Wang Yaqiao, dared not even think.

"Yunong, now I send you to Hefei; the intent is precisely to carry one sentence to the brother you swore with in Shanghai in those years, Wang Yaqiao: let him from now on behave honestly as a man, and no longer be permitted to stand courtyard-against-courtyard [分庭抗礼] with me, Chiang Chung-cheng." Dai Chunfeng, seeing the rickshaw already nearing the provincial Tongmenghui dormitory, suddenly grew nervous at heart. He now after all was greatly different from before: before, he worked in Shanghai under Wang Yaqiao's hand; but today he had become Chiang Kai-shek's special-operations man. The purpose of his coming from Nanjing to Hefei this time was absolutely not to talk old times with his former sworn elder brother Wang Yaqiao, but, bearing Chiang Kai-shek's special mission, to come strike the warning bell for Wang Yaqiao. In the world there was no one who understood the Hatchet Gang's master Wang Yaqiao better than Dai Chunfeng. In his heart he had no bottom-count [没有底数] whether he could smoothly complete the persuasion task Chiang Kai-shek gave him. Just then the rickshaw man said: "Sir, this is the Tongmenghui dormitory you were seeking!"

At this time, upstairs in the provincial Tongmenghui, before an oval floor-length window, silently stood a person of five-short build [五短身材]. This was precisely the Wang Yaqiao whom Dai Chunfeng was anxious to find! He was at the window thinking his heart's affairs. After the attempt on Chen Diaoyuan in Nanjing failed to succeed, Wang Yaqiao quietly slipped back to Hefei. He continued with Hatchet Gang members plotting a second assassination of Chen Diaoyuan.

"Ninth Master, now Chen Diaoyuan is requesting of Chiang Kai-shek a transfer away from Anhui. Though we did not kill him in Nanjing, we frightened him badly. He tells everyone he meets: Anhui is no place for me, Chen, to linger; rather than one day meet the Hatchet Gang's assassination, better to change ground and be an official." On the eve of Wang Yaqiao's plotting the second assassination of Chen Diaoyuan, suddenly one day, to his residence came a mysterious figure. This person was tall of build, imposing of countenance; beneath two thick brows, a pair of tiger eyes. When Wang Yaqiao looked — it was the second elder-brother-in-teaching [二师兄] of his founding of the Hatchet Gang in Shanghai, Wang Leping. Close behind Wang Leping followed a person of clear, fine features — he was Wang Yaqiao's closest friend, named Yu Likui, a military man. When Wang Leping and Yu Likui reported the news of Chen Diaoyuan's transfer from Anhui to Wang Yaqiao, he heard and greatly rejoiced, saying: "Ha-ha. Did not Chen Diaoyuan at the start throw himself on Chiang Kai-shek precisely to scheme the Anhui provincial chairmanship? Now he has governed not yet two years — why does he spur his horse back to the mountains? Can it be he does not know Anhui is a feng-shui treasure ground for scraping the people's wealth?"

Wang Leping and Wang Yaqiao from early years in Shanghai had a deep private friendship; this time he and Yu Likui had gone specially to Nanjing to steal secret intelligence, then come to Hefei to report to the Wang Yaqiao who was preparing the second attempt on Chen. He said: "Ninth Brother, it is thus: last time, though you did not kill Chen Diaoyuan, you did after all shoot dead his trusted man Zhang Qiubai on the spot. Chen Diaoyuan has already felt that you, Ninth Brother, are not to be provoked. He knows that if he continues in office in Anhui, though he may scrape the people's wealth and stuff his private purse, with you, Ninth Brother Wang, present, sooner or later there will be a second, a third assassination. Chen Diaoyuan holds his life dear before all; how dare he stay on here waiting for your Hatchet Gang to fire on him?"

Wang Yaqiao laughed: "Chen Diaoyuan is clever after his kind. If he truly leaves Anhui, he picks up one life. Otherwise, my brothers below could hardly guarantee that some day they would not send a bullet into his brain!"

Yu Likui counseled Wang Yaqiao, saying: "Ninth Brother, Chen Diaoyuan's evils in Anhui are full to the brim [恶贯满盈]; now is exactly the best moment to put hands on him. Can it be we let him do his evils, then pat his backside and flee with his life?"

Wang Yaqiao rationally waved his hand: "Likui, these words miss. From the day our Hatchet Gang was founded in Shanghai, my — Wang Jiuguang's — guiding principle has been: kill the evil to succor the many, and not wantonly kill men who have examined and awakened themselves. Now Chen Diaoyuan has already startled awake, knows too that evil-doing must have an ill end, and has handed Chiang Kai-shek his resignation — then let him go, that is all. So long as he does no evil on Anhui ground, we simply pay him no mind. My moving on him at the start was not for one-self private hatred either, but through shooting Chen Diaoyuan dead I wished to give Chiang Kai-shek a color to look at [给…颜色看看]. You must all know: the target our Hatchet Gang strikes has always been Chiang Kai-shek, this hateful autocrat and thief of the people [独夫民贼]!"

Sure enough, on the day after Wang Leping and Yu Likui reported the news, Chen Diaoyuan was, by one paper of transfer order from Chiang Kai-shek, dispatched to the Northern Expedition Army's First Group Army's 37th Army to serve as army commander. Not long after, Fang Zhenwu came to Hefei and took over the provincial chairmanship. Fang Zhenwu, though he got a province's chairmanship, was not of Chiang Kai-shek's direct line [嫡系]; hence contradictions grew daily and frictions rose on four sides. Therefore Wang Yaqiao foresaw this was a good opportunity to launch topple-Chiang activity within Anhui's borders.

"Ninth Brother, toppling Chiang is of course what the multitude's hopes turn to; but I have doubted from the start — can this united front of yours succeed?" Soon after Chen Diaoyuan left Anhui, Wang Yaqiao convened an emergency meeting of the Hatchet Gang. Wang Leping and Yu Likui, hearing at the meeting Wang Yaqiao's topple-Chiang proposal of linking Fang Zhenwu, Shi Yousan and Bo Wenwei, were both greatly stirred. Especially the old Tongmenghui member Wang Leping, who had long-standing enmity with Chiang Kai-shek, supported most strongly the topple-Chiang united front Wang Yaqiao was forming. But he still held doubts whether Shi Yousan and the rest would truly turn their spears. He said, not without worry: "The one I fear most is Shi Yousan. This man indeed holds accumulated hatred and old grudge toward Chiang, but everyone knows his words and deeds do not agree; he constantly breaks faith with people. Will he really topple Chiang together with us?"

"Shi Yousan is indeed crafty of nature, but he does after all station large armies in Jiangsu — this is a strength that cannot be overlooked." Wang Yaqiao had made careful analysis of the warlord strengths of every part of Jiangnan, so when he spoke it came out head-to-head orderly [头头是道]. He said to Wang Leping and Yu Likui: "General Bo Wenwei is the commander-in-chief of our anti-Chiang alliance. He advocates linking every feudal lord of every road who can be linked, to form one powerful anti-Chiang front; only thus can the many years' grand design of our Hatchet Gang be realized. Now Fang Zhenwu has agreed to lend us one arm's strength in the anti-Chiang camp — this is the condition of success. Fang Zhenwu's condition for uniting with us is precisely that Shi Yousan's force must take part; only then dare he rise; otherwise Chiang Kai-shek cannot be beaten down."

Wang Leping deeply held this to be so: "This plan is excellent — only, who can be the persuader to Shi Yousan?"

"Naturally I mount the horse myself. I believe Shi Yousan will not fail to give me face." Wang Yaqiao clearly held a formed bamboo in the chest [胸有成竹] about how to draw in topple-Chiang forces. He cast his gaze at Yu Likui, long silent, and said: "What I worry over now is instead the Independent Fourth Brigade at Changzhou. I wonder — could Brother Likui go in my stead to persuade them?"

Yu Likui had in early years received Wang Yaqiao's deep kindness, and admired Wang to the point of all five bodily parts cast to the ground [五体投地]. Now, seeing Wang Yaqiao hand the heavy charge of turning the Changzhou Fourth Brigade to him, he patted his chest and said: "Ninth Brother, if you trust me, let me go to Changzhou myself. Everyone knows Brigade Commander Peng Jianguo is my close friend of many years; I know too that Peng Jianguo hates Chiang Kai-shek to the marrow of the bone. So my going to Changzhou will certainly unfurl the banner in victory [旗开得胜]!"

"Excellent!" Wang Yaqiao for many years had kept in his heart one long-cherished wish [夙愿] — to bring Chiang Kai-shek crashing down; now this wish, under his running of the campaign-tent's calculations [运筹帷幄], was step by step becoming a strategic plan ready to be put into action. He said: "When the time comes, three roads rise together: in the north Fang Zhenwu, in the south Changzhou's Peng Jianguo, in the west Shi Yousan. And of course, in the middle there is also our Hatchet Gang as the rear echo — this forms the Three-Kingdoms-one-side array. No fear Chiang Kai-shek is not destroyed!"

Wang Yaqiao was standing at the window thinking the grand topple-Chiang design when he suddenly saw a disciple come up from downstairs and report to him: "Ninth Master, there is a Nanjing guest come calling; he says he must see the Ninth Master face to face."

"Oh? A Nanjing guest?" Wang Yaqiao doubtfully took the card the disciple handed up and looked — on it, unexpectedly, a strange name: Dai Li! He secretly ate a start; ten-thousand-fold he had not thought that just as he was scheming to form an encirclement around Chiang Kai-shek, the Dai Chunfeng who in former days in Shanghai had bowed to him as elder brother would suddenly come to Hefei. Wang Yaqiao mused aloud: "What manner of man is Dai Li? Can it truly be the Dai Chunfeng of before? If it is truly he, what does he come here from Nanjing to do — can it be that the Chiang fellow has already sensed something?" Wang Yaqiao's heart grew a nest of doubt; his first intent was to refuse the guest, but he thought again, and waved his hand at the disciple after all, saying, "Let him come in!"

### 6. Dai Li and Wang Yaqiao

The Nanjing guest who appeared before Wang Yaqiao hurriedly removed his hat and bowed low in salute, saying: "Ninth Brother — can it be you no longer know even your little brother?"

"Dai Chunfeng? It really is you!" Wang Yaqiao looked the guest carefully up and down, then asked, "When did you change your name?"

Dai Chunfeng before Wang Yaqiao was forever completely respectful, his bearing careful — cautious in the small [谨小慎微], just as in those years in Shanghai when he prostrated himself at the Hatchet Gang's gate. He knew that in those years of scraping a meal in Shanghai, it was this Wang Yaqiao before him who had taken him in; his own later gradual rise in Shanghai was also not unconnected with Wang Yaqiao. That Dai Chunfeng came to know Dai Jitao and the brothers Chen Lifu and Chen Guofu in Shanghai — Wang Yaqiao was the middleman. Of course, the reason Dai Chunfeng later left Shanghai was that in Wang Yaqiao's Hatchet Gang he never got the position he felt his due; so in 1925 he suddenly went far off to Guangdong. The origin was that one day he saw on the street a newspaper carrying the report of Chiang Kai-shek presiding over the opening ceremony at the Whampoa Academy. Dai Chunfeng also saw the paper print a photograph of Chiang Kai-shek and Dai Jitao together. So one day, he came quietly to Wang Yaqiao's sitting room in the Anhui Guildhall, cupped his hands in a bow, and said: "Ninth Brother, I want to go to Guangzhou and look around."

"You want to go to Guangzhou?" Wang Yaqiao at the time could not yet understand why Dai Chunfeng, who had sworn the bond and exchanged cards with him, should suddenly grow a mind to withdraw. But he did not much obstruct him, saying, "Chunfeng, it seems the water in this pond of mine is shallow — it cannot keep a big fish like you! Well and good; you wish to go, I do not bar you. But you must tell Ninth Brother: what exactly do you intend by going to Guangdong? Can it be you too go to throw yourself on Chiang Kai-shek?"

Dai Chunfeng knew Wang Yaqiao opposed Chiang Kai-shek from the bone marrow, so he hastily covered, saying: "Ninth Brother, you wrong my heart. Chiang Kai-shek and I have never been carts running the same road — on what ground would I go throw myself on him? I only want to enter the military academy. Ninth Brother perhaps knows: I, Chunfeng, and you, Ninth Brother, are greatly unalike. You in Shanghai and Anhui have long been a figure who has broken open his own ground, but what number am I? Though Ninth Brother thinks well of me, I know that in the Jiangning region it is hard for me to rise. So I think, if I am to have a day of coming out ahead, best is the road of soldiering. Therefore I mean to sit the examination for the Whampoa Academy. Of course, if little brother should one day truly rise in the military world, I will absolutely never forget Ninth Brother's recognition-kindness to me!"

"Very well." Wang Yaqiao was an open-handed fellow [豁达汉子]; though from the heart he opposed Dai Chunfeng's going to Guangdong, and knew the Whampoa Military Officers' Academy Dai Chunfeng sought was precisely Chiang Kai-shek's sphere of power, he still swallowed down the words of retention and against his heart declared: "By worthy younger brother's talent and learning, perhaps to seek development in the military world is the more fitting. Only I have one sentence of counsel: to the academy, to be an officer — I support you one hundred times over. What I fear is: will you in the future become Chiang Kai-shek's accomplice-in-evil [帮凶]? If so, you and I can only sever relations!"

"Ninth Brother, be at ease!" Dai Chunfeng ten-thousand-fold had not expected Wang Yaqiao to see instantly through his intent. In truth, at that time only he himself knew clearly why he was going to Guangdong. Rather than say he wished to attach himself to the Nationalist elder Dai Jitao whom he had met in Shanghai, better to say he was going to throw himself on his Zhejiang fellow-townsman Chiang Kai-shek. The Dai Chunfeng of that time, though sworn with Wang Yaqiao as brothers of life and death, had already discovered that the everywhere-hacking-and-chopping Hatchet Gang was fated never to make a great climate. But Chiang Kai-shek grasped military power and would certainly have great making in the future; so he resolved to go to Guangdong in the character of a fellow-provincial to seek audience of Chiang Kai-shek — just as in those years he had come to Shanghai on an Anhui ancestral registration to seek audience of Wang Yaqiao. Dai Chunfeng knew well that Wang Yaqiao was Chiang Kai-shek's death-enemy, so he could only speak words against his heart: "It is under your full-strength completing [鼎力成全] that I, Dai Chunfeng, have become something with a man's shape and a dog's manner [人模狗样]. Now though I go to sit for the academy, it is for the road ahead — a thing done because there is no helping it!"

"Good then — go!" Wang Yaqiao, seeing Dai Chunfeng so earnest, set a farewell banquet for him, and before long the two parted sprinkling tears. Now several years' time had flicked past, and Wang Yaqiao ten-thousand-fold had not thought Dai Chunfeng would turn into Dai Li. Moreover, as he knew it, Dai Chunfeng after leaving him in 1925 had indeed become a cadet of the sixth class of the Whampoa Academy, cavalry section. Later, the dare-to-kill, dare-to-grapple rush this man displayed in the army drew Chiang Kai-shek's notice. Dai Li accordingly followed Chiang Kai-shek through the famous Northern Expedition. But Dai Chunfeng did not, as he had promised Wang Yaqiao before leaving Shanghai for Guangdong, keep far from Chiang Kai-shek. Rather, on the ground that Zhejiang's Jiangshan and Fenghua are near as elbow to knee [近在咫尺], he quickly, on the Eastern Route Army's northern march, obtained Chiang Kai-shek's trust. Later, Dai Chunfeng also received a four-character inscription written by Chiang Kai-shek's own brush: "Hardship Surpassing All" [艰苦卓绝].

What especially startled Wang Yaqiao greatly was that Dai Chunfeng, since following Chiang Kai-shek on the Northern Expedition, this knocked-head younger brother who before his face had vowed oath upon oath to take soldiering as his life's honor, should actually take up the hateful special-agent role of wartime. Wang Yaqiao learned from Dai Jitao's later talk that Dai Chunfeng, on the Northern Expedition road, had received Chiang Kai-shek's secret commission and gone many times to Tianjin, Beiping, Taiyuan, Zhengzhou and Xi'an of the five northern provinces, spying for the Chiang side on the military movements of the Beiyang warlords. From that time Dai Chunfeng had become Chiang Kai-shek's special-operations man. Thinking of all this, Wang Yaqiao could not but grow secret guard against Dai, and said: "Are you not doing gloriously over there with the Chiang fellow? Why then run to Hefei? Can it be you want me, Wang Jiuguang, to do a turn as Zhou Weilong?"

"Ah — no, no, Ninth Brother, you misunderstand!" Dai Li ten-thousand-fold had not thought Wang Yaqiao would still speak as before — one needle to see blood [一针见血], sparing no face. Dai Li also knew the meaning in Wang Yaqiao's words: clearly he spoke of the old affair of those years when Dai turned Zhou Weilong at Wuhan. That was after the Nationalist Party met the strait of the Ninghan split, when Chiang Kai-shek sent him to Wuhan to spy out the military secrets of Tang Shengzhi's force. Unexpectedly Dai Li's traces were early perceived by Tang Shengzhi, and so, just when Dai Li had stolen the intelligence and was preparing quietly to leave Wuhan, Tang Shengzhi suddenly ordered the net drawn in, and Dai Li thus became a prisoner in Tang Shengzhi's jail. He not only lost his freedom; the intelligence he had gotten fell at the same time into Tang Shengzhi's hand. By rights Dai Li might that time have died in Tang Shengzhi's hand; but Dai Li's life was fated not to end: he learned that the jail confining him was under the oversight of gendarme company commander Zhou Weilong, and Zhou Weilong was his classmate from the Whampoa Academy. Dai Li, already sentenced to death by Tang Shengzhi, precisely by urgently begging to see Zhou Weilong on the night before execution — his round of talk (chiefly words to the effect that if Zhou Weilong would follow him to Chiang Kai-shek, a major-general's rank could be had) — actually moved this Whampoa schoolmate's heart. In those years it was thus, under Zhou Weilong's escort, that the two fled together back to Nanjing. Now Dai Li, seeing Wang Yaqiao use the Zhou Weilong allusion to needle him awake, knew for himself that Wang Yaqiao held his relation with Chiang Kai-shek like a thing in the palm [了若指掌], so he hastened to protest: "Ninth Brother, I am not that kind who forgets feeling. In the early Shanghai years I, Dai Yunong, had nothing to lean on; if the Ninth Brother had not then acted on honor and taken an unknown outlander like me under his command, would I, Dai Yunong, have today?"

Wang Yaqiao, seeing Dai Li recover his former deference and timidity, only then motioned him to sit, and ordered tea and refreshments brought, saying: "You know Zhou Weilong and I are not one road of man, so do not lay plans upon my body. Chunfeng — if you have truly come by the way to Hefei to look in on me, then we are brothers hereafter as before. If you have come as the Chiang fellow's persuader, then do not blame me for refusing to know old feeling."

"So it is, so it is." Dai Li before coming this time had known that the task Chiang Kai-shek gave him was a bone hard to gnaw: Wang Yaqiao was absolutely not one to be persuaded by flowered words and clever talk. Dai Li, seeing Wang Yaqiao seal his mouth shut at first meeting, could only swallow down all the persuasion-speech he had prepared, and stood there trading laughs with Wang Yaqiao, dealing in empty coils [虚与委蛇], saying: "In truth the bitterness in my heart, Ninth Brother, you cannot know. Though I have now become one of Mr. Chiang's men, these last years in the Northern Expedition army they have run me everywhere, listening out military intelligence — no more than a bitter errand. That kind of solo-peddler's errand is absolutely not what I wished either. Where does 'thriving' come into the talk?"

Wang Yaqiao understood the man Dai Li best of all. Seeing Dai Li look left and right and speak of other things [顾左右而言他], he knew his own words just now had struck the other's vital point, and he gave a cold heh-heh laugh: "Spare me that talk. If you are out of favor before the Chiang fellow, what then is that 'Ten-Man Team' about? In truth, many inside the Nationalist Party now understand your relation with Chiang. That being so, why speak boundary-less ghost-talk before me?"

Dai Li froze there, half a stretch without words. He had not expected Wang Yaqiao to be as shrewd as ever — to hold like a thing in the palm even the secret that he, on Chiang Kai-shek's confidential order, had organized ten men, his old Whampoa friends Zhou Weilong, Zhang Yanyuan and the rest, into a secret special-services group. Seeing the other side so clear about his identity and his purpose in coming, to keep acting by the art of deceiving-heaven-crossing-the-sea [瞒天过海] was plainly unfit; so he gravely cupped his hands to Wang Yaqiao: "Ninth Brother, since the talk has come to this layer, I too will speak no false words before a true man. I tell you the truth: this time I came to Hefei, it was indeed Mr. Chiang who sent me."

"Hm? Were you not here to look in on me?" — one cold laugh of disdain from Wang Yaqiao. "Good. Straight and quick. Dai Chunfeng, let us then open the skylight and speak bright words. I, Wang Jiuguang, walk without changing my name, sit without altering my surname. I am in Hefei; old Chiang sits guard in Nanjing; well-water does not trouble river-water between us. What honored business brings his envoy here?"

Dai Li, seeing Wang Yaqiao still there with cold words and stern speech, thought: better simply to show every bottom card, and see how this Wang Yaqiao answers. So he said: "Ninth Brother, you know it too — I am now the foreman of Mr. Chiang's 'Ten-Man Team' below his hand, and so I can know some secrets touching the National Government. Since this spring, the military and political men of every road in the Jiangning region have formed one undercurrent of arms extremely unfavorable to Mr. Chiang. In particular, certain people are brewing a terrible topple-Chiang plot; several roads of men and horses are merging streams, preparing to launch military action. Therefore, before Mr. Chiang takes suppressive action, he specially sent little brother ahead to Hefei to pass the Ninth Brother a word! For I am after all your knocked-head brother of those years, a man who received the Ninth Brother's recognition-kindness; there is absolutely no principle by which I could know and not report!"

Dai Li's words were to Wang Yaqiao no less than thunder from a clear sky [晴天霹雳]. This said that Chiang Kai-shek already knew clearly he was at present plotting a rising with Anhui provincial chairman Fang Zhenwu and the Jiangsu garrison commander Shi Yousan — knew even, like a thing in the palm, that he had secretly sent his close friends Wang Leping and Yu Likui to set moving the Changzhou Fourth Brigade's anti-Chiang work. Wang Yaqiao froze on the spot, for Dai Li's intelligence was simply too exact; plain to see, someone had secretly leaked the anti-Chiang activity he and Bo Wenwei and the rest were plotting. Even so, Wang Yaqiao could not expose one thread of flinching before Dai Li. Feigning calm, he threw back his face and laughed loud: "Fine fellow, Dai Chunfeng — you have indeed made something of yourself. Actually running to my place to act Chiang Kai-shek's envoy of intimidation! Good. You say someone is secretly setting several strands of strength to merge — what does that mean? I, Wang Jiuguang, one flat-headed commoner [平头百姓] — can it be I too possess the capacity to set the horse-columns of every road in motion?"

Dai Li had supposed that with his words' light touch Wang Yaqiao would surely speak out the truth, whereupon he would apply threat and intimidation, thereby to achieve Chiang Kai-shek's aim of moving first to control others [先发制人]. Ten-thousand-fold he had not thought that Wang Yaqiao would not only not be misled, but would counter with defense-as-attack — which made Dai Li's position extremely awkward. Knowing further urging useless, in three sentences and two more he hastily drew in his troops, saying: "Ninth Brother thinks too much. I too merely heard it on the roads. I meant well from the start — mistook those flying words for true, and so came first to report, my intent to counsel the Ninth Brother to walk carefully. For at present, at Mr. Chiang's side, I can still get a word up. If the Ninth Brother had truly boarded another's thief-ship in error, to rein the horse at the cliff now would be no loss of the hero. Should Mr. Chiang truly discover someone laying plans against him, he will absolutely not again handle it lightly-sketchily as in the past. This time he will open the ring of killing wide; would it not then be repentance too late? As I now see, since the Ninth Brother has not one gourd-tangle [瓜葛] with those military strengths stirring behind the curtain, little brother's heart too is set down!"

That same night, Wang Yaqiao without stir of color set a small washing-of-dust banquet for Dai Li, and together they talked the old feeling of the eight-bows friendship of former days. At next day's light, Dai Li hurriedly took leave of Wang Yaqiao and rode the express train back to Nanjing, to report back to Chiang Kai-shek the course of his failure to turn Wang Yaqiao.

After Dai Li left, Wang Yaqiao thought and thought and grew the more afraid: he suddenly realized the Dai Li who came the evening before was simply Jiang Gan stealing the letters out of the Romance of the Three Kingdoms. Though he and Dai Li in those years on the Shanghai waterfront had a friendship of life and death, and he had saved the livelihood-pressed Dai Yunong, yet today's Dai Chunfeng had not only changed the name of those years — even his heart had gone over in submission to his enemy Chiang Kai-shek. The meaning that leaked from Dai's language was very clear: in the course of his plotting in secret with Bo Wenwei, Wang Leping, Yu Likui and the rest for Fang Zhenwu, Shi Yousan and the Changzhou Fourth Brigade to mount an anti-Chiang mutiny, a terrible traitor had certainly appeared. Otherwise Dai Li could absolutely not have said to him "several strands of military force are stirring." Wang Yaqiao for the moment could not determine at which link of their tightly-sealed mutiny-plotting the fault had come. But the years of revolving among every faction's political strengths along the Nanjing-Shanghai line told him: certainly someone had secretly gone over to Chiang Kai-shek, or been bought by Dai Li's "Ten-Man Team."

"If someone has secretly surrendered and informed, then the Chiang fellow will certainly strike first to gain the upper hand. That way, some of us can hardly escape calamity!" When Bo Wenwei at his residence saw a Wang Yaqiao panicked of face, and heard what Dai Li had come to Hefei the night before to say, his mood too grew tight. For Fang Zhenwu was new in office; though he had the anti-Chiang mind, he had at present no concrete anti-Chiang preparation. Shi Yousan, though under Wang Yaqiao's several persuasions he had agreed to rise together with all, was after all a general who constantly went out and came back on his word [出尔反尔] — not to be deeply trusted. As for the Fourth Brigade that Wang Leping and Yu Likui were persuading at Changzhou, Brigade Commander Peng Jianguo was still in the midst of setting his hand to preparation. If at this moment Chiang Kai-shek got advance knowledge of their "Three Kingdoms, one side" action plan and took military action of the quick-knife-cutting-tangled-hemp kind, the consequences would be beyond imagining. Therefore Bo Wenwei at once struck the table with decision and cried: "Now the chief thing for us is: move at once — to raise troops immediately is the higher plan."

"Best exactly so!" Wang Yaqiao too fell in with Bo Wenwei's decision without prior counsel [不谋而合].

The two men decided on the spot to drive at once to General Fang Zhenwu's residence in Hefei's city district, to consult with him urgently on a plan of moving early against Chiang. But — greatly out of Wang Yaqiao's and Bo Wenwei's reckoning — that very morning early, Fang Zhenwu had gone to Nanjing.

"What? Why has General Fang suddenly gone to Nanjing?" Arriving at the Fang residence, Bo Wenwei and Wang Yaqiao heard that Fang Zhenwu the night before had suddenly received a secret telegram sent by Chiang Kai-shek in person, requiring him to hasten to the capital to attend an important military conference; so at dawn, taking only a secretary and a few guards, he had set out light and simply attended. Wang Yaqiao, color failing in great alarm, cried: "Bad, bad! On this journey the general meets much ill and little luck — certainly it is Chiang Kai-shek's plot. We should at once send men to chase General Fang back!"

But Bo Wenwei gave a long sigh: "Jiuguang — late, too late. By now General Fang has long since reached Nanjing. Were you the fleet-footed courier himself, you could hardly overtake him now."

Wang Yaqiao and Bo Wenwei, in the car returning to the provincial Tongmenghui headquarters, suddenly saw a guard block the car head-on; the two hurriedly ordered a halt. That guard, face overcast, stepped up and reported: "Certain news just now from Nanjing — General Fang Zhenwu has just been arrested by Chiang Kai-shek and put in prison!"

"What did you say?" Wang Yaqiao, hearing the news of Fang Zhenwu's loss of freedom in Nanjing, was at once so angered his face went white; one mouthful of fresh blood came out, and he pitched to the ground…

### 7. Who is the traitor?

When Wang Yaqiao came back to consciousness, he found himself already lying at home.

"Jiuguang — you have come round!" Keeping watch at the bed's edge was a woman grown flower-fair of face and moon-fair of feature [花容月貌]. She was Wang Yaqiao's rouge-and-powder confidante [红粉知己] Wang Yaying. This Wang Yaying's original name was Cong Yunyu, of Hubei stock. From her youth she yearned toward revolution; in the Wuchang first rising of 1911 she served as detachment commander in the most famous women's organization of the day, the "Northern Expedition Women's Restoration Army." At that time Wang Yaqiao happened also to be in Wuhan, and in one fierce battle Wang Yaqiao saved Cong Yunyu's life. Cong Yunyu, love-struck at first sight on the battleground, from then on followed at Wang Yaqiao's side. In 1914 she came with Wang Yaqiao from Hubei to Hefei, Anhui, and before long they were joined as husband and wife.

Cong Yunyu, to fuse her own life completely with the greatly-wise, greatly-brave Wang Yaqiao, of her own will followed her husband's surname and changed her name to Wang Yaying. Now, seeing Wang Yaqiao — his engineered anti-Chiang rising foiled — spit blood in one burst of rage and faint away, she could not help weeping in grief: "Jiuguang, even a heaven-sized matter must be withstood. Since you would do great deeds that startle all under heaven, you must have the gall-spirit for great deeds! Otherwise, how are great deeds ever made?"

Wang Yaqiao, hearing Wang Yaying's words, gripped her hand tight and said: "Yaying, what you say has much reason. Only my heart will not rest: why did this one good enterprise break in a traitor's hand?"

"Who is the traitor?"

"This fellow who surrendered to Chiang Kai-shek — at present I too do not know who he is. But sooner or later I will know. Once I seize this man, I will certainly make him pay the price of betraying the revolution with his head!"

Just as Wang Yaqiao was racking his brains over Fang Zhenwu's peril in Nanjing, a still greater death-news suddenly passed from Shanghai to Anhui: his cherished friend, the Tongmenghui elder Wang Leping who this time had plotted the anti-Chiang mutiny together with him, had actually been assassinated by special agents sent by Dai Li!

"Heaven! Brother Leping — why should you meet such a doom?" Wang Yaqiao, hearing the death-news — that Wang Leping, deep in the night of February 17, at his Avenue Joffre residence, had been shot dead in a burst of guns by agents lying ready near the Xiafei lanes — rose again in bitter weeping. From grief at the loss of his cherished friend, rage-fire attacked the heart; another mouthful of fresh blood sprayed out, and he fainted once more on the bed.

When he woke, he found among those watching at the bed not only his wife Wang Yaying but the Hatchet Gang mainstays Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai, come specially from Shanghai to bring him the news. All, seeing Wang Yaqiao wake at last from the faint, cried out and pressed forward, calling: "Ninth Master, you must hold up! Though heaven itself fall, our Hatchet Gang cannot be without the Ninth Master!"

Wang Yaying, seeing Wang Yaqiao's face haggard but his eyes jetting the light of hatred, knew that under this blow his spirit was in extremity of pain, and hastened to urge: "The world's affairs are hard to reckon; man's life has no constancy. Jiuguang, since Chiang Kai-shek's allotted years are not yet run out, let us simply draw the lesson of this failed toppling. So long as you live, the Hatchet Gang exists. And so long as our Hatchet Gang neither perishes nor is extinguished, the revolution will have hope. Though our hand has slipped for now, there will yet be a day of rising again in the eastern hills [东山再起]."

"Right. I, Wang Jiuguang, am absolutely no paper-pasted man. Besides, we still have the Fourth Brigade at Changzhou. If only Yu Likui and Peng Jianguo pull the force over, we still have one thread of hope. The Fourth Brigade's battle-strength is especially strong; if I go there myself to move Peng Jianguo, then march on Nanjing, we can still strike Chiang Kai-shek a blow with his hands unready. What fear we cannot rescue Fang Zhenwu?"

But after this round of Wang Yaqiao's words, the people around him, for reasons unknown, all made no sound. Even Wang Yaying was avoiding Wang Yaqiao's gaze — which made him feel great bafflement.

"Yaying — what is wrong with you all? Why the bitter brows and grieving faces? Can it be that after this one blow we cannot raise ourselves again?" Wang Yaqiao, unable for the moment to understand why his wife's face showed shrinking, fixed on her and roared the question. But Wang Yaying did not answer; she only cast him one timid glance, unwilling to let out the words she wished at that moment to say.

"Jimin, Hongtai — what is this with you two? Can it be that because Chiang Kai-shek killed Wang Leping, we are all cowed?" Wang Yaqiao at that time still did not know how grave the things already happening outside were — still less that Chiang Kai-shek was everywhere massing heavy troops, closing on Hefei, Anhui, the old nest of Wang Yaqiao's Hatchet Gang. Seeing Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai, who had always held confidence in attacking Chiang, now also hang their heads with drained spirit, he could not hold back and barked: "Why do you not speak? Have you all turned into bear-sack soft eggs [熊包软蛋]? I, Wang Jiuguang, have no love for that kind. I am a man who would rather live standing than die kneeling."

Yet though Wang Yaqiao's liver-fire was up, Wang Yaying and Xuan Jimin, Wu Hongtai still hung their heads, one word unissued.

In the hard-to-bear silence Wang Yaqiao felt a shadowed threat, and he said with sudden waking: "Yaying — none of you dare look at me with your eyes; there must be something in your hearts. It would not be that the Changzhou side too has gone wrong?"

Wang Yaying, seeing Wang Yaqiao ask straight to the point, knew further concealment useless, and said with one sigh: "Jiuguang, we did not mean to tell you the situation outside. Because you can bear no more shocks to the spirit. Especially General Bo Wenwei telephoned again just now to say: on no account tell him at this time what has happened at Changzhou — all fear your anger will wound your body, that you cannot bear blows coming one upon another. But you press the chase and will not let go. Ai — things being as they are, there is nothing but to tell you true! Jiuguang, the Changzhou side too is without hope!…"

"What did you say?" Wang Yaqiao suddenly saw his wife's tears falling free, and knew the great matter had gone ill. The crown of his head at once rang with a boom; one spell of dizziness, and he nearly pitched onto the bed again. Only this time he did not vomit blood as the times before; he held his body up with toughness, though his face turned from red to white and the cold sweat came pattering down his forehead [扑簌簌]. From his wife's wanting-to-speak-then-stopping look he had already foreboded the gravity of the situation. In fact, the rising he had laid heavy hope on — the one Yu Likui was moving Fourth Brigade commander Peng Jianguo to launch at Changzhou — had likewise died in the womb [胎死腹中]. Wang Yaqiao, shaken to fury, barked: "Can it be Peng Jianguo too cannot be relied on?"

"No, no — not that Brigade Commander Peng cannot be relied on, but that a traitor appeared! Before Yu Likui and Commander Peng could rise, Chiang Kai-shek sent troops out first!" Xuan Jimin, seeing Wang Yaying had already broken the matter open, simply reported to the anxious-hearted Wang Yaqiao, one by one and according to fact, all the news he and Wu Hongtai had brought from Changzhou.

It had been thus: Yu Likui, carrying Wang Yaqiao's and Bo Wenwei's orders, had long since slipped secretly to Changzhou. Going with Yu Likui at the time were also the Hatchet Gang's crack killers Xuan Jimin, Wu Hongtai and others. After they reached Changzhou, the turning-work advanced very smoothly. Brigade Commander Peng Jianguo, a man of long patriotic heart, hearing his old friend Yu Likui convey the great plan of Wang Yaqiao's and Bo Wenwei's joint move against Chiang, approved it strongly, saying: "Chiang the Baldhead should have come down long ago. After Mr. Sun died of illness he openly betrayed the revolution and butchered revolutionaries, and long ago roused the nation's common wrath. Since we are soldiers, it is our duty to purge the traitor for the country. So long as Wang Jiuguang and General Bo Wenwei trust me, I, Peng Jianguo, am willing to act jointly with Fang Zhenwu — only I fear this man Shi Yousan cannot be relied on."

Yu Likui said: "For Shi Yousan's force, Ninth Master Wang goes himself to persuade — believe he has ten-tenths' grip of it. Besides, Shi Yousan has ever suffered Chiang Kai-shek's cold treatment and squeezing-aside; now is the great ripe hour for toppling Chiang — why would he not take part?"

Just as Yu Likui and Peng Jianguo and the rest were at Changzhou counseling how to rise, from within there suddenly came a traitor. Chiang Kai-shek, receiving the secret report, at flying speed sent heavy troops from Nanjing to attack Changzhou. Peng Jianguo, Yu Likui and the others, preparation incomplete, went hurriedly into line to meet the battle; the result: Peng Jianguo's army, few against many, fought only a few rounds, casualties grievous, and the remnant mostly surrendered to Chiang Kai-shek. With this the Changzhou anti-Chiang mutiny died in the womb. Peng Jianguo led the remnant force in a break through the ring and fled along the rail toward the Shanghai-Hangzhou line; Yu Likui, in this sudden assault, was arrested by the troops Chiang Kai-shek sent, straightway carried by special car secretly to Nanjing and held in Tangshan Prison — where he became prison-mate to General Fang Zhenwu, arrested in the earlier period.

Wu Hongtai reported further: "Shi Yousan's force too met Chiang Kai-shek's suppression at the same time and dissolved. And of course the cause of that was also a traitor's informing. Thus at one stroke all three roads of anti-Chiang force are told: earth collapsed and tiles scattered [土崩瓦解]!"

Wang Yaqiao sat fixed on the sickbed. Hearing the two reports of Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai, though deeply cut, this time he neither cried out nor fainted, nor spat blood; he only buried the breast-full of pain and hate deep in his heart, and said in pain to them all: "This defeat — it is that Chiang Kai-shek's life was not fated to end. And blame too our reckoning of the situation, which was over-hopeful. What we must do first now is: find out clearly — who, in the end, is the traitor?!"

Wang Yaying, gazing at a Wang Yaqiao with eyes glaring round and both fists clenched in unresting hate, for a moment could hardly answer: "Jiuguang, a traitor there certainly is. But since he sold us in the dark, he is certainly a man skilled at hiding. The two characters 'traitor' are not written on a face — how would you have us say who the traitor is?"

Xuan Jimin, seeing Wang Yaqiao wishing he could seize the traitor this instant and kill him with a thousand knives to loosen the hate on his heart, yet for the moment could supply no reliable evidence: "The lady speaks rightly. We know only that inside the topple-Chiang forces someone certainly passed intelligence darkly to Chiang; but who exactly did it is for the moment hard to feel out."

"Wu Hongtai — why do you not speak? Do you know what person has been pulling down my stage [拆台]?"

Wu Hongtai shook his head: "Ninth Master, I too do not know who did it."

Wang Yaqiao mused: "This time, in Hefei and Wuhu, I took counsel darkly with General Bo Wenwei many times on the anti-Chiang matter; those who knew the inside can be counted on fingers. None but high military men party to the affair — for example General Fang Zhenwu, Mr. Wang Leping; and of course Shi Yousan and Peng Jianguo. Beyond these, only the few heart-and-belly men at my side; counting you in, no more than seven or eight persons knew the inside. Then, if there is a traitor, he can only be a man of this circle. And these are all the friends I trust most. Tell me — why would such a man report my great plan to my sworn enemy Chiang Kai-shek?"

This one passage of Wang Yaqiao's set both Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai on edge, for they too stood in the ranks of Wang Yaqiao's suspicion. The two looked face to face, for the moment awkward and wordless, hard put to answer. Wang Yaying, seeing it, spoke up quickly: "Jimin, Hongtai — what the Ninth Master says is also plain truth: those who knew the mutiny's secrets were indeed all his heart-and-belly intimates. Yet the thing happened precisely among these men. So, if we do not quickly search it to water-fallen-stone-showing [水落石出], then should the spy remain hereafter at the Ninth Master's side, would he not be a bomb of standing danger at every moment?"

Xuan Jimin said frankly: "Ninth Master, we are men who have followed you out of life and into death; we would absolutely never do that heaven-wounding, reason-injuring thing. If we sold the Ninth Master we would be selling ourselves. In the earlier assassination of Chen Diaoyuan we were all participants. Even if we wished to sell the Ninth Master, we would not be trusted at Chiang Kai-shek's place — for on our hands, after all, is Zhang Qiubai's blood!"

Wu Hongtai too vowed oath on oath: "Ninth Master, what Brother Jimin says is exactly right. Now that a traitor has come from within, however much more we protest, the doubt is hard to loosen. I think the most important thing now is: send me and Brother Jimin to Shanghai once!"

Wang Yaqiao, though he understood Xuan Jimin's and Wu Hongtai's protests, still carried doubt on doubt in his heart. For from Dai Li's crossing the river from Nanjing to play persuader, to Fang Zhenwu's detention by Chiang in Nanjing, Wang Yaqiao had all along suspected whether among those at his side lay hidden a spy placed by Chiang Kai-shek. Otherwise this topple-Chiang great plan, so long in the laying with Bo Wenwei, Fang Zhenwu and the rest, would absolutely not have collapsed on the brink of success [功败垂成]. Now, seeing both Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai show faces of firm resolve, he quickly struck the table once: "You two brothers weigh it too heavy. I, Wang Jiuguang, am absolutely not the small man who cannot set his heart even on brothers of life and death. Only this affair came out exceeding strange. Who, in the end, stole our core secrets? Let this man once be found, and I, Wang Jiuguang, will surely tear him to ten thousand pieces. For this man not only wrecked my great topple-Chiang plan and dragged General Fang Zhenwu and Brother Yu Likui into calamity — what pains the heart to its root is that my most true friend Wang Leping died cruelly under the Chiang gang's guns! If I do not track down that informing spy, how shall I, Wang Jiuguang, stand as a man in the world?"

"Ninth Master — this is exactly why I beg for battle and would go myself to Shanghai!" Wu Hongtai, seeing Wang Yaqiao's words come with sound and tears together, out of the lungs' depths, stepped one pace forward, gravely cupped his hands and vowed: "I dare be certain: the traitor is not in Hefei — he must be in Shanghai!"

Wang Yaqiao looked at the resource-full, many-schemed Wu Hongtai: "By what do you see he is in Shanghai?"

Wu Hongtai said: "Mr. Wang Leping died cruelly precisely in Shanghai. That evening, someone knew in advance that Wang Leping would return to his residence near the Xiafei lanes off Avenue Joffre; therefore the special-operations men lay beforehand along the road Wang Leping's motorcar must pass. The moment Wang Leping stepped down from the car, the guns sounded. And the guns were fired in the dark, moreover. This shows the man who reported Wang Leping's movements to Chiang must be in Shanghai. How could we, in Anhui, have foreseen that Wang Leping would return that evening to the Avenue Joffre residence?"

Wang Yaqiao, hearing Wu Hongtai's analysis, his two eyes suddenly brightened; he brought his hand down on the table with a crack: "There is reason in it! Hongtai, I knew you had a head above other men's. If you can find in Shanghai the man who sold Mr. Wang Leping, then he is the traitor who informed to Chiang. And then you will be the merit-vassal of our Hatchet Gang."

Two days later, Wu Hongtai and Xuan Jimin, got up as merchants, taking a few small disciples, secretly left Hefei and slipped — spirits unknowing, ghosts unaware [神不知鬼不觉] — into a Shanghai suffused with the smell of blood. The moment they reached Shanghai they found that the friends formerly connected with the Hatchet Gang mostly kept far off — all fearing that contact with Xuan and Wu would draw the notice of Chiang Kai-shek's hidden watchers. So Wu Hongtai, Xuan Jimin and the rest did not lodge at the old Anhui Guildhall, but rented an unremarkable little courtyard near Jing'an Temple. By day they hid in this courtyard; only at night did they go out and move. Wu Hongtai had concluded the traitor who sold Wang Leping was in Shanghai — but in the vastness of the Shanghai waterfront, in that boundless sea of people, where do you go to find one traitor who appears a spirit and vanishes a ghost? Yet Wu Hongtai and Xuan Jimin indeed had some craft in them: on only their ninth day in Shanghai they discovered a suspicious man — and at once they fixed on him as the shameless traitor who had sent Wang Yaqiao's topple-Chiang plan flowing east with the river. His name was Zhao Tieqiao!
