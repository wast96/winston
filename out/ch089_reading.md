### Funeral Carriages

Thirty or forty years ago, before the motorcar had come into its own, the horse-carriage cut a great dash. The carriage-firms too stood ranged one against another thick as fish-scales, and the most famous of them was the Longfei firm by the Racecourse, with over a hundred carriages. In those days the great men who came to Shanghai all rode past in brand-new carriages drawn by a pair of horses abreast, clattering by with a shout; and after them the idle rich sojourners of the city, and the star girls of the brothels, all went about by carriage too—for at that time there was no smarter way to ride, and no second kind of conveyance to be had but the carriage.

Ever since the motorcar came into vogue, the carriage has slowly fallen behind, and the carriage-firms that once stood so thick have closed one by one; the greatest of them, the Longfei firm, was long ago reorganized into the Yunfei Motor Company. The one or two hundred broken-down carriages that now remain go, in the ordinary way of things, all but unregarded; only when a family loses one of its own and a funeral is got up, to carry the kin and friends to see off the dead, is one hailed—and the name "funeral carriage" could not fit it better. For the rest, a few stand waiting at the steamer wharves and the railway stations to tout for out-of-town custom, and do no more than carry the luggage.
