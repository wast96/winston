### The Yu Tune and the Ma Tune

When a storyteller performs from the platform, if his is one of the sung "little books"—the ballad-tales set to string and voice—he must always open with a prelude-song, and only when the prelude is done does he take up the tale proper. In the style of these preludes there has long been a division between the Yu tune and the Ma tune. The Yu tune was the invention of Yu Xiushan, in the Jiaqing and Daoguang years of the Qing; its melody is delicate and refined, like a young girl's whispered confidences at a green-latticed window, and winning to the ear. The Ma tune was originated by Ma Rufei in the Xianfeng and Tongzhi years; its melody is plain and direct, with no lingering after-notes. Yet in their day it was the Ma tune that swept all before it, and its students were many; the Yu tune had had its vogue in earlier times, but it never carried the field as the Ma tune did. Today's tanci singers have each long since had their own masters and founded their own schools, and the Yu tune and the Ma tune handed down from the old generation are long past anyone's lips.
