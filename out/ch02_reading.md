# China's King of Assassins: Wang Yaqiao

## Chapter Two: First Rehearsal for the Murder of Chiang Kai-shek

### 5. The Bad Blood Between Wang Yaqiao and Chiang Kai-shek Ran Back a Long Way

March 1928, Hefei.

Toward evening a rickshaw came up a nearly empty road toward the Tongmenghui dormitory compound in the heart of the city. In it sat a gentleman in a fedora and a long gray cotton gown. Fresh from the railway station, and not wanting to be recognized, he had pulled the brim down low over a long, horse-like face. He was Dai Chunfeng.

He rode the flying rickshaw alone with his thoughts. Before his eyes hung one face that would not leave: a man of middling build, square-jawed, a handlebar mustache on his lip — the man who was giving Chiang Kai-shek such headaches, Wang Yaqiao.

"Take my card in to the Ninth Master, if you please. Say a man named Dai Chunfeng begs to see him!" Dai Chunfeng was born at Xiakou, a market town in Jiangshan county, Zhejiang, and whenever he thought back on his teens, knocking about the Shanghai waterfront, what he remembered was the kindness of the man who had first seen something in him: the chief of the Hatchet Gang, Wang Yaqiao. He had run away from the Jiangshan countryside and served first as an orderly under the Zhejiang warlord Zhou Fengqi; army discipline did not agree with him, and he deserted alone to Shanghai to scrape a living in the glitter of the ten-li foreign strip. He could still see it plainly: it was spring, he was at the end of every road in Shanghai, and one day down Nanjing Road came a marching column of men carrying great axes polished bright as snow. Beside him the onlookers sighed with admiration. "Take a look, boy — there goes Ninth Master Wang, and that is a real hero. Another Anhui man who came to Shanghai to make his fortune, and look — he's made his weather here. The Hatchet Gang! Did you ever see such swagger?" "Look at Wang Yaqiao. That's what a man is. Stormed the world with one hatchet in his hand, and now on the Shanghai waterfront even Huang Jinrong and Du Yuesheng give him his room!"

"Wang Yaqiao?!" At the name, Dai Chunfeng started — and kept it to himself.

"That's right. You've been alive for nothing, boy — in Shanghai, and you don't know the great name of Ninth Master Wang?" He stood rooted at the roadside, and someone, seeing him gape after the striding figure, jeered at him: "You really are a useless little biesan! Go on — clear off. A man who doesn't know Ninth Master Wang — do you even count as one?"

In truth, how could Dai Chunfeng not have known Wang Yaqiao? Years before, when he was begging along the streets and a motorcar knocked him senseless across the roadway, it was Wang Yaqiao who had saved his life. And now the Hatchet Gang's swagger shook him to the bone, and a light went on in a young man who had run out of roads. He turned it over for several days and made up his mind: he would stake his whole hope of rising on that Anhui man. So one day, gathering his nerve, he presented himself at the Anhui Guildhall. He had no calling card; he had sat up through the night writing a letter recommending himself, in the old manner of Mao Sui, and at the gate he presented it with both hands to the Hatchet Gang disciple on watch. Then he waited, carefully, outside — sure that a master as famous as Wang Yaqiao would never trouble himself over a down-and-out nobody. Yet the letter had scarcely gone in when word came out: "Boy — the Ninth Master invites you in!" Dai Chunfeng could not believe his ears. "The Ninth Master… wants to see me?" The man looked at him standing frozen. "What are you gawking at? Or must the Ninth Master come out and fetch you himself?"

That woke him. He hurried after the man, around a spirit-screen and into the main hall, stepping soft — and found several men around a table of mahjong, deep in the clatter of the bamboo-grove wars. Dai Chunfeng stood carefully to one side and did not dare open his mouth. He waited some two hours by the clock before anyone at the Eight Immortals table looked up. "Hey — young fellow. They tell me your people are Anhui too?"

Dai Chunfeng stole a glance. The man questioning him was the same Wang Yaqiao he had seen at the corner of Nanjing Road two days before — there was no mistaking that mustache. He bent himself double in a flurry of respect. "Ninth Master, above me — yes, yes. My forebears did indeed make their living on Anhui ground in the early days; the Ninth Master and I are countrymen exactly. That is why I dared come throw myself on you — in hope of a place in Shanghai to plant my feet and call a life!"

Wang Yaqiao studied him. The manner was coarse, but inside the coarseness was a contained, capable carefulness that ordinary men don't carry, and it made him look twice. "If you're an Anhui man," he said, "why does your letter call you a native of Jiangshan, Zhejiang? Isn't that a contradiction? You wouldn't be some swindler passing yourself off, now?"

Dai Chunfeng half rose, smiling apology. "The Ninth Master misunderstands. I'm a man down on his luck, yes — but not so far down that I'd falsify my ancestors for a bowl of rice. I came to the Ninth Master not for office, first, and not for money, second. I came in admiration of the Ninth Master's character — above all that high principle of his, honor upheld and wealth scattered. I came only to pay my respects, and to ask the Ninth Master for a bowl of rice. Nothing more."

"Good. Good, good!" The talk was not a vulgar man's talk, and the eyes had a burning shine to them; Wang Yaqiao knew at once that in time this one would stand no lower than himself. He was up in a moment and crossed to him. "Dai Chunfeng. Shanghai today is a place where officials and bandits run the streets together and every color of man is tumbled in with the rest — fish and dragons in one water. That you'd come to me at such an hour speaks for an upright character. So: stay, under my gate. And hear me well — so long as there is a Wang Yaqiao, you will never want for your bowl of rice."

From that day Dai Chunfeng was a junior disciple of the Hatchet Gang in Wang Yaqiao's Anhui Guildhall. In time Wang Yaqiao saw that the young man had not only a gift for pleasing but the cool shrewdness a gang cannot do without, and he set special store by him. What startled him most was the nerve: Dai Chunfeng, the most negligible of junior disciples, had daring out of all proportion to his station. They were even alike — almost identical — in their weakness for women. Like knows like: Wang Yaqiao in the end not only raised Dai Chunfeng to real duties but smeared blood in covenant with him, and they became sworn brothers of the exchanged-card, knocked-head kind. When Dai Chunfeng first stood at the Guildhall gate, he would not have dared dream it.

"Yunong, I am sending you to Hefei to carry one sentence to that brother you swore with in Shanghai — Wang Yaqiao. Tell him: from now on he behaves himself, and he does not set up court against me, Chiang Chung-cheng, ever again." The rickshaw was drawing near the provincial Tongmenghui dormitory now, and Dai Chunfeng felt the nerves pull tight in him. He was not what he had been. Once he had been Wang Yaqiao's man in Shanghai; now he was Chiang Kai-shek's operative. He had not come from Nanjing to talk over old times with his sworn elder brother; he had come on Chiang Kai-shek's particular errand, to ring a warning bell in Wang Yaqiao's ear. No man alive understood the master of the Hatchet Gang better than Dai Chunfeng — which is exactly why he had no confidence at all in the errand. Then the rickshaw man spoke: "Sir — this is the Tongmenghui dormitory you wanted!"

At that hour, upstairs in the provincial Tongmenghui building, a short, compact figure stood motionless at an oval floor-length window: the very Wang Yaqiao whom Dai Chunfeng was so anxious to find, alone at the glass with his thoughts. After the attempt on Chen Diaoyuan failed at Nanjing, he had slipped quietly back to Hefei, and he had gone on plotting a second strike with the men of the Hatchet Gang.

"Ninth Master — Chen Diaoyuan is petitioning Chiang Kai-shek for a transfer out of Anhui this minute. We didn't kill him at Nanjing, but we frightened him half to death. He tells everyone he meets that Anhui is no place for a man named Chen to linger; better an office somewhere else than the Hatchet Gang's bullet some morning." That was the news that came, on the eve of the second attempt, when a mysterious figure appeared at Wang Yaqiao's residence: a tall man, imposing, a pair of tiger's eyes under heavy brows — Wang Leping, his second elder brother from the founding of the Hatchet Gang in Shanghai. Close behind Wang Leping came a fine-featured man, a soldier, Wang Yaqiao's close friend Yu Likui. When they gave him the news of Chen Diaoyuan's transfer, Wang Yaqiao laughed with delight. "Ha! Didn't Chen Diaoyuan sell himself to Chiang Kai-shek precisely to buy the chairmanship of Anhui? Not two years in office — and now he spurs his horse back over the hills? Has he forgotten what fine feng-shui Anhui has for squeezing the people's wealth?"

Wang Leping and Wang Yaqiao went back years in Shanghai, and this time he and Yu Likui had gone specially to Nanjing to steal intelligence and carried it to Hefei, to the man preparing the second attempt. "It's like this, Ninth Brother. You didn't kill Chen Diaoyuan last time — but you shot his man Zhang Qiubai dead on the spot. Chen Diaoyuan has learned that the Ninth Brother is not to be provoked. He knows that if he stays in office in Anhui he can squeeze the province and stuff his purse, but with Ninth Brother Wang alive there will be a second attempt, and a third. Chen Diaoyuan loves his life before everything. You think he dares sit here waiting for the Hatchet Gang to fire?"

Wang Yaqiao laughed. "Clever enough, in his way. If he truly leaves Anhui, he picks up his life and carries it off. Otherwise my brothers could hardly promise that some morning a bullet wouldn't find its way into that skull!"

Yu Likui pressed him: "Ninth Brother, Chen Diaoyuan's evils in Anhui are full to the brim. Now is the moment to strike. Are we to let him do his wickedness, pat his backside, and stroll away alive?"

Wang Yaqiao waved the thought down, deliberate and cool. "There you are wrong, Likui. From the day we founded the Hatchet Gang in Shanghai, my rule — Wang Jiuguang's rule — has been this: kill the wicked to succor the many, and never waste a man who has waked to what he was. Chen Diaoyuan has had his fright; he knows now that evil ends badly; he has handed Chiang Kai-shek his resignation. So let him go. As long as he works no evil on Anhui ground, we simply let him be. When I moved against him it was never for a private grudge in the first place — I meant the killing of Chen Diaoyuan to show Chiang Kai-shek what color his future is. All of you remember: the target of this Hatchet Gang was, and is, Chiang Kai-shek — that detestable autocrat, that thief of the people!"

And sure enough: the day after Wang Leping and Yu Likui brought their news, one sheet of transfer paper from Chiang Kai-shek packed Chen Diaoyuan off to command the 37th Army of the Northern Expedition's First Group Army. Soon after, Fang Zhenwu arrived in Hefei to take up the provincial chairmanship. Fang Zhenwu had gotten a province — but he was no man of Chiang Kai-shek's own line, and frictions bred daily, sparks on every side. Wang Yaqiao saw his opening: the ground of Anhui itself was ready for a rising against Chiang.

"Ninth Brother — toppling Chiang is what the whole world hopes for, of course. But I keep doubting: can this united front of yours hold?" Soon after Chen Diaoyuan left the province, Wang Yaqiao called an emergency council of the Hatchet Gang. When Wang Leping and Yu Likui heard his plan — to link Fang Zhenwu, Shi Yousan and Bo Wenwei into one front against Chiang — both were stirred; Wang Leping above all, an old Tongmenghui man with his own long score against Chiang Kai-shek, backed the united front with his whole heart. But he could not stop doubting Shi Yousan. "Shi Yousan is the one who worries me," he said. "He hates Chiang, yes, and the hate is old. But everyone alive knows the man's word and his deed live apart. He breaks faith the way other men breathe. Will he really rise with us?"

"Shi Yousan is a twisting soul, granted — but he keeps a mass of troops quartered in Jiangsu, and that is a weight nobody can wave away." Wang Yaqiao had studied the strength of every warlord camp in Jiangnan, and when he laid it out, it came in good order. He turned to Wang Leping and Yu Likui. "General Bo Wenwei is commander-in-chief of our alliance. His doctrine is to link every lord of every road who can be linked, and stand them up in one great wall against Chiang — only so can the Hatchet Gang's long design be realized. Now Fang Zhenwu has agreed to lend us his arm inside the anti-Chiang camp; that is what makes success possible. And Fang Zhenwu's condition for joining is exactly this: Shi Yousan's army must be in, or he dares not rise — without it, Chiang Kai-shek cannot be brought down."

Wang Leping nodded slowly. "A fine plan. Only — who bells that cat? Who talks Shi Yousan around?"

"I ride out myself, naturally. Shi Yousan will not refuse me my face." Wang Yaqiao plainly had the whole recruitment drawn in his chest already. He turned his gaze on Yu Likui, who had been silent a long while. "What worries me now is the Independent Fourth Brigade at Changzhou. Brother Likui — could you go in my place and win them over?"

Yu Likui owed Wang Yaqiao old kindnesses and admired him past telling. Handed the heaviest charge of all — turning the Changzhou Fourth Brigade — he slapped his chest. "Ninth Brother, if you trust me, I'll go to Changzhou myself. Everyone knows Brigade Commander Peng Jianguo has been my close friend for years — and I know he hates Chiang Kai-shek to the marrow. Send me, and the banners will open in victory!"

"Splendid!" For years Wang Yaqiao had nursed one wish: to bring Chiang Kai-shek down. Now, under his hand, the wish was hardening into a plan of campaign fit to be launched. "When the hour comes, three roads rise at once: Fang Zhenwu in the north, Peng Jianguo at Changzhou in the south, Shi Yousan in the west. And in the middle, the Hatchet Gang stands as the echo behind them all. That is the board of the Three Kingdoms set against one man. No fear then that Chiang Kai-shek does not fall!"

He was standing at the window with the whole grand design turning in his head when a disciple came up from below. "Ninth Master — a guest from Nanjing, come calling. He says he must see the Ninth Master face to face."

"Oh? From Nanjing?" Puzzled, Wang Yaqiao took the card the disciple held up — and on it stood a stranger's name: Dai Li. He started, and hid it. Of all moments — just as he was drawing the noose around Chiang Kai-shek — here came the Dai Chunfeng who had once bowed to him as elder brother in Shanghai, suddenly arrived in Hefei. He turned the card over in his mind. "Who is this Dai Li? Can it really be the old Dai Chunfeng? And if it is — what brings him here from Nanjing? Has the Chiang fellow smelled something?" His first instinct was to refuse the visitor. Then he thought again, and flicked his hand at the disciple after all. "Let him in!"

### 6. Dai Li and Wang Yaqiao

The guest from Nanjing came in, snatched off his hat, and bowed low. "Ninth Brother — don't say you no longer know your own little brother?"

"Dai Chunfeng? So it really is you!" Wang Yaqiao looked him carefully up and down. "And when did you change your name?"

Before Wang Yaqiao, Dai Chunfeng was forever the perfect junior — careful in the smallest thing, exactly as in the years when he had knelt at the Hatchet Gang's gate. He knew what he owed: it was this man who had taken him in when he was scraping for meals in Shanghai, and his slow rise there afterward had not been unconnected with Wang Yaqiao either. When Dai Chunfeng came to know Dai Jitao, and the brothers Chen Lifu and Chen Guofu, it was Wang Yaqiao who stood as the introducer. And the reason he finally left Shanghai was that in Wang Yaqiao's Hatchet Gang the position he considered his due never came; so in 1925 he abruptly went far south to Guangdong. It began with a newspaper he saw in the street one day, carrying the report of Chiang Kai-shek presiding over the opening ceremony of the Whampoa Academy — and a photograph of Chiang Kai-shek and Dai Jitao side by side. So one day he came quietly to Wang Yaqiao's sitting room in the Anhui Guildhall, cupped his hands, and bowed. "Ninth Brother — I want to go to Guangzhou and have a look around."

"Guangzhou?" Wang Yaqiao could not, at the time, understand why a man who had sworn the bond and exchanged the cards with him should suddenly want out. But he did not stand in the way. "Chunfeng — it seems the water in my pond runs shallow, too shallow to keep a big fish like you! Well then. You want to go; I won't bar the road. But tell your Ninth Brother the truth: what is it you mean to do in Guangdong? Not go over to Chiang Kai-shek, surely?"

Dai Chunfeng knew Wang Yaqiao opposed Chiang Kai-shek from the marrow out, and he covered fast. "Ninth Brother, you do my heart a wrong. Chiang Kai-shek and I were never carts on the same road — what would I be to him? I only want the military academy. You know it yourself, Ninth Brother: you and I are not alike. In Shanghai, in Anhui, you broke open your own ground long ago — and what number am I? You think well of me, but I know the truth: around Jiangning, a man like me will never rise. If I'm to have my day, it will be by the soldier's road. So I mean to sit the examinations for Whampoa. And if your little brother ever truly makes his way in the army — the Ninth Brother's kindness will not be forgotten. Never!"

"Very well." Wang Yaqiao was an open-handed man. In his heart he was against it, and he knew perfectly well that the Whampoa Military Academy was Chiang Kai-shek's own ground; but he swallowed the words that would have kept Dai there, and said, against his heart: "With your talents, perhaps the army is the fitter road after all. One word of counsel, though, and only one. Go to the academy, be an officer — I support it a hundred times over. What I fear is this: will you end as Chiang Kai-shek's accomplice? Because if that day comes, you and I are finished!"

"Ninth Brother, set your heart at rest!" Dai Chunfeng had never imagined Wang Yaqiao would see to the bottom of him at one look. For in truth only he himself knew why he was going. Say rather than that he was going south to attach himself to the party elder Dai Jitao, whom he'd met in Shanghai — he was going to attach himself to his fellow Zhejiang man, Chiang Kai-shek. Sworn brother of life and death to Wang Yaqiao he might be; he had already seen that a Hatchet Gang forever hacking and chopping was fated never to make real weather, while Chiang Kai-shek held the army in his hand and the future with it. So he had resolved to go to Guangdong and present himself to Chiang as a fellow provincial — exactly as, years before, he had presented himself to Wang Yaqiao on the strength of Anhui ancestors. He knew perfectly well the two men were mortal enemies; so he lied, from the heart outward. "Everything I am, I am because you made me — a creature with a man's shape on him at all. If I go now to sit for the academy, it's for the road ahead, and only because there's no other way!"

"Then go — and good fortune!" Moved by all that earnestness, Wang Yaqiao set a farewell banquet, and soon after, the two men parted in tears. Now the years had flicked past, and Wang Yaqiao had never dreamed Dai Chunfeng would come back as Dai Li. What he did know was this: after leaving him in 1925, Dai Chunfeng had indeed entered the Whampoa Academy's sixth class, in the cavalry section. In the army, the man's dare-anything ferocity caught Chiang Kai-shek's eye. He marched with Chiang through the famous Northern Expedition. And he did not — as he had promised in Shanghai before going south — keep his distance from Chiang Kai-shek. On the plea that Jiangshan and Chiang's own Fenghua sit nearly elbow to elbow in Zhejiang, he worked his way quickly into Chiang Kai-shek's trust along the Eastern Route's march north. Later there came, in Chiang Kai-shek's own brushwork, a four-character inscription in his honor: "Hardship Surpassing All."

What startled Wang Yaqiao most was what followed the Northern Expedition: this knocked-head little brother, who had sworn oath upon oath that soldiering would be his life's one honor, took up instead the loathsome wartime trade of the secret agent. From Dai Jitao's later talk, Wang Yaqiao learned that Dai Chunfeng, on the Northern Expedition road, went out repeatedly on Chiang Kai-shek's secret commission through the five northern provinces — Tianjin, Beiping, Taiyuan, Zhengzhou, Xi'an — spying out the Beiyang warlords' military dispositions for the Chiang camp. From then on, Dai Chunfeng was Chiang Kai-shek's operative. Thinking of all this, Wang Yaqiao could not help the guard rising in him, and he said: "Aren't you doing famously over there with the Chiang fellow? What brings you running to Hefei? Or am I — Wang Jiuguang — to be your next Zhou Weilong?"

"Ah — no, no, Ninth Brother, you misunderstand me!" Dai Li had not expected the old Wang Yaqiao, the one needle that always found blood, sparing nobody's face. He knew exactly what the words meant: the old business at Wuhan, when he had turned Zhou Weilong. That was after the party split between Nanjing and Wuhan, when Chiang Kai-shek sent him into Wuhan to steal the military secrets of Tang Shengzhi's army. But Tang Shengzhi had marked his traces early, and just as Dai Li, intelligence in hand, was preparing to slip out of Wuhan, Tang ordered the net drawn — and Dai Li became a prisoner in Tang Shengzhi's jail. He lost his liberty, and the intelligence went with it, into Tang Shengzhi's hands. By rights he should have died there. But his life was not fated to end: he learned that the prison holding him stood under the watch of a gendarme company commander named Zhou Weilong — his own classmate from Whampoa. Already under sentence of death, Dai Li begged an urgent audience with Zhou on the night before the execution, and that one conversation — the substance of it being that if Zhou Weilong came over to Chiang Kai-shek with him, a major general's rank was waiting — actually moved his old schoolmate's heart. And so, under Zhou Weilong's own escort, the two of them fled together back to Nanjing. Now, hearing Wang Yaqiao ring that story at him like a bell, Dai Li understood that his standing with Chiang Kai-shek lay in Wang Yaqiao's palm like a counted coin, and he rushed to protest: "Ninth Brother, I am not a man who forgets what he owes. In the old Shanghai days I had nothing and no one — if the Ninth Brother hadn't acted on honor and taken in a nameless outlander, would there be a Dai Yunong today at all?"

Only when Wang Yaqiao saw the old deference back in him — the old timidity — did he wave him to a seat and send for tea. "You know very well Zhou Weilong and I are not the same breed of man. So don't come working your plans on me. Chunfeng — if you have truly dropped in on Hefei to see an old friend, then we are brothers, now as before. But if you have come as the Chiang fellow's persuader, don't blame me when old feeling goes unrecognized."

"Of course, of course." Dai Li had known before he ever set out that Chiang Kai-shek had handed him a bone too hard for gnawing; Wang Yaqiao was not a man to be talked around with flowered words. With his mouth sealed shut at the first exchange, he could only swallow the whole speech he had prepared, and stand there trading pleasantries, coiling and dodging. "The truth is, Ninth Brother, you can't know the bitterness I carry. Yes, I'm one of Mr. Chiang's men now — and for years on the Northern Expedition they ran me from one end of the map to the other, sniffing out military intelligence. A bitter errand, no more. That peddler's-round of a life was never what I wanted either. Where does 'thriving' come into it?"

No man knew Dai Li better. Watching him glance left and right and talk of everything else, Wang Yaqiao knew his needle had gone home, and he laughed a cold heh-heh. "Spare me. If you're out of favor with the Chiang fellow, what, pray, is this 'Ten-Man Team'? Half the Nationalist Party knows by now what you are to Chiang. So why stand in front of me talking ghost-talk with no edges to it?"

Dai Li froze, a long moment without words. The old shrewdness was all still there — the man held even this in his palm: that on Chiang Kai-shek's confidential order he had gathered ten old Whampoa friends, Zhou Weilong, Zhang Yanyuan and the rest, into a secret special-services group. With his identity and his errand laid this bare, playing hide-the-sky-and-cross-the-sea any further was pointless. He cupped his hands gravely. "Ninth Brother — since the talk has come down to this layer, I'll tell no lies in front of a true man. The truth, then: this trip to Hefei, it was indeed Mr. Chiang who sent me."

"Oh? And here I thought you'd come to look in on me." One cold, contemptuous laugh. "Good. Quick and clean. Dai Chunfeng, let's open the skylight and talk in the light. I am Wang Jiuguang: I walk without changing my name and sit without changing my surname. I keep to Hefei; old Chiang keeps his seat in Nanjing; well-water need not trouble river-water. So what precious business does his envoy have with me?"

Cold words, and a colder edge on them. Dai Li thought: then let him see every card, and we'll see how he answers. "Ninth Brother, you know how it stands. I am foreman now of Mr. Chiang's Ten-Man Team, and things touching the National Government come to my ears. Since spring, the military men and politicians of every road around Jiangning have been running together into one undercurrent, and it flows against Mr. Chiang. More than that: certain people are brewing a terrible plot to bring him down — several columns merging into one, preparing an armed rising. So before Mr. Chiang moves to crush it, he sent your little brother ahead to Hefei, to pass the Ninth Brother a word! You were my sworn brother; I knocked my head to you; I owe you my start. There is no law in heaven by which I could know of this and not warn you!"

To Wang Yaqiao the words were thunder out of a clear sky. They meant Chiang Kai-shek already knew he was plotting a rising with Fang Zhenwu, chairman of Anhui, and with Shi Yousan of the Jiangsu garrison — knew, down to the palm lines, that his trusted friends Wang Leping and Yu Likui were at that moment working on the Fourth Brigade at Changzhou. He went rigid. The intelligence was too exact. Someone had leaked the whole conspiracy — his, and Bo Wenwei's, and the rest. And yet not one thread of flinching could show. He threw back his head and laughed. "Well, well — Dai Chunfeng, all grown up! Running to my door to play Chiang Kai-shek's envoy of intimidation! Good. You say someone is merging 'several strands of force' in the dark. Meaning what? I am Wang Jiuguang, one flat-capped commoner. Am I supposed to have the power to set armies marching?"

Dai Li had reckoned that one light touch would spring the truth from him, and that threat and menace could then finish what Chiang Kai-shek wanted finished before anyone else moved. Instead Wang Yaqiao stood unmoved and turned defense into attack — and Dai Li's footing was suddenly very awkward. Knowing more pressure was useless, he wound it up in a few short sentences and withdrew his troops. "The Ninth Brother reads too much into it. Roadside talk, no more, and I took it for true — my mistake, and an honest one. I only meant to bring warning first, and counsel the Ninth Brother to walk with care. While I still have a word's worth of standing at Mr. Chiang's side, I'd use it for you. If the Ninth Brother had really boarded some stranger's pirate ship, reining the horse at the cliff-edge now would be no disgrace to a hero. Because if Mr. Chiang truly finds men plotting against him, there will be no light-handed sketchwork this time. This time the ring of killing opens wide — and then repentance comes too late. But seeing now that the Ninth Brother has not one tangled vine connecting him to these restless armies in the wings — your little brother's heart can rest!"

That night, Wang Yaqiao, giving nothing away, set a quiet banquet to wash the dust off his guest, and the two of them talked over the old eight-bows brotherhood of better days. At first light Dai Li took a hurried leave and rode the express back to Nanjing, to report to Chiang Kai-shek how the turning of Wang Yaqiao had failed.

It was after Dai Li left that the fear crept in, and grew. It came to Wang Yaqiao suddenly that the previous night's visitor had been Jiang Gan come to steal the letters, straight out of the Three Kingdoms. Yes, he and Dai Li had been friends of life and death on the Shanghai waterfront; yes, he had saved a starving Dai Yunong. But this Dai Chunfeng had changed more than his name: the heart itself had gone over, to Chiang Kai-shek. And what had leaked between his words was perfectly clear. Somewhere inside the conspiracy — his own, with Bo Wenwei, Wang Leping, Yu Likui; the plan for Fang Zhenwu, Shi Yousan and the Changzhou Fourth Brigade to rise in mutiny together — a traitor was standing. Otherwise Dai Li could never have said the words "several strands of military force are stirring." Wang Yaqiao could not yet tell where the seal had broken in a plot so tightly caulked. But years of maneuvering among every faction along the Nanjing-Shanghai line told him plainly: someone had gone over to Chiang Kai-shek in the dark — or been bought by Dai Li's Ten-Man Team.

"If someone has turned informer, then the Chiang fellow will strike first — count on it. And that means some of us are marked for ruin!" When Bo Wenwei received a panicked Wang Yaqiao at his residence and heard what Dai Li had come to Hefei to say, the general's own composure tightened. Fang Zhenwu was new in his post: the will to move against Chiang was there, the concrete preparations were not. Shi Yousan had agreed, under repeated persuasion, to rise with the rest — but the man's word turned back on itself as a habit of nature, and no weight could be rested on it. As for the Fourth Brigade at Changzhou, which Wang Leping and Yu Likui were working on, Commander Peng Jianguo was only beginning his preparations. If Chiang Kai-shek learned of the "Three Kingdoms against one" design now, and answered it with the quick knife that cuts tangled hemp, the consequences would not bear thinking about. Bo Wenwei's fist came down on the table. "What matters now is one thing: move. Raise the troops at once — that is the higher strategy."

"Nothing better!" Wang Yaqiao had come, unprompted, to the same resolve.

The two men decided on the spot to drive to General Fang Zhenwu's quarters in the city and settle, urgently, a plan for striking early. But — utterly against all reckoning — Fang Zhenwu had left for Nanjing early that same morning.

"What? Why would General Fang suddenly go to Nanjing?" At the Fang residence they heard it: in the night a secret telegram had come from Chiang Kai-shek personally, summoning him at once to the capital for an important military conference; at dawn he had set off traveling light, a secretary and a few guards, nothing more. The color went out of Wang Yaqiao's face. "Bad — bad! There's more ill than luck on that road — it is Chiang Kai-shek's trap, nothing surer. Send men after him — bring the general back!"

Bo Wenwei only gave a long sigh. "Jiuguang. Late — too late. By now General Fang is in Nanjing already. You could be the wind itself and not overtake him now."

On the road back to the provincial Tongmenghui headquarters, a guard stepped out and blocked the car head-on; they shouted the driver to a halt. The guard's face was dark. He came forward and reported: "Confirmed news from Nanjing, just arrived. General Fang Zhenwu has been arrested by Chiang Kai-shek and taken to prison!"

"What did you say?" At the news that Fang Zhenwu had lost his liberty in Nanjing, the blood left Wang Yaqiao's face; a mouthful of bright red came up and out, and he pitched to the ground…

### 7. Who Is the Traitor?

When Wang Yaqiao swam back to consciousness he was lying at home.

"Jiuguang — you've come round!" Keeping watch at the edge of the bed was a woman with a face like flowers and the moon: Wang Yaying, the beloved of his heart. Wang Yaying's original name was Cong Yunyu; she was Hubei-born, and from girlhood the revolution had been her longing. In the first rising at Wuchang in 1911 she served as a detachment commander of the most famous women's organization of the day, the Women's Northern Expedition Restoration Army. Wang Yaqiao happened then to be in Wuhan too, and in one fierce engagement he saved Cong Yunyu's life. She fell in love with him there on the battleground, and from then on she followed at his side. In 1914 she came with him from Hubei to Hefei, and before long they were husband and wife.

To fuse her life wholly with this man of great cunning and great courage, Cong Yunyu of her own will took her husband's surname and became Wang Yaying. Now, watching him spit blood and faint in a single burst of fury over the wreck of the rising, she wept and would not spare him: "Jiuguang, a matter the size of heaven still has to be withstood. You want to do deeds that startle the world under heaven — then you must carry the gall for them! Or how are great deeds ever done?"

Wang Yaqiao gripped her hand hard. "Yaying, there is reason in what you say. But my heart will not lie level. Why did a thing this good have to break in the hand of a traitor?"

"And who is the traitor?"

"This creature who sold himself to Chiang Kai-shek — I don't know yet who he is. But sooner or later I will. And when I have him, he pays for betraying the revolution with his head!"

And while Wang Yaqiao lay racking himself over Fang Zhenwu's peril in Nanjing, a worse grief came out of Shanghai into Anhui: his cherished friend, the Tongmenghui elder who had plotted this very mutiny at his side — Wang Leping — had been assassinated by agents sent by Dai Li!

"Heaven! Brother Leping — why should this doom fall on you?" The news was this: deep in the night of February 17, at his residence off Avenue Joffre, Wang Leping had been shot dead in a hail of bullets by agents lying in wait near the Xiafei lanes. Wang Yaqiao rose up weeping; the rage-fire of grief attacked his heart; another mouthful of blood sprayed out, and he went down again onto the bed.

When he woke, there were more faces at the bedside than his wife's: the Hatchet Gang mainstays Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai had come specially from Shanghai with the news. Seeing him wake at last, they all pressed forward, crying out: "Ninth Master, you must hold! If heaven itself falls, the Hatchet Gang cannot be without its Ninth Master!"

Wang Yaying looked at the haggard face, the eyes jetting hatred, and knew his spirit was at the extremity of pain. She hurried to steady him. "The world's affairs are past reckoning; nothing in a life holds constant. Jiuguang — since Chiang Kai-shek's allotted years are not yet spent, let us simply take the lesson of this defeat and keep it. As long as you live, the Hatchet Gang lives. And as long as the Hatchet Gang neither dies nor is extinguished, the revolution keeps its hope. Our hand slipped this once. The day of rising again in the eastern hills will still come."

"Right. Wang Jiuguang is not a man pasted together out of paper. Besides — there is still the Fourth Brigade at Changzhou. If Yu Likui and Peng Jianguo bring that force over, we hold a thread of hope yet. The Fourth Brigade can fight. Let me go there myself and set Peng Jianguo moving, then march on Nanjing — we can still catch Chiang Kai-shek with his hands empty. Who says we cannot bring Fang Zhenwu out?"

But at this the people around him, for no reason he could see, all went silent. Even Wang Yaying was avoiding his eyes. He stared from face to face, baffled.

"Yaying — what is wrong with all of you? Why the long faces? Has one blow finished us for good?" He could not understand the shrinking in his wife's face, and he roared the question at her. She did not answer; she only flicked him one timid glance and held whatever it was behind her teeth.

"Jimin! Hongtai! And what's the matter with you two? Has Chiang Kai-shek killed Wang Leping and frightened the whole pack of us into silence?" Wang Yaqiao did not know, then, how bad things outside had already become — did not know Chiang Kai-shek was massing troops from every direction and closing on Hefei itself, the old nest of the Hatchet Gang. But the sight of Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai — men who had never once lost faith in the war on Chiang — standing there with hanging heads and drained faces, was more than he could bear. "Why won't you speak? Have you all turned soft-shelled? I have no use for that breed. I am a man who would rather live on his feet than die on his knees."

And yet, though his liver-fire was fairly alight now, Wang Yaying, Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai went on standing with lowered heads, and not one word.

In that unbearable silence Wang Yaqiao felt the threat take shape, and the understanding came all at once. "Yaying. Not one of you dares look me in the eye. There is something you are carrying. Don't tell me — Changzhou has gone wrong too?"

He had asked the exact question, and Wang Yaying knew that hiding it further served nothing. She sighed once. "Jiuguang — we did not mean to tell you what has happened outside. You cannot take another shock; you simply cannot. General Bo Wenwei telephoned again just now to say: whatever happens, do not tell him about Changzhou now — everyone fears the rage will tear your body apart, blow after blow with no rest between. But you hunt it and hunt it and will not let go — ai, and now there is nothing left but the truth. Jiuguang: Changzhou is finished too!…"

"What did you say?" He saw the tears come free on his wife's face, and knew. His skull rang like a struck bell; the room turned once; he nearly went down onto the bed again. Yet this time no blood came. He held himself upright by main toughness while his face went from red to white and the cold sweat pattered off his forehead. From his wife's half-spoken look he had already guessed how bad it was. And it was this bad: the rising he had staked his hope on — Yu Likui moving Commander Peng Jianguo's Fourth Brigade at Changzhou — had died in the womb like the rest. His fury broke out in a shout: "Is Peng Jianguo not to be relied on either?"

"No — no! It isn't that Commander Peng failed us — a traitor surfaced! Before Yu Likui and Commander Peng could even rise, Chiang Kai-shek's troops struck first!" Now that Wang Yaying had broken the seal, Xuan Jimin simply reported everything he and Wu Hongtai had carried from Changzhou, fact upon fact, to the burning man in the bed.

It had gone like this. Yu Likui, carrying the orders of Wang Yaqiao and Bo Wenwei, had slipped into Changzhou long before, and with him went the Hatchet Gang's crack killers, Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai among them. The work of turning the brigade went smoothly from the start. Peng Jianguo was a patriot of long standing, and when his old friend Yu Likui laid out the joint design of Wang Yaqiao and Bo Wenwei, he came in with his whole chest: "Chiang the Baldhead should have fallen long ago. The moment Mr. Sun died of his illness, the man betrayed the revolution in the open and butchered revolutionaries — the whole nation's anger has been banked up for years. We are soldiers; purging the country's traitor is precisely our duty. If Wang Jiuguang and General Bo Wenwei trust me, then I, Peng Jianguo, will act jointly with Fang Zhenwu. Only — that Shi Yousan. He is the one I don't trust."

Yu Likui answered: "Ninth Master Wang goes to Shi Yousan himself — trust him to hold that thread with a full grip. Besides, Shi Yousan has eaten Chiang Kai-shek's cold rice and been elbowed aside for years. With the great hour of toppling Chiang upon us, why would he sit out?"

And just as Yu Likui, Peng Jianguo and the others sat in Changzhou settling how the rising should go, the traitor inside them did his work. On the secret report, Chiang Kai-shek sent heavy troops racing from Nanjing against Changzhou. Peng Jianguo and Yu Likui, their preparations half-made, scrambled into line to meet them; the brigade, few against many, broke inside a few exchanges, its casualties terrible, and most of what remained surrendered to Chiang Kai-shek. So the Changzhou mutiny died in the womb. Peng Jianguo cut his way out of the ring with the remnant and fled down the rail line toward Shanghai and Hangzhou; Yu Likui was taken in the first shock of the assault by Chiang Kai-shek's troops, carried secretly to Nanjing in a special car, and locked in Tangshan Prison — where he joined, as prison-mate, the earlier-taken General Fang Zhenwu.

Wu Hongtai had more: "Shi Yousan's force was crushed by Chiang Kai-shek and scattered at the same hour — and there too, a traitor's information did the work. So all three roads of the rising are gone at one stroke: the earth caved and the tiles flew!"

Wang Yaqiao sat very still on the sickbed and heard the two reports, Xuan Jimin's and Wu Hongtai's, to their end. Deep as the cut went, this time there was no cry, no faint, no blood. He buried the whole breastful of pain and hatred where no one could see it, and said to them all, in pain: "This defeat means one thing — heaven was not ready to collect Chiang Kai-shek. And blame us too: we counted our chickens in too bright a light. What we do first now is this: find out — who is the traitor?!"

Wang Yaying looked at him — eyes glaring round, both fists white — and struggled for an answer. "Jiuguang, a traitor there certainly is. But a man who sold us from the dark is a man who knows how to stay hidden. The word 'traitor' isn't written on anyone's face. How would you have us name him?"

Xuan Jimin watched Wang Yaqiao burn to have the man that instant, to kill him with a thousand cuts and cool his heart — and had no evidence to offer. "The lady is right. We know only this much: someone inside the movement fed intelligence to Chiang. Who it was, no one can yet feel out."

"Wu Hongtai. Why are you silent? Do you know who has been sawing my stage down?"

Wu Hongtai shook his head. "Ninth Master, I don't know who did it either."

Wang Yaqiao turned it over slowly. "In Hefei and in Wuhu I sat with General Bo Wenwei time after time over this. The men who knew the inside could be counted on one hand's fingers. Senior military men, party to the thing itself: General Fang Zhenwu, Mr. Wang Leping — and Shi Yousan, and Peng Jianguo. Past them, only the few men closest to my own heart. Count yourselves in, and the whole circle of knowledge is seven or eight men. If there is a traitor, he stands inside that circle. And every man in it is a friend I trust to the bone. So tell me — why would such a man carry my great plan to my sworn enemy, Chiang Kai-shek?"

The words drew the air tight around Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai, for they too stood inside the circle of suspicion. They looked at each other, awkward, wordless. Wang Yaying stepped in quickly. "Jimin — Hongtai — what the Ninth Master says is only the truth: the men who knew were all his own intimates. And yet it was among exactly those men that the thing happened. If we don't drag it into the light quickly — if the spy stays on at the Ninth Master's side — isn't he a bomb standing armed at every hour?"

Xuan Jimin answered without flinching. "Ninth Master, we are the men who have gone with you out of life and into death. That heaven-offending thing we would never do — to sell the Ninth Master is to sell ourselves. We were in the Chen Diaoyuan attempt, all of us. Even if we wanted to sell you, Chiang Kai-shek could never trust us — Zhang Qiubai's blood is on our hands!"

And Wu Hongtai swore oath on oath beside him: "Ninth Master, Brother Jimin has it exactly. With a traitor inside, no amount of protesting will loosen the doubt. What matters now is one thing — send me and Brother Jimin to Shanghai!"

Wang Yaqiao understood their protests; the doubt would still not lie down. From the day Dai Li crossed the river to play persuader, through Fang Zhenwu's seizure in Nanjing, he had been asking himself without rest whether a planted man of Chiang Kai-shek's stood hidden somewhere at his side. Nothing else explained how a design so long in the making — his, Bo Wenwei's, Fang Zhenwu's — could collapse on the very brink. But now, seeing the set faces on Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai, he struck the table once. "You two weigh my words too heavy. Wang Jiuguang is not so small a man that he cannot rest his heart on brothers of life and death. But this thing stinks of the strange. Who reached in and took the core of our secrets? Let me once find him and I will tear him into ten thousand pieces. This man wrecked the great plan; he dragged General Fang and Brother Likui down into calamity; and — the wound that aches to the root — my truest friend Wang Leping lies shot dead under the Chiang gang's guns! If I do not run down the spy who informed, how does Wang Jiuguang stand up in the world and call himself a man?"

"Ninth Master — that is exactly why I beg for this battle and ask to go to Shanghai myself!" Wu Hongtai heard the words come with tears in the voice, straight up out of the lungs, and he stepped forward and swore with cupped hands: "I will stake anything on it: the traitor is not in Hefei. He is in Shanghai!"

Wang Yaqiao studied the resourceful Wu Hongtai. "And what shows he is in Shanghai?"

"Mr. Wang Leping died in Shanghai," said Wu Hongtai. "That evening, someone knew in advance that he would return to his residence by the Xiafei lanes off Avenue Joffre. The agents were posted, before the fact, on the one road his motorcar had to take. The moment he stepped down from the car, the guns spoke — and they were fired out of darkness, at that. Whoever reported Wang Leping's movements to Chiang was in Shanghai to see them. How could any of us, sitting in Anhui, have foreseen that on that particular evening Wang Leping would go home to Avenue Joffre?"

Wang Yaqiao's eyes came alight, and his palm cracked down on the table. "There's the reasoning! Hongtai, I always said you carry a better head than other men. Find me the man in Shanghai who sold Mr. Wang Leping, and you have found the traitor who informs to Chiang — and the Hatchet Gang will owe you its debt of honor."

Two days later, Wu Hongtai and Xuan Jimin, dressed as merchants and trailing a few junior disciples, slipped out of Hefei and entered — unseen by spirits, unknown to ghosts — a Shanghai that reeked of blood. They found at once that the old friends of the Hatchet Gang now kept their distance, every one of them afraid that contact with Xuan and Wu would draw the eyes of Chiang Kai-shek's watchers. So instead of putting up at the Anhui Guildhall, they rented an unremarkable little courtyard near Jing'an Temple, lay hidden through the days, and moved only by night. Wu Hongtai had reasoned the traitor into Shanghai — but Shanghai is enormous, and in that shoreless sea of faces, where do you lay hands on one man who comes like a spirit and goes like a ghost? Yet Wu Hongtai and Xuan Jimin had craft in them, and on only their ninth day in the city they marked a suspicious man — and knew him at once for the shameless traitor who had sent Wang Yaqiao's whole design down the river to the sea. His name was Zhao Tieqiao!
