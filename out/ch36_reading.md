## An Unsigned Letter

*(A relic of a martyr who fell at Longhua.)*

I have long wanted to write you a letter, but did not know how to set pen to paper so that it would give nothing away.

Perhaps I ought to set it down in invisible ink, or work it into a passage of Morse code; but all such means are only an attempt to keep it from being deciphered, should it ever be found. And what I truly wish to say to you is no secret — it might be written upon the clouds, or written upon the water, for anyone in the world to see, yet it is written to you alone. As with every word I have spoken in this life, caught by your eyes and your ears, it is like Braille, or like Esperanto: its raised points, its mimicry of natural speech, that faint prickling, that syntax, taken down and kept by your fingertips.

I never once thought that we would part. Though every minute, every second, might be the moment of our parting forever. And could we but watch each other go, that would already be good fortune.

You will most likely never read this letter; I may already be gone, already far from you, in some place I do not know.

I do not know where I ought to wait for you, that you might find me. But you will know — will you not?

We do not look to be reunited in another world; what we love with all our hearts is only the place where we once were — and even if in the days to come no one remembers us, this is still the one place for which we are willing to give everything.

I love to hear you tell those stories of the plants — how the double-petaled flowers, through the atrophy and mutation of stamen and pistil, come to be the more brilliant, while the single-petaled flowers are the stronger to propagate.

Someday go again to Longhua — in the third or fourth month, when the peach blossoms open; climb the Bao'en Pagoda and look upon Longhua once more for me, and upon Shanghai. And that peach orchard east of the Bao'en Pagoda — look at the flowers there, the red, the white, and the mingled red-and-white.

The things we have seen, and the things we have not. To hear you tell of it all — our past, and the future of this world.

Sometimes, in the dark of night, I seem to see myself. I see myself gazing at you — anywhere in this world, gazing at you always, gazing at those blissful, entrancing stars in the night sky.
