### Our Friends the Harbour Pilots

The steamers that ply to foreign parts each carry a harbour pilot regularly attached to the ship, and his office is of the first importance: when the vessel, out on the open sea, comes to make port, it must steer by the pilot's directions, or it will lose its way and run into unlooked-for danger.

Now the Street of Mystery — that is, North Sichuan Road — has, along its northern stretch and the western reach of Avenue Joffre, its "pilots" too; only their office is not to bring steamers into harbour — it is a byname for a kind of pimping. Every den where soul and flesh are put up for sale they know inside out, be it Russian salt-meat, Korean salt-meat, or dwarf's salt-meat: they have the whole map of it at their fingers' ends. Toward dusk each day, along North Sichuan Road and Range Road and the Avenue Joffre quarter, they go pacing back and forth, peering this way and that with their thievish, furtive looks; and when they spy a man smartly dressed, strolling at his leisure, they sidle up and murmur low: "Sir — shall I take you for a bit of fun with a Japanese miss... a Russian miss...?" If you are willing to go, you have only to give a nod, and he will lead you there. At the end you give him four mao for his pilot's fee — though should you fall in with a sharper of the Brother Sheep sort, he will haggle you up and down, and it may be a dollar, or two; there is no fixed rate to it!
