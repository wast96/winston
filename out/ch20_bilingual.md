## H2 Chapter 20. A Meeting Met with a Smile Is Not Easily Won

> 丛锋一袭长衫，头发强硬地挺拔，他很精神，脸上带着久别重逢的笑容。荣华穿一件绣着梅花的湖色旗袍，窄身修腰，明艳动人。他们活像一幅水墨人物画，在夜霭的掩护下，朦朦胧胧，如梦如烟般呈现在阿初面前。
Cong Feng wore a long gown, his hair standing stiff and erect; he was full of spirit, and on his face was the smile of friends long parted and met again. Ronghua wore a lake-blue qipao embroidered with plum blossoms, close-cut and slender at the waist, radiant and lovely. The two of them were for all the world like a figure painting in ink-wash, and under the cover of the evening mist they appeared before A-Chu dim and blurred, dreamlike and faint as smoke.

> 阿初不由自主地向前挪动了一下，挪动始于内心的感触，他的心在震动。
In spite of himself A-Chu edged a step forward; the movement began in the stirrings of his heart, and his heart was shaken.

> 丛锋没有过激的动作，他用手指了指阿初，再指荣华，那意思，是他吗？你的同志？荣华摇头，那么……他的手指向自己，我的朋友？他的眼神在询问阿初。
Cong Feng made no rash gesture; he pointed a finger at A-Chu, then at Ronghua, as much as to ask, Is it him? Your comrade? Ronghua shook her head. Then . . . his finger turned toward himself, My friend? His eyes put the question to A-Chu.

> 阿初的眼光感到一种说不出来得痛和悲凉，丛锋的世界里，自己应该是善良的、正直的，有朝气的、自信的，活在阳光底的。而现在的自己活在阴谋里，暴虐、杀人、狂野，他相信，这是自己的精神遭受摧残后的一种变异。自己再也不是一个健康的人、正常的人，自己就像一个疯子。惭愧和怨愤一点一滴渗透到阿初的心灵，巨大的精神落差使他无法面对丛锋那久违的、亲切的、热情的、温暖的、包容的目光。阿初心中的酸痛渐渐化做充溢的泪花。
A-Chu’s gaze filled with a pain and a desolation he could not put into words. In Cong Feng’s world he ought to be a good man, an upright man, a man full of vigor and confidence, a man who lived in the sunlight. But the man he was now lived amid intrigue — cruel, murderous, savage; and he believed this to be a mutation his spirit had undergone after its ravaging. He was no longer a healthy man, a normal man; he was like a madman. Shame and rancor seeped drop by drop into A-Chu’s soul, and the vast fall of his spirit made him unable to meet that long-missed, kindly, ardent, warm, all-forgiving gaze of Cong Feng’s. The soreness in A-Chu’s heart turned little by little into brimming tears.

> 丛锋从阿初湿润的眼眶里找到了答案。
In A-Chu’s moistened eyes Cong Feng found his answer.

> “阿初！”他向阿初走过来，舒展双臂，敞开怀抱。
“A-Chu!” He came toward A-Chu, spreading his arms, throwing his embrace wide open.

> 阿初动作有点僵，不过，他很快适应过来，尽可能放轻松地绽放出英国式的礼貌微笑。他们紧紧拥抱在一起。久久地捶打对方的背，孕育了片刻的温情于瞬间爆发，阿初的泪终于夺眶而出。
A-Chu’s movements were a little stiff, but he adjusted quickly, and, as easily as he could manage, broke into a polite English smile. They held each other tight, pounding each other on the back for a long while; the tenderness that had gathered for a moment burst out all at once, and at last A-Chu’s tears broke from his eyes.

> “丈夫重知己。”丛锋深有感触地说。
“A true man prizes the friend who knows his heart,” Cong Feng said with deep feeling.

> “万里同一乡。”阿初有些哽咽。
“Ten thousand li apart, yet of one same country,” A-Chu said, a little choked.

> “脱胎换骨了？”丛锋放松手臂，审视阿初。
“Reborn — body remade, bones made new?” Cong Feng loosened his arms and looked A-Chu over.

> “你也是。”阿初说。
“You too,” said A-Chu.

> “想我和丛惠吗？”
“Have you missed me, and Cong Hui?”

> “深心挂念。”
“Deep in my heart, always.”

> 两个人开心一笑，再次握手。
The two of them laughed happily and shook hands again.

> “我来介绍一下。”丛锋拉起荣华的手。“我太太。”
“Let me introduce you,” Cong Feng said, taking Ronghua by the hand. “My wife.”

> 阿初笑得很幽默。
A-Chu’s smile was full of quiet humor.

> “我们认识的。”荣华干脆说穿。
“We already know each other,” said Ronghua, laying it bare without ado.

> “认识？”丛锋很意外。
“Know each other?” This took Cong Feng by surprise.

> “我们两个很小就认识。”阿初补充一句。
“The two of us have known each other since we were very small,” A-Chu added.

> “哦。”丛锋理会了。“青梅竹马？初恋情人？”
“Ah.” Cong Feng took it in. “Childhood sweethearts — green plums and bamboo hobby-horses? First loves?”

> “哥哥和妹妹。”荣华含蓄地笑。
“Elder brother and little sister,” Ronghua said with a veiled smile.

> “小姐与家奴。”阿初不避讳。
“Young mistress and house-slave,” said A-Chu, making no bones of it.

> 丛锋明白过来。“姓荣的？荣家的小姐。”
Cong Feng understood. “One of the Rongs? A young lady of the Rong house.”

> 两个人不约而同地颔首。
The two of them nodded as one, without prior agreement.

> 丛锋爽朗地笑起来。“大水冲了龙王庙，一家人啊。”他伸开双臂搭在两个人的肩上。“走，到我屋里去谈谈。”
Cong Feng laughed heartily. “The floodwaters have washed out the Dragon King’s own temple — we’re all of one family after all.” He spread his arms and laid them across the shoulders of the two of them. “Come — let’s go into my room and talk.”

> “这……不太方便吧？”阿初看荣华。
“That . . . mightn’t be quite convenient?” A-Chu looked at Ronghua.

> “这有什么不方便。家人团聚啊。”丛锋的兴致很高，浑然忘了所在之地。阿初再次用眼神问询荣华，荣华点头默许。
“What could be inconvenient about it? A family reunion!” Cong Feng was in high spirits, wholly forgetting where they were. Again A-Chu put the question to Ronghua with his eyes, and Ronghua nodded her silent consent.

> “麻烦你二小姐，门口有我的司机，您去告诉他，我今夜留在这里了。”阿初客气地说。
“May I trouble you, Second Miss — my driver is at the gate; would you go and tell him I’ll be staying here tonight,” A-Chu said courteously.

> “好的。”荣华转过身去，微风中，听着两个久别的朋友讲话。
“Very well.” Ronghua turned away, and in the light breeze listened to the two long-parted friends talk.

> “你现在做什么？”丛锋问。
“What are you doing now?” Cong Feng asked.

> “实业。”
“Industry and trade.”

> “怎么，不做医生了？”丛锋真得很惊异。
“What — no longer a doctor?” Cong Feng was truly astonished.

> “医家要有割股救人之心。坦率地说，现在的我，做不到。既然做不到，何必勉强自己呢？你呢？还是政治？”
“A physician must have the heart to cut the flesh from his own thigh to save another. Frankly, the man I am now cannot do it. And since I cannot, why force myself? And you? Still politics?”

> “政治和实业也不分家。”
“Politics and industry are not two separate households either.”

> “聪明人说得每一句话都是聪明话。”
“Every sentence a clever man speaks is a clever man’s sentence.”

> “我觉得你变许多。”
“I think you’ve changed a great deal.”

> “哪里？”
“In what way?”

> “这里。”丛锋指着自己的大脑，而后，注视阿初的双眸。“你的眼睛，像深不可测的大海。”
“Here.” Cong Feng pointed at his own head, and then gazed into A-Chu’s eyes. “Your eyes are like a sea too deep to fathom.”

> 阿初故作惊奇地说：“哇，怎么开始读雪莱了？我一直以为你不喜欢他。”
A-Chu said, with feigned surprise: “Why — have you taken to reading Shelley? I always thought you disliked him.”

> “那你认为我应该读谁的诗歌？”
“Then whose poetry do you think I ought to read?”

> “普希金啊。俄国口味，最适合你。大海啊，你这自由的元素。”
“Pushkin, of course. The Russian taste suits you best. O sea — thou free element!”

> 荣华听到此处，觉得阿初的确深不可测，他在暗示丛锋来自苏联。什么意思呢？她抬起头来，皓月清盈，回转身去，阿初浅笑回眸，正好与荣华深邃的目光交汇。阿初在风中凝视她片刻，然后，随丛锋步入浓荫底的小径，茫茫尘寰中，阿初身若纤尘，消失在荣华的视线中。
Hearing this, Ronghua thought A-Chu was indeed unfathomable: he was hinting that Cong Feng came from the Soviet Union. What did he mean by it? She raised her head — the bright moon hung clear and full — and turned back around; A-Chu, glancing back with a faint smile, met Ronghua’s deep gaze just so. For a moment A-Chu regarded her in the wind, and then, following Cong Feng, he stepped onto the path beneath the heavy shade, and in the vast dusty world A-Chu, no more than a mote, vanished from Ronghua’s sight.

> 杨慕次在沪中长官公署上班。勤务兵小吴告诉他，中午十二点，有个穿旗袍的女人来找他，说家里出了点事，约他下午两点到“英伦茶室”见面。
Yang Muci was at his post in the Central Shanghai Commandant’s Office. His orderly, Little Wu, told him that at twelve noon a woman in a qipao had come looking for him, saying that something had happened at home, and asking him to meet her at two in the afternoon at the English Tearoom.

> 阿次想了想，心里有些忐忑不安，究竟是谁呢？他第一个想到了丽丽，因为荣华是绝不可能大摇大摆地找上门来。除非，“家”里真出了大事。
A-Ci thought it over, uneasy at heart. Who on earth could it be? The first person he thought of was Lili, for Ronghua could never come swaggering openly to his door — unless something truly serious had happened within the “family.”

> 下午两点过十分，杨慕次来到了“英伦茶室”，茶室布置得古典而华丽，典型的英国风格。柔和的壁灯下，他看见了一张和自己一模一样的脸。
At ten past two in the afternoon Yang Muci arrived at the English Tearoom. The place was appointed in a style at once classical and sumptuous, English to the life. Under the soft wall-lamps he saw a face exactly like his own.

> 他有点难以置信。
He could hardly believe it.

> 不可捉摸。
It baffled all understanding.

> “坐。”阿初说。
“Sit,” said A-Chu.

> 阿次异常诧异。诧异归诧异，坐归坐。
A-Ci was extraordinarily astonished. Astonished he might be, but he sat all the same.

> “你迟到了。”阿初拿起一张《英伦时报》来看。“你有迟到的习惯吗？”
“You’re late.” A-Chu picked up a copy of the English Times and looked at it. “Are you in the habit of being late?”

> “不。”阿次机械地回答。
“No,” A-Ci answered mechanically.

> “不用紧张。”
“No need to be nervous.”

> “没有啊。”慕次定了定神，反应过来了。“是您约我出来得？”
“I’m not.” Muci steadied himself and came to his senses. “It was you who asked me to come?”

> “你以为呢？”阿初一边翻阅报纸，一边说话。“喝点什么？”
“What did you suppose?” A-Chu said, turning the pages of the newspaper as he spoke. “What will you drink?”

> “红茶。”
“Black tea.”

> “Bellboy。”阿初放下报纸，吩咐闻声而来得侍应生。“一小壶咖啡，一杯红茶，再上一盘甜点，点心不要太腻。”
“Bellboy.” A-Chu set down the paper and gave his order to the waiter who came at the call. “A small pot of coffee, a cup of black tea, and a plate of sweets besides — nothing too rich.”

> “好的，先生。”侍应生退下。
“Very good, sir.” The waiter withdrew.

> “初先生？是吧？”慕次微笑地问。
“Mr. Chu? Is that right?” Muci asked with a smile.

> “杨先生，杨慕初。”
“Mr. Yang. Yang Muchu.”

> 慕次的笑容凝固在阿初的话尾。“您喜欢开玩笑。”
Muci’s smile froze at the tail of A-Chu’s words. “You like your little jokes.”

> “我不开玩笑，我为人很古板。”
“I don’t make jokes; I’m a very stiff, old-fashioned sort of man.”

> 阿次从烟盒里掏出一支烟来，递给阿初。“吸烟吗？”
A-Ci drew a cigarette from his case and held it out to A-Chu. “Do you smoke?”

> “我不吸烟。”阿初说。
“I don’t smoke,” said A-Chu.

> 阿次从口袋里掏出打火机，正要点烟，他发现阿初盯着他看，有点不自在，出于尊重对方，阿次礼貌性地征询阿初是否介意他吸烟。“可以吗？”
A-Ci took a lighter from his pocket, and was just about to light up when he found A-Chu staring at him. A little ill at ease, and out of respect for the other man, A-Ci politely inquired whether A-Chu would mind his smoking. “May I?”

> “不可以。”阿初说。
“You may not,” said A-Chu.

> “啊？”阿次以为自己听错了。
“What?” A-Ci thought he had misheard.

> “我说，不可以。”阿初严肃地说。“以后在我的面前，你不可以吸烟。”
“I said, you may not.” A-Chu spoke gravely. “From now on you may not smoke in my presence.”

> “我只是出于礼貌，征询一下你的同意。并不等于你可以替我做决定。”
“I was only being polite in asking your leave. That doesn’t mean you get to make my decisions for me.”

> “无论你处于何种立场，你征询了我的意见，你就应该尊重我的决定。”
“Whatever your standpoint, once you have asked my opinion, you ought to respect my decision.”

> “我跟你素不相识。”
“You and I are perfect strangers.”

> “素不相识，就可以言而无信吗？”
“And because we are strangers, may a man go back on his word?”

> “我没答应你什么啊？”阿次觉得自己很冤，负气地把烟掷在桌上。
“I never promised you anything,” said A-Ci, feeling himself hard done by, and in a fit of pique threw the cigarette down on the table.

> 侍应生过来摆咖啡、红茶、点心，然后，礼貌地请二人享用，退下。
The waiter came and set out the coffee, the black tea, and the sweets, then politely bade the two of them enjoy, and withdrew.

> “您叫我来，有什么事吗？大家开门见山吧。”
“You called me here — is there some matter? Let us come straight to the point.”

> “好啊，我曾经救过你的朋友余先生。你应该知道是吧？”阿初漫不经心地说。
“Very well. I once saved your friend Mr. Yu’s life. You must know that, I take it,” A-Chu said carelessly.

> “余先生？我认识好几位余先生呢，您说的是哪一位？”
“Mr. Yu? I know several Mr. Yus; which one do you mean?”

> “你不记得，也无所谓，你还有位朋友刚从苏联……”
“If you don’t remember, no matter. You have another friend, just back from the Soviet Union—”

> “初先生！”慕次立即打断他的话。
“Mr. Chu!” Muci cut him off at once.

> “我话还没有讲完呢，你这样肆意打断我的话，很没有家教。”
“I hadn’t finished. To cut a man off so wantonly shows very poor breeding.”

> “你！”慕次长吁了一口气，低声问：“你到底要什么？”
“You—!” Muci let out a long breath and asked in a low voice: “What is it you want?”

> “余先生上次忘了付医药费。”
“Mr. Yu forgot to pay his medical bill last time.”

> “明白。明白了。”慕次准备掏钱。“您说，他欠您多少？我付钱。”
“I see. I understand.” Muci made ready to take out his money. “Tell me — how much does he owe you? I’ll pay.”

> “一百万！”
“One million!”

> “一百万？”阿次惊叫起来。
“One million?” A-Ci cried out.

> “怎么？听不懂吗？我想我说的话还算是通俗易懂。”阿初平静地说。
“What of it? Don’t you follow? I should have thought I spoke plainly enough,” A-Chu said calmly.

> 阿次觉得这个人简直不可理喻。“你知道，我一个月薪水是多少钱？”
A-Ci found the man simply beyond all reason. “Do you know what my monthly salary is?”

> “这是你的私人隐私，跟我没有任何关系。”
“That is your own private affair, and no concern of mine.”

> “我觉得你有必要知道，我一个少校副官、一个月的薪水是八十块。”慕次说。
“I think you had better know it: I am a major and an adjutant, and my salary is eighty dollars a month,” said Muci.

> “一百万，这笔钱的数目不算大，尤其是对上海杨家来说，简直九牛一毛。”
“One million — the sum is not a large one, least of all for the Yang family of Shanghai; a mere hair off nine oxen.”

> “初先生，您可能对我的了解还不够。我杨慕次不是一个可以令人随意挟制，而予取予夺的人。”
“Mr. Chu, perhaps you do not know me well enough. I, Yang Muci, am not a man to be coerced at will and squeezed for whatever one pleases.”

> “予取予夺，也是与生俱来得，是父母赐予的恩惠。”
“To be squeezed for whatever one pleases is itself a thing one is born to — a bounty bestowed by one’s parents.”

> “真是笑话。您是叫我一个七尺汉子，去向父母伸手，索要钱财？”
“What a joke. You would have me, a grown man of seven feet, hold out my hand to my parents and beg them for money?”

> “这一点，我们不谋而合。”
“On that point, you and I are of one mind without ever having conferred.”

> 慕次忍无可忍，倏地站起来，冷冰冰地说：“中国人有句老话，叫：自取其辱，不知道初先生听说过没有？”
Muci, past all endurance, stood up abruptly and said icily: “There’s an old Chinese saying — ‘to bring shame upon oneself.’ I wonder whether Mr. Chu has heard it?”

> “中国人还有句老话，叫做：长兄为父，不知道杨先生听说过没有？”阿初不急不缓地说。长兄为父，四个字，令杨慕次在惊愕之余坐下来。
“The Chinese have another old saying,” said A-Chu, neither hurried nor slow, “‘the eldest brother stands as a father.’ I wonder whether Mr. Yang has heard that?” Those four words — the eldest brother stands as a father — for all his amazement, brought Yang Muci back down into his seat.

> “危言耸听。”
“Scaremongering.”

> “不妨看看我们的脸。”
“You might do worse than look at our two faces.”

> “人有相似，物有相同。”
“Men may resemble one another, as things may be alike.”

> “如果，你觉得是我信口开河，干吗还要坐下来？你大可以对我嗤之以鼻，拂袖而去啊？”
“If you think I’m talking through my hat, why did you sit down again? You might well have curled your lip at me and swept off in a huff.”

> “你，你以为你是谁啊？”阿次放肆地冷笑。“你以为你叫杨慕初，就可以在我的面前摆哥哥的谱？我哥哥死了，许多年了。你认为你可以从坟墓里爬出来吗？”
“You — who do you think you are?” A-Ci sneered insolently. “You think that because you call yourself Yang Muchu you can put on an elder brother’s airs before me? My brother died — many years ago. Do you imagine you can crawl up out of the grave?”

> “谁告诉你，你哥哥死了？你父亲？还是你母亲？”阿初问，表情阴恻恻，令阿次很不舒服。“我实话告诉你，我虽然不是从坟墓里爬出来得，却是令尊大人和令堂大人亲自从坟墓里把我挖掘出来得，值得庆幸的是，我埋藏在泥底深渊的尸骨，二十年了，居然没有寒透。我的残肢缝缝补补还可以用，哦，忘了告诉你，我是学医的，这方面很善长。”
“And who told you your brother was dead? Your father? Or your mother?” A-Chu asked, his expression darkly sinister, so that it made A-Ci most uncomfortable. “I’ll tell you the truth: I did not crawl up out of the grave myself — rather it was your honored father and your honored mother who dug me up out of it with their own hands. And the wonder of it is that my bones, buried these twenty years in the deep abyss of the mire, somehow never went cold through. My maimed limbs, stitched and patched, can serve yet. Oh — I forgot to tell you: I studied medicine, and I’m rather good at that sort of thing.”

> “我觉得你应该去看看心理医生。”
“I think you ought to go and see a psychiatrist.”

> “我跟你开个玩笑，吓倒了？”阿初笑起来。“昨天晚上，我呢，遇见一个老友，从国外刚回来，我们聊天聊到天亮。我告诉他，发生在我身上的故事，你猜他怎么样？他也被吓倒了。”
“I make you one little joke, and it frightens you off?” A-Chu laughed. “Last night, as it happens, I met an old friend just back from abroad, and we talked till daybreak. I told him the story of what had befallen me — and guess how he took it? He was frightened off too.”

> “你们聊了一夜，在哪里？”
“You talked the night through — where?”

> “梅花巷。”
“Plum Blossom Lane.”

> 慕次依旧不动声色。“聊什么？”
Muci still gave nothing away. “Talked of what?”

> “聊得多了。譬如，北高加索民族的解放运动，血与火的斗争，为了'被侮辱与损害的'人去夺取政权，纯粹的俄式革命观点。还想听吗？”阿初问阿次，阿次做了一个停止的手势。
“Of a great many things. The liberation movement of the peoples of the North Caucasus, for one; the struggle of blood and fire; the seizing of power for the sake of ‘the insulted and the injured’; pure Russian-style views of revolution. Do you want to hear more?” A-Chu asked A-Ci, and A-Ci made a gesture for him to stop.

> “初先生。”阿次郑重其事地坐直了身。
“Mr. Chu.” A-Ci sat up straight, in deep earnest.

> “叫我杨先生。”
“Call me Mr. Yang.”

> “好吧，杨先生，我知道，您的社会名誉一直都很好。”
“Very well, Mr. Yang. I know that your reputation in society has always stood high.”

> “你错了。我的社会名誉一直都不好。荣家的私生子，来历不明的医学博士，忘恩寡情的小人，放高利贷的伪君子。等等，等等。”
“You’re wrong. My reputation in society has always stood low. The Rong family’s bastard; a doctor of medicine of unknown origins; an ungrateful, cold-hearted cur; a usurer and a hypocrite. And so on, and so forth.”

> “杨先生您曾经是一位医生，医者父母心，您绝对不是一个眼睛里只有钱的人……或许，你有什么特别的原因？”
“Mr. Yang, you were once a physician — and a physician has a parent’s heart. You are surely not a man who has eyes for nothing but money . . . Perhaps there is some special reason of yours?”

> “你用不着替我曲为辩解。”阿初说。“我就是一个见钱眼开的人。”
“You needn’t twist yourself into knots making excuses for me,” said A-Chu. “I am simply a man whose eyes light up at the sight of money.”

> 话又断了。阿次喝茶继续想办法。
The talk broke off again. A-Ci drank his tea and went on casting about for some way through.

> “我们……”
“We—”

> “什么？”阿初问。
“What?” A-Chu asked.

> “大家……”阿次的态度开始妥协。
“The two of us—” A-Ci’s manner began to soften toward compromise.

> “啊？”
“Well?”

> “彼此……”
“Between us—”

> “你想说什么？想说什么，就说。不要吞吞吐吐的。”
“What is it you want to say? If you have something to say, say it. Don’t hum and haw.”

> “我觉得你对我的态度过于霸道。我们大家能不能心平气和的彼此冷静冷静，再好好谈谈。”
“I feel your manner toward me is overbearing in the extreme. Can we not, all of us, cool our tempers and calm ourselves, and then talk it over properly?”

> “你跟我要'民主'？”第一次有人在自己面前要求“民主”，阿初突然内心悲凉起来，自己真的变了。
“You’re asking me for ‘democracy’?” It was the first time anyone had ever demanded “democracy” of him, and all at once a desolation rose in A-Chu’s heart: he had truly changed.

> “你不觉得我在你面前丧失了基本'民权'吗？”阿次觉得眼前这个人，应该有商量的余地。
“Don’t you think I’ve been stripped of my basic ‘civil rights’ in your presence?” A-Ci felt that there ought to be some room for negotiation with the man before him.

> 阿初喝了一口咖啡，说：“我们中国，有五千年的文化。从大汉朝到前清，想鱼跃龙门的举子们，在参加考试的时候，都要写一篇'策论'。大家各说各话，从不交流。上司和下属也没有什么可以平等的对话。我跟你之间的关系，是'利害'关系、'利益'关系。我救了你朋友的命，替你保守秘密。你付出金钱来封我的口，天经地义！所以，我们没必要对话，我们之间如果有对话，那就是'讨价还价'。明明是一件共存获益的好事，我不想变成市场交易。”
A-Chu took a sip of his coffee and said: “We Chinese have five thousand years of culture. From the great Han dynasty down to the late Qing, the candidates who hoped to leap the Dragon Gate had, when they sat their examinations, to write out a ‘policy dissertation.’ Each man spoke his own piece; there was never any exchange. Between superior and subordinate, too, there is no such thing as a dialogue between equals. The relation between you and me is a relation of ‘interest’ — of profit and loss. I saved your friend’s life; I keep your secret for you. You pay out money to seal my mouth: it is the way of heaven and earth! And so there is no need for us to hold a dialogue; if there were any dialogue between us, it would be nothing but ‘haggling over the price.’ This is plainly a good thing, to our mutual benefit and survival — I have no wish to turn it into a transaction in the marketplace.”

> “我之所以想跟你继续谈，是因为我从我朋友口中所知道、所了解的初先生，跟我现在所见所闻的您，差别太大，距离太远。我想你这样做，一定有你的苦衷。大家都是青年人，有困难、有问题，你可以提出来，我们可以互相帮助。何必要用'胁迫'的手段呢？”
“The reason I want to go on talking with you is that the Mr. Chu I came to know and understand from my friend’s lips differs too greatly, stands too far off, from the you I now see and hear. I think there must be some bitter difficulty behind your doing this. We are both young men; if you have hardships, if you have troubles, you can lay them out, and we can help each other. Why must you resort to ‘coercion’?”

> “你从你朋友的口中……知道我？了解我？哪位朋友？荣华吧？”阿初笑起来。“你知道荣华是谁？我是谁吗？荣华是荣家的二小姐，而我是荣家的家奴。一个家奴在小姐面前永远都是和顺的、谦恭的。”
“From your friend’s lips . . . you came to know me? to understand me? Which friend? Ronghua, I suppose?” A-Chu laughed. “Do you know who Ronghua is, and who I am? Ronghua is the Second Miss of the Rong house, and I am the Rong house’s slave. A slave, before the young mistress, is forever mild and forever meek.”

> “水无有不下，人无有不善。”阿次给阿初续咖啡。
“There is no water that does not run downward, and no man who is not good at heart.” A-Ci refilled A-Chu’s coffee.

> “你相信这句话吗？”阿初逼视着阿次的眼睛问。
“Do you believe that saying?” A-Chu asked, staring hard into A-Ci’s eyes.

> “我相信你。”阿次直视着阿初锋芒凌厉的目光答。
“I believe in you,” A-Ci answered, looking straight back into A-Chu’s keen, cutting gaze.

> 阿初“哼”笑了一声。“我是一个可以'不计其功'，但是，不能'不谋其利'的人。我跟你在一起浪费了太多的时间。我希望尽快地看到这笔钱！如果一星期后，我没有拿到钱，我就到上海警备司令部侦缉处去向你的顶头上司要钱！！”
A-Chu gave a snorting laugh. “I am a man who can ‘reckon nothing of the merit,’ but who cannot ‘take no thought for the gain.’ I have wasted far too much time in your company. I want to see this money as soon as may be! If a week from now I have not received it, I’ll go to the Detective Division of the Shanghai Garrison Command and ask your immediate superior for it!”

> “你敢！”
“You wouldn’t dare!”

> “我敢！”
“I would dare!”

> “你不怕有命挣没命花。”
“Aren’t you afraid you’ll have the life to earn it and no life to spend it?”

> “这句话说得好极了。有点意思了。你知道吗？从头到尾，也就只有这句话提醒我，我和你是介于一种相互利用的关系。我差一点就被你伪装起来得君子情怀所迷惑，在过去，这是对付我的杀手锏。现在，不同了。”阿初站了起来，对咖啡馆的侍者说：“结账。”回头对阿次说：“你付钱。”不待阿次回答，阿初已经走到门边，他从容地笑看阿次，说：“今天的谈话只是一场敲诈勒索的预演，精彩的好戏还在后面。”
“Now that is very well said. That has something to it. Do you know — from first to last, it is only that one sentence that has reminded me you and I stand in a relation of mutual use. I very nearly let myself be taken in by that gentleman’s sentiment you dress yourself up in; once, that was the trump card played against me. Now, it is otherwise.” A-Chu stood up and said to the café waiter: “The bill.” Then, turning back to A-Ci: “You pay.” Without waiting for A-Ci to answer, A-Chu had already reached the door; unhurried, he looked back at A-Ci with a smile and said: “Today’s talk was only a rehearsal for a piece of blackmail and extortion; the real show, the good one, is still to come.”

> “你为什么要这样做？”阿次被他激得心里冒火。
“Why are you doing this?” A-Ci, goaded by him, felt fire rise in his breast.

> “为了杨家。”阿初严肃地说。
“For the Yang family,” A-Chu said gravely.

> 为了杨家？
For the Yang family?

> “提防你的父亲和母亲。”
“Be on your guard against your father and your mother.”

> “你叫我提防自己最亲的亲人。”
“You would have me guard against my own nearest and dearest.”

> “你没有亲人了。除了我。”阿初说完，甩手出门。玻璃弹簧门荡起来，荡得慕次心乱如麻。
“You have no kin left. Except me.” With that A-Chu flung out through the door. The glass spring-door swung to and fro, and its swinging left Muci’s heart in a tangled snarl.

> 夜九点钟。
*Nine o’clock at night.*

> 自鸣钟叮叮当当地响个不停。客厅里开着悬吊的莲花灯，流光轻盈软美，阿初刚洗完澡，他穿了件宽松的猩红色睡袍，头发很湿润，他站在客厅的小柜前煮咖啡。
The chiming clock rang out ding-dong without a stop. In the sitting room a hanging lotus lamp was lit, its flowing light airy and soft and lovely; A-Chu, fresh from his bath, wore a loose scarlet dressing gown, his hair still wet, and stood before a little cabinet in the sitting room making coffee.

> 他喜欢闻咖啡豆沸腾起来得醇美香味，每当他感觉自己很疲倦，负荷过重的时候，他就通过这种方式舒缓情绪。
He loved to breathe the mellow fragrance of the coffee beans as they came to the boil; whenever he felt worn out and overburdened, this was the way he eased his feelings.

> 岳嬷嬷走进来，问：“先生，您饿了吗？我去给您煮消夜。”
Amah A-Yue came in and asked: “Sir, are you hungry? I’ll go and make you a bit of supper.”

> “不用了。”阿初说。“岳嬷嬷，您过来坐吧，您喝咖啡吗？”
“There’s no need,” said A-Chu. “Amah A-Yue, come and sit down. Will you have some coffee?”

> 岳嬷嬷笑着说：“我不喝那洋玩意，喝了，睡不着觉。”她的脸因为曾经烧伤的缘故，笑起来很可怖。
Amah A-Yue said with a smile: “I’ll not drink that foreign stuff — it keeps me from sleeping.” Because her face had once been burned, her smile was a fearful thing to see.

> 阿初贴着她的身子坐下，他从岳嬷嬷的眉眼中看出来她的忧伤和劳累，她从前的容貌一定是不差的。
A-Chu sat down close beside her; in Amah A-Yue’s brows and eyes he read her grief and her weariness. Her looks in former days must have been far from plain.

> “荣儿最近怎么样？”
“How has Rong’er been lately?”

> “少爷啊，他每天都读书，学看哪些西洋画。哪些洋人的画很不雅，他们的神仙有的不穿衣服。我都叫少爷不要看了。都是您给少爷请的那位家教汤先生，满口的艺术、宗教的胡诌。还有啊，以前少爷很规矩的，现在经常去舞场、赌场……”
“The young master, sir — he reads every day, and studies those Western paintings. Some of those foreigners’ paintings are most indecent; some of their gods and immortals wear no clothes at all. I’ve told the young master again and again not to look at them. It’s all that tutor, Mr. Tang, you engaged for him, with his mouthful of nonsense about art and religion. And another thing — the young master used to be so well behaved, and now he’s forever off to the dance halls and the gambling dens . . .”

> “他去赌场，输多还是赢多？”阿初问。
“When he goes to the gambling dens, does he lose more, or win more?” A-Chu asked.

> “这倒不清楚，好像不输不赢。”
“That I couldn’t say — it seems he neither loses nor wins.”

> 阿初无奈地摇摇头。
A-Chu shook his head helplessly.

> “怎么了？”岳嬷嬷紧张起来。
“What is it?” Amah A-Yue grew anxious.

> “没事，没事。”阿初正说话间，荣初回来了。
“It’s nothing, nothing.” Even as A-Chu spoke, Rong Chu came home.

> “说什么呢？”荣初笑着走进来。他穿着黑色的燕尾服，黑领结，打扮的非常漂亮。他亲昵地弯下腰去和岳嬷嬷打招呼：“嬷嬷晚上好。”然后他直起腰，对阿初说：“晚上好，舅舅。很抱歉，打断你们的谈话了。冒昧地问一句，你们的谈话跟我有关吗？”
“What are you talking about?” Rong Chu came in smiling. He wore a black tailcoat and a black bow tie, dressed up very handsomely. He bent down affectionately to greet Amah A-Yue: “Good evening, Amah.” Then he straightened up and said to A-Chu: “Good evening, Uncle. I’m very sorry to have broken in on your talk. If I may make so bold as to ask — does your talk have anything to do with me?”

> 阿初用手一指荣初，肯定地说：“顺风耳。”
A-Chu pointed a finger at Rong Chu and said with conviction: “Ears-that-hear-on-the-wind.”

> “你吃饭了吗？”岳嬷嬷问。
“Have you eaten?” Amah A-Yue asked.

> “吃了一点点。”荣初说。“您知道吗，那些贵族小姐交朋友的条件很苛刻，为了保持端庄的仪态，只有牺牲掉我的胃。”
“A little,” said Rong Chu. “Do you know, those aristocratic young ladies set the harshest terms on making friends; to keep up a dignified bearing, one has no choice but to sacrifice one’s stomach.”

> “我去给你做消夜。”岳嬷嬷好像找到用武之地般欢喜起来。
“I’ll go and make you some supper.” Amah A-Yue brightened, as though she had found a field for her talents at last.

> “谢谢嬷嬷。”荣初说。
“Thank you, Amah,” said Rong Chu.

> 岳嬷嬷出去了。
Amah A-Yue went out.

> “最近怎么样？”阿初问。
“How have things been lately?” A-Chu asked.

> “很无聊。”荣初陪阿初坐在小柜边的齐腰凳上，解开领结。“汤少的这一套生活方式，根本就不适合我。又枯燥，又没意义。”
“Very dull.” Rong Chu sat down with A-Chu on the waist-high stool by the little cabinet and undid his bow tie. “This whole way of living of Young Tang’s simply doesn’t suit me. It’s dreary, and it has no meaning.”

> “吃喝玩乐也会闷吗？”阿初倒咖啡。问他。“你喝吗？”
“Can eating and drinking and making merry be tedious too?” A-Chu poured the coffee and asked him: “Will you have some?”

> “不，太苦了。”
“No, it’s too bitter.”

> “嫌苦，我给你加点奶。”阿初打开玻璃酒柜，拿了一个空瓷杯出来。“前两天你好像有话要跟我说。”他倒咖啡，加奶。
“If you find it bitter, I’ll add a little milk.” A-Chu opened the glass liquor cabinet and took out an empty porcelain cup. “A day or two ago you seemed to have something to tell me.” He poured the coffee and added the milk.

> “没，没有。”荣初接过杯子称谢。“我想为您做事，舅舅。”他说，神态很自负，也很诚恳。“我不想这样灯红酒绿的荒废下去。”
“No — nothing.” Rong Chu took the cup with thanks. “I want to work for you, Uncle.” He spoke with a manner at once proud and earnest. “I don’t want to go on wasting myself away like this, amid the red lanterns and green wine.”

> “你想为我做事，首先，你就要先学会做自己。我要你做的事，就是你必须在三个月内学会做自己，做荣家的小少爷。你要弄明白一个道理，你不是在伪装自己，你本身就属于这个阶级。”
“If you want to work for me, the first thing is that you must learn to be yourself. The task I set you is just this: within three months you must learn to be yourself — to be the young master of the Rong house. You must get one thing clear: you are not disguising yourself; you belong, in your very being, to this class.”

> “我很累。”
“I’m very tired.”

> “我知道。我们有明确的目的，为达到这个目的，我将不择手段。”阿初说。“你知道吗？你到现在为止，仍然没有进入状态。你没有。我要你学会在酒会上高谈阔论，谈得云山雾罩，吹得天花乱坠。你呢，总是蛰蛰蝎蝎的，没有激情。我要你，习惯豪华赌场挥金如土的气氛，我要你，让人知道你一天输了三十万也不心疼，几十万的输赢对你来讲，是常事。可是你做不到。我要你学习贵族礼仪，学会做一个甘受女人气的男人，你依旧……依旧是不能胜任，我在你身上找不到一点点兰台公子的风流情韵，哪怕是唐璜式的采花气息。”荣初想解释，阿初食指和中指并拢轻摇：“所有这些简明易晓的事情，你都疲于应付。荣儿，大战在即，你是我手中最后出的一张底牌，我需要你在关键时刻，做出对敌的致命一击！你不要让我失望。”
“I know. We have a clear purpose, and to reach it I will stop at nothing,” said A-Chu. “Do you know — up to now you still haven’t got into your part. You haven’t. I want you to learn to hold forth grandly at a cocktail party, to talk till the hills are lost in cloud and mist, to boast till the very flowers rain down from heaven. But you — you’re always so timid and fidgety, without any fire. I want you to grow used to the air of a grand casino where money is flung about like dirt; I want you to let it be known that you dropped three hundred thousand in a single day without turning a hair — that a win or a loss of a few hundred thousand is an everyday thing to you. And this you cannot do. I want you to study aristocratic manners, to learn to be a man who will suffer a woman’s tempers gladly; and still you . . . still you are not equal to it. I can find in you not the least trace of the dashing charm of a young gallant of the Orchid Terrace, not even so much as a Don Juan’s whiff of flower-plucking.” Rong Chu made to explain; A-Chu pressed his forefinger and middle finger together and waved them gently. “All these plain and easy things wear you out to keep up with. Rong’er, the great battle is upon us; you are the last card in my hand, my hole card, and I need you, at the crucial moment, to strike the enemy one deadly blow! Do not disappoint me.”

> 荣初手中的咖啡杯摇晃了一下，少许的咖啡水汁渐起，他掏出一条手绢揩拭，手绢很惹眼，绣的兰草，颜色幽蓝。
The coffee cup in Rong Chu’s hand gave a shake, and a little of the coffee slopped up; he drew out a handkerchief to wipe it. The handkerchief caught the eye — embroidered with orchids, in a deep, secret blue.

> “你有女人了？”阿初冷不防地问一句。
“You’ve got a woman?” A-Chu asked, out of nowhere.

> 荣初条件反射般地说：“没有。”
As if by reflex Rong Chu said: “No.”

> “那就是有了。”
“Then you have.”

> 荣初的反应过敏，恰好证明了阿初的判断。“我希望你现在暂时放弃情缘，一心一意的为我做事。我不想看见一张简陋的床在浪漫的瞬间压垮我精心构建的大厦模型。你明白吗？”
Rong Chu’s over-quick reaction proved A-Chu’s guess exactly. “I want you, for the present, to give up affairs of the heart, and to work for me with a single mind. I have no wish to see some shabby little bed, in one romantic instant, come crashing down through the grand edifice I have so carefully built to model. Do you understand?”

> “明白。”
“I understand.”

> “好。等这件事情结束以后，我会补偿你的。我会给你一个好的环境、干净的环境，让你过上一种安静、富足、平庸的生活。”
“Good. When this business is over, I’ll make it up to you. I’ll give you a good setting, a clean setting, and let you live a quiet, well-off, ordinary life.”

> “谢谢舅舅。”
“Thank you, Uncle.”

> “少爷，来吃消夜。”岳嬷嬷在门外说。
“Young master, come and have your supper,” Amah A-Yue said from beyond the door.

> 荣初应声：“就来了，嬷嬷。”
Rong Chu answered: “Coming, Amah.”

> “去吧。”阿初说，荣初正待转身，阿初又叫住他，替他整了整黑色领结，摁住他的双肩，意味深长地说：“学会骄傲！”
“Go on,” said A-Chu. Rong Chu was just about to turn away when A-Chu called him back, straightened the black bow tie for him, pressed down on both his shoulders, and said, with deep meaning: “Learn to be proud!”

> 窗外淅淅沥沥地下起了小雨，桌球室里灯光幽黄，绿色球桌边上，杨慕次和父亲“杨羽柏”正在专心致志地对局。
Outside the window a fine rain had begun to patter down. In the billiard room the light was a dim amber, and at the edge of the green table Yang Muci and his father, “Yang Yubo,” were bent in deep concentration over their game.

> “我们父子已经很久没有在一起打球了。”杨羽桦温和地说。
“It’s been a long while since we father and son played a game together,” Yang Yuhua said gently.

> “是啊，有五年了，五年没在一起。”杨慕次击了一下红球，然后击蓝球，紧接着再击红球，最后击粉红球时又落了空，他负气地把球杆掷在台球桌上。
“Yes — it’s been five years; five years apart.” Yang Muci struck a red ball, then the blue, and hard upon that another red, but when at last he went for the pink he missed, and in a fit of temper he flung the cue down on the billiard table.

> “你的注意力一直不集中。”杨羽桦俯身眯眼，仔细地注视着桌上嚣张的红球，他击了一下红球，把粉红球排列起来，一杆击中。
“Your attention keeps wandering.” Yang Yuhua bent low and narrowed his eyes, watching closely the insolent red ball on the table; he struck the red, lined up the pink, and with one stroke potted it.

> “宝刀不老。”慕次赞了一句。
“The old blade has lost none of its edge,” Muci said in praise.

> 杨羽桦对着桌子弯下腰，继续攻击。
Yang Yuhua bent over the table and pressed on with his attack.

> “你这么晚了回家来，不单单是陪我打球的吧？”杨羽桦又中一杆。
“Coming home so late as this — it isn’t only to play billiards with me, surely?” Yang Yuhua potted another.

> “我……我想跟父亲借点钱。”虽然话很生硬，不过，慕次还是硬着头皮说了。
“I—I’d like to borrow some money from you, Father.” Stiff though the words were, Muci steeled himself and said them.

> 杨羽桦还在专心打球。问：“要多少？”
Yang Yuhua was still intent on his game. He asked: “How much?”

> “我只是跟您借……”
“I only want to borrow—”

> 杨羽桦问：“多少？”
Yang Yuhua asked: “How much?”

> “一百万。”慕次把头转过去，看窗外。
“One million.” Muci turned his head aside and looked out of the window.

> “啪”的一声，粉红球滚到一边，杨羽桦这一次没有击中。
Crack — the pink ball rolled off to one side; this time Yang Yuhua had missed.

> 杨羽桦放下球杆，走到白色的壁柜边，打开密码柜，拿出支票本来，掏出钢笔签名。当慕次轻轻转过脸时，一张一百万的兑现支票已经递到了他的面前。
Yang Yuhua set down his cue, walked to the white wall-cabinet, opened a combination safe, took out a checkbook, drew out his pen, and signed. When Muci gently turned his face back, a cashable check for one million was already held out before him.

> 慕次有些意外。他没有想到父亲出手如此爽快。
Muci was somewhat taken aback. He had not expected his father to be so open-handed.

> “您不问我为什么？”
“You don’t ask me why?”

> “你长这么大了，第一次正式开口问我要钱，做父亲的没有理由拒绝你。儿子。其实，我等待这一天已经很久了。”
“You’ve grown to be a man, and this is the first time you have ever formally opened your mouth to ask me for money; a father has no reason to refuse you. My son. The truth is, I have waited a very long time for this day.”

> “为什么？”
“Why?”

> “因为我爱你，儿子。”
“Because I love you, my son.”

> 慕次感动。“不过，爸爸，我一向节俭朴素，作风低调。这一次突然狮子大开口，您不觉得我的生活里出现了某种问题吗？”
Muci was moved. “But, Papa — I have always been frugal and plain, and kept a low profile. This time I’ve suddenly asked for the earth; doesn’t it strike you that some trouble may have arisen in my life?”

> “傻儿子，只要是能够拿钱解决的问题，就一定不是问题。”
“Silly boy — any trouble that money can settle is no trouble at all.”

> “很精辟。”慕次说。
“Very shrewdly put,” said Muci.

> “你呢，多用些心思在事业上。一个男人什么都可以没有，但是不能没有事业。没有事业的人，他们做人没有目标，盲目的生活，本身就是可悲的。你呢，从政也好、从军也好、从商也好，爸爸都不干预，随你的兴趣去做。重要的是，无论你做什么，你都要做到最好。因为，你是我的儿子，杨家的唯一的儿子。我希望在有生之年，看到你平安、幸福、快乐地生活，娶妻生子，继承我们杨家的香火。”杨羽桦言犹未尽，慕次却已深感父爱绵绵。“对不起，爸爸。”慕次深怀歉意地说。“许多年来，我都自以为您很讨厌我，您很早就送我去了寄宿学校，就是节假日我也很少看到您的身影，您让我养成了孤僻、冷静、独立的习惯，最初，我不否认地说，我对您充满了畏惧和恨意。”
“As for you, put more of your mind on your career. A man may go without anything else, but he cannot go without a calling. Men who have no calling have no aim in their lives; and to live blindly is a pitiable thing in itself. Whether you go into politics, or the army, or into trade, your papa will not interfere — follow your own bent. What matters is that, whatever you do, you do it to the utmost; for you are my son, the only son of the house of Yang. I hope, while I still have life in me, to see you live in safety and happiness and joy, to take a wife and father children, and to carry on the incense of our house of Yang.” Yang Yuhua had not yet said all he meant to say, but already Muci felt the unbroken tenderness of a father’s love. “I’m sorry, Papa,” Muci said, full of contrition. “For many years I believed that you disliked me. You sent me away to boarding school when I was very young, and even on holidays I seldom saw so much as your shadow; you made me grow up solitary, cool-headed, and self-reliant. At first — I won’t deny it — I was full of fear of you, and of hatred.”

> “后来呢？”
“And afterward?”

> “后来，您为了我能读名校，四处奔波。为了我能出国留学，您花费了大量的金钱，让我顺利地读预科，在没有任何升学压力的情况下，获得了优异的成绩。”
“Afterward — you ran about everywhere so that I might attend the best schools. For me to study abroad you spent great sums of money, saw me smoothly through the preparatory course, and, with no pressure of examinations upon me, I came away with distinguished results.”

> “我为你感到骄傲，儿子。”杨羽桦大发感慨。“我记得你少年时，在学校里极不驯服，不肯依附老师与学长，你喜欢斗争，你一直在斗争，就像一匹脱缰野马，我总以为你会因此而断送前程，感谢老天，没有毁掉你。你是个非常优秀的人，孩子。不像你妹妹，整天只知道吃喝玩乐，长夜就是她的舞台，夜店就是她的天堂。这个时候，正是她狂欢买醉的时候，她挥霍无度，不懂得珍惜人生。当然，她自己很快乐。她快乐，我就开心。同样，你成功，爸爸也会感到很幸福。”
“I’m proud of you, my son.” Yang Yuhua gave vent to a rush of feeling. “I remember that as a boy you were utterly unruly at school, unwilling to attach yourself to teachers or older students; you loved to fight, you were forever fighting, like a wild horse that had slipped its halter. I always thought you would ruin your prospects by it — thank Heaven, it did not destroy you. You are a most excellent man, my child. Not like your sister, who knows nothing all day but eating, drinking, and pleasure; the long night is her stage, the nightclub her paradise. Just now is exactly her hour for reveling and drinking herself drunk; she squanders without measure, and does not know how to cherish life. But she is happy in her own way, of course. When she is happy, I am glad. And in the same way, when you succeed, your papa will feel most blessed.”

> “这些年来，我在外面风里雨里火里水里磨炼，我学会了感恩，爸爸。请您原谅我过去对您种种排斥、疏远、不理智的行为。”
“In these years, tempered out in the wind and the rain, the fire and the water, I have learned to be grateful, Papa. Please forgive me my past rejections of you, my coldness, my unreasoning behavior.”

> “如果我早知道一百万可以买回我儿子的心里话，我说什么，也不会等到你今天向我开口，我就是硬塞强给，也要你收下这笔钱。”
“Had I known sooner that one million could buy back my son’s true heart, I’d never, whatever else, have waited for you to open your mouth to me today — I would have pressed it upon you, forced it into your hands, and made you take the money.”

> “我会还您的，爸爸。”
“I’ll repay you, Papa.”

> “傻孩子，我的钱最终还不都是你的钱。”杨羽桦爽朗地笑起来。
“Silly child — is not all my money your money in the end?” Yang Yuhua laughed heartily.

> 慕次心中释然，拿起球杆，说：“胜负未分呢，再来。”
Muci felt eased at heart; he took up his cue and said: “The game’s not decided yet — again.”

> “怎么，刚才你故意放水啊？”
“What’s this — were you throwing the game just now, on purpose?”

> “我想让爸爸高兴，一渠流水两家分嘛。”
“I wanted to make you happy, Papa — one channel of water, shared between two households.”

> “怎么，你跟我不是一家人啊？”
“How’s that — aren’t you and I of one household?”

> 慕次和父亲玩到夜里十二点半，父子俩都倦了，才去睡。他们互道晚安，在楼下分手。慕次的房间在二楼的右走廊后侧，他平常很少回家，他的房间每天都有女佣清洁，所以很干净。
Muci and his father played until half past twelve at night, and only when both were tired did they go to bed. They bade each other good night and parted downstairs. Muci’s room was at the rear of the right-hand corridor on the second floor; he rarely came home as a rule, and a maidservant cleaned his room every day, so that it was very tidy.

> 他打开灯，脱了外套。
He switched on the light and took off his coat.

> 他在灯下反复地看着那张“一百万”的支票，支票上浮现出阿初的模样，脑海里又想起了那句令自己胆寒的一句话。“你没有亲人了。除了我……你没有亲人了。除了我……你没有亲人了。除了我……”他用力敲了一下自己的头。
Under the lamp he looked again and again at that check for “one million,” and on its face there rose up the image of A-Chu, and into his mind came once more that sentence that had chilled him to the bone. “You have no kin left. Except me . . . You have no kin left. Except me . . . You have no kin left. Except me . . .” He struck his own head hard.

> 外面仍在下雨。
Outside it was still raining.

> 他走到阳台上，深吸了一口气，伸开四肢，活动活动，在湿润的空气中洗涤自己的心肺。不经意地一抬头，他发现有一个黑色的影子在草地上移动。
He walked out onto the balcony, drew a deep breath, stretched his limbs and worked them loose, and washed out his heart and lungs in the moist air. Chancing to raise his head, he saw a black shadow moving across the grass.

> 他敏感地感觉到情况的异常。
Keenly he sensed that something was amiss.

> 深更半夜，有谁会在雨地里徘徊？
In the dead of night, who would be pacing about in the rain?

> 他穿起衣服，蹑手蹑脚地出了门，下了楼。他很快地来到草坪上，他仔细观察了一下四周，确认黑影的方向，然后向花园走去。
He put on his clothes, slipped out the door on tiptoe, and went downstairs. Quickly he came out onto the lawn, looked carefully all about him, made sure of the shadow’s direction, and then set off toward the garden.

> 花园里很幽暗，一株株梨花树两边分开。
The garden was deep in gloom, the pear trees standing parted to either side, one by one.

> 慕次清晰地听到了电波声，尽管声音很微弱，很细微，但是，职业的敏锐迫使他在瞬间做出了最专业的判断。
Muci clearly heard the sound of radio waves; faint and slight as it was, his professional sharpness forced him in an instant to the most professional of judgments.

> 自己的家里隐藏着电台，隐藏意味着有不可告人的秘密，自己家里有专业的谍报人员。他离声音越来越近了，他的心跳声吞吸着近在咫尺的电波声，慕次第一次对自己的判断感到紧张，前所未有的紧张。潜藏在大脑第三度空间的危险的信号，正式激活了。
A radio transmitter lay hidden in his own home — and to hide it meant there was a secret that could not be told; there was a trained intelligence agent in his own house. Closer and closer he drew to the sound; the beating of his heart swallowed up the radio signal now but an arm’s length away. For the first time Muci felt a tension at his own judgment, a tension he had never known before. The danger signal that lay buried in the third dimension of his brain had been formally switched on.

> 他在冒险。
He was taking a risk.

> 突然，电波声消逝了。
Suddenly the sound of the radio waves died away.

> 一阵冷风袭来。吹过梨花树的枝枝蔓蔓，层层细叶因为冷风的偷袭而发出“沙沙”的细微声响。风和叶的摩擦和着慕次的皮鞋“簌簌”声，让人感觉到寒从脚上起，冷由心底生。慕次感觉自己不像是这所庭院的主人，而是一个中途的闯入者。
A gust of cold wind came sweeping in. It blew through the tangled boughs and runners of the pear trees, and layer upon layer of fine leaves, ambushed by the cold wind, gave off a faint rustling sough. The friction of wind and leaf, keeping time with the soft crunch of Muci’s leather shoes, made one feel a chill rise up from the feet and a cold well up from the bottom of the heart. Muci felt himself no master of this courtyard, but an intruder come halfway in.

> 风停了，花园里很安静，安静往往伴随着危机。
The wind dropped; the garden was very still, and stillness is often the companion of crisis.

> 一只光滑的手臂像蛇一样蜿蜒攀升，手影爬到了阿次的背上，冰凉的指尖马上就要触到他的颈……
A smooth arm, snakelike, came winding and climbing up; the shadow of a hand crept onto A-Ci’s back, and its ice-cold fingertips were on the very point of touching his neck . . .
