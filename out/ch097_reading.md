### The Set Meal

Nowadays, apart from the aristocratic great restaurants, every eating-house large and small sells a thing called the "set meal," the price of which differs from house to house, running from two *mao* up to five or six; the fare is two dishes and a soup, and of rice there is no limit, the diner eating till he is full. Since the set meal came into vogue, the general run of men who buy their dinners have flocked to it, and the Tianjin houses, the Sichuan houses, the Anhui houses, the local houses and the rest all sell it now; a man of small appetite cannot even finish it clean. And if three friends share two portions between them, the dishes are combined into one for them, with only a single serving of plain rice added at the end—there is no cheaper way of eating than this most economical of methods.

The institution of the set meal was first devised, so it is said, by a few Muslim eating-houses outside the Old North Gate (they were opened by followers of Islam), and had been selling for many years; they fixed the price at three *mao* a serving, reckoned in small silver. That the eating-houses of every regional guild now sell set meals is very likely borrowed from the Muslim houses' way of doing things.
