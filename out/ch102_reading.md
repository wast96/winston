### Room and Board at 280 Cash a Day

Fifty years ago, the largest inns of Shanghai (in those days all called "guest-lodges") charged each guest for room and board only two hundred and eighty cash of standard coin a day; the next sort, where the guest found his own food, charged only a hundred cash for the room, or eighty; and the meanest little inns needed only forty cash a day, or twenty-eight. At such rates one may see how low the cost of living then was.

Nowadays a day's room at a great hotel costs the higher sort ten-odd dollars up to several tens, and the lower sort several dollars, while even the meanest little inn needs two or three *mao* a day per guest. Grant that the hotels of today are grandly built and fully appointed—still the price of a room has risen more than tenfold, nay, a hundredfold and more.

Two hundred and eighty cash of standard coin, converted at today's silver price, come to less than a *mao*—not enough, to say nothing of the room, even to cover the tip to the tea-boy. Set past against present, and the two are as far apart as heaven and earth!
