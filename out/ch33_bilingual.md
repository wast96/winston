## H2 Chapter 33. When the False Is Made True, the True Turns False as Well

> 杨思桐瞪大了眼睛，站在疯狂的父亲面前，不，不如说她此刻正站在母亲的尸体旁边，她惊恐万状，由于通宵达旦地荒淫娱乐生活，她那张缺乏精神的脸，顿时变成死灰色。
Yang Sitong stared wide-eyed, standing before her deranged father; no, better to say she was standing at this moment beside her mother's corpse. In utter terror, and from a whole night of dissolute pleasures, her spiritless face turned all at once the color of dead ashes.

> 她惨厉地尖叫，止不住地尖叫。
She screamed hideously, screamed without stopping.

> “思桐，你听我说。”杨羽桦完全不知所措，女儿，是他生命中唯一牵挂的亲人，是他致命的弱点。
"Sitong, listen to me." Yang Yuhua was utterly at a loss; his daughter was the one kin he cared for in all his life, his fatal weak point.

> 他不能失去女儿的爱。
He could not lose his daughter's love.

> “思桐！”
"Sitong!"

> “不要过来！不要过来！”杨思桐发了疯似的朝楼上跑去，她一边跑，一边拼命喊叫，杨羽桦听不清女儿嘴里在喊什么，但是，他知道女儿的大脑里，此时此刻，应该是一片空白。自己没办法跟她解释，就算跟她解释，女儿也未必能听得进去，她毕竟亲眼看到父亲正在处理母亲的尸体。女儿能不能原谅自己，已经不再重要了，重要的是自己必须面对“流亡生涯”了。
"Don't come over! Don't come over!" Yang Sitong ran madly for the stairs, and as she ran she screamed with all her might. Yang Yuhua could not make out what his daughter was crying, but he knew that in his daughter's mind, at this moment, there must be a total blank. He had no way to explain to her; and even if he explained, his daughter would not necessarily take it in, for she had after all seen with her own eyes her father disposing of her mother's corpse. Whether his daughter could forgive him no longer mattered; what mattered was that he must now face a "life of exile."

> 他长叹了一口气……
He heaved a long sigh...

> 突然，杨羽桦的心绷紧了，因为他清晰地听到了杨家庭院里开进了大卡车的声音。他预感事情不妙了，有人要“赶狗入穷巷”。
Suddenly Yang Yuhua's heart drew taut, for he heard clearly the sound of a large truck driving into the Yang family courtyard. He had a foreboding that things had gone wrong: someone meant to "drive the dog into the blind alley."

> 杨羽桦急忙从客厅窗帘看过去，看大门的佣人正和一个女军官说话、交涉，一大队侦缉处的人马已经从卡车上跳下来，长驱直入了。
Yang Yuhua hurried to look through the drawing-room curtains: the servant who watched the gate was talking, parleying, with a woman officer, and a great troop of Detective Division men had already leapt down from the truck and were driving straight in.

> 他没有看到杨慕次的身影。
He did not see Yang Muci among them.

> 客厅里一片猩红，院子里一片嘈杂。
In the drawing room all was scarlet; in the courtyard all was clamor.

> 来不及打扫了。
There was no time to clean up.

> 如果此刻不逃，现场活捉，杨羽桦将以杀妻的罪名入罪。
If he did not flee now, and were taken alive at the scene, Yang Yuhua would be charged with the crime of murdering his wife.

> 仅此一项，足以致死。
On that count alone, it was enough to bring him to death.

> 杨羽桦跑了。
Yang Yuhua fled.

> 他从后花园一个狭小的窄门仓皇地逃了。
He fled in a panic through a narrow little door in the back garden.

> 华美书店里很安静，因为客人稀少的缘故，打理生意的荣归无精打采地双手勾着肩无聊地站在柜上，一缕缕阳光从窗外洒进来，照的冰凉的厚木书架暖洋洋的，只不过由于店主人的形像很颓废，连累的整个书店都很灰色，书架上的图书也显得极不精神，好像满书室弥漫的不是淡淡的墨香，而是陈旧的书本受了潮所发出的郁闷气味。
The Huamei Bookstore was very quiet, for want of customers; Rong Gui, minding the business, stood listlessly at the counter with both hands hooked over his shoulders in boredom. Threads of sunlight spilled in from outside the window, warming the cold, thick wooden shelves; only, because the shopkeeper's own image was so dispirited, the whole bookshop was drab and grey along with him, and even the books on the shelves looked utterly lifeless, as though what filled the room were not the faint fragrance of ink but the stale, stuffy smell of old books gone damp.

> 一个面貌清雅的女学生，手里拎着一个蓝色绣花书包，站在书架旁痴痴地看书，她已经看了很久很久，没有要买的意思，也没有要走的迹像，她一直静静地、悠悠地站在那里看书，荣归不时抬头瞄一瞄，女子有时也在书架上换着翻书。
A student girl of refined, elegant looks, carrying a blue embroidered book-bag in her hand, stood beside the shelves absorbed in a book; she had been reading a very, very long time, with no sign of wanting to buy, and no sign of leaving. She went on standing there quietly, unhurriedly, reading; Rong Gui glanced up now and then to steal a look, and now and then the girl too would change the book she was leafing through on the shelf.

> “您需要我帮忙吗？”荣归实在是忍不住了，想借此干预一下这个白看书的女孩子，哪怕她自己觉得不好意思，掏腰包好歹买一本呢。
"Is there something I can help you with?" Rong Gui really could not hold back; he wanted by this means to interfere a little with this girl reading for free, so that, even if she felt embarrassed, she might dig into her purse and buy at least one book.

> “谢谢。”女学生很稳重地把手上的书放下，荣归看了看，那本略为卷曲的书皮，书名是：《爱丽丝漫游奇遇记》英文版。
"Thank you." The student girl set down the book in her hand very composedly. Rong Gui looked; the slightly curled cover bore the title: Alice's Adventures in Wonderland, the English edition.

> “小姐喜欢这本书？”荣归说。
"You like this book, miss?" said Rong Gui.

> “看看而已。这本书多少钱？”
"Only looking. How much is this book?"

> “一元五角。”荣归说。“您喜欢，不如买下来。”
"One yuan five," said Rong Gui. "If you like it, why not buy it?"

> “可是，你，你这本书上的插页很模糊，你看。”女子用手摩挲着书页，很不舍的样子。
"But you, your book here has very blurred plates. Look." The girl stroked the pages with her hand, with a most reluctant air.

> “像这种手工蚀刻铜板上压印出来得书籍，并不是每一本都清晰可读得。”一个稳重而沉闷的声音飘了过来，荣升不知何时走了进来，他声调不高，很慢、很温和，算是替荣归解释。
"With books printed from hand-etched copper plates, not every copy comes out clear and legible." A steady, muffled voice came drifting over; Rong Sheng had come in at some point unnoticed. His tone was not loud, very slow, very gentle, by way of explaining for Rong Gui.

> “可是，可是我的钱不够。”
"But, but I haven't enough money."

> “哦，你差多少？”荣升问。
"Oh, how much are you short?" Rong Sheng asked.

> “我身上只有五角钱。”女学生低下头。
"I've only got five jiao on me." The student girl bowed her head.

> 荣升笑笑，对荣归说：“替小姐包起来吧，我替她买了。”他从口袋里掏出二块钱来，正准备递给荣归，谁知那女学生满脸通红，摆手说：“我不要了。”竟慌慌张张放下书本，逃也似地向外走。由于她走的过于急促，不提防在门口撞到一个新进门的客人。只听“哎哟”一声，荣升向门口望去，蓝色绣花书包翻了个身，跳进门，可知女学生跌得不轻。
Rong Sheng smiled and said to Rong Gui, "Wrap it up for the young lady; I'll buy it for her." He drew two dollars from his pocket and was about to hand them to Rong Gui, when the student girl, her whole face flushing red, waved her hands and said, "I don't want it any more." And she actually set the book down in a fluster and made for the door as if in flight. Because she went too hurriedly, she ran, off her guard, into a customer just stepping in at the door. There came only an "Aiyo"; Rong Sheng looked toward the door: the blue embroidered book-bag flipped over and jumped in through the door, so that the student girl must have taken no light fall.

> 荣升和荣归都不约而同地向书店门口走去，荣升一抬头，他竟然怔住了，眼前有一个衣着华丽的少妇，正皱着眉头，扶着左手胳膊，大约被撞在左胳膊上了，她淡淡地嗔怪那女学生走路如此不小心，那女学生说声：对不起，一溜烟地跑了。
Rong Sheng and Rong Gui, as if by one accord, both walked toward the bookshop door; Rong Sheng lifted his head, and was actually struck dumb. Before his eyes was a richly dressed young married woman, frowning, holding her left arm; she had probably been struck on the left arm. She mildly reproached the girl for walking so carelessly; the girl said "Sorry," and dashed off in a wisp of smoke.

> 那少妇不是别人，正是和雅淑。
That young woman was none other than He Yashu.

> 旧情人当面相遇，四目环顾，雅淑的气血霎时不流畅了，显得异常尴尬，荣升很大度，他主动上前打招呼。
Old lovers meeting face to face, their four eyes taking each other in; Yashu's blood and breath at once ran less than smoothly, and she looked exceedingly awkward. Rong Sheng was very magnanimous; he came forward of his own accord to greet her.

> “很久不见。”
"Long time no see."

> “是。”雅淑很局促，很不自然。
"Yes." Yashu was very ill at ease, very unnatural.

> 看穿着打扮，一定是钓到“金龟婿”了，荣升心里想。
Judging by her dress and adornment, she had surely hooked a "golden-turtle son-in-law," Rong Sheng thought.

> “来买书啊？看我能不能帮你。”荣升说。
"Come to buy books? Let me see if I can help you," said Rong Sheng.

> “谢谢，我替我家先生来买几本书。”雅淑平静地说。
"Thank you; I've come to buy a few books for my husband," Yashu said calmly.

> “您要买什么书？这边请。”荣归很高兴地引领客人入店。
"What books would you like? This way, please." Rong Gui, delighted, led the customer into the shop.

> 荣升看着雅淑进店，依旧雍容华贵，气质脱俗，不仅没有了当日的装模作样，还平添了几许妩媚。
Rong Sheng watched Yashu go in, still stately and splendid, refined in bearing; not only had she lost the affectation of former days, she had gained besides a few added touches of allure.

> 他低头看见了被遗忘在门槛上的蓝色绣花书包，他把书包捡起来，书包里散发出新鲜花朵的泥土清香，他很诧异。
He bowed his head and saw the blue embroidered book-bag left forgotten on the threshold. He picked it up; from the bag came the fresh, earthy fragrance of flowers, which surprised him.

> 荣升把书包打开，里面有一个笔记本，丝质封面，上面用钢笔写着：“明轩”两个字，笔记本里滑落出一张名片：陈氏温室花房，订购鲜花，代送花篮。
Rong Sheng opened the bag; inside was a notebook with a silk cover, on which two characters were written in pen: "Mingxuan." From within the notebook slipped a name-card: Chen's Greenhouse Flower-House; fresh flowers to order, flower baskets delivered on your behalf.

> 他听见书店里荣归讨好客人的笑声，随手把名片揣到口袋里，复又走进门去，看雅淑正娴雅地端坐在椅子上，荣归跑前跑后地照书单取书，忙得不亦乐乎。
He heard within the shop Rong Gui's ingratiating laughter as he flattered the customer, and casually tucked the name-card into his pocket. He went back in through the door and saw Yashu sitting demurely and gracefully on a chair, while Rong Gui ran back and forth fetching books by her list, busy and happy as could be.

> 荣升心里很不自在，他想，男人真是一个奇怪的动物，明明是自己主动放弃的“饰品”，为什么，这“饰品”一旦别在了其他男人身上，自己仿佛就是有一口气舒不下喉咙，连胸口也感觉有些堵塞，鼻尖上隐隐冒出酸气来。
Rong Sheng felt very ill at ease within. He thought: man really is a strange animal. This was an "ornament" he himself had chosen to give up; why was it that, the moment this "ornament" was pinned onto another man, it seemed there was a breath he could not let down his throat, and even his chest felt somewhat choked, and a faint sourness stole to the tip of his nose?

> “《文史通义》、《一七六九年游记》、《柏拉图精神哲学》、《社会改良各面观》、《欧洲木刻版画册》，哦，还有一本新到的《西学博览》，您看看，是不是齐全？还差一本罗素的《算理哲学》，我把书名记下了，改天有了，替您留着。”荣归赔着笑，很热情。
"Wenshi Tongyi, Travels of the Year 1769, Plato's Philosophy of the Spirit, Views on the Several Aspects of Social Reform, An Album of European Woodcuts; oh, and a newly arrived Broad Survey of Western Learning. Have a look; is it complete? There's still one short, Russell's Philosophy of Mathematics; I've noted the title down, and when we have it another day, I'll keep it for you." Rong Gui, all smiles, was very warm.

> “谢谢。上次梁启超先生那本……”
"Thank you. That book of Mr. Liang Qichao's last time..."

> “您说那本《文野三界之别》吧？最近缺货，这样吧，您可以先付一块钱的预付额，等书一到，我连《算理哲学》一起亲自送到府上去。”
"You mean On the Distinction of the Three Realms of Civilized and Barbarous? It's out of stock lately. Let's do this: you can pay one dollar in advance, and when the book comes in, I'll deliver it to your home in person, together with the Philosophy of Mathematics."

> “好吧，我给你留个地址和电话。”雅淑站起来。
"All right; I'll leave you an address and a phone number." Yashu stood up.

> 荣归急忙把纸和笔送上，雅淑把薄薄的信笺掂在手心上写，不得力，荣归立即拿了本书给她掂着写。
Rong Gui hurriedly brought over paper and pen; Yashu balanced the thin sheet of letter-paper on her palm to write, with no firm surface, and Rong Gui at once took a book for her to write against.

> 写完地址和电话号码，雅淑把信笺递给荣归，赫然发现手上这本书是《乐府》，她翻开扉页，上面引用了一段“铙歌十八曲?上邪”，她微微一颤，心里做了一个决定。
When she had written the address and telephone number, Yashu handed the letter-paper to Rong Gui, and noticed with a start that the book in her hand was the Yuefu. She opened the flyleaf; on it was quoted a passage from "The Eighteen Nao Songs: Shang Ye." She gave a slight tremble, and made a decision in her heart.

> “麻烦你，把这本书也包起来，今天下午五点钟以前，送到长乐街18号，我加倍付你钱。”雅淑说。
"Might I trouble you to wrap up this book as well; before five o'clock this afternoon, deliver it to No. 18 Changle Street, and I'll pay you double," Yashu said.

> “谢谢。我一定准时送到。”荣归满口答应。
"Thank you. I'll be sure to deliver it on time." Rong Gui agreed readily.

> 荣升心里很狐疑，他自认还是比较了解雅淑的喜好，那些书，并不是雅淑爱读得，倒像是……他脑海里隐隐浮现出阿初阳光般灿烂笑容。
Rong Sheng was very much puzzled at heart; he reckoned he understood Yashu's tastes fairly well, and those books were not ones Yashu loved to read; rather they looked like... and into his mind there rose faintly A-Chu's sunlike, radiant smile.

> 他的眼睛有意地下放到那张薄薄的书单上，熟悉而流畅的笔迹几乎以招摇而炫耀的姿态闯入眼帘，准确无误地告知了自己，雅淑的男人，就是阿初。
His eyes dropped, of set purpose, to that thin book-list; the familiar, fluent handwriting burst into his sight almost in a posture of flaunting display, and told him with unerring accuracy that Yashu's man was none other than A-Chu.

> 不可思议。
Unbelievable.

> 一时间，咸酸冷暖涌上心头，几乎碾压不住荣升内心的波澜。他的脸色很难看，就像刚刚吞食了一块脆冷坚硬的薄冰，凉凉的、滑滑的，说不出的滋味。
For a moment salt and sour, cold and warmth surged into his heart; he could scarcely crush down the waves within him. His face was very ugly, as though he had just swallowed a slab of crisp, cold, hard thin ice, chill and slippery, an indescribable taste.

> 这时，荣归似乎察觉到大哥心里的不快，他以为是自己招呼客人，而慢待了这位神仙，赶紧跑过来致歉。“大哥，您上次要的《楚辞校补》和《诗经通义》，我已经包好了，您要不要带上？还是晚上我给您专程送过去？”
Just then Rong Gui seemed to sense the displeasure in his elder brother's heart; he supposed it was his own fault for attending to the customer and so slighting this deity, and hurried over to apologize. "Elder brother, the Supplement to the Collation of the Songs of Chu and the Comprehensive Meaning of the Book of Songs you asked for last time; I've wrapped them up. Will you take them with you? Or shall I make a special trip to bring them over to you this evening?"

> “带上吧，你也够累的，不用两边跑。”荣升淡淡地说了句体恤话，荣归很高兴地答应了。
"Take them with me; you've had enough to tire you, no need to run back and forth." Rong Sheng said a word of sympathy mildly, and Rong Gui agreed with pleasure.

> 雅淑买完了书，从书店里出来，招手叫了辆黄包车。
Yashu, her books bought, came out of the bookshop and hailed a rickshaw with a wave.

> 荣升几乎是同雅淑一道出来得，他看见了阿初。愈发困惑不解了。
Rong Sheng came out almost together with Yashu; he saw A-Chu, and grew all the more baffled and unable to understand.

> 因为阿初穿了一身笔挺的德式军装，坐在一辆军用吉普上。阿初大约也同一时间看见了荣升，他微微一怔，随即像陌路人一样收回目光，从容地发动吉普，不紧不慢地跟着雅淑的黄包车，从荣大少爷的眼皮低下开过去。
For A-Chu wore a trim German-style military uniform, seated in a military jeep. A-Chu had probably seen Rong Sheng at the same moment; he gave a slight start, then, like a stranger, drew back his gaze, calmly started the jeep, and, unhurried, followed Yashu's rickshaw, driving past under the very eyes of the young Master Rong.

> 不致一句问候，不多看旧东家一眼，甚至连车都懒得下，大摇大摆穿梭而去。荣升只觉得手脚冰凉，他认为自己被故意轻慢了。
Not so much as a word of greeting, not a second glance at his old employer, too lazy even to get out of the car; grandly, brazenly, he threaded away. Rong Sheng felt his hands and feet go cold; he reckoned he had been slighted on purpose.

> “小人得志。”他说。
"The petty man triumphant," he said.

> “大哥。”荣归是赶着送他出来得，他也看见了阿初，虽然有些疑惑，但是并没什么感触。“是不是认错人了？”
"Elder brother." Rong Gui had hurried out to see him off; he too had seen A-Chu, and though a little puzzled, felt nothing much by it. "Have you mistaken him for someone else?"

> “你认为，我会认错他吗？”荣升冷笑。
"Do you think I would mistake him?" Rong Sheng sneered.

> “可是他开的是军用车。”
"But he was driving a military car."

> 荣升不说话了，倒不是他对自己的判断犹疑，而是，他奇怪，为什么雅淑和阿初会一前一后的离开？
Rong Sheng said no more; not that he wavered in his own judgment, but that he wondered why Yashu and A-Chu should leave one close behind the other.

> 不太正常。
It was not quite normal.

> 一阵急促的电话铃声响起。
A sharp, urgent telephone bell rang out.

> 一只白皙的手拿起了电话。
A pale, fair hand picked up the telephone.

> “喂？”
"Hello?"

> “鱼咬钩了。”
"The fish has taken the hook."

> “什么鱼？”
"What fish?"

> “鲨鱼。”
"A shark."

> “比目鱼呢？”
"And the flounder?"

> “在证券交易所。”
"At the Stock Exchange."

> “继续监视。”
"Keep up the surveillance."

> 电话挂断了。
The phone was hung up.

> 客厅里自鸣钟响了，时针指向三点半……
In the drawing room the chiming clock sounded; the hour-hand pointed to three thirty...

> 与此同时，一辆黑色的汽车缓缓驶进一座幽静的宅院，长乐街18号，也就是最早韩正齐给阿初找的房子，离市区有一段距离，好处是没有喧嚣，没有通宵达旦营业的酒楼、宾馆。阿初有段日子没过来了。
At the same moment, a black motorcar rolled slowly into a secluded, quiet residence, No. 18 Changle Street; that is, the house Han Zhengqi had found for A-Chu at the very beginning, some way from the city center. Its virtue was that there was no hubbub, no wine-houses or hotels doing business the night through. A-Chu had not been over for some days.

> 自从他搬到梅花巷后，他也就断断续续地过来喝过几次岳嬷嬷炖的汤，每次都是岳嬷嬷打电话叫他去的，不过，今天特别，阿初是一大清早就亲自打电话过来，说是想喝岳嬷嬷熬的粥，下午趁空闲回家。
Ever since he moved to Plum Blossom Lane, he had come over only now and then, a few times, to drink the soup Amah A-Yue stewed, each time because Amah A-Yue had telephoned to call him. Today, though, was special: A-Chu had telephoned in person early in the morning, saying he wanted to drink the congee Amah A-Yue boiled, and would come home in the afternoon when he had a free moment.

Amah A-Yue was delighted; she bought a great deal of provisions: lotus seeds, longan, lily bulb, jujubes heaped the stove-top. She was busy in the kitchen all the while; a fragrant, steaming pot of lotus-seed-and-longan congee, perfect in color, scent, and flavor.

"The master's home." Amah A-Yue, hearing his footsteps, welcomed him into the drawing room with a pleasant, smiling face.

Liu A-Si was helping A-Chu off with his fur coat. A-Chu seemed extraordinarily tired, yawning, his manner listless and weary. Yet his hair looked freshly combed, very neat, with drops of water still clinging to the strands.

"Master, you've washed your hair?" Amah A-Yue asked.

> “啊。”阿初应声，慢慢转过身来。“最近也不知怎么搞的，睡眠特别好，无论往哪里一靠，都会糊里糊涂地睡过去。刚才发困，干脆洗个头，算是自己给自己提提神。”
"Ah." A-Chu answered, and slowly turned around. "Lately I don't know how it is; I've been sleeping especially well. Wherever I lean I fall muddledly asleep. Just now I felt drowsy, so I simply washed my hair, by way of freshening myself up a bit."

On his feet he wore a pair of cloth shoes, made by Amah A-Yue's own hands, with a snowy river-plum scene embroidered on the uppers; countrified they might be, but they were also quite out of the common. Only, today the ice-and-snow plum blossoms on A-Chu's shoe-uppers seemed a little swollen.

> “先生，您的脚？”岳嬷嬷关心地俯下身去看。
"Master, your foot?" Amah A-Yue bent down with concern to look.

"It's nothing, nothing." A-Chu was a little embarrassed. "Didn't I say I felt drowsy? I took a fall at the door of the Stock Exchange."

> “厉害吗？”
"Is it bad?"

> “没事，没大碍。”
"It's nothing, no great harm."

> “先生，我扶你进去坐吧。”刘阿四说。
"Master, let me help you in to sit down," said Liu A-Si.

> “不用了，有岳嬷嬷在呢，你去吧，我有事再叫你。”阿初很自然地把手伸过来，搭在岳嬷嬷肩膀上。
"No need; Amah A-Yue's here. Off you go; I'll call you if I need anything." Quite naturally A-Chu put out his hand and laid it on Amah A-Yue's shoulder.

> 岳嬷嬷忙伸手扶助阿初的腰，服侍他坐下。刘阿四转身出去，顺手带上门。
Amah A-Yue hastily reached out to support A-Chu by the waist, and served him to a seat. Liu A-Si turned and went out, pulling the door to behind him as he went.

> “岳嬷嬷。”阿初说。“您今年有多大年纪了？”
"Amah A-Yue," said A-Chu. "How old are you this year?"

> “我是小姐的乳娘，大太太的陪嫁丫鬟，今年啊，六十二岁了。”
"I was the young mistress's wet-nurse, a dowry-maid who came with the First Madam. This year, I'm sixty-two."

> “六十二了？您辛苦一辈子了，没想过回老家养老吗？”
"Sixty-two? You've toiled a whole lifetime. Have you never thought of going back to your old home to spend your last years?"

> 岳嬷嬷笑起来。“我是家生子，哪里有亲人，故里？”
Amah A-Yue laughed. "I'm a house-born bondservant; where would I have kin, or a native place?"

> “对不起，我的错。”阿初似乎想站起来，又困顿地陷在沙发里。他很烦躁，自己压了压情绪，咳嗽了几声，说：“岳嬷嬷，我想喝点粥。麻烦你。”
"Forgive me; my mistake." A-Chu seemed to want to stand, but sank drowsily back into the sofa. He was much on edge; he pressed his feelings down, coughed a few times, and said, "Amah A-Yue, I'd like a little congee. If you please."

> “好的，先生，您等等。”岳嬷嬷脚步轻捷地去了。
"Of course, Master; wait a moment." Amah A-Yue went off with quick, light steps.

> 阿初的头往沙发上一仰，客厅顶上悬吊的莲花灯，毫不吝啬地将柔和的流光投射在阿初脸上，阿初在享受梦幻般的光影时，嘴角浮现出诡异的微笑。
A-Chu tipped his head back on the sofa; the lotus-flower lamp hanging from the drawing-room ceiling, unstinting, cast its soft, flowing light over A-Chu's face. As A-Chu enjoyed the dreamlike play of light and shadow, an uncanny smile surfaced at the corner of his mouth.

> 岳嬷嬷端着精心熬制的“莲子龙眼粥”走到阿初面前，阿初还在闭目养神。
Amah A-Yue came before A-Chu bearing the carefully stewed "lotus-seed-and-longan congee"; A-Chu was still resting with his eyes closed.

> “先生，喝粥吧。”岳嬷嬷说。
"Master, drink your congee," said Amah A-Yue.

> “谢谢。”阿初睁开蒙胧的双眼，看见热气腾腾的粥碗，他伸双手接过去。
"Thank you." A-Chu opened his hazy eyes, saw the steaming bowl of congee, and took it in both hands.

> 岳嬷嬷在他的身边坐下。
Amah A-Yue sat down beside him.

> “先生，你很累吗？”
"Master, are you very tired?"

> “是啊。”阿初一边喝粥，一边回答。
"Yes." A-Chu answered even as he drank the congee.

> 莲子龙眼粥的热气升腾成雾状的白烟，阿初觉得有些恍惚。从恍惚中好像看见一块发亮的木符，距离自己大约不到10厘米，他很奇怪，木符怎么会有如此奇妙的光泽呢？他控制不住自己去凝视那个在眼前晃来晃去的发亮物体，发亮的木符像一块铁磁石，而阿初像一粒铁屑，他被牢牢吸住了。他感觉周围安静极了，眼睛一团模糊，思想一片空白。
The steam of the lotus-seed-and-longan congee rose in a mist of white; A-Chu felt somewhat in a daze. Out of the daze he seemed to see a gleaming wooden talisman, less than ten centimetres or so from him. He wondered greatly: how could a wooden talisman have so marvelous a luster? He could not stop himself from gazing at that gleaming object swaying to and fro before his eyes; the gleaming talisman was like a lodestone, and A-Chu like a speck of iron filings, held fast to it. He felt all around him utterly still, his eyes one blur, his mind a total blank.

> “先生，这里没有打扰你的东西……除了我说话的声音和时钟的滴答声，你什么也听不见……是吗？先生？”
"Master, there is nothing here to disturb you... apart from the sound of my voice and the ticking of the clock, you can hear nothing... isn't that so, Master?"

> “是。”阿初答。
"Yes," A-Chu answered.

> “您感觉到什么了吗？”
"Do you feel anything?"

> “很暖和。”
"Very warm."

> “您指的是身体上的感觉吗？”
"Do you mean a bodily feeling?"

> “是。”
"Yes."

> 答话的人处于静止状态，问话的人处于引导状态，神秘的空气渗透到客厅里每一个角落，一块平凡的木符诡异的传递信息。仿佛形成第三种空间，无边无际，虚无缥缈。
The one answering was in a state of stillness, the one questioning in a state of guidance; a mysterious air seeped into every corner of the drawing room, and a commonplace wooden talisman uncannily conveyed its message. It was as though a third kind of space had formed, boundless and without end, void and unreal.

> “阿初”被人“成功”的催眠了。
"A-Chu" had been "successfully" hypnotized.

> 因为不是第一次催眠成功，所以催眠的人有十足的把握和耐心。
Because it was not the first successful hypnosis, the one doing the hypnotizing had full confidence and patience.

"Do you need me?" A warm woman's hand took hold of A-Chu's hand.

> “我需要帮助。”阿初的手软绵绵的，没有力气。
"I need help." A-Chu's hand was soft and limp, without strength.

> “我会给你帮助。”女人说。“你夜里还经常做噩梦吗？”
"I will give you help," the woman said. "Do you still often have nightmares at night?"

"Yes, very frightful dreams. The sound of a spade, the sound of water, they will not be dispelled; I want to seize hold of it, and in an instant it is gone."

> “也许是前世的梦。”
"Perhaps they are dreams of a former life."

> “天空很低。水在流淌，不停地流。”
"The sky is very low. Water is flowing, flowing without stop."

> “看得清水的颜色吗？”
"Can you see the color of the water clearly?"

> “很脏，像墨渍，乌贼的汁掉进烂橘子的筒里，混浊，液体很稠。”
"Very dirty, like ink-stains, like cuttlefish ink dropped into a bucket of rotten oranges, turbid, the liquid very thick."

> “有风吗？”
"Is there wind?"

> “风很大。铁锹声没有停过，声音频率很快。”
"The wind is very strong. The sound of the spade has never stopped; its rhythm is very quick."

Even if the shards of memory drift before your eyes, it can only mean that the evil is still spreading; it will, without your knowing, call up the pain left lingering in your body. Wipe it out, wipe out the shards of memory in your brain, sweep them clean with all your strength."

> “怎么清除？”
"How do I sweep them clean?"

> “忘记它，你跟着我来，你会忘记一切烦恼，你会知道另外一些秘密……”
"Forget it. Follow me, and you will forget all your troubles; you will come to know certain other secrets..."

> “秘密？”
"Secrets?"

> “是的，你要提防你身边的人，他们每一个人都在暗地里算计你，你的弟弟，很危险，你要设法远离他，尽管远离亲情对你来说很痛苦。你要信任你身边的女人。”
"Yes. You must be on your guard against the people at your side; every one of them is secretly scheming against you. Your younger brother is very dangerous; you must find a way to keep far from him, though to keep far from kinship is very painful for you. You must trust the woman at your side."

> “所有的？”
"All of them?"

> “当然，女人会使你远离罪恶。”
"Of course; a woman will keep you far from evil."

> “徐玉真？假母亲？”
"Xu Yuzhen? The false mother?"

> “她当然一定要死，不要放过她。她曾经腰斩过你的亲身母亲，那具骸骨，记得吗？”
"She, of course, most certainly must die; do not let her go. She once cut your own birth mother in two at the waist; that skeleton, do you remember?"

> “记得。”
"I remember."

> “那具骸骨很年轻。”
"That skeleton was very young."

> “是吗？当时，觉得不对劲。”
"Was it? At the time, I felt something was not right."

> “你只要记着那具骸骨是你的母亲，就够了。”
"You need only remember that the skeleton is your mother; that is enough."

> “好。”阿初越来越机械。
"All right." A-Chu grew more and more mechanical.

"Forget the nightmare; the sound of the spade you speak of does not exist at all. It is fictitious, an illusion."

> “是幻觉。”
"It is an illusion."

> “你要借机在上海滩掀起腥风血雨，杀一批人。”
"You are to seize the chance to raise a bloody storm over Shanghai, and kill a batch of people."

> “什么人？”
"What people?"

> “当年害过你的人，害过你父亲的人，你要心狠手辣，将来，你会有很好地前程。”
"Those who wronged you in that year, who wronged your father; you must be ruthless and merciless. In future, you will have a fine prospect before you."

> “我不想再杀人。”
"I don't want to kill any more."

> “慢慢来，你会习惯的。我会慢慢引导你，你要绝对信任我，每当你握住我的手的时候，你会感觉到很温暖。”
"Slowly; you will get used to it. I will guide you slowly. You must trust me absolutely. Each time you take hold of my hand, you will feel very warm."

> “是。”
"Yes."

> “我的手温暖吗？”
"Is my hand warm?"

> “不止是温暖，我感觉肤质很细腻。”阿初的声音突然变得很奇怪。“岳嬷嬷，你的真实年龄只有四十多岁吧？看来我说准了。”
"More than warm; I feel the skin is very fine." A-Chu's voice turned suddenly very strange. "Amah A-Yue, your true age is only a little over forty, isn't it? It seems I've guessed right."

> 岳嬷嬷张着大嘴，一阵笑，一阵貌如憨厚地傻笑，丑陋的眼睑因笑声而凹陷。
Amah A-Yue opened her great mouth in a burst of laughter, a spell of seemingly honest, foolish laughter; her ugly eyelids sank in with the sound of it.

> “先生，你？你醒了？刚才你做梦了。”岳嬷嬷的手依旧镇定自若地握着阿初的手。
"Master, you, you're awake? You were just dreaming." Amah A-Yue's hand still held A-Chu's hand as calmly and steadily as ever.

> “行了。”阿初站起来，目光如电。“你真够本事的，应该不是第一次，你催眠的功夫不错，什么时候教教我？”
"That's enough." A-Chu stood up, his gaze like lightning. "You're a real hand at this; it can't be the first time. Your hypnotism is skillful. When will you teach me?"

> 岳嬷嬷的脸色黑起来。
Amah A-Yue's face went black.

> “站着别动！”岳嬷嬷掏出了手枪。
"Stand still, don't move!" Amah A-Yue whipped out a pistol.

A-Chu stood bolt upright without stirring a hair. "Amah A-Yue, if you kill me, won't the twenty-odd years of your suffering have been endured for nothing?"

"Don't forget, I still hold one counterweight in my hand: Rong Chu. He can perfectly well replace you, and cover me."

"Rong'er?" A-Chu laughed wantonly. "Do you think he has the ability? Better to go on working with me. I believe you'll certainly attain your original aim. You want the false 'Xu Yuzhen' dead so badly; you must hate her to the bone. You, you'd be Japanese, wouldn't you? You live in Edo, don't you?"

> “你是怎么知道的？”岳嬷嬷的脸应紧张而扭曲的更加厉害。
"How did you know?" Amah A-Yue's face twisted the more fiercely with tension.

> “江梅雪景，很像富士山顶的风光。”阿初突然开口说日语了。“富士山顶雪飘飘，此景五分属江户。”他指了指鞋面花样。
"A snowy river-plum scene is very like the view from the summit of Mount Fuji." A-Chu suddenly began to speak Japanese. "Snow drifts and drifts on Fuji's peak; half of this scene belongs to Edo." He pointed at the pattern on the shoe-uppers.

"You, who are you?"

> “多此一问。”
"A needless question."

"You're not Yang Muchu?!" Amah A-Yue's voice was shrill and piercing.

"Were you looking for me?" The drawing-room door opened; Yang Muchu, in a trim military uniform, stood in the doorway.

Amah A-Yue, in fright, had barely turned her head when Yang Muci, disguised as A-Chu, struck swiftly; with practised movements he knocked the gun from her hand and subdued Amah A-Yue to the floor.

"You? How did you find out? How did you find out?!" Pinned to the floor, Amah A-Yue screamed with all her might, most unwilling to yield.

"Calm down, calm down." Muci cuffed her with the utmost speed. "Keep a little professional style." He picked the pistol up off the ground and flung Amah A-Yue hard onto the sofa.

> “你怎么样？”阿初走进来问。
"How are you?" A-Chu came in and asked.

> “你的鞋小了一码，不合我穿，我的脚疼死了。”慕次跳着脚说。
"Your shoes are a size too small; they don't fit me. My feet are killing me." Muci said, hopping on one foot.

> 阿初矜持地笑笑。
A-Chu gave a reserved smile.

> “你演得不错。”阿初说。
"You played it well," A-Chu said.

> “谢谢。”杨慕次的枪依旧指着岳嬷嬷。说：“中文不错，很流利。”
"Thank you." Yang Muci's gun still pointed at Amah A-Yue. He said, "Your Chinese is good, very fluent."

"No wonder, no wonder..." Amah A-Yue struggled to speak. "No wonder you had to bathe."

> “他身上有烟味。”阿初淡淡地解释。“你知道，我不吸烟。”
"He had the smell of tobacco on him," A-Chu explained mildly. "You know I don't smoke."

> “你们？你们是怎么知道的？怎么知道的？”岳嬷嬷苟延残喘地反复问着同一句话。
"You? How did you find out? How did you find out?" Amah A-Yue, at her last gasp, kept asking the same question over and over.

In your eagerness for merit you made a fatal mistake: you kept feeding me medicinal meals to hypnotize me, and you forgot that I am a doctor, an excellent doctor." A-Chu said.
