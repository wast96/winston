## H2 Chapter 15. Only in the End Do You Know How High the Source

> 灯光幽暗，同济医院的太平间里清冷而宁静。死去的人安详地躺着，像熟睡的婴儿。这是往生者在人世间的最后一个驿站。四太太、荣荣、小护士她们将在此处洗净红尘中的风雨尘沙，听着感伤离乱的悲歌，踏进另一个世界的门槛。
The lamplight was dim; in the mortuary of the Tongji Hospital it was cold and still. The dead lay serene, like infants deep in sleep. This was the last posting-station of the departed in the world of men. Here the Fourth Madam, Rongrong, the young nurse — here they would wash away the wind and rain and dust of the red world of mortals, and, to the mournful song of sorrow and parting, step across the threshold into another world.

> 阿初不知道到底有没有天堂和地狱？另一个世界到底存在不存在？他都不去想了。他只想在凌晨前补给她们一个完整的身体、美丽的容颜。她们毕竟都是女人，哪一个女人不爱美丽和尊严？
A-Chu did not know whether, in the end, there was a heaven and a hell. Whether another world existed at all — he no longer let himself think of it. He wished only, before the dawn came, to give them back a whole body, a beautiful face. They were women, after all, and what woman does not love beauty and dignity?

> 已经半夜三点了，阿初仍然无声地站在冷却了的尸体面前工作。他一针一针地缝制着她们的残肢。浩荡的忧愁，一寸一寸地挤到阿初的肺腑深衷；血浸的苍凉，一点一点地腐蚀了阿初烈性男儿铁铸的钢肠。
It was already three in the dead of night, and A-Chu still stood in silence, working before the cooled bodies. Stitch by stitch he sewed their broken limbs together. A vast tide of grief pressed, inch by inch, into the very depths of A-Chu’s breast; a blood-soaked desolation gnawed, bit by bit, at the iron-cast, steel-forged bowels of this fierce and manly man.

> 阿初痛心疾首。
A-Chu was sick at heart, his very head aching with grief.

> 夏跃春、韩禹、汤少礼在停尸房的门口陪着阿初。
Xia Yuechun, Han Yu, and Tang Shaoli kept A-Chu company at the door of the mortuary.

> 夏跃春和韩禹是在事发之后，第一时间赶来得现场，他们原想帮着阿初一起动手的，但是，阿初不肯。他们走也不是，留也不是，干脆在门口坐一宿。汤少受不了这罪，躺在长凳上，头枕着夏跃春的腿，睡得死沉沉的，嘴角不时流着口涎，弄得夏跃春的前膝的西裤上湿漉漉的。
Xia Yuechun and Han Yu had rushed to the scene the moment the thing happened. They had meant, at first, to lend A-Chu their hands in the work, but A-Chu would not have it. To go seemed wrong and to stay seemed wrong, so they simply sat out the night at the door. Young Tang could not bear the ordeal; he lay on a long bench, his head pillowed on Xia Yuechun’s leg, sleeping like the dead, drool now and then trickling from the corner of his mouth, until the knee of Xia Yuechun’s Western trousers was soaked through.

> 韩禹抽着烟，一根接一根，来回踱着步。
Han Yu smoked, one cigarette after another, pacing back and forth.

> 大约凌晨五点钟，疲惫的阿初走了出来。
At about five in the small hours, the exhausted A-Chu came out.

> “你怎么样？”韩禹问。
“How are you holding up?” Han Yu asked.

> 阿初惨然一笑。“漏网之鱼，不知道是不是应该开香槟庆贺重生呢？”说着，他看见了疲倦的夏跃春和沉睡的汤少。阿初迅即脱了上身的西装，折叠了成枕头状，轻轻地把汤少的头移到“西服”枕上，解放了夏跃春。
A-Chu smiled wretchedly. “A fish that slipped the net — I wonder whether I ought to open champagne and toast my rebirth.” As he spoke, he caught sight of the weary Xia Yuechun and the fast-asleep Young Tang. At once A-Chu stripped off his jacket, folded it into the shape of a pillow, and gently moved Young Tang’s head onto that “pillow” of Western dress, setting Xia Yuechun free.

> 夏跃春站起来，差点栽下去，腿麻了。自己使劲揉了揉腿。
Xia Yuechun stood up and nearly pitched over — his leg had gone numb. He rubbed hard at it.

> “我就怕他醒了，要吸。”夏跃春对阿初说。
“I was only afraid he’d wake and want a smoke,” Xia Yuechun said to A-Chu.

> “我们出去说吧。”阿初领头走出阴森森的停尸房甬道。乍一出来，看见晨曦微吐的鱼白色天空，阿初心生寒意，如果，昨天雅淑不投河，那么，今天自己就和这朗朗青天永诀了。
“Let’s go outside to talk.” A-Chu led the way out of the grim, ghostly passage of the mortuary. Stepping out all at once and seeing the fish-belly white of the sky where the first light of dawn faintly showed, A-Chu felt a chill rise in him: had Yashu not thrown herself into the river yesterday, then today he too would have taken his eternal leave of this bright, clear blue heaven.

> “有烟吗？”阿初问。
“Have you a cigarette?” A-Chu asked.

> 韩禹二话不说，立马将烟递了过去。
Without a word Han Yu handed one over at once.

> 阿初嘴衔着香烟，韩禹把打火机凑过去，阿初点燃烟。他刚吸了一口，呛得咳嗽了一声，接着再吸，再咳。
A-Chu held the cigarette between his lips; Han Yu brought the lighter close, and A-Chu lit it. He had barely drawn one breath when it caught in his throat and set him coughing; he drew again, and coughed again.

> “行不行啊？”韩禹担心地说：“不行，别逞能。这玩意不是什么好东西，当不了灵丹妙药。”
“Can you manage it?” Han Yu said, worried. “If you can’t, don’t force it. This thing is no good to anyone; it won’t serve as some miracle elixir.”

> “你知道是谁干的吗？”夏跃春问。
“Do you know who did it?” Xia Yuechun asked.

> “知道又怎么样？”阿初继续咳嗽。
“And what if I do?” A-Chu went on coughing.

> “杀人偿命！”韩禹说。
“A life for a life!” said Han Yu.

> “他们一定会偿命的！不过，不是现在。”阿初说。
“They will pay with their lives, that is certain! Only — not now,” said A-Chu.

> “什么意思？”夏跃春疑惑起来。“你不会蠢到自己去解决吧？”
“What do you mean?” Xia Yuechun grew puzzled. “You’re not fool enough to settle it yourself, surely?”

> “你怕他们有后台是不是？”韩禹拍了拍自己的胸脯，说：“不是我吹！在上海滩谁敢不给我家老爷子三分薄面？”
“You’re afraid they have backers, is that it?” Han Yu patted his own chest and said: “I’m not boasting — but on the Shanghai Bund, who would dare not show my old man a little face?”

> “韩禹的父亲是上海警察局的副局长韩正齐。”夏跃春补充了一句。“你的事，他一定会帮忙的。”
“Han Yu’s father is Han Zhengqi, the deputy chief of the Shanghai Police Bureau,” Xia Yuechun put in. “In a matter of yours, he would surely help.”

> 阿初猛烈地咳嗽起来，烟吞到咽喉里，灼逼的眼泪直流，呛到无法说话。
A-Chu fell into a violent fit of coughing; the smoke had gone down into his throat, and scalding tears ran from his eyes as he choked past all speaking.

> “慢点，慢点。”夏跃春替他拍着背。“抽什么烟啊。”他顺势把阿初手上的烟抢过来，丢在地上，猛踩了一脚。
“Easy, easy.” Xia Yuechun patted him on the back. “What do you want with a cigarette?” And with that he snatched the cigarette from A-Chu’s hand, threw it to the ground, and stamped hard on it.

> 韩正齐？当这个名字灌输到阿初耳膜的时候，阿初的心弦为之一颤。不过，同名同姓也是不可避免的。
Han Zhengqi? When that name poured into A-Chu’s eardrums, a string in his heart trembled at it. And yet two men of the same name and surname could not, after all, be ruled out.

> 可是，既然有一线希望，何不去碰碰运气？他在想。
But since there was a thread of hope, why not go and try his luck? So he thought.

> 也许，他真的是那个失踪已久，差点做了自己姐夫的人呢？
Perhaps he truly was that man, so long vanished, who had all but become his own brother-in-law?

> 四太太和荣荣“回家”了。她们的尸体放在了灵堂里的棺椁中。
The Fourth Madam and Rongrong had “gone home.” Their bodies were laid in the coffins in the mourning hall.

> 常言道：“死者为大。”
As the common saying goes: “The dead take precedence over all.”

> 荣府大门敞开，白色的灯笼高挂，暗示着四太太和荣荣可以从荣家大门里出殡。
The great gate of the Rong mansion stood open, white lanterns hung high, a sign that the Fourth Madam and Rongrong might be borne out to burial through the main gate of the Rong house.

> 四太太是荣家的姨太太，新婚抬进门时，走的是偏门，显得鬼鬼祟祟的。没想到，死后可以风风光光的从大门抬出去。
The Fourth Madam was a concubine of the Rong house; when she was newly married and carried in, she had come through the side gate, in a manner furtive and slinking. Who would have thought that in death she might be carried out through the main gate in full glory and state?

> 丫鬟和仆人们都穿着麻布丧服，一个个哭丧着脸。也有一、两个不识趣的仆人站在院子里暗地里嚷嚷，说：同济医院的爆炸案，是因为四太太暗地里曾经放过高利贷，想必是有人寻仇；还有大小姐荣荣，今天换一个男朋友，明天换一个小明星，后天换个小老板。你知道，哪个男人想不通呢？
The maidservants and menservants all wore mourning of hempen cloth, every face drawn long with grief. There were, too, one or two servants without any tact who stood in the courtyard muttering under their breath, saying: the explosion at the Tongji Hospital was because the Fourth Madam had once, in secret, lent money at usurer’s rates, and someone, no doubt, had come seeking revenge; and then there was the eldest young lady, Rongrong — today a new boyfriend, tomorrow some little film star, the day after some petty boss. You know how it is: what man wouldn’t take it hard?

> 三太太彻底垮了。
The Third Madam had utterly collapsed.

> 自打四太太在同济医院被炸的消息传来，她就有点兔死狐悲，正伤心呢，才听得荣荣出事了！三太太简直就像晴空里被劈了炸雷，懵了。哭也哭不出来，脸上直抽筋，一下就昏厥过去了。人事不知！
From the moment word came that the Fourth Madam had been blown up at the Tongji Hospital, she had felt something of the fox that grieves at the hare’s death; and in the very midst of her sorrow she heard that Rongrong too had met with disaster! It was as though a thunderbolt had split down out of a clear sky upon the Third Madam, and she was struck senseless. She could not even weep; her face twitched and cramped, and all at once she fainted dead away. Lost to the world!

> 等她醒来得时候，听得满屋子的哭声。荣华和荣升都在床前陪着她，杏儿凄风苦雨地站在门边。
When she came to, she heard weeping filling the whole room. Ronghua and Rong Sheng were both at her bedside, and Xing’er stood at the door, wretched as wind and rain.

> “荣荣？我的荣荣呢？”三太太挣扎着起来。“荣荣，刚才叫我呢。我的儿！荣荣！”她鞋也没穿，就往外走。荣华抱着她，说：“妈，荣荣不在了。”
“Rongrong? Where is my Rongrong?” The Third Madam struggled up. “Rongrong was calling for me just now. My child! Rongrong!” Without even her shoes on, she started for the door. Ronghua held her and said: “Mother, Rongrong is gone.”

> “不在了？这么大一个活人啊！”三太太跺着脚，跳起来。“不可能！我的荣荣啊……”三太太顺势坐下来哭。杏儿替她穿了鞋，要扶她起来。三太太想了想，荣荣呢？还没见着面呢？三太太不知哪里来得力气，一下就冲了出去。杏儿扶着门大哭不止。
“Gone? A living person, and grown so tall!” The Third Madam stamped her feet and sprang up. “It can’t be! Oh, my Rongrong…” and she sank down where she stood and wept. Xing’er put her shoes on for her and made to help her up. Then the Third Madam thought: Rongrong — she had not even seen her yet? From she knew not where the Third Madam found the strength, and in one rush she bolted out. Xing’er clung to the doorframe, weeping without cease.

> 荣升和荣华赶紧一同跟出来，一直追到灵堂。
Rong Sheng and Ronghua hurried out together after her, pursuing her all the way to the mourning hall.

> 灵堂上分左右放置着两副棺椁。左边写：慈母西归；右边是：仙姬回航。三太太也是读过书的人，大抵知道女儿的方向。她呆呆地站在荣荣的棺椁面前，猛地推开棺材盖子，一只手哆哆嗦嗦地去揭荣荣脸上的白布。
In the mourning hall two coffins were set out, to left and right. On the left was written: A loving mother returns to the west; on the right: An immortal maiden sails home. The Third Madam was a woman of some reading, and could well enough tell which way her daughter lay. She stood blankly before Rongrong’s coffin, then with a sudden thrust pushed the lid aside, and with one trembling hand went to lift the white cloth from Rongrong’s face.

> 大家都屏神敛气地站着。
Everyone stood holding their breath, drawing their spirits in.

> 白布揭开了，是荣荣。
The white cloth was lifted away: it was Rongrong.

> 香脂腻粉扑在荣荣青春无忧的脸颊上，显得十分凄惨，简直惨不忍睹！三太太嚎哭起来，这是实实在在的痛！剜了心尖七寸肉的惨痛！绝望的哀嚎，号叫！
Fragrant balm and greasy powder had been dabbed onto Rongrong’s young, carefree cheeks, and the effect was pitiful in the extreme — a sight scarcely to be borne! The Third Madam broke into howling grief; this was pain wholly real! The agony of seven inches of flesh gouged from the very tip of the heart! A despairing wail, a shriek!

> 三太太此时此刻看到了阿初。
At that very moment the Third Madam caught sight of A-Chu.

> 阿初很平静，几乎是引颈以待。
A-Chu was very calm — all but stretching out his neck to await the blow.

> 怒火焚烧着三太太的心！她挣开荣华的手，恶狠狠地扑到阿初身上，去撕咬阿初的肉，去扯裂阿初的头发。
Fury blazed in the Third Madam’s heart! She tore free of Ronghua’s hand and flung herself viciously upon A-Chu, tearing and biting at his flesh, ripping at his hair.

> “是你！是你害死了我的荣荣！你为什么要无缘无故搬出去住。你要在家里，荣荣怎么会去医院看你？荣荣不去医院，怎么会没了？是你啊，刽子手！你还我荣荣啊！”
“It was you! It was you who did my Rongrong to death! Why did you move out to live elsewhere, for no reason at all? Had you been here in the house, why would Rongrong ever have gone to the hospital to see you? And had she not gone to the hospital, how would she be gone? It was you, you butcher! Give me back my Rongrong!”

> 荣华和荣升拼命地将三太太从阿初身边拉开。但是，三太太的疯劲上来了，谁也拦不住。三太太的手指向了荣升，尖声大叫：“你们，你们沆瀣一气，沆瀣一气，害死了我的荣荣！你们开心了，得意了。你们，你们不得好死。”
Ronghua and Rong Sheng dragged with all their might to pull the Third Madam away from A-Chu. But the Third Madam’s madness was upon her, and no one could hold her back. She leveled her finger at Rong Sheng and shrieked: “The two of you — you’re all of a foul band together, all of a foul band, and you’ve done my Rongrong to death! You’re pleased now, you’re satisfied. You — you’ll die a bad death, the pair of you.”

> “我要杀了你！杀死你！我要你们陪葬！全陪葬！！你们一个也别想活着。”
“I’ll kill you! Kill you dead! I’ll have you buried with them — all of you buried with them! Not one of you shall think to go on living.”

> “别以为我不知道，二太太是怎么死的？四太太好端端的怎么也死了？下一个轮到谁？轮到我了。”
“Don’t think I don’t know how the Second Madam died. And the Fourth Madam, healthy and whole — how is it she died too? Whose turn is next? It’s my turn now.”

> “住口！”大太太正颜厉色地呵斥三太太。三太太的眼睛都绿了，可是她的腿不争气，突然身子倾斜下去，荣华伸手架住母亲。
“Be silent!” The First Madam, her face set stern and severe, rebuked the Third Madam. The Third Madam’s eyes had gone green with rage, but her legs would not do her bidding; all at once her body tilted and slid, and Ronghua reached out to prop her mother up.

> 丽水陪着大太太走到灵堂中央。
Lishui walked with the First Madam to the center of the mourning hall.

> “简直成了人间地狱了。”大太太目光灼人，紧绷着脸，直逼荣升和荣华。“像什么样子？当我是死人啊！一个家里，死了个姨太太，死了一个女孩子，天就塌了吗？！地陷了吗？！老爷死的时候，怎么不见这么伤心？啊？老爷死的时候，老太太死的时候，你们谁来帮过忙？你们谁来嚎过丧？！对，哪怕是虚情假意的泪水，你们都吝啬地存放起来。”大太太气度雍容，严词毒句，字字诛心。在漫长的家族权利的斗争中，大太太从未放弃过正妻的尊严和刚毅。荣老爷死的时候，正值荣升在国外为“情”羁留，家里没有孝子，作为儿子的荣升对此感到惭愧。
“This has become nothing less than a hell on earth.” The First Madam’s gaze scorched, her face drawn tight, bearing straight down upon Rong Sheng and Ronghua. “What sort of a spectacle is this? You take me for a dead woman! In one household a concubine has died, a girl has died — has the sky fallen in for that?! Has the earth caved in?! When the Master died, where was all this grief? Eh? When the Master died, when the Old Madam died, which of you came to lend a hand? Which of you wailed at the mourning?! Yes — even your false, feigned tears you hoarded up and stinted.” The First Madam’s bearing was stately and composed, her words stern and venomous, every word striking at the heart. Through the long struggle for power within the family, the First Madam had never once let go the dignity and iron resolve of the principal wife. When old Master Rong died, Rong Sheng had been detained abroad for the sake of “love,” and there had been no filial son in the house; and Rong Sheng, as a son, felt the shame of it.

> “谁家里没有死过人？指桑骂槐，搅得家宅不宁。我知道，有人是过腻了锦衣玉食、四平八稳的日子，不想过好日子，就趁早给我从荣家滚出去！滚出这个家！如果，还想在荣家讨生活，就给我老老实实的把不干不净的嘴巴缝起来。”
“What household has never had a death in it? Pointing at the mulberry to curse the locust, stirring up the whole house so there’s no peace in it. I know how it is — there are those who have grown sick of fine clothes and rich food and a life set four-square and steady, who have no wish for the good life; well then, the sooner they get out of the Rong house for me, the better! Get out of this family! And if you still mean to scrape a living in the Rong house, then you’ll honestly sew up that filthy, foul mouth of yours for me.”

> 三太太迟钝无力地靠在荣华身上，在大太太强势的压迫下，她把剩余的怨毒全化做滔滔泪水。
Numb and strengthless, the Third Madam leaned against Ronghua; under the First Madam’s overpowering pressure she dissolved what venom was left in her into a flood of pouring tears.

> 聪慧的女儿夹在嫡母与生母之间，竭力分担着生母所承受的痛楚和羞辱。敏感地感受着生母在这一刻泪水里的慈爱。荣华无声地把生母揽进怀中，有意低回的目光和嫡母凌厉的目光交接。
The clever daughter, caught between the principal mother and the mother who bore her, strove with all her power to share the pain and humiliation her own mother bore, feeling keenly the tenderness in her mother’s tears at that moment. Without a word Ronghua gathered her mother into her arms, and let her own deliberately lowered, circling gaze meet the First Madam’s fierce and cutting one.

> “姨奶奶刚刚失去了孩子。母亲。”荣华回大太太的话，很干净、很简短、很含蓄。
“The aunt-mistress has only just lost her child, Mother.” Ronghua’s answer to the First Madam was very clean, very brief, very restrained.

> “丧失理智的人，应该待在病床上，而不是出来闹丧、谩骂。”大太太说。“有些人以为，可以借着四太太的死来生事，借题发挥，说几句令人隐晦难懂的话，借以浇心中块垒。那就大错特错了！”大太太走到阿初跟前，说：“四太太和大小姐是死在你的诊室里的，死于非命。我希望，你有所解释，或是澄清。我已经派人去请警察局的韩局长了，这件事，我一定要查个水落石出。绝不授人口实。”大太太来到四太太的棺椁前，轻轻叹息了一声，哽咽了一声。
“One who has lost her reason ought to be kept to a sickbed, not come out to make a scene at a funeral and hurl abuse,” said the First Madam. “There are those who imagine they may seize on the Fourth Madam’s death to stir up trouble, to make a pretext of it and say a few dark, riddling words, and so pour out the pent-up grievance in their breasts. In that they are gravely, gravely mistaken!” The First Madam walked over to A-Chu and said: “The Fourth Madam and the eldest young lady died in your consulting room — died by violence. I expect some explanation from you, or some clearing of the matter. I have already sent for Chief Han of the Police Bureau; this affair I will see traced to the very bottom, till the water sinks and the stones show through. I will give no one a handle to use against us.” The First Madam came before the Fourth Madam’s coffin, sighed softly, and choked back a sob.

> 想着四太太刚进门的样子，姣美动人；
She thought of how the Fourth Madam had looked when first she came through the gate — how lovely, how moving;

> 想着四太太被炸得血肉横飞，惨状毕呈；
she thought of the Fourth Madam blown to bloody shreds, the whole grisly sight laid bare;

> 想着二十年前的荣家，华灯烟火，鲜衣美食，雨丝风片，鸳鸯蝴蝶；于今，人死黄泉，子嗣单薄，生意艰难，现状堪忧。
she thought of the Rong house twenty years ago — its splendid lamps and fireworks, its fine clothes and rich food, its threads of rain and flakes of breeze, its mandarin ducks and butterflies; and now — the dead gone to the Yellow Springs, the line of heirs grown thin, the business hard-pressed, the present state a thing to fear.

> 仿佛冥冥中有一阵悲风袭来，不由得心中百念丛生，伤心难忍，怆然涕下。说：“妹妹，可怜你的命太薄。就这样不明不白的没了……”
As though out of the unseen dark a gust of grieving wind swept in upon her, a hundred thoughts sprang up unbidden in her heart; unable to bear the sorrow, she let her tears fall in desolation, and said: “Little sister, pity you, your lot was too thin. To be gone like this, in doubt and darkness…”

> 大太太此刻的悲哀湮没了肃杀之气，抽泣着回头吩咐荣华说：“四太太和荣荣的丧事，就由你来操办吧，不要委屈了她们。”
In that moment the First Madam’s grief drowned out the killing sternness in her, and, sobbing, she turned and charged Ronghua: “The funerals of the Fourth Madam and Rongrong — let you be the one to see to them, and do not stint them or do them any wrong.”

> “是的，母亲。”荣华答应着。
“Yes, Mother,” Ronghua assented.

> “可惜啊，妹妹你跟前连一个披麻戴孝的人都没有。”大太太这句话是有的放矢，递给阿初一个暗示，他应该出来做孝子。
“What a pity — little sister, you have not even one soul at your side to wear the hemp and don the mourning.” These words of the First Madam’s were an arrow aimed at a mark, passing A-Chu a hint that he ought to come forward as a filial son.

> 可是，阿初不吭声。
But A-Chu made not a sound.

> 大太太脸上有些薄怒，说：“阿初，你说说看，谁该出来做孝子？”
A thin anger showed on the First Madam’s face, and she said: “A-Chu, tell me, then — who ought to come forward as the filial son?”

> 阿初说：“大太太，孝子，应该由荣家的人来做。”
A-Chu said: “First Madam, the filial son ought to be one of the Rong family.”

> 大太太冷笑了一声。“你很聪明啊，孝子，应该由荣家的人来做。你是想让大少爷给姨奶奶披麻戴孝呢？还是你自己想做荣家的少爷呢？”
The First Madam gave a cold laugh. “You are very clever — the filial son ought to be one of the Rong family. Would you have the eldest young master wear hemp and mourning for the aunt-mistress? Or is it that you would be a young master of the Rong house yourself?”

> 阿初还没来得及应声，红儿上气不接下气地跑进来，一边喘气一边喊：“大太太，大太太！”
Before A-Chu could get out an answer, Hong’er came running in, gasping for breath, panting and calling out as she came: “First Madam, First Madam!”

> “怎么了？”大太太大声呵斥着她。
“What is it?” the First Madam barked at her.

> “有，有个人，说是小少爷。”
“There’s — there’s a man, says he’s the young master — the little young master.”

> “什么？什么小少爷？”
“What? What little young master?”

> “说是荣家小少爷回来了！”
“Says the little young master of the Rong house has come home!”

> 大太太的头“嗡”的一声震响。
The First Madam’s head rang with a sudden “buzz.”

> 阿初知道谁来了。
A-Chu knew who had come.

> 三太太突然把头伸出来，嘻嘻哼笑起来。“分家产的回来了，分，分家产的。”荣华把她的头轻轻地带回怀里。
The Third Madam suddenly thrust out her head and gave a snickering, humming laugh. “The one who’s come to split the estate is back — to split, to split the estate.” Ronghua gently drew her head back into her arms.

> 大太太立定身形，问：“在哪里？”
The First Madam planted herself and asked: “Where is he?”

> “在，在院子里。”红儿战兢兢指着灵堂外。
“In — in the courtyard.” Hong’er, trembling, pointed outside the mourning hall.

> “来得不巧啊。”大太太冷哼一声，对众人说：“跟我来。”
“He’s come at an awkward hour.” The First Madam gave a cold snort and said to them all: “Come with me.”

> 院子中间，一字排开六个穿短褂的汉子，荣初一身缟素，肃立中央。大家看见荣初的时候，都暗地吸了一口凉气，这个年轻人的眉眼的确很像四太太。
In the middle of the courtyard stood six stout fellows in short jackets, ranged in a line, with Rong Chu, all in unhemmed white mourning, standing solemn at their center. When they set eyes on Rong Chu, they all secretly drew a cold breath: the young man’s brows and eyes were indeed very like the Fourth Madam’s.

> “你是谁？”大太太站在阶前，仰面质问。
“Who are you?” The First Madam stood at the head of the steps and, lifting her face, demanded it of him.

> “不孝子荣初，给母亲请安！”荣初就地跪下，给大太太磕头。
“Your unfilial son Rong Chu pays his respects to his mother!” Rong Chu knelt where he stood and kowtowed to the First Madam.

> “慢着！”大太太高声喝止。“先生您弄错了吧？这里是荣府！可不是大杂院，菜市场。您要认母亲，得看准了地方。不要以为，道听途说的故事，就可以作为登堂入室的理由。”
“Hold!” The First Madam cried out to stop him. “You have made a mistake, sir, have you not? This is the Rong mansion — not some crowded tenement, some vegetable market. If you would claim a mother, you must be sure you have the right place. Do not imagine that a tale picked up by hearsay can serve as your reason for mounting the hall and entering the chamber.”

> “我的生母，的的确确是府上的姨奶奶。儿子不是来滋事的，也不是来谋家业的。一个姨奶奶有什么私产可以交代的？所以，请母亲不要赶儿子走，儿子就跪在这里，给姨奶奶守灵。姨奶奶出殡之日，就是儿子离家之时。丧母之痛，乞母亲宽恩，容儿子略尽孝道。惊扰之处，请母亲见谅。”荣初说完，结结实实给大太太磕了三个响头，血滴在青砖上。
“The mother who bore me was indeed, beyond all doubt, an aunt-mistress of your household. Your son has not come to make trouble, nor to scheme after the estate. What private property has an aunt-mistress to account for? And so I beg you, Mother, do not drive your son away; your son will kneel here and keep vigil for the aunt-mistress. The day the aunt-mistress is borne out to burial shall be the day your son leaves this house. In the pain of losing a mother, I beg my mother’s clemency, that your son may fulfill a little of the filial way. For the disturbance I have caused, I beg my mother’s pardon.” When Rong Chu had done, he kowtowed three solid, resounding times to the First Madam, and blood dripped onto the grey flagstones.

> “分家产的，一点不错，他长得像四太太。分家产的来了。”三太太喃喃地说。“我们荣荣也要分一份，现在就分，出了门，就不认了。”
“The one to split the estate — quite right, he’s the very image of the Fourth Madam. The estate-splitter has come,” the Third Madam murmured. “Our Rongrong is to have a share too, split it now, for once she’s out the door there’s no owning her anymore.”

> 大太太感觉空气中都染着血腥味，她实在是呆不下去了，转身就走。走之前冷冰冰地抛下一句话。“七日后出殡。以后，我再也不要见到来路不明的人！”
The First Madam felt the very air stained with the reek of blood; she truly could not stay a moment longer, and turned to go. Before she left she flung down one icy sentence: “The burial is in seven days. And after that, I will not have any person of unknown provenance in my sight again!”

> 一语双关，阿初知道，最后一句话，大太太是说给自己听的。
It was a saying with a double edge; A-Chu knew that the last sentence the First Madam had spoken for him to hear.

> “梨花落，杏花开，梦绕长安十二街。夜深和露立苍苔，到晚来辗转书斋外。纸儿、笔
“Pear blossoms fall, apricot blossoms open, my dreams wind round the twelve avenues of Chang’an. Deep in the night I stand on the green moss in the dew; and when evening comes I toss and turn outside the study. Paper, brush,

> 儿、墨儿、砚儿，件件般般都是郎君在，泪洒空斋，只落得望穿秋水不见一书来。”灵堂里的留声机里放着四太太爱听、爱唱的评弹段子。清风朗月过滤着凄凄惶惶的雅韵，院子里，模糊的炉火掩映着阿初的脸，看不清他此时此刻的表情，不过，从纸蝶漫飞的火盆里，大抵知道他的思绪是不平静的。
“ink and inkstone—each thing, every one, has my lord in it; my tears wet the empty study, and all it comes to is that I gaze till my eyes break through the autumn waters, and not one letter comes.” In the mourning hall the phonograph was playing the pingtan piece the Fourth Madam had loved to hear and to sing. The clear breeze and bright moon strained the plaintive, forlorn refinement of it; in the courtyard the blurred glow of the brazier fire played over A-Chu’s face, so that his expression at that moment could not be clearly read — though from the paper butterflies drifting up out of the fire basin, one could gather well enough that his thoughts were far from calm.

> 荣初依然一动不动地跪在青砖上。
Rong Chu still knelt, unmoving, upon the grey flagstones.

> “到我身边来。”阿初面无表情地招呼着自己的亲侄。
“Come to my side.” Without any expression on his face, A-Chu called to his own nephew.

> 荣初膝行了几步，安静地跪在阿初身边，火盆里的纸钱烧卷了，烟和灰飘起来，杨慕初顺手把手里厚厚的一叠纸钱分了些给他，荣初没有伸手接。
Rong Chu shuffled forward a few steps on his knees and knelt quietly at A-Chu’s side. In the fire basin the paper money curled as it burned, and smoke and ash drifted up. Yang Muchu made to divide off some of the thick sheaf of paper money in his hand and pass it to him, but Rong Chu did not reach out to take it.

> “为什么？”阿初问。
“Why not?” A-Chu asked.

> “我母亲不需要。她在黄泉路上，不是等钱用，她在等仇人的血。”
“My mother has no need of it. On the road to the Yellow Springs she does not wait for money to spend; she waits for the blood of her enemies.”

> 阿初默默放下纸钱，徐徐站立。“你多大？”
A-Chu silently laid the paper money down and slowly rose to his feet. “How old are you?”

> “二十岁。”
“Twenty.”

> “读过书吗？”
“Have you had any schooling?”

> “读过一点点。”
“A very little.”

> “读了些什么书？”
“What books did you read?”

> “忠孝节义的书。”荣初咬着牙，黑着脸说。
“Books of loyalty and filial piety, of honor and righteousness,” said Rong Chu, gritting his teeth, his face dark.

> “你恨我吗？”阿初问的直截了当。
“Do you hate me?” A-Chu asked, blunt and direct.

> “谈不上。我，其实心里怨恨母亲，怨她为什么把我扔在外面二十年，恨她，恨她没给我尽孝的机会。子欲养而亲不在！”
“It doesn’t come to that. In truth it is my mother I resent in my heart — resent her for casting me outside these twenty years, hate her, hate her for giving me no chance to be a filial son. The child would tend the parent, but the parent is gone!”

> “是啊，仇恨，使她放弃了一切，善良，又使她挽回了一切。但是，杀戮却仍然发生了……”
“Yes — hatred made her give up everything, and goodness won everything back again. And yet the killing happened all the same…”

> “是你，你没有勇气承担责任！”
“It was you — you had not the courage to bear the responsibility!”

> 阿初心中的隐痛又被勾了起来。“你的母亲就像是绿呢赌桌前的一个大赌徒，她把一生的积蓄都押在了我的身上。她要的是'双'，开的是'单'。滚动的骰子没有按照规定的路线去执行，去贯彻。她输得很惨。可能是老天怜悯她的付出，老天爷偷了懒，老天让那个坐庄的人去让她赢！虽然赢得代价更惨烈。终究是她赢了！她要的并不是死后备极哀荣，而是堂堂正正的回'家'！她赢了！”
The hidden ache in A-Chu’s heart was hooked up once more. “Your mother was like a great gambler at the green-baize table who had staked the savings of a whole life upon me. She called for ‘evens,’ and ‘odds’ came up. The rolling dice did not run the course laid down for them, did not carry it through. She lost, and lost bitterly. Perhaps Heaven took pity on all she had given; Heaven, the old man up above, played truant, and let the one who kept the bank hand her the win! — though the winning cost the more cruelly for it. In the end she won! What she wanted was not to be honored to the utmost after death, but to come ‘home’ openly and with her head held high! She won!”

> 当阿初说完这番话后，荣初知道，母亲的付出终有了回报。他把脸埋在孝衣里，开始哽咽。
When A-Chu had finished speaking, Rong Chu knew that all his mother had given had won its reward at last. He buried his face in his mourning clothes and began to choke with sobs.

> “哭出来吧。”阿初说。“你应该让你的母亲听到你的声音，这样，她走得会安心。”
“Let it out and weep,” said A-Chu. “You should let your mother hear your voice; that way she will go with her mind at ease.”

> 荣初大哭起来，像个大孩子。
Rong Chu burst into loud weeping, like a great child.

> 荣华默默地站在灵堂上，听着老唱片夹杂着男子哀鸣的悲声。“悲哀！你看他绿窗灯火照楼台，哪还记凄风苦雨卧倒长街！人生莫做亏心事，处处风声是祸胎。孽火如雷，拉入阴阳界，索还命债。”
Ronghua stood in silence in the mourning hall, listening to the old record with its mournful sound threaded through by a man’s grieving cry. “O sorrow! See how the lamplight at his green window shines upon the tower — how should he still remember the one who lay down in the long street through the bitter wind and rain! In this life do no deed against your conscience, for on every side the very wind carries the seed of calamity. The fire of sin comes down like thunder, dragging him across the border of the living and the dead, to render up the debt of a life.”

> 不死的魂魄，即将重返人间。
The undying soul was about to return once more to the world of men.

> 荣家的灵堂，祭奠亡灵的人络绎不绝，大多数是荣家生意场上的朋友，由荣华支应着，其余的吊客由阿初出面应酬。
At the Rong family’s mourning hall the mourners paying respects to the departed came in an unbroken stream, most of them the Rong family’s friends from the world of business; these Ronghua attended to, while the remaining callers of condolence A-Chu came forward to receive.

> 大太太推病不至，大家心里有数，毕竟死的是姨太太和庶出的女儿。
The First Madam pleaded illness and did not appear, and everyone understood well enough why: the dead were, after all, only a concubine and a daughter of concubine’s issue.

> 上海药业的同行来了；
The colleagues of the Shanghai medicine trade came;

> 上海各报社的记者来了；
the reporters of Shanghai’s various newspapers came;

> 同济医院的同事们来了；
the colleagues from the Tongji Hospital came;

> 汤少和夏跃春来了；
Young Tang and Xia Yuechun came;

> 上海警察局副局长韩正齐来了。
Han Zhengqi, deputy chief of the Shanghai Police Bureau, came.

> 由于，韩禹提前给阿初打过招呼，所以，阿初是整装以待。
Because Han Yu had given A-Chu warning beforehand, A-Chu was dressed and ready and waiting.

> 韩正齐是以一个标准的军人形像出现的。他性格坚忍，行事果决。每于濒临绝境处，得以死里求生。二十年来得奋斗，使自己的生命没有虚掷在残破的情爱里。他是一个把现实和幻想分得很清楚的一个人。在寒夜的陋室里，自己坚忍不拔的精神像一盏明灯自信地投射出光明。自己走到今天，唯一愧对的人，是自己心爱的女人。
Han Zhengqi appeared in the very image of the model soldier. His nature was hard and enduring, his conduct decisive. Again and again, at the edge of a hopeless pass, he had wrested life out of death. Twenty years of struggle had kept his life from being squandered away in a broken love. He was a man who kept reality and fancy clearly apart. In the mean room of a cold night, his own unbending, dauntless spirit had cast out its light, confident as a bright lamp. In coming to where he stood today, the one person he had wronged was the woman he loved.

> 他到荣家，一是吊丧，二来是荣家大太太亲自给自己打了电话，请自己一定过府来一趟。荣家毕竟是名门望族，家人无端死于非命，的确应该彻查起因，深窥底奥。
He had come to the Rong house, first, to mourn, and second, because the Rong family’s First Madam had telephoned him herself and begged him to be sure to make the visit. The Rong family was, after all, a house of name and standing; when its people died a violent death for no cause, the origins of it truly ought to be searched to the bottom, its inmost depths peered into.

> 韩正齐在韩禹的引领下，走到了阿初面前。他看见阿初的表情先是很惊愕，继而就有些模糊的影像隐约而现，熟悉的面孔，亲切的笑容，居然令韩正齐从骨子里对阿初生出几分敬畏之心。
Led by Han Yu, Han Zhengqi came before A-Chu. When he saw A-Chu his look was at first one of great astonishment, and then some blurred image loomed dimly up — a familiar face, a warm and kindly smile — so that, of all things, from the very marrow of him there rose in Han Zhengqi a certain awe of A-Chu.

> 阿初穿了一件雪青色长袍，这件袍子的绣工，是源于四太太绘就的莲花，蕴涵着旧时代的色彩，又像是一件蓄含着旧情事的器皿，散发着四太太温柔的鼻息和香醇的春意。
A-Chu wore a long gown of snow-lilac, and the embroidery of that gown had its origin in a lotus the Fourth Madam had drawn; it held the coloring of an older age, and was like a vessel storing up some old affair of the heart, giving off the Fourth Madam’s gentle breath and the mellow fragrance of spring.

> 阿初是故意穿出来见韩正齐的，他虽然不知道对面人是否是故人，但是，一旦是故人，看见这件寄寓所思、深怀所念的袍子，就该对他礼让三分。
A-Chu had worn it out to meet Han Zhengqi on purpose. Though he did not yet know whether the man before him was one from the old days, he reasoned that, if he were, then at the sight of this gown in which so much longing and remembrance was lodged, he ought to yield to him a measure of deference.

> 其实，阿初忘了，不仅仅是这件袍子能揭示自己的身份，自己的容貌，也是一张堂皇的名片。
In truth, A-Chu had forgotten that it was not only this gown that could disclose his identity: his own face was itself a splendid calling card.

> 风生萍末，斗转星移，二十年来什么都在变。唯一没变的是血缘。
The wind rises from the tip of the duckweed, the Dipper turns and the stars shift; in twenty years everything had changed. The one thing unchanged was the tie of blood.

> 阿初看到了他所希望看到的一幕。
A-Chu saw the very scene he had hoped to see.

> 韩正齐居然不等韩禹介绍，主动迎上阿初，说：“这位想必就是荣家的初先生吧？听小儿常常谈及您。哦，忘了自我介绍了，敝人韩正齐。”接着，他屈尊俯就地伸出手来。阿初不卑不亢地伸出手来握紧韩局长的手，说：“小弟杨慕初。”
Han Zhengqi, of all things, did not wait for Han Yu’s introduction, but came forward to A-Chu of his own accord and said: “This gentleman must surely be Mr. Chu of the Rong house? My son speaks of you often. Ah — I forgot to introduce myself; I am Han Zhengqi.” And with that he condescended to put out his hand. Neither servile nor haughty, A-Chu put out his own and gripped Chief Han’s hand firmly, and said: “Yang Muchu, your younger brother.”

> 韩禹是第一次听到这个名字，很奇怪。
It was the first time Han Yu had heard that name, and he thought it very strange.

> 韩正齐显然不是第一次听到这个名字，“您？知道我是谁吗？”韩正齐试探地问。
Han Zhengqi plainly was not hearing the name for the first time. “You — do you know who I am?” Han Zhengqi asked, feeling his way.

> 阿初似笑非笑地说：“正如您知道我是谁。”阿初具有穿透力的目光让韩正齐感到“金龙帮”复活了，自己在这个年轻人眼里，难以隐匿任何秘密。
With a look that was neither quite a smile nor quite none, A-Chu said: “Even as you know who I am.” A-Chu’s piercing gaze made Han Zhengqi feel that the “Golden Dragon Society” had come back to life, and that before this young man’s eyes he could hide no secret at all.

> “多情儿女江湖老，二十年风霜雪雨，甘饴苦涩，一路上备尝艰辛吧？”
“The tender-hearted grow old in the wandering world; twenty years of wind and frost and snow and rain, of sweet and bitter both — the whole road, I daresay, you have tasted its every hardship?”

> “不，不。”阿初温文尔雅地说：“尝鼎一脔，初领其味。”
“No, no,” said A-Chu, gentle and refined. “One has tasted but a single morsel from the cauldron, and only begun to know its flavor.”

> “哦？”阿初的回答，令韩正齐颇感意外，继而问：“其味如何？”
“Oh?” A-Chu’s answer rather surprised Han Zhengqi, and then he asked: “And how does it taste?”

> 阿初笑了，说：“白刃在前，烈火在后。”
A-Chu smiled and said: “A bare blade before, and raging fire behind.”

> “杨先生可否借一步说话？”这是单方面邀请密谈。
“Might I have a word with you aside, Mr. Yang?” This was a one-sided invitation to a private talk.

> 阿初说：“正合我意。请……”
A-Chu said: “It suits me exactly. After you…”

> 韩禹傻痴痴地看着父亲和阿初并肩而去，一脑子糨糊，汤少和夏跃春过来问他，你们家老爷子，平常不是很难讲话吗？今天变了天了？礼贤下士？
Han Yu watched dumbly as his father and A-Chu walked off side by side, his head all a paste of confusion. Young Tang and Xia Yuechun came over to ask him: isn’t your old man usually so hard to talk to? Has the weather turned today? Bowing to the worthy and stooping to the lowly, is he?

> “我还二丈金刚摸不着头脑呢。”韩禹说。
“I’m every bit as much in the dark about it as you are,” said Han Yu.

> “他们讲什么？”夏跃春好奇地问。
“What are they talking about?” Xia Yuechun asked, curious.

> “什么白刃、烈火吧。”
“Something about bare blades and raging fire, I think.”

> “坏了，坏了。”汤少笑嘻嘻地说：“阿初教唆你们家老爷子杀人放火。”
“This is bad, this is bad,” said Young Tang, grinning. “A-Chu’s putting your old man up to murder and arson.”

> “正经点。”韩禹推了汤少一把，突然想起来了。“对了，你们知道阿初姓什么吗？”
“Be serious.” Han Yu gave Young Tang a shove, and then a thought struck him. “By the way — do you two know A-Chu’s surname?”

> “姓什么？”二人几乎异口同声地问。
“What is it?” the two of them asked almost with one voice.

> “姓杨！”韩禹很有把握地说。“对，姓杨，没错！”
“It’s Yang!” said Han Yu with great certainty. “Yes — Yang, that’s right!”

> 阿初并没有把韩正齐领进“墨菊斋”，而是别有用心地把他引进了四太太生前的居所“红梨阁”。
A-Chu did not lead Han Zhengqi into the Moju Studio, but with a purpose of his own drew him instead into the Hongli Pavilion, the dwelling where the Fourth Madam had lived.

> “红梨阁”，院子不大，但是很精致，很别致。窗明几净，疏草淡花。悠然的环境，迎面送给人一片清新的空气和舒适的宁静。静得可以听见“草”的嘘唏，漾开了阿初和韩正齐的怀旧情愫。
The Hongli Pavilion — its courtyard was not large, but it was exquisite, out of the common run. Bright windows and clean tables, sparse grasses and pale flowers. Its leisured air offered up, to any who came, a breath of fresh clean air and a comfortable stillness — a stillness so deep one could hear the “grasses” sigh, and it spread wide the old remembrance in A-Chu and Han Zhengqi both.

> 一花一草，都是阿初童年记忆的回眸。
Each flower, each blade of grass, was a backward glance into A-Chu’s childhood memory.

> 寸草、花瓣都浸含着韩正齐“爱”的残迹。
Every inch of grass, every petal, was steeped in the lingering traces of Han Zhengqi’s “love.”

> 他们走进房间，阿初吩咐小丫鬟沏茶。韩正齐趁机审视了房间的装潢、摆设，的确像极了当年小姐的香闺。
They went into the room, and A-Chu bade the little maid make tea. Han Zhengqi took the chance to look over the room’s furnishing and arrangement; it was indeed the very likeness of the young lady’s boudoir of those years.

> 她一直活在回忆里。
She had lived, all along, in her memories.

> 不知是她的不幸？还是自己的不幸？
Was it her misfortune, he wondered, or his own?

> 如果她不任性，如果她肯听自己一句话，如果当年她放弃，也许，今天，他们正快乐的生活在一起，而不是像现在一样，天人永诀。
Had she not been so willful, had she been willing to heed one word of his, had she given it up back then — perhaps, today, they would be living happily together, and not, as now, parted forever between the living and the dead.

> 仿佛一切都是静止的，四太太还活着，没有什么刻意要扫除的伤心痕迹。只有丫鬟红儿发髻两头上，带着纸扎的素花，提醒着阿初，斯人已乘黄鹤去……此地唯余恨悠悠。
It was as though everything stood still, as though the Fourth Madam were still alive, with no traces of grief that anyone had troubled to sweep away. Only the plain paper flowers on either side of the maid Hong’er’s coiled hair reminded A-Chu that she who was gone had ridden off on the yellow crane… and here only an endless regret remained.

> 房间正中挂着四太太盛装艳饰的相片，她笑得很含蓄。虽然，韩正齐看见阿初的时候，就有了一定的思想准备，进得房来，又有了旧感情回眸般的铺垫。但是，冷不防看见故人柔谐婉媚的遗照，还是感到震惊。
In the very center of the room hung a photograph of the Fourth Madam in full dress and bright adornment; her smile was very restrained. Though Han Zhengqi had, on first seeing A-Chu, been to some degree prepared in his mind, and though coming into the room had laid down beneath him a kind of backward glance of old feeling — still, to see, all unguarded, this portrait left by one from the old days, so soft and winning and sweet, shook him even so.

> 他强自镇定，脸上没有多余的表情。
He forced himself to composure, and let no needless expression show on his face.

> “您一点也不伤心。”阿初站在他背后说着不冷不热的话。显然，韩正齐看见四太太遗照的瞬间感觉，离阿初的想像，有很大的距离。
“You are not grieved in the least.” A-Chu stood behind him, saying the words neither cold nor warm. Plainly, what Han Zhengqi felt in the instant of seeing the Fourth Madam’s portrait was a great distance from what A-Chu had imagined.

> “我很伤感。”韩正齐说。
“I am much grieved,” said Han Zhengqi.

> “您是不是，早已遗忘了她的存在？”
“Is it not that you had long since forgotten she ever existed?”

> “是的。我不否认。”
“It is. I will not deny it.”

> “您很坦率。”阿初不想再做徒劳的辨别了。这个人的确是韩正齐，是四太太的情人。阿初索性单刀直入了。“您曾真心爱过我姐姐吗？”
“You are very candid.” A-Chu had no wish to go on with fruitless testing. This man was indeed Han Zhengqi, the Fourth Madam’s lover. A-Chu came, then, straight to the point, a single blade driven home. “Did you ever truly love my sister?”

> “也曾刻骨铭心。”
“There was a time it was carved into my very bones.”

> “您为什么要抛弃她？不告而别？二十年来您没有想过，您的所作所为，对她造成的伤害吗？”
“Then why did you cast her off? Leave without a word of farewell? In twenty years have you never thought what harm your doings did her?”

> “当年，我不能选择。没得选。”韩正齐喃喃地说。
“In those days I had no choice. There was no choosing,” Han Zhengqi murmured.

> “为什么？”
“Why not?”

> “您对我不了解，少爷。就是小姐，她对我的过去，也是一无所知。我是一个乡下人，十六岁那年，就在乡下讨了老婆，后来，还有了个儿子。也就是韩禹。”
“You do not know me, young master. Even the young lady knew nothing at all of my past. I was a countryman; in the year I was sixteen I took a wife back in the village, and later there was a son besides. That is to say — Han Yu.”

> 阿初蓦得坐下，轻轻地说：“我猜到了，韩禹比我还大一岁。”
A-Chu sat down abruptly and said softly: “I had guessed as much — Han Yu is a year older than I am.”

> “乡下日子难熬，逢旱遇涝的，没个吃饱饭的日子。那时候，我年纪轻，血气方刚，就去吃了军粮。我在连绵不断的军阀混战中度过了自己的军旅生涯，我十分厌倦无休无止的征伐和血腥，退役来到上海。刚到上海的时候，举目无亲，四处碰壁。后来，遇见你、你的母亲，是她救了我，把我带进了杨家。你父亲知道我会些拳脚功夫，就介绍我加入了你外公组建的社团'金龙帮'，还雇我做了你家的司机。那时候，你姐姐才十七岁。”
“Life in the countryside was hard to bear — drought one year, flood the next, never a day of eating one’s fill. In those days I was young, hot-blooded and full of vigor, and so I went off to eat army rations. I passed my whole soldiering life in the endless, unbroken wars of the warlords, and I grew heartily sick of the ceaseless campaigning and bloodshed, and left the ranks and came to Shanghai. When first I came to Shanghai I had not a soul to turn to, and knocked against a wall on every side. Then I met you, and your mother — it was she who saved me and brought me into the Yang house. Your father, knowing I had some skill of fist and foot, introduced me into the society your maternal grandfather had founded, the ‘Golden Dragon Society,’ and hired me besides as your family’s driver. At that time your sister was but seventeen.”

> “您欺骗了她，不是吗？”
“You deceived her, did you not?”

> “没有。我想她一定是知道的。那个年代，在我当时的年纪，不可能还是独身。只是，她和我都不愿意去捅破这层窗户纸罢了。我当时真得很爱她。爱得很深。”
“No. I think she must have known. In that day and age, at the age I then was, it was not possible that I should still be unmarried. It was only that neither she nor I was willing to poke through that paper over the window. In those days I truly loved her. Loved her deeply.”

> “有多深？”
“How deeply?”

> “肯为她去死！”韩正齐毫不犹豫地表态。
“Deeply enough to die for her!” Han Zhengqi declared without the least hesitation.

> “可是您现在活着，活得很滋润。她却死了，死得很悲惨。”
“And yet here you are, alive — living very comfortably. While she is dead, dead most wretchedly.”

> “少爷！”
“Young master!”

> “不！这个称呼太别扭了。”阿初居然笑起来。“我听着十分恶心。您是什么身份？我是什么地位？今非昔比！”
“No! That form of address is far too awkward.” A-Chu, of all things, began to laugh. “It sickens me to hear it. What is your station? What is mine? Today is not what yesterday was!”

> “少爷！”韩正齐突然摘下帽子，平放在手，跪倒尘埃。
“Young master!” Han Zhengqi suddenly took off his hat, laid it flat on his hand, and knelt down into the dust.

> 房间里的气氛一下子紧张起来，空气里像掺了凝固剂，阿初没有动，他用衣袖轻拂了一下四太太的梳妆匣子，吹了一口气上去，用手指抹去一丝雾气。说：“您曾经救过我的命，不必行此大礼。”
The air of the room grew all at once taut, as though a coagulant had been mixed into it. A-Chu did not stir; he flicked lightly with his sleeve at the Fourth Madam’s dressing case, breathed a breath upon it, and wiped a wisp of mist away with his finger. He said: “You once saved my life; there is no need for so great a courtesy as this.”

> 韩正齐没有动，他说：“您的母亲曾经救过我的命。少爷。可是，我没有出手救过您。从来没有。”
Han Zhengqi did not move. He said: “Your mother once saved my life, young master. But I never once lifted a hand to save you. Never.”

> 杨慕初略为倾斜的身子，缓缓伸直。“您说什么？”
Yang Muchu’s slightly leaning body slowly drew straight. “What are you saying?”

> “二十年前，你们东躲西藏的时候，岳嬷嬷找到了我，是她告诉我，老爷遇害的消息。我连夜把小姐安全地送出了城区。可是，小姐她不肯走了，她意志很坚决，她要复仇，用极端的方式，用、用你来作饵，用你来执行人世间最残酷的刑罚……我想竭力阻止她的盲动，可是，我失败了。”
“Twenty years ago, when you were all fleeing and hiding east and west, the nurse A-Yue found me out; it was she who told me the news that the Master had been murdered. That very night I saw the young lady safely out of the city district. But the young lady would not go on; her will was set hard — she would have her revenge, by the most extreme of means, using — using you as the bait, using you to carry out the cruelest punishment in this world of men… I tried with all my strength to stop her blind rashness, but I failed.”

> 简直不可思议！
It was scarcely to be believed!

> 杨慕初似乎又坠入了另一个迷雾重重的迷宫。韩正齐和四太太所叙述的故事完全不同。当然，是细节不同。可是，细节往往是决定成败和虚实的。
Yang Muchu felt as though he had fallen once more into another labyrinth thick with fog. The story Han Zhengqi told and the story the Fourth Madam had told were utterly different. In the details, of course. But it is the details, often enough, that decide success and failure, the true and the false.

> 有人在说谎。
Someone was lying.

> 或是想掩盖“真相”。
Or wished to cover over the “truth.”

> “真相”是什么？
What was the “truth”?

> 或许，他只是为自己辩护，以求良心的解放。
Perhaps he was only defending himself, to win his conscience its release.

> “那天，我经过内心的挣扎，终于答应了小姐的要求。我把你们安置在小旅馆后，我就去想办法联络社团里的兄弟。在半路上，我被人跟踪了。我被一群日本浪人给围攻了。他们肆意地殴打我，他们把我关在一个隐秘的地窖里，那感觉就像是被人给活埋了。没有人知道我的存在。我喝阴沟里的水，吃香灰。我原以为，就此和人间诀别了。可是，天无绝人之路，就在我奄奄一息的时候，有人发现了我。是当地的农民发现了我，他们救了我。等我醒来得时候，身无分文，衣衫褴褛。半个月后，当我重新走到上海滩的洋灰马路上，再没有人知道我是谁了。那个小旅馆，也被人砸了。我和你们彻底失去了联系。后来，我回到乡下，隐居了。”
“That day, after a struggle within me, I agreed at last to the young lady’s demand. Having settled you all in a small inn, I went off to find some way to reach the brothers of the society. On the road I was followed. I was set upon and surrounded by a band of Japanese ronin. They beat me at their pleasure, and shut me up in a hidden cellar — the feeling of it was like being buried alive. No one knew I existed. I drank the water of the gutter and ate the ash of the incense. I thought that this was my parting from the world of the living. But Heaven leaves no man wholly without a road: just as I lay at my last gasp, someone found me. It was the local peasants who found me, and they saved me. When I came to, I had not a copper on me and my clothes hung in rags. Half a month later, when I walked once more onto the cement roads of the Shanghai Bund, there was no one left who knew who I was. That little inn, too, had been smashed up. I had lost you all utterly. Afterward I went back to the countryside and lived in seclusion.”

> “隐居了多久？”
“How long did you live in seclusion?”

> “大约两年。一次偶然的机会，我和当年在军队中认识的朋友相遇了。因为，战场上我曾经救过他的命，而他当时已升任上海龙华分局的局长了，他很同情我的处境，于是，他介绍我加入了警界。”
“About two years. By a chance meeting I came upon a friend I had known in the army in those days. Because I had once saved his life on the battlefield, and because by then he had risen to be chief of the Longhua sub-bureau in Shanghai, he pitied my circumstances greatly, and so brought me into the police.”

> “于是，就有了您今天的富贵荣华？”杨慕初说。
“And so came all your wealth and rank and glory today?” said Yang Muchu.

> “是的。”
“Yes.”

> “您为什么二十年来，对杨家的灭门惨案一直保持缄默呢？您有权利将凶手绳之以法！可是，您什么都没有做。为什么？”
“Why, in twenty years, have you kept silent about the massacre that wiped out the Yang house? You had the power to bring the murderer to justice under the law! And yet you did nothing at all. Why?”

> 韩正齐无法回答。因为他知道，如果自己将所有“真相”和盘托出，大小姐的冤魂将永生不得安宁。但是，现在少爷在逼自己回答，那就不如成全了故人吧。
Han Zhengqi could not answer. For he knew that if he laid out all the “truth” together on the dish, the wronged soul of the young lady would find no peace for all eternity. But now the young master was pressing him for an answer, and so it were better, perhaps, to grant the one from the old days her wish.

> “大小姐曾经亲口对我说过，杨家的事，一定要由杨家的人来完成。我知道，你们一定都活着，二十年来你们一定朝着预订的轨迹在行走，我没有权利去干预你们的复仇计划。”
“The young lady once told me with her own lips: the affairs of the Yang house must be accomplished by the people of the Yang house. I knew that you must all be alive, that for twenty years you must be moving along the track laid down beforehand, and that I had no right to meddle in your plan of revenge.”

> “这个理由，太过牵强。”阿初从四太太的梳妆匣子里取出一朵银白色的珠花，他仔细地看着珠花的结构。“你看，珠花很漂亮，结构巧妙，状貌雅致。在太阳底下看它，银色的一簇枝蔓会焕发出金黄色的光泽。穿珠子的链子很讲究，不能有偏差，一有偏差，它就散了。就像记忆的链条，不能断，断章取义，故事也就不合情理了。”说着说着，阿初把珠花的链子给扯断了，一颗颗圆润、饱满的珠子跳跃似的四处飞溅。有一颗甚至直接弹到了韩正齐的面颊。“明明是'死'的物件，给它一点生命的活力，它就会以艺术生命的态势复活。同样，明明是脉络分明是事情，你给他设置一点障碍，哪怕是一点点，他就真伪莫辨了。”
“That reason is far too strained.” A-Chu took from the Fourth Madam’s dressing case a silver-white beaded ornament, and looked closely at the make of it. “See — the beaded flower is very lovely, cunning in its make, elegant in its form. Look at it in the sun, and the silver cluster of stems and tendrils will glow with a golden luster. The thread that strings the beads is a nice matter; it can bear no error, and at the least error it falls apart. Just like the chain of memory: it cannot be broken; take the passage out of its context, and the story no longer holds to sense or reason.” And as he spoke, A-Chu snapped the thread of the beaded flower, and the round, full beads, one after another, went leaping and scattering in every direction. One even sprang straight onto Han Zhengqi’s cheek. “A thing plainly ‘dead,’ give it a little living vigor, and it will come alive again in the posture of an artist’s life. Just so, a matter whose every vein and thread lies plain to see — set the least obstacle in its way, even a very little one, and true and false can no longer be told apart.”

> “现实很残酷。少爷，我希望您不要道听途说。”
“Reality is cruel. Young master, I hope you will not go by hearsay.”

> “您认为是我道听途说，导致歧义横生吗？那么，我姐姐的杀身之祸呢？怎么算？他们想要我死。知道吗？您二十年前安闲地从灭顶之灾中全身而退，二十年来对我们姐弟不闻不问。恕我坦率直言，您根本不配让我姐姐怀念了二十年。”
“You think it is my going by hearsay that has bred all this ambiguity? Then what of the disaster that cost my sister her life? How is that to be reckoned? They wanted me dead — do you know that? Twenty years ago you withdrew, at your ease, whole and entire from a drowning catastrophe, and for twenty years you asked no word of us, brother and sister. Forgive my speaking plainly: you are not in the least worthy of the twenty years my sister spent remembering you.”

> “可是这二十年来精神的折磨胜过了肉体上的痛苦。苦不堪言。”
“But these twenty years the torment of the spirit has outweighed any suffering of the flesh. A bitterness beyond all telling.”

> “您为此自责？忏悔？”
“And for this you reproach yourself? Repent?”

> “是的。”
“I do.”

> “一个有勇气自责的人，也就是一个还有救的人。”阿初从梳妆匣子里扔出一张发黄的“拜师帖”，那帖子落在韩正齐的膝前。“我给您三条路走，第一条路，很简单，拿了你二十年前的'拜师帖'，转身就走，我也免了你的三刀六洞。从此之后，彼此路人。第二条路，你现在就把枪掏出来，毙了我。以你现在的地位，你有一万个理由来解释'枪击案'发生的过程。您可以合情、合理、合法的杀了我。从此以后，再没有人来打搅您平静而美满的生活。第三条路，您把这张帖子拣起来，重新交到我手上。从此，听候我的调遣。三选其一。”
“A man with the courage to reproach himself is a man who may yet be saved.” A-Chu tossed out of the dressing case a yellowed “master-pledge card,” and it fell before Han Zhengqi’s knees. “I give you three roads to walk. The first road is very simple: take up your ‘master-pledge card’ of twenty years ago, turn on your heel, and go, and I will spare you the three knives and the six holes. From this day on, strangers to each other. The second road: draw your pistol now, this moment, and shoot me dead. With the standing you have today, you have ten thousand reasons to explain the course of a ‘shooting incident.’ You may kill me by feeling, by reason, and by law all at once. From then on, no one will ever come to trouble your calm and happy life again. The third road: pick this card up and put it back into my hand. From then on, you await my orders. Choose one of the three.”

> 韩正齐选了第三条路，不是因为阿初，而是为了大小姐。他想替她达成所愿，以赎前愆。他把“拜师帖”恭恭敬敬地送回到阿初手上，阿初接过来，说：“过去的事，一笔勾销。你起来吧。”
Han Zhengqi chose the third road — not for A-Chu’s sake, but for the young lady’s. He wished to accomplish her desire in her stead, and so redeem his old offense. He put the “master-pledge card” respectfully back into A-Chu’s hand, and A-Chu took it and said: “What is past is struck off the account with a single stroke. Get up.”

> 韩正齐站起来，听候阿初的吩咐。
Han Zhengqi rose to his feet, and awaited A-Chu’s bidding.

> “你到外面替我寻一处宅子，不要大，尽量隐秘些。我姐姐出殡后，我就搬过去住。其他的事，以后再说。”
“Find me a house somewhere outside — not large; make it as secret as you can. Once my sister is borne out to burial, I will move there. The rest we will speak of later.”

> “好的。”韩正齐应声，又说：“要不要预备几个丫鬟？”
“Very well.” Han Zhengqi answered, and added: “Shall I engage a few maidservants?”

> “不用了。”阿初说。“我习惯自己动手。”
“There is no need,” said A-Chu. “I am used to doing things with my own hands.”

> “听小儿说，您在英国很勤勉，很用功。他们这些留学生都以你为荣。”这倒不是奉承话，的确是韩禹说的。阿初也不否认。“对，我很勤勉。我不像韩禹，有人供养。我得自己养活自己。”
“My son tells me you were very diligent in England, very hard-working. All those students abroad took pride in you.” This was no flattery; Han Yu had truly said it. Nor did A-Chu deny it. “Yes, I was diligent. I am not like Han Yu, with someone to keep me. I had to keep myself alive.”

> 一句话，切中要害，韩正齐很尴尬。
The one remark struck home to the vital point, and Han Zhengqi was much abashed.

> “你去吧。大太太还等着你呢。时间久了，大家都要起疑心。”
“Go, then. The First Madam is still waiting for you. If it drags on too long, everyone will begin to suspect.”

> “是的，少爷。”
“Yes, young master.”

> “以后不要叫我少爷，我们循规蹈矩吧，按帮里的规矩，叫我先生。”
“Do not call me young master anymore. Let us keep to the proprieties — by the rule of the society, you will call me Xiansheng.”

> “好的，先生。”
“Very well, Xiansheng.”

> 韩正齐躬身退出门去，小丫鬟红儿一直在院门口候着他。然后，引领他去见大太太。韩正齐回首看去，院内寂寂无声，他叹了一口气，想着：昧良心出于无奈，只为红颜。他希望少爷不要深究过去，但是，为时已晚。
Han Zhengqi bowed and withdrew through the door. The little maid Hong’er had been waiting for him all the while at the courtyard gate, and now led him off to see the First Madam. Han Zhengqi looked back: within the courtyard all was silent and still. He sighed, and thought: I went against my conscience out of no choice of mine, and all for a lovely face. He hoped the young master would not probe the past too deeply — but it was already too late.

> 阿初此刻仰面看着四太太的遗像，他想问四太太，当年是谁救了自己？自己见韩正齐是经过了精心准备的，谈话内容也是提前酝酿的。韩正齐是没有任何防范的，他的话，不像有假。
A-Chu now lifted his face to the Fourth Madam’s portrait; he wanted to ask her: who was it, in those days, that had saved him? His meeting with Han Zhengqi he had prepared with great care, and the matter of their talk he had brewed up beforehand. Han Zhengqi had been on no guard at all, and his words did not seem to hold any falsehood.

> 阿初相信自己的判断力。
A-Chu trusted his own power of judgment.

> 玄机，不是不可破。
The hidden mystery was not one that could not be broken.

> 需要时间。
It needed time.

> 七天后，出殡的日子到了。
Seven days later, the day of the burial came.

> 荣初以孝子的身份捧着四太太的灵，阿初和韩禹、夏跃春和汤少礼等四人穿着清一色的黑色丧服扶着四太太的棺，荣升和荣华扶着荣荣的棺，一同起灵。整个出殡的队伍，没有旗杆挂灵，没有唢呐吹丧，没有纸人纸马，却显得异常整齐肃穆，引得路人注目。
Rong Chu, in the office of filial son, bore the Fourth Madam’s spirit-tablet, while A-Chu, Han Yu, Xia Yuechun, and Tang Shaoli — four men in mourning all of one unbroken black — steadied the Fourth Madam’s coffin, and Rong Sheng and Ronghua steadied Rongrong’s, and together they raised the biers. The whole funeral procession had no banner-poles hung with streamers, no suona wailing the dirge, no paper figures or paper horses — and yet it seemed uncommonly ordered and grave, and drew the eyes of the passersby.

> 一行人安安静静扶棺走过长街……
The company, all in quiet, steadied the coffins and walked on down the long street…

> 一路上都有巡警在维持秩序……
All along the way patrolmen kept the order…

> 韩正齐默默地跟在最后，目送曾经心爱的女人，走完她人生最后一程。
Han Zhengqi followed silently at the very last, seeing off with his eyes the woman he had once loved, as she walked the final stage of her life’s road.

> 阿初要走了，真正的离开荣家。
A-Chu was leaving now, truly leaving the Rong house.

> 荣升冷眼看着这几天来，家里出来进去的这些人的颜色，这些人都不是等闲之辈。他问都不必问，闻一闻就知道这些人来自江湖。
With a cold eye Rong Sheng had watched, these past days, the look of the men who came and went about the house; not one of them was of the common sort. He had no need even to ask — one sniff, and he knew these men came from the wandering world.

> 他在等，等阿初来辞行。
He was waiting — waiting for A-Chu to come and take his leave.

> 阿初来了，他穿着中式长袍，手腕上翻卷着整齐、雪白的袖口，头发梳理的一丝不苟，脚下是一双布鞋。气度娴雅，气韵如虹。
A-Chu came. He wore a Chinese long gown, the trim, snow-white cuffs turned back at his wrists, his hair combed without a strand out of place, and on his feet a pair of cloth shoes. His bearing was graceful and refined, the very air of him like a rainbow.

> “出息了？”荣升半带嘲讽、半含惋惜地说。
“Made something of yourself, have you?” said Rong Sheng, half in mockery, half in regret.

> 阿初陪了笑，说：“哪里话，少爷。”
A-Chu answered with a smile: “You do me too much honor, young master.”

> “少爷？”荣升不轻不重地甩了一句话出来：“我看你比我还像少爷。前呼后拥的，连警察局局长都抢着替你开车门。”
“Young master?” Rong Sheng flung out a remark, neither light nor heavy: “It seems to me you look more the young master than I do. A whole train of attendants before and behind — even the chief of the Police Bureau vies to open your car door for you.”

> 阿初低了头，不说话。
A-Chu lowered his head and said nothing.

> “这就走了？是吧？”
“Off you go, then? Is that it?”

> “是。”
“Yes.”

> “可惜了。”
“A pity.”

> “少爷，人在江湖，身不由己。”
“Young master — once a man is in the wandering world, he is no longer his own master.”

> “哦？你还知道此去难以回头啊。我平素教导你的话，你还记得多少？”
“Oh? So you know, at least, that once you go this way there is no turning back. How much do you still remember of what I taught you all this while?”

> “句句在耳，字字存心。”
“Every sentence is in my ear, every word kept in my heart.”

> “为人之道？”
“The way of being a man?”

> “为人之道，择善而从。养浩然正气，树松柏节操。不可蔑弃廉耻，媚世随俗。”
“The way of being a man: choose the good, and follow it. Nourish the vast, upright spirit; set up the constancy of pine and cypress. One must not spurn a sense of shame, nor fawn upon the age and drift with the vulgar crowd.”

> “还有呢？”
“And what else?”

> “没有了。”
“Nothing else.”

> “人禽之界呢？”
“And the line between man and beast?”

> “少爷……”
“Young master…”

> “人禽之界，至关大要！”
“The line between man and beast — it is of the very greatest moment!”

> “少爷，你就当自己从来没有教导过阿初，放阿初和光同尘去吧。”阿初诚心诚意地跪下，给荣升磕了一个头。“从此得失成毁，均与荣家无干。”当他站起身形时，荣升从他眼睛里看到了久违的锐气和锋芒。
“Young master — take it that you never taught A-Chu at all, and let A-Chu go to soften his light and mingle with the dust.” In all sincerity of heart A-Chu knelt and kowtowed once to Rong Sheng. “From this day, gain and loss, making and marring, shall have nothing to do with the Rong house.” When he rose to his feet, Rong Sheng saw in his eyes a keenness and an edge long unseen.

> “我知道留你不住，我的话，你也未必肯听。指望你出去后，安分守己，不要为非作歹。这把扇子，是我昨天晚上替你写的，留着做个纪念吧。”
“I know I cannot keep you, and that you will not necessarily heed my words. I can only hope that once you are out there you will keep to your place and mind yourself, and do no evil. This fan I wrote out for you last night; keep it as a memento.”

> “谢谢少爷。”阿初双手接过扇子，说：“阿初告辞了。少爷珍重。”他回转身去，一脸寒霜，步履坚定，衣袂飘扬，如风过柳，走出了“墨菊斋”的大门。
“Thank you, young master.” A-Chu took the fan in both hands and said: “A-Chu takes his leave. Keep well, young master.” He turned about, his face all frost, his step firm, his sleeves streaming like the wind passing over a willow, and walked out through the gate of the Moju Studio.

> 手下人等，依次相随，小丫鬟们静静无声地看着，就像阿初刚回国的那一天。
His men followed after him in their order, and the little maidservants watched in silence, just as they had on the day A-Chu first came home from abroad.

> 荣家大门口，来了九辆汽车，其中三辆是警察局的，一辆是韩正齐的私车，三辆是“金龙帮”的，另外，两辆是社团的“友帮”，专门给“金龙帮”新掌门来捧场面的。
At the great gate of the Rong house nine motorcars had come: three of them from the Police Bureau, one Han Zhengqi’s private car, three belonging to the “Golden Dragon Society,” and two more from “allied societies,” come especially to lend face to the new chief of the “Golden Dragon Society.”

> 仆人阿福看得目瞪口呆。
The servant A-Fu looked on dumbstruck, mouth agape and eyes staring.

> 阿初上了韩正齐的车。他把少爷送给自己的扇子打开，扇面上写了一首诗。那是一首唐代香严閒禅师咏“瀑布”的名句：“千岩万壑不辞劳，远看方知出处高，溪涧岂能留得住，终归大海作波涛。”
A-Chu got into Han Zhengqi’s car. He opened the fan the young master had given him: on its face was written a poem — those famous lines on “The Waterfall” by the Tang Chan master Xiangyan Xian: “Through a thousand crags and ten thousand ravines it grudges no toil; only from afar do you know how high its source lies. How could the brooks and gorges ever hold it back? — in the end it reaches the great sea and becomes the breakers.”

> 阿初想了想，问韩正齐，有没有纸墨笔砚？
A-Chu thought a moment, then asked Han Zhengqi whether he had paper, ink, brush, and inkstone.

> 韩正齐吩咐手下去找，一会儿，从卖字摊上全搬来了。阿初把自己随身的扇子展开，写了一首诗，叫阿福给荣升送去。然后，头也不回地说声：走。九辆车首尾相连、风驰电掣而去……
Han Zhengqi ordered his men to go and find them, and in a little while they had brought the whole set over from a street letter-writer’s stall. A-Chu spread open his own fan, wrote out a poem, and had A-Fu carry it to Rong Sheng. Then, without so much as turning his head, he said the one word: Go. The nine cars, linked head to tail, went tearing off like the wind and the lightning…

> “墨菊斋”里，荣升打开了阿福送来得扇子，扇面上是阿初回赠荣升的一首诗：“一落千丈身飘摇，到底方知出处高。非是溪涧留不住，洗涤乾坤化怒涛。”
In the Moju Studio, Rong Sheng opened the fan A-Fu had brought: on its face was the poem A-Chu had written back to him in return — “Fallen a thousand fathoms sheer, the body tossed and adrift, only in the end do you know how high the source once lay. It is not that the brooks and gorges could not hold it back: it washes clean all heaven and earth, and turns itself to raging waves.”
