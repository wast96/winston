### Flowers on the Tree

The four words "flowers on the tree" do not really mean that a tree will bloom. Say party A has some matter he cannot handle himself and engages another to see to it for him; it is agreed in advance that A will pay not a cent toward the costs or the fee, and that only after the matter is won will a certain percentage of A's gains be handed over to the man who did the work. This is what is called "flowers on the tree." Cases of this sort most often concern debts—engaging a lawyer to demand payment, say, or hiring some man of muscle to collect an overdue debt—and all of them go by the name "flowers on the tree." This kind is legitimate.

There is also an illegitimate kind. Suppose party A has secretly done something against the law, and party B gets wind of it and reports it to C and D and the rest, who band together to extort money from A, threatening that if he refuses they will noise it abroad. Here too B stipulates beforehand that, once A's money is in hand, a certain share shall be paid out as the fee for C, D and the others; and this too is called "flowers on the tree." But A's breaking of the law is a thing for the law of the land to punish; and for these unconcerned parties—B, C, and D—to butt in and extort his money is itself the crime of obtaining money by fraud.
