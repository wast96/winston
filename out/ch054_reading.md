### Posters over the Piss-Pit

Step into any of those public pits to make water, lift your head and take a look, and there are the handbills of the ×× Hall and the ×× Bureau, purveyors of venereal cures, pasted up thick as the stars in reds and greens, till the eye fairly swims. Their bills announce that they treat, as their special line, chancre, gonorrhoea, bubo, syphilis, impotence, leucorrhoea and the whole run of such complaints; and of their art they never fail to promise "root cure guaranteed, childbearing unimpaired," "results the same day, wholly cured in seven," "no need of the knife," "never to recur," and a great many grand names besides — the wording of one Bureau and one Hall so like the next that there is no telling them apart.

Looking at all these gaudy red-and-green handbills over the piss-pits, one grasps how many the venereal doctors are, and how wide the reach of the pox. But by the account of those who know the inside of it, the "miracle" they crow of — "effective the same day, wholly cured in seven" — is in every case worked by dosing with calomel, and the effect is indeed swift; only, those cured by this method are all bound to relapse in time, and once the thing recurs their very lives hang in the balance.

As for that other boast, "no need of the knife" — there too the ground is none too firm! This spring past, a bathhouse attendant, one So-and-so, took a bubo to a doctor of some Hall or other on Dongxinqiao Street in the French Concession to be treated. The doctor set to with the knife; but his hand was unskilled, he severed a blood vessel by mistake, and in a moment the blood ran all over the floor. So-and-so dropped and did not rise, and his life was there and then given up to the shades. After the calamity the daily papers of the day carried the story, and the man's family went to law with the doctor over it; how the case came out in the end there is no knowing. Alas!
