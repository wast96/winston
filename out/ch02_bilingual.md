## H2 Chapter 2. Born at Dawn to Spring’s Light, Wrapped in Mist by Dusk

> 1931年，英国，卡迪芙。
*1931. England. Cardiff.*

> 清晨的阳光洒满了幽静的竹林，阿初和丛惠骑着脚踏车穿过沾染了春色的小径。两个人恣意的笑声回荡在春风里。
The early morning sun poured over a quiet bamboo grove as A-Chu and Cong Hui rode their bicycles down a lane touched with the colors of spring. Their unrestrained laughter echoed on the spring breeze.

> 阿初和丛惠是二年前在英国皇家医学院霍尔曼先生的研究室里相识的。丛惠第一眼看到阿初时，就不自觉地喜欢上了他，阿初淡淡而有神的眸子，聪明又谦逊的语言，甚至略显羞怯地微笑，都深深吸引着丛惠。而丛惠的出现，也使阿初平淡的生活平添了一层斑斓有趣的色彩。
A-Chu and Cong Hui had met two years before, in Mr. Holman’s research laboratory at the Royal Medical College. From the first glance she had at A-Chu, Cong Hui found herself, in spite of herself, drawn to him: his calm, bright eyes, his clever and modest way of speaking, even his faintly shy smile—all of it drew Cong Hui deeply in. And Cong Hui’s appearance, in turn, added to A-Chu’s plain existence a layer of vivid and interesting color.

> 阿初知道，自己和丛惠的差距很大，丛惠是当地富商的独生女，祖上是华侨。而自己既没有地位，也没有上流社会的身份。自己是荣家四太太十几年前从大街上捡回来得孤儿，从小就负责照料着荣家大少爷荣升的生活，说好听一点，他是荣家的养子，说露骨一点，不过是荣家的一个特殊家奴。这个家奴之所以特殊，是因为他受到了良好的教育。
A-Chu knew well the great gulf between himself and Cong Hui. Cong Hui was the only daughter of a wealthy local merchant, her forebears overseas Chinese, while he had neither standing nor any place in polite society. He was an orphan whom the Rong family’s Fourth Madam had picked up off the street more than ten years before, charged from childhood with tending to the daily life of the Rong family’s eldest son, Rong Sheng. To put it kindly, he was the Rong family’s foster son; to put it baldly, he was no more than a household servant of a special sort. What made this servant special was that he had been given a good education.

> 阿初先是陪着大少爷上完了四年私塾的课程，又被四太太送到洋学堂续读了五年的书，其间，选学了西医学。1924年，由于大少爷新娶的大少奶奶意外辞世，受到打击的荣升执意要离开上海，说要去海外发展，在四太太的努力下，16岁的阿初跟随荣升来到了英国伦敦。
A-Chu had first kept the eldest young master company through four years of lessons at a private tutor’s, and had then been sent by Fourth Madam to continue his studies for five years at a foreign-style school, where along the way he had chosen to study Western medicine. In 1924, when the eldest young master’s newly wedded wife died unexpectedly, the stricken Rong Sheng insisted on leaving Shanghai, saying he would go abroad to make his way; and through Fourth Madam’s efforts, the sixteen-year-old A-Chu accompanied Rong Sheng to London, in England.

> 二年后，荣升在英国不但没有丝毫地发展，反而花光了身上所有的钱。就在荣升一筹莫展之际，阿初以优异的成绩考取了英国皇家医学院，并获得当年全额奖学金。在荣升朋友的资助下，阿初和荣升来到了卡迪芙。阿初一边学习，一边打工，在学习和工作中，阿初找到了自信和自尊。荣升不肯回国，他蜷缩在一个租借的阁楼里，消磨着岁月和光阴。
Two years on, Rong Sheng had made not the slightest headway in England and had instead run through every last penny he carried. Just as Rong Sheng was at his wits’ end, A-Chu, by his outstanding results, won a place at the Royal Medical College along with that year’s full scholarship. With the help of a friend of Rong Sheng’s, A-Chu and Rong Sheng came to Cardiff. A-Chu studied and worked by turns, and in his studies and his work he found confidence and self-respect. Rong Sheng would not go home; he curled himself up in a rented garret, whiling away the months and years.

> 丛惠并不知道阿初的过去，她只看到了阿初的未来。丛惠是一个富于同情心，有正义感的女孩，阿初和她在一起的时候，常常得到精神上和心理上的双重愉悦。
Cong Hui knew nothing of A-Chu’s past; she saw only A-Chu’s future. She was a girl rich in sympathy and with a strong sense of justice, and when A-Chu was with her he often found a double pleasure, both of the spirit and of the mind.

> “初同学，您会继续留在医学院霍尔曼先生的研究室里深造吗？”丛惠很关心初毕业后地去留问题。
“Classmate Chu, will you stay on for further study in Mr. Holman’s laboratory at the college?” Cong Hui was much concerned with the question of where Chu would go once he graduated.

> “我想，我还没有做出最后的决定。”
“I don’t think I’ve made my final decision yet.”

> “我很好奇，是什么阻挠您前进的步伐？”丛惠问。
“I’m curious—what is it that holds back your forward step?” Cong Hui asked.

> 阿初无法回答。“我想尽快结束校园生活，并很快就业。”
A-Chu could not answer. “I want to be done with student life as soon as I can, and find work quickly.”

> “钱对您很重要吗？”
“Is money very important to you?”

> “是的。我需要钱。”阿初简洁地说。
“Yes. I need money,” A-Chu said simply.

> “我想到您家里去做客。”丛惠提出了一个新要求。“我想，一个绅士是没有理由拒绝一个女子合情合理的要求的。对吗？初同学？”
“I should like to come and visit your home,” Cong Hui said, putting a new request to him. “I don’t think a gentleman has any reason to refuse a lady’s perfectly reasonable request. Am I right, Classmate Chu?”

> “只怕您去了以后，会受到一些刺激。”
“I only fear that once you’ve been, it may give you rather a shock.”

> “我喜欢刺激。”丛惠的脚踏车回过头来，刹住。
“I like a shock.” Cong Hui swung her bicycle around and braked.

> 阿初的脚踏车头和丛惠的脚踏车头靠在一起。
The front of A-Chu’s bicycle came to rest against the front of Cong Hui’s.

> “瞧，你的车向我的车发出了诚挚的邀请。”丛惠得意地笑。
“Look—your bicycle has sent mine a most sincere invitation,” Cong Hui laughed, pleased with herself.

> 初也笑了。“那么，请您的车随着我的车来吧。”初的脚踏车一下冲出去，丛惠笑着去追。天空底一片朝霞沿着高云飞去。
Chu laughed too. “Then let your bicycle follow after mine.” Chu’s bicycle shot forward all at once, and Cong Hui gave chase, laughing. Overhead a stretch of dawn cloud went streaming away along the high clouds.

> 初和丛惠刚刚走进一条狭小的小巷，就听见一阵强烈、刺耳的尖叫声，接着就是玻璃器皿所发出的尖锐地粉碎声。初知道发生了什么事，他迅速骑车冲进院门，房东太太站在楼梯口，张大着嘴，叫着阿初的名字。阿初把脚踏车往院子里一扔，从房东太太身边掠过，大跨步冲上楼去。丛惠很诧异地架好车，跟随着阿初的脚步，走上楼去。
Chu and Cong Hui had only just turned into a narrow lane when they heard a violent, piercing scream, followed by the sharp shatter of breaking glassware. Chu knew what had happened. He rode his bicycle swiftly in through the courtyard gate; the landlady stood at the foot of the stairs, her mouth wide, calling A-Chu’s name. A-Chu flung his bicycle down in the yard, brushed past the landlady, and bounded up the stairs in great strides. Cong Hui, greatly startled, propped her own bicycle up and followed A-Chu’s steps up the stairs.

> 楼上一片狼藉。荣升是粗暴的，粗暴的令人憎恶。
Upstairs was a scene of wreckage. Rong Sheng was violent—violent to the point of loathsomeness.

> 他发疯似的撕咬一切可以撕咬的东西，不断地撞击一切可以摧毁的家具。他像狼一样地号叫。对一切试图阻止他行动的人，进行谩骂，甚至攻击。
Like a madman he tore and bit at everything that could be torn and bitten, hurled himself again and again at every piece of furniture that could be smashed. He howled like a wolf. At anyone who tried to stop him he flung curses, and even blows.

> “冷静一点。”阿初将荣升拦腰抱住，“冷静一点。”
“Calm yourself.” A-Chu caught Rong Sheng about the waist. “Calm yourself.”

> “你是个贼！”荣升暴跳如雷地用手上的一管箫袭击阿初。“你是个贼！我的烟枪呢？烟枪呢？你想害死我！你这个忘恩负义的贼！”
“You’re a thief!” Raging and stamping, Rong Sheng struck at A-Chu with the xiao flute in his hand. “You’re a thief! Where’s my opium pipe? Where’s the pipe? You want to be the death of me! You ungrateful thief!”

> “他怎么了？”丛惠想援助阿初，制止荣升的疯狂。可是，在靠近荣升的一刹那，险些遭到荣升手上武器的迎头痛击。
“What’s the matter with him?” Cong Hui moved to help A-Chu and check Rong Sheng’s frenzy, but the instant she came near him she was nearly dealt a full blow by the weapon in his hand.

> “小心一点，丛惠。”阿初强有力的手，暂时制服住荣升。“我需要您帮忙。书房的抽屉里有针药，我得给他打一针吗啡。让他安静下来。”
“Careful, Cong Hui.” A-Chu’s powerful hands subdued Rong Sheng for the moment. “I need your help. There’s a hypodermic in the study drawer—I have to give him a shot of morphine to quiet him down.”

> “为什么不杀了我！这对你来说很容易！我为什么要你做医生，因为，杀人对医生来讲很简单。”荣升在挣扎。但是，荣升很清楚的知道，他需要针药的帮助。
“Why not just kill me! It would be easy enough for you! Why did I ever have you become a doctor—because for a doctor, killing is a simple thing.” Rong Sheng struggled on. And yet Rong Sheng knew perfectly well that he needed the help of that injection.

> “他在侮辱我们的职业，阿初。”丛惠大声叫着。“他是个瘾君子！初，你不该留着他。他太危险。”丛惠一边说，一边照着阿初的吩咐去做，她很快拿到了针药。“您应该把他送到戒毒所去，或者，是监狱。”她把灌好针药的针递到阿初的手上。“我竟不知道，这就是你在卡迪芙的家庭生活。”
“He is insulting our profession, A-Chu.” Cong Hui cried out. “He’s a drug addict! Chu, you shouldn’t keep him. He’s too dangerous.” Even as she spoke she did as A-Chu had told her, and quickly had the hypodermic in hand. “You ought to send him to a treatment clinic—or else to prison.” She placed the filled syringe in A-Chu’s hand. “I had no idea this was the home life you led in Cardiff.”

> “你这个恶毒的女人！你给我滚出去！”喘息未定的荣升，试图再次咆哮。
“You wicked woman! Get out of here!” Rong Sheng, his breath not yet steadied, tried to roar once more.

> “小心针！”阿初将针药全部注入了荣升体内。荣升号叫了一声，他似乎已失去了部分战斗力。可是，当他的眼睛扫到丛惠的身上时，依旧喘着气地骂：“滚出去！恶毒的女人！你！你根本不配做医生。”荣升将手上的箫掷过去。
“Mind the needle!” A-Chu emptied the whole dose into Rong Sheng’s body. Rong Sheng gave a cry and seemed to lose part of his fight; but when his eyes swept over Cong Hui he cursed on, still gasping: “Get out! Wicked woman! You—you aren’t fit to be a doctor at all.” And Rong Sheng flung the xiao in his hand at her.

> 强弩之末，箫掉在丛惠的脚尖。
A spent arrow at the end of its flight, the flute fell at the tips of Cong Hui’s toes.

> “初，你是不是对这种场面已经司空见惯了？”丛惠问。阿初却一心一意的将荣升扶上床，“他需要休息。”初说。
“Chu, are these scenes something you’ve grown quite used to?” Cong Hui asked. But A-Chu was helping Rong Sheng onto the bed with all his attention. “He needs rest,” Chu said.

> 当阿初回过头来时，丛惠已经离开了。
When A-Chu turned around, Cong Hui had already gone.

> “请等一等！。”阿初从里面追到楼梯口。“请您原谅他的过失，丛惠同学。”
“Please wait!” A-Chu chased her from inside to the top of the stairs. “Please forgive his conduct, Classmate Cong Hui.”

> “很抱歉，初。我不能原谅他的粗暴和无礼。”
“I’m sorry, Chu. I cannot forgive his violence and his rudeness.”

> “他是病人。他需要我们的帮助。”
“He is a sick man. He needs our help.”

> 丛惠小姐摇了摇头。“不，阿初。需要帮助的不是他，而是您自己。您不应该这样无条件地服从一个精神濒临崩溃的人。不是吗？初同学？”
Miss Cong Hui shook her head. “No, A-Chu. It is not he who needs help, but you yourself. You should not submit so unconditionally to a man on the very brink of a breakdown. Is that not so, Classmate Chu?”

> “丛惠同学，他不仅仅是一个病人，他也是我们荣家的少主人。”
“Classmate Cong Hui, he is not merely a sick man—he is also the young master of our house of Rong.”

> “您说的是您的家庭？他是您家庭的主人？”
“You mean your family? He is the master of your family?”

> “可以这样理解。丛惠同学。”
“You may understand it so, Classmate Cong Hui.”

> “初同学，不，初先生，我想直率地告诉您，您所谓的家庭，正是您急需摆脱的枷锁。我感觉得到，您的家庭虽然远隔万里，却依然有强大的力量束缚着您的心灵。这非常可怕。您的服从、您的温和、您的忍让、甚至您所有的情性都是在您所谓的家庭里养成的，所以，您还没有意识到这种制度的黑暗！您受过高等教育，您的所见所闻，难道还不足以使您觉醒吗？”丛惠很激动。“我为此感到非常遗憾。”
“Classmate Chu—no, Mr. Chu—I will tell you plainly: this so-called family of yours is precisely the yoke you so urgently need to throw off. I can feel it—your family, though ten thousand miles away, still binds your very soul with a mighty force. It is a fearful thing. Your submissiveness, your mildness, your forbearance, even the whole of your disposition, were all formed within this so-called family of yours, and so you have not yet awakened to the darkness of such a system! You have had a higher education; is what you have seen and heard truly not enough to rouse you?” Cong Hui was greatly moved. “I am deeply sorry for it.”

> “丛惠同学，您不必过于激动，我家少爷是一个很可怜的人，离乡背井，孤独无趣，毒瘾缠身。深思其故，也是为情所害。”
“Classmate Cong Hui, you needn’t work yourself up so. My young master is a most pitiable man—far from home and native place, lonely and joyless, in the grip of an addiction. If you think deeply on the cause of it, he too was undone by love.”

> “为情所害？”
“Undone by love?”

> “是的。六年前，少爷曾经有过一次短暂而甜蜜的婚姻生活，少奶奶聪慧美丽，是世间少有的才女。他们非常相爱，爱到不能没有彼此。”
“Yes. Six years ago the young master had a brief, sweet married life. His wife was clever and beautiful, a woman of talent such as the world seldom sees. They loved each other deeply—loved each other so that neither could do without the other.”

> “想必，那个女子离开了他。”
“That woman left him, I suppose.”

> “对。”
“Yes.”

> “为什么？”
“Why?”

> “疾病。”
“Illness.”

> 丛惠小姐停住了脚步，说：“我可以理解他的心情。但是，不能原谅他的做法。自暴自弃，毕竟是懦夫的表现。你认为呢？初？”
Miss Cong Hui halted her steps. “I can understand his feelings. But I cannot forgive what he has made of himself. To give oneself up to ruin is, in the end, the mark of a coward. What do you think, Chu?”

> “我没有爱过。丛惠同学。”阿初答得很认真。
“I have never loved, Classmate Cong Hui,” A-Chu answered in all earnestness.

> 丛惠笑了，说：“我可以预先告诉你答案。有没有兴趣听？”
Cong Hui laughed. “I can tell you the answer in advance. Would you care to hear it?”

> “丛惠同学，不瞒你说，我也想有缠绵婉转的恋爱；我也想有温柔断肠的相思。可是，我只要一想到少爷的痛苦和自残，自己对'爱情'的憧憬就打了一个很大的折扣。自怜的悲哀胜过了'爱'的喜悦。”
“Classmate Cong Hui, I’ll not hide it from you—I too should like a love that winds tender and deep; I too should like the sweet, heart-breaking ache of longing. But the moment I think of the young master’s suffering, of how he has torn himself apart, my own dream of ‘love’ is discounted very greatly. The sorrow of self-pity outweighs the joy of ‘love.’”

> “初，我今天才真正了解了你的内心。”丛惠说。“我相信，'爱'的喜悦一定会赐予一颗善良的心。”
“Chu, only today have I truly understood your heart,” Cong Hui said. “I believe that the joy of ‘love’ will surely be granted to a kind heart.”

> 阿初送走了丛惠，就像送走了心底的一片彩云，自己是不是对丛惠萌发了爱意呢？当他回到阁楼上时，房间里弥漫着的“死气”，又将自己拉回到现实中，荣升蜷曲着身体，低缓地呻吟。
Seeing Cong Hui off was, for A-Chu, like seeing off a patch of bright cloud from the bottom of his heart. Had a love for Cong Hui begun to bud in him? When he returned to the garret, the “dead air” that hung about the room dragged him back to reality: Rong Sheng lay curled in on himself, moaning low and slow.

> 阿初打扫完“战场”，将荣升的箫拾起来，这是荣升心爱之物，在八年异国的生活里，几乎每一夜，阿初都是在这无穷无尽、缠绵悱恻的箫声中进入梦乡的。他把箫小心翼翼地挂上墙。
When A-Chu had finished clearing up the “battlefield,” he picked up Rong Sheng’s xiao. It was a thing Rong Sheng cherished; through eight years of life in a foreign land, on nearly every night A-Chu had drifted off to sleep to that endless, plaintive, heart-clinging sound of the flute. He hung it up on the wall with the greatest care.

> 阿初削了一个苹果，放在果盘上。“您好点了吗？”
A-Chu peeled an apple and set it on a dish. “Are you feeling any better?”

> 荣升冷冰冰地说：“还没有被你害死。”
“Not dead at your hands yet,” said Rong Sheng coldly.

> 阿初知道，荣升态度的优劣与他身体的优劣是呈正比的，显然，他的精神状态已得到了一个适当的缓冲。
A-Chu knew that the state of Rong Sheng’s temper stood in direct proportion to the state of his body; clearly his spirits had been given a fair cushioning.

> “您吃一点苹果吧，这对您的睡眠有帮助。”阿初扶起荣升，荣升挣扎着撑起来，张开略为干燥的嘴唇，咀嚼着苹果片。“她跟你讲什么？”
“Have a little of the apple—it will help you sleep.” A-Chu propped Rong Sheng up; Rong Sheng struggled to hold himself up, parted his somewhat dry lips, and chewed a slice of apple. “What did she say to you?”

> “谁？”阿初问。
“Who?” A-Chu asked.

> “你的同学。”
“Your classmate.”

> “她，她说您的身体正在康复中。”
“She—she said your body is on the mend.”

> “撒谎。”
“You’re lying.”

> 阿初看着少爷的脸色已逐渐好转，言语之间似乎也显得温和。于是，说：“少爷自己难道感觉不到吗？”
A-Chu saw that the young master’s color was gradually improving, and that his words too seemed to have grown gentler. So he said, “Can the young master truly not feel it himself?”

> “感觉到什么？”
“Feel what?”

> “现在你毒瘾发作的时间越来越少，而且，得到了很有效的控制，这是一个良好的开端。”
“The spells of your addiction come less and less often now, and they’ve been very effectively brought under control. It’s a good beginning.”

> “你是不是已经决定继续跟着霍尔曼教授工作？”荣升问得很突然。但是，阿初并不回避这个问题。“是，霍尔曼先生建议我继续留在学院里工作。”
“Have you decided to go on working under Professor Holman?” Rong Sheng asked out of nowhere. But A-Chu did not dodge the question. “Yes—Mr. Holman has suggested I stay on and work at the college.”

> “你的态度呢？”
“And your own inclination?”

> 阿初迟疑了片刻。“说实话，我还没有具体的想法。”
A-Chu hesitated a moment. “To be honest, I have no settled thoughts yet.”

> “你，觉得人生很快乐吗？”
“Do you—find life a very happy thing?”

> “我想，是的。少爷。”
“I think, yes, young master.”

> 荣升鄙夷不屑地“哼”了一声。“人间哀乐，实不可测。”
Rong Sheng gave a scornful “Hmph.” “The sorrows and joys of this world are truly beyond all reckoning.”

> 阿初很快回应了一句。“怨天尤人，亦不可取。”
A-Chu answered him at once. “And to rail against Heaven and blame others is no worthier a course.”

> “你以为现在你功成名就了，就可以嚣嚣于人前了。”
“You think that now you’ve made your name and won your success, you may carry on so brazenly before people.”

> 阿初平静地说：“阿初正像阳光下的春草，不断地发荣滋长。而少爷是已然经历过酽春艳阳的夏花。所以，您对秋霜怀着巨大的恐惧和犹疑。其实，阿初和少爷一样，对前途茫然不可知。唯一的一点坚强，来自于我积极向上的精神。因为，我知道，寒冬过后，会有明媚的春光。”
A-Chu said calmly, “A-Chu is like the spring grass in the sunlight, forever flourishing and growing on and on. But the young master is a summer flower that has already lived through the deep of spring and the blaze of its sun; and so you hold toward the autumn frost a vast fear, a vast hesitation. In truth, A-Chu is no different from the young master: toward what lies ahead I too am lost and cannot know it. My one small strength comes from the striving, upward spirit within me. For I know that after the bitter winter there will come the bright glory of spring.”

> “诡辩！”不过，荣升讲这句话的时候，脸上居然有了一丝笑容。
“Sophistry!” And yet, as Rong Sheng spoke the word, there was, of all things, the trace of a smile on his face.

> 寒冬过后，真的会有明媚的春光吗？
After the bitter winter—would the bright glory of spring truly come?

> 丛惠知道自己的真实身份后，会怎样对待自己？
Once Cong Hui knew his true station, how would she treat him?

> 阿初想。
A-Chu wondered.

> 一个月后，阿初收到了丛惠的邀请函。
A month later, A-Chu received an invitation from Cong Hui.

> 丛惠热忱地邀请阿初作为自己的舞伴，参加医学院的礼拜日舞会。当大红请柬落在荣升的书桌上时，已经是晚上八点钟了，那浓郁的香气和粉红色的信签，使荣升感到阿初即将恋爱。
Cong Hui warmly invited A-Chu to be her dancing partner at the college’s Sunday ball. By the time the crimson invitation card came to rest on Rong Sheng’s writing desk it was already eight o’clock in the evening, and the rich fragrance of it and the pink of the notepaper told Rong Sheng that A-Chu was on the verge of falling in love.

> 荣升站在落地窗前，看着阿初在路灯下洗衣服，看着他帮着怀孕十个月的邻居玛丽亚搬木盆，看着他和房东太太讲闲话，听着他爽朗地笑声，突然，荣升开始羡慕阿初了。羡慕他的自由，羡慕他的健康，羡慕他即将拥有的人生。
Rong Sheng stood at the French window, watching A-Chu wash clothes beneath the street lamp, watching him help their neighbor Maria—ten months gone with child—carry a wooden tub, watching him gossip with the landlady, listening to his hearty laughter; and all at once Rong Sheng began to envy A-Chu. He envied him his freedom, envied him his health, envied him the life he was about to possess.

> 荣升有节奏地敲了敲落地窗，阿初抬起头来，荣升离开了窗子，阿初知道，少爷在叫自己，他有事吩咐。
Rong Sheng tapped a rhythm on the French window; A-Chu looked up, and Rong Sheng stepped away from it. A-Chu understood that the young master was calling him, that he had some order to give.

> 当阿初走进房间时，惊奇地发现荣升居然自己整理了书房。
When A-Chu came into the room, he was astonished to find that Rong Sheng had actually tidied the study himself.

> “您叫我？”
“You called for me?”

> “怎么？你还不打算去吗？”
“Well? Are you still not planning to go?”

> “我，还在考虑。”
“I—am still thinking it over.”

> “考虑？什么意思？”
“Thinking it over? What do you mean?”

> “听说她家里人，也在被邀请的范围内。”
“I hear her family will be among those invited as well.”

> 荣升瞥了阿初一眼。“你不想对她负责任吗？”
Rong Sheng shot A-Chu a glance. “Do you not mean to take responsibility for her?”

> “不是，我不想让她有压力。”
“It isn’t that—I don’t want to put her under any pressure.”

> “没有压力，哪来得动力。你过来。”荣升从衣柜里拿出一套黑色毛料礼服，那是荣升当年穿过的结婚礼服，在当时的上海是最时髦、最昂贵的，就是在巴黎，也算是服装业的精品。
“Without pressure, where does the drive come from? Come here.” Rong Sheng took from the wardrobe a suit of formal dress in black wool—the very wedding suit Rong Sheng had worn in his day, the most fashionable and costly to be had in the Shanghai of that time, and reckoned, even in Paris, a masterpiece of the tailor’s art.

> “穿给我看看。”
“Put it on and let me see.”

> “不太好吧。”阿初有些受宠若惊的感觉，这套礼服像征着荣升的过去，所有美丽的回忆都镶嵌在礼服的扣子里、领子里、袖子里，阿初觉得自己承受不起。更怕自己穿上这件礼服勾起少爷的伤心事，那就得不偿失了。
“I don’t think it’s quite right.” A-Chu felt a little overwhelmed by the honor. The suit stood for Rong Sheng’s past; all his beautiful memories were set into its buttons, its collar, its sleeves, and A-Chu felt himself unworthy to bear it. Still more he feared that wearing it might stir up the young master’s grief, and then the loss would outweigh the gain.

> “我叫你穿你就穿，搁着也是搁着。难道等虫蚀了、毛翻了，再拿出去扔。”
“When I tell you to wear it, you wear it. It only sits there otherwise. Am I to wait for the moths to eat it and the nap to lift, and then take it out to throw away?”

> 阿初穿上礼服，显得英俊挺拔。
A-Chu put the suit on, and looked handsome and upright in it.

> “你瞧这衣服，既合身又贴身，倒像是专门为你定做的。既然丛惠小姐邀请你做她的舞伴，总不能显得太寒酸，今天晚上，也许是你人生新的起点，玩得开心点。”荣升的脸上透出无名的哀愁。
“Look at that—it fits you well, sits close to you, as if it had been made to your measure. Since Miss Cong Hui has asked you to be her partner, you can’t very well look shabby. Tonight may be a new starting point in your life. Enjoy yourself.” A nameless sorrow showed on Rong Sheng’s face.

> “谢谢。”阿初尽量掩饰自己内心的兴奋。
“Thank you.” A-Chu did his best to conceal the excitement within him.

> “去吧，去享受你的人生。”荣升说，“不必为我担心。”他转身上楼去了。
“Go on—go and enjoy your life,” Rong Sheng said. “There’s no need to worry over me.” And he turned and went up the stairs.

> 阿初望着荣升的背影消逝在楼梯的尽头，才感觉原先自己的顾虑有些多余，而此时，时钟指向八点二十分，自己的命运应该由自己掌握。于是，他转过身，迅速走出门，跑步穿过小院，控制不住情绪地大喊了一声，不，不是控制不住情绪，而是有意放纵自己，放纵自由的灵魂。当阿初像旋风一样卷到院门时，正好给房东太太撞了个满怀。
Watching Rong Sheng’s back disappear at the top of the stairs, A-Chu felt that his earlier misgivings had been rather beside the point. The clock now stood at twenty past eight; his fate ought to be his own to hold. So he turned, hurried out the door, ran across the little courtyard, and let out a shout he could not hold in—no, not that he could not hold it in, but that he meant to let himself go, to set his free soul loose. As A-Chu came sweeping like a whirlwind to the courtyard gate, he ran full into the landlady.

> “Sohandsome，guy。”房东太太由衷地发出赞美声。“You must will be the focus of all the women's attention tonight。”
“So handsome, guy.” The landlady sang his praises from the heart. “You must will be the focus of all the women’s attention tonight.”

> “Thanks！”阿初由于兴奋，脸上泛出红色的光彩，他就势和房东太太拥抱，迎着晚霞，迈着欢快的步伐，哼着悠扬的舞曲，向医学院走去。在阿初的眼里，今天的空气格外新鲜，今夜的星空格外灿烂。
“Thanks!” Flushed bright with excitement, A-Chu took the chance to embrace the landlady, and set off toward the college into the glow of the sunset, his step light and gay, humming a lilting dance tune. In A-Chu’s eyes the air today was uncommonly fresh, and the starry sky tonight uncommonly brilliant.

> 当阿初走进灯火辉煌的舞池时，才知道，这是一个由中国留学生主办的晚会，全体到场人员一律用中文交谈，舞曲也是以中国音乐为主，所以，每一个到场的中国人都会有一种亲切感，仿佛这里不是异国他乡，而是在自己的祖国。
Only when A-Chu walked into the brilliantly lit dance floor did he learn that this was an evening hosted by Chinese students abroad. Everyone present spoke only Chinese, and the dance music was chiefly Chinese as well, so that every Chinese there felt a warmth of belonging, as if this were not a foreign land at all but their own homeland.

> 丛惠用最亲切的话语和最迷人的仪态出现在阿初面前。他们像情侣一样在舞池里徜徉，一曲又一曲，从快三步跳到慢三步，他们在情意绵绵中第一次亲密接触到对方的肢体，两个人似乎都有些忘我地陶醉。
Cong Hui appeared before A-Chu with the warmest of words and the most enchanting of manners. Like lovers they drifted about the floor, dance after dance, from the quick waltz to the slow, and there, wrapped in tenderness, they touched each other’s bodies closely for the first time, both of them, it seemed, a little lost in the rapture of it.

> 丛惠挽着阿初的胳膊从舞池里走出来，穿梭在一群衣冠楚楚的绅士和淑女们中间，初看着那些陌生的面孔，听着他们嘴里讲一些新鲜的论题，阿初就有一种莫名的兴奋。
Cong Hui, on A-Chu’s arm, came off the dance floor and threaded through a company of impeccably dressed gentlemen and ladies. Looking at those unfamiliar faces and hearing from their mouths a number of fresh topics of debate, A-Chu felt an excitement he could not name.

> “走，我带你去认识认识我的朋友。”丛惠拉着阿初，挤进一群新锐贵族打扮的人群中。
“Come, let me introduce you to my friends.” Cong Hui drew A-Chu along, pressing into a knot of people got up in the style of a new and dashing aristocracy.

> 此刻，丛锋占据着发言的有利位置，慷慨激昂地说：“1848年2月在伦敦出版的《共产党宣言》就是一篇极富战斗力的政治檄文！它甚至比法国大革命的《人权宣言》更具备号召力！”
At that moment Cong Feng held the vantage of the floor, and was declaiming with fervor: “The Communist Manifesto, published in London in February 1848, is a political manifesto of the most militant power! It has even more force to rally men than the Declaration of the Rights of Man of the French Revolution!”

> “很遗憾，我没有读过这本书。”韩禹说。
“I regret that I have not read the book,” said Han Yu.

> “我手上有一本1888年的英文版《共产党宣言》，可以借给你阅读。”丛锋接着说：“资本主义已成病树沉舟……”
“I have an 1888 English edition of the Communist Manifesto—I can lend it to you.” Cong Feng went on: “Capitalism is already a diseased tree, a sinking ship…”

> “不，我不同意你的观点。”夏跃春打断了丛锋的发言。“私有制是不可能被消灭的！”
“No, I don’t share your view.” Xia Yuechun cut across Cong Feng. “Private property can never be abolished!”

> “先生们，先生们，请原谅我冒昧地打扰了你们的清谈。”有着非常亲和力的丛惠将阿初推到了社交的前台。“认识一下，我最亲密的朋友阿初！他也是我今夜的舞伴！”
“Gentlemen, gentlemen, forgive me for so rudely breaking in upon your discourse.” The warmly winning Cong Hui pushed A-Chu to the front of the company. “Do meet my dearest friend, A-Chu! He is also my partner for tonight!”

> 夏跃春笑着伸出手来和阿初握手。“只要你不是她今夜的新郎，就证明我还有机会。”大家不约而同地笑起来。
Xia Yuechun laughed and put out his hand to shake A-Chu’s. “So long as you’re not her bridegroom for tonight, it proves I still have a chance.” Everyone laughed as one.

> “我叫丛锋，是丛惠的堂兄。早就听说过你的名字，初先生，您是唯一一个连续四年获得英国皇家医学院全额奖学金的中国人。我们为你感到骄傲和自豪。”
“My name is Cong Feng—I am Cong Hui’s elder cousin. I’ve long known your name, Mr. Chu. You are the only Chinese to have won the Royal Medical College’s full scholarship four years running. We take great pride in you.”

> 热烈的掌声毫不吝啬地响了起来，这突如其来得荣耀感，令阿初有些无所适从。丛惠察觉到阿初的窘态，于是，开始转移目标。“我的兄长们，刚才在谈论什么话题？”
Warm applause rang out without stint, and this sudden sense of honor left A-Chu rather at a loss. Cong Hui, noticing his awkwardness, moved to shift the subject. “My dear cousins, what were you discussing just now?”

> “我们谈论的话题是：社会主义和资本主义的利弊。”韩禹答。“丛惠小姐，有什么高论？请赐教。”
“Our subject was the merits and defects of socialism and capitalism,” Han Yu answered. “Miss Cong Hui, have you some lofty opinion to offer? Do enlighten us.”

> “这个题目对我而言，实在是太大了。我现在最感兴趣的是如何在中国建立一个民主的制度。使个人价值得到充分的尊重，从而实现对人权的保护。”丛惠一边说，一边用眼角的余光去探测阿初的表情。
“The subject is really too large for me. What interests me most just now is how to build a democratic system in China—so that the worth of the individual is fully respected, and thereby the protection of human rights achieved.” As she spoke, Cong Hui probed A-Chu’s expression out of the corner of her eye.

> 丛锋已经看出其中端倪，说：“这个题目，应该是针对初先生提出来得。舍妹认为初先生不应该屈服于大家庭的权威。不知道，初先生是否同意舍妹的观点？”
Cong Feng had already caught the drift of it. “This subject, I take it, is aimed at Mr. Chu. My cousin holds that Mr. Chu ought not to submit to the authority of the great family. I wonder whether Mr. Chu agrees with her?”

> 阿初支吾了一下，说：“我已经习惯，甚至依赖着大家庭的权威。如果说，这种权威轰然倒地，我不知道，自己会走向何方？”
A-Chu hemmed and hawed a moment. “I have grown used to the authority of the great family—have even come to depend on it. If that authority were to come crashing down, I do not know where I should turn.”

> 丛锋大声地说：“先生们，有人说，我们这个世纪的主要知识活动之一，就是质疑权威！”
Cong Feng said loudly, “Gentlemen, it has been said that one of the chief intellectual undertakings of our century is to question authority!”

> “你说的权威，是否针对传统的权威？”有人高声问。
“This authority you speak of—do you mean the traditional authorities?” someone asked in a raised voice.

> “对！”丛锋铿锵有力地回答。
“Just so!” Cong Feng answered, ringing and firm.

> “是否包括上帝？”
“Does that include God?”

> “对！也包括上帝！”丛锋极具煽动性的肢体语言令阿初的心底激起了从未有过的波澜和强大的震撼。
“Yes! God too!” Cong Feng’s intensely rousing gestures stirred in A-Chu’s heart a swell and a mighty shock such as he had never known before.

> 那一夜，初在荣升的箫声中失眠了。
That night, Chu lay sleepless to the sound of Rong Sheng’s flute.

> 初和丛惠很快恋爱了。就像天空中的彩云追月，又像俗人们口中常说的烈火干柴，在彼此“爱情”的初级阶段，丛惠是积极主动的。每当丛惠提起要和阿初到巴黎去开一家诊所，阿初就会借故推脱，可是，丛惠是不死心的，她不遗余力地鼓动阿初，要他随自己而去，去开创美好的未来和新的生活。可是，每当阿初要下决心时，眼前就会浮现出四太太的影子。四太太的殷殷嘱托，四太太的慈爱关怀，四太太的希望，甚至四太太的眼泪，都会牵制住阿初那跃跃欲试的越轨之心。
Chu and Cong Hui soon fell in love. Like the bright clouds chasing the moon across the sky, or, as common folk are fond of saying, like fierce fire and dry kindling—in this early stage of their “love,” Cong Hui was the eager, forward one. Whenever Cong Hui raised the idea of opening a clinic with A-Chu in Paris, A-Chu would put her off on some pretext; but Cong Hui would not give up, and spared no effort to urge him to come away with her and build a fine future and a new life. Yet whenever A-Chu was about to make up his mind, the shadow of Fourth Madam would rise up before his eyes. Fourth Madam’s earnest charges to him, Fourth Madam’s loving care, Fourth Madam’s hopes, even Fourth Madam’s tears—all of them would rein in the straying heart that was so ready to leap.

> 阿初可以背叛荣家，但是，不能背叛四太太。哪怕是思想上的背叛。
A-Chu could betray the house of Rong, but he could not betray Fourth Madam—not even in his thoughts.

> 可是，该来得总要来。就在阿初举棋不定的时候，丽水小姐从伦敦来到了卡迪芙。
But what must come will come. Just as A-Chu wavered, poised over the board and unable to move his piece, Miss Lishui arrived in Cardiff from London.

> 江丽水是荣家大太太的远房侄女，少年时父母双亡，大太太极为怜惜，将她留在荣家抚养。丽水自认是林黛玉的苦命，贾探春的才情，薛宝钗的心眼，史湘云的气度。在家里长辈面前总是小心翼翼，在下人面前颐指气使，在姐妹面前尖酸刻薄，比荣荣和荣华两个正儿八经的主子，还难伺候。阿初从认识她第一天起，就认定她是晴雯的嘴，司棋的德行，袭人的面孔，鸳鸯的傲气。所以，当阿初走下楼梯，在院子里看见风尘仆仆的丽水时，居然有些手足无措了。
Jiang Lishui was a distant niece of the Rong family’s First Madam. Both her parents had died when she was young, and First Madam, taking great pity on her, had kept her to be raised in the Rong household. Lishui fancied herself to have the hard fate of Lin Daiyu, the talent of Jia Tanchun, the cunning of Xue Baochai, and the bearing of Shi Xiangyun. Before the elders of the house she was forever cautious and demure; before the servants she was imperious; before her sisters she was sharp and cutting—harder to wait upon than Rongrong and Ronghua, the two genuine young mistresses. From the first day A-Chu knew her, he had set her down as having the tongue of Qingwen, the ways of Siqi, the face of Xiren, and the pride of Yuanyang. And so, when A-Chu came down the stairs and saw the travel-worn Lishui in the courtyard, he was, of all things, rather at a loss what to do.

> “想不到我会来吧？”丽水放下手中的皮箱，“去，替我把车钱付了。”她用手指了指门外停着的一辆汽车。
“Didn’t expect me, did you?” Lishui set down the suitcase in her hand. “Go, pay the fare for me.” She pointed at a motorcar standing outside the gate.

> “你怎么来得？”阿初也不知道自己会问出这样一句话。
“How did you come here?” A-Chu himself did not know how such a question had come out of him.

> “你怕啦？”丽水冷冷地说。“你们一到伦敦，就像泥牛入海。八年没有音讯。要不是我在最新的医学杂志上找到你初先生的博士论文，恐怕我现在，还在伦敦街头讨饭！”
“Frightened, are you?” Lishui said coldly. “The two of you reached London and then vanished like clay oxen into the sea—eight years without a word. If I hadn’t found your doctoral paper, Mr. Chu, in the latest medical journal, I daresay I’d still be begging in the streets of London this very moment!”

> 阿初替丽水付了车费，过来帮她拎皮箱。“你也不怕是同名同姓。”
A-Chu paid Lishui’s fare and came to help carry her suitcase. “Weren’t you afraid it was merely a case of the same name?”

> “要是有名有姓，我还真不敢相信您初先生已经是赫赫有名的英国皇家医学院的博士了。想必，再过两年，您就是一位十足的英国绅士了。忘恩负义的东西！你吃着我们荣家、喝着我们荣家，花着我们荣家的钱，居然，不肯冠上我们荣家的姓氏--”丽水义愤填膺地说着。“我不用想，闻也闻到您初先生厚颜无耻的味道了。”
“If it had had a full name attached, I truly wouldn’t have dared believe that you, Mr. Chu, were already the illustrious doctor of the Royal Medical College. Another two years, no doubt, and you’ll be a thorough English gentleman. You ungrateful thing! You have eaten off the Rong family, drunk off the Rong family, spent the Rong family’s money—and yet you refuse to take the Rong family’s surname—” Lishui spoke, swelling with righteous indignation. “I needn’t even think about it; I can smell the stink of your barefaced shamelessness, Mr. Chu, from here.”

> 阿初并不生气，只是轻描淡写地说：“我从来没有怀疑过您的嗅觉，它甚至比猎犬还灵。”
A-Chu was not angered, and only said lightly, “I have never doubted your sense of smell—it is keener even than a hound’s.”

> 阿初快步走上楼。
A-Chu walked briskly up the stairs.

> “你给我站住。”丽水赶上去，要发作。就听得阿初大声喊着：“少爷，江姑娘来了。”
“Stop right there.” Lishui hurried after him, on the point of an outburst, when she heard A-Chu call out loudly, “Young master, Miss Jiang has come.”

> 荣升手里攥着一管箫，转过身。“表姐？”
Rong Sheng, a xiao clutched in his hand, turned around. “Cousin?”

> 丽水此刻看见荣升，千种委屈爬上心尖，她嘴唇嚅动，一跺脚，一拍胸、一扬脖，大哭起来。“表弟啊！我的表弟啊！我总算找到你了。可怜我那姑父啊，可怜哪！”
The moment Lishui set eyes on Rong Sheng, a thousand griefs crept up to the tip of her heart; her lips quivered, and with a stamp of her foot, a beat upon her breast, and a lift of her chin she burst out weeping. “Cousin! Oh, my dear cousin! I’ve found you at last. My poor uncle—oh, the pity of it!”

> 荣升的脸色变得苍白。“我父亲怎么了？”
The color drained from Rong Sheng’s face. “What has happened to my father?”

> “可怜我那姑父，三年前病故了！”
“My poor uncle—he died of illness three years ago!”

> 荣升手上的箫落地了。
The xiao fell from Rong Sheng’s hand to the floor.

> 姐弟俩抱头痛哭。
Cousin and cousin fell into each other’s arms and wept bitterly.

> 阿初下意识地明白了，自己可以离开了。
A-Chu understood, without quite thinking it, that now he might leave.

> 原来，自从荣升和阿初离开伦敦后，就如断线的风筝，与家庭断绝了音信。大太太在家如坐针毡，时刻不安。丽水于是自告奋勇到英国来寻找荣升，不料，丽水所认识的荣升好友，移民去了加拿大。丽水不甘失败，到处登报，四处刊登寻人启事，可是，徒劳无功。而荣家又传来了荣老爷病故的噩耗，使丽水觉得自己有辱使命，无颜以对荣家。于是，在英国报馆找了个差事干，继续留在英国找荣升。三年来得辛苦，并没有捕捉到荣升的影子。又遇到经济大萧条，报馆裁员，丢了饭碗。就在丽水徘徊在饥寒交迫的边缘时，转机来了。她的一个朋友在英国皇家医学院的杂志上，发现了她要寻找的线索，于是，她通过英国皇家医学院博士通讯录，顺利地找到了这里。
The truth was that, ever since Rong Sheng and A-Chu had left London, they had been like a kite with its string cut, all word between them and the family broken off. First Madam at home had sat as if on pins and needles, uneasy every hour. So Lishui had volunteered to come to England in search of Rong Sheng—only to find that the friend of Rong Sheng’s she knew had emigrated to Canada. Unwilling to accept defeat, Lishui placed notices everywhere, ran missing-person advertisements far and wide, all to no purpose. Then came from the Rong house the dreadful news of Master Rong’s death, which made Lishui feel she had failed in her charge and could not face the family. So she took a post at an English newspaper office and stayed on in England to keep searching for Rong Sheng. Three years of toil, and she had not caught so much as his shadow. Then the Great Depression came upon her; the paper cut its staff, and she lost her livelihood. And just as Lishui hovered on the edge of cold and hunger, her fortunes turned. A friend of hers came upon the very clue she sought in a journal of the Royal Medical College; and so, through the College’s directory of its doctors, she had found her way straight here.

> 丽水来了。她给死气沉沉的阁楼带来了勃勃生气，也给阿初带来了麻烦。首先是她无休无止的对阿初呼来唤去，其次，是她大手大脚的花费金钱，令阿初不能忍受。阿初的出诊费几乎被她挥霍尽了，丽水还觉得理所当然。不过，阿初对丽水还是心存感谢，至少，她坚定了阿初离开的决心。荣升身边有人照顾，阿初也不必背负忘恩负义的恶名，他甚至感激苍天的安排，总要自己走得心安理得。
Lishui had come. She brought a bustling life to the lifeless garret, and she brought A-Chu trouble as well. First there was her endless ordering of A-Chu about; then there was her free and lavish way with money, which A-Chu could not bear. She squandered nearly the whole of A-Chu’s consultation fees, and thought it no more than her due besides. And yet A-Chu was, on the whole, grateful to Lishui, for at the least she had firmed his resolve to leave. With someone now at Rong Sheng’s side to care for him, A-Chu need no longer bear the odious name of an ingrate; he was even thankful to Heaven for the arrangement of it, that he might go with an easy conscience after all.

> 阿初决定和丛惠远走高飞。
A-Chu resolved to fly far away with Cong Hui.

> 阿初和丛惠买了去巴黎的船票，他们购置了新衣物，出发前一天，他们请丛锋、夏跃春等人吃了一席酒，阿初执意由自己付钱。
A-Chu and Cong Hui bought their passage for Paris and laid in new clothes; and the day before they were to set out, they gave a dinner for Cong Feng, Xia Yuechun, and the others, at which A-Chu insisted on paying himself.

> 阿初给荣升写了一封信，并将自己存在银行里的一笔款子取出来，连同信一起放进了一个大信封。他把这封厚厚的信放进了书房的抽屉里。他希望自己走后，丽水和荣升好好利用这笔钱，或者是，尽快回国。
A-Chu wrote Rong Sheng a letter, drew a sum of money he had put by in the bank, and placed it together with the letter into a large envelope. This thick letter he put into the drawer of the study desk. He hoped that after he was gone Lishui and Rong Sheng would make good use of the money—or else return home as soon as they could.

> 为了不引起丽水的怀疑，阿初提前把整理好的行李放到了房东太太的屋里，这样，无论什么时候，他都可以不引人注目的离开。
To avoid rousing Lishui’s suspicions, A-Chu had beforehand moved his packed luggage into the landlady’s room, so that whenever the time came he could slip away unnoticed.

> 可是，正当他一切都安排好以后，却发生了一件意想不到的事。
But just when he had everything arranged, something unlooked-for happened.

> 荣升病了。病得很严重。
Rong Sheng fell ill. Gravely ill.

> 荣升连续发烧，可能是因为父亲的死，使他感到内疚和痛苦，他曾连续在风雨中一整夜一整夜地吹箫，直到他倒下。
Rong Sheng ran a fever that would not break. Perhaps it was that his father’s death had filled him with guilt and grief; he had played the xiao out in the wind and rain, whole night after whole night, until he collapsed.

> 丽水为荣升的病焦虑不安，阿初却没有时间再纠缠下去了。
Lishui fretted anxiously over Rong Sheng’s illness, but A-Chu had no more time to be tangled up in it.

> “我已经给少爷打了退烧针，等他醒了，你给他熬点粥喝。明天，夏先生会来复诊。我跟他讲好了，他不收你费用。”阿初穿上外套，准备离开。
“I’ve given the young master an injection to bring the fever down. When he wakes, make him some rice gruel. Mr. Xia will come tomorrow to see him again—I’ve arranged it with him, he’ll take no fee from you.” A-Chu pulled on his coat, ready to leave.

> 丽水冲到门口，挡住门。“不行！今天你哪里都不许去。”
Lishui rushed to the door and blocked it. “No! Today you may go nowhere at all.”

> “没事的。”阿初并不想和她发生争执。
“It will be all right.” A-Chu had no wish to quarrel with her.

> “我说不行就不行！他在发高烧，你居然要出去。”
“When I say no, it is no! He’s burning with fever, and here you are wanting to go out.”

> “这里又不是监狱，我为什么不能出去。你不要自己吓自己，少爷不会有事的。我是医生，我向你保证。”
“This is not a prison—why should I not go out? Don’t frighten yourself. Nothing will happen to the young master. I am a doctor; I give you my word.”

> “你能保证什么？”
“What can you guarantee?”

> 突然，院子里传来奔跑声。
Suddenly there came the sound of running feet in the courtyard.

> “Chew! Chew! Maria is feeling bad，she is dying！Comeon，let's goto see what，shappening！”房东太太涨红了脸大喊大叫。
“Chew! Chew! Maria is feeling bad, she is dying! Come on, let’s go to see what’s happening!” The landlady, her face flushed crimson, shouted and cried out.

> “玛丽亚要生了。”阿初马上反应过来。
“Maria’s about to give birth,” A-Chu grasped at once.

> “谁？谁要生了？”就在丽水一愣神之际，阿初推开她，走出房门，此时，天上下起了小雨，房东太太为阿初披上一件雨衣，他们一起走进了玛丽亚的房间。
“Who? Who’s giving birth?” In the instant Lishui stood dazed, A-Chu pushed past her and went out the door. A light rain had begun to fall; the landlady threw a raincoat over A-Chu’s shoulders, and together they went into Maria’s room.

> “Help！Help me！I'm dying！”玛丽亚脸色苍白，在床上痛苦地呻吟。血从褥子里渗出来，阿初什么杂念都没有了，打开行李箱，拿出医疗器械，戴上消毒手套，为玛丽亚接生。
“Help! Help me! I’m dying!” Maria, her face white, moaned in agony on the bed. Blood was seeping through the bedding. Every idle thought left A-Chu; he opened his kit, took out his instruments, drew on sterile gloves, and delivered Maria’s child.

> 当新生婴儿的啼哭声划破夜空时，阿初开始在雨地里拼命地狂奔--他甚至连玛丽亚一句感谢的话也没听，他现在需要的是时间，他需要时间停下来，哪怕是一分一秒，自己的世界都会被改变。
When the newborn’s cry split the night sky, A-Chu began to run for all he was worth through the rain—he did not stay even to hear a single word of thanks from Maria. What he needed now was time; he needed time to stop, if only for one minute, one second, and his whole world would be changed.

> 阿初还没有跑到港口，就已经听到了海轮起航的汽笛声。他没有因此停止奔跑的速度，他需要这艘船，他需要丛惠，他需要离开这里，他需要获得自由的新生。
A-Chu had not yet reached the harbor when he heard the whistle of the steamer putting out to sea. He did not slacken his running for it; he needed that ship, he needed Cong Hui, he needed to leave this place, he needed to win a free new life.

> 他跑得精疲力竭，最后摔倒在泥水中，他感到自己永远爬不起来了。
He ran until he was utterly spent, and at last fell in the mud and water, feeling that he would never rise again.

> “初先生，你很不守时。”丛锋提着一盏马灯，出现在阿初面前。
“Mr. Chu, you keep very poor time.” Cong Feng appeared before A-Chu, a storm lantern in his hand.

> “丛惠呢？”阿初站起来，用手抹去脸上的水。
“Where is Cong Hui?” A-Chu stood up and wiped the water from his face with his hand.

> “她走了，她是一个讲信用，守时间的人。”丛锋说。
“She has gone. She is a person who keeps her word and keeps her time,” Cong Feng said.

> 阿初突然觉得丛惠和丛锋的冷酷。“为什么不等我？今天走不了，还有明天。”
A-Chu felt all at once the hardness of Cong Hui and Cong Feng. “Why not wait for me? If we couldn’t leave today, there was still tomorrow.”

> “是我要她走的。”
“It was I who told her to go.”

> “为什么？”
“Why?”

> “因为你根本不爱她！”
“Because you do not love her at all!”

> “不！我爱她！”阿初大声地吼叫。
“No! I love her!” A-Chu roared.

> “不，你不爱她！”丛锋的音频在提高。
“No, you do not love her!” Cong Feng’s voice rose higher.

> “我爱她！！”阿初几乎疯狂地喊叫。
“I love her!” A-Chu shouted almost like a madman.

> “你不爱她！”丛锋的眼睛里透着寒光。“你不爱她，你爱的是平等和自由！不是吗？尊敬的初先生，我没有说错吧。”
“You do not love her!” A cold light shone in Cong Feng’s eyes. “You do not love her; what you love is equality and freedom! Is that not so, esteemed Mr. Chu? I am not mistaken, am I?”

> 阿初被彻底打哑了。
A-Chu was struck utterly dumb.

> 丛锋并没有就此放过他的打算，而是，再次发动攻击。“初先生，您能告诉我，您贵姓吗？”
Cong Feng had no intention of letting him off at that, and launched a fresh attack. “Mr. Chu, would you tell me your honorable surname?”

> 初不回答。
Chu did not answer.

> “如果我没有记错，您应该姓荣。可是为什么你不肯让人称呼你荣先生呢？因为你自卑！你是荣家的家奴！”
“If my memory serves, your name should be Rong. So why won’t you let anyone call you Mr. Rong? Because you are ashamed of yourself! You are a house servant of the Rong family!”

> “不！”阿初此刻想逃。
“No!” In that moment A-Chu wanted to flee.

> “你虽然受过高等教育，却无法摆脱寄人篱下的阴影。极度的自卑造成你极度的自尊！你骨子里恨透了自己的地位和出身，所以，你不愿意让人称呼你真实的姓氏，你更愿意让人称呼你为初先生。表面上你对我们这些贵族子弟谦虚和蔼，心底下不知道怎样地嘲笑和轻蔑我们。你很自私！你之所以'爱'上丛惠，是因为丛惠给了你平等的观念，丛惠给了你自由的空间，丛惠让你感受到了幸福。她把一切都给了你，可是，你为她做过什么？你守着那行尸走肉的少爷，过着清教徒一样的生活，你困守在该死的感恩报恩的儒家思想里，断送掉自己的宝贵的青春。你还想让丛惠也陪着你消耗掉她的一生吗？”
“You may have had a higher education, but you cannot shake off the shadow of living under another man’s roof. Your extreme self-abasement has bred in you an extreme pride! In your very bones you loathe your own station and your own origins, and so you will not let anyone call you by your true surname; you would rather be called Mr. Chu. On the surface you are modest and gentle with those of us born to the gentry, but deep down there is no telling how you mock us and despise us. You are a selfish man! The reason you ‘fell in love’ with Cong Hui is that Cong Hui gave you a notion of equality, Cong Hui gave you a space to be free, Cong Hui let you feel what happiness is. She gave you everything—yet what have you ever done for her? You keep watch over that walking corpse of a young master, living the life of a puritan, penned in by that damnable Confucian doctrine of gratitude and repayment, and throwing away your own precious youth. And now you would have Cong Hui waste away her whole life at your side as well?”

> 初流泪了。“您无权指责我，尊贵的先生。我承认，我爱自由！爱平等！我也爱丛惠！丛惠给了我许多美丽的幻想，我在她的身上，甚至看到了未来家庭的和睦，个人奋斗的目标。但是，我不是一个自私的人，坦率地讲，我在国外待了八年，这八年来，我所接受的教育，带给了我思想上的光明，这不是一时一刻能做到的。也不是令妹所赐予的！我之所以有所彷徨、有所顾虑，是因为我觉得，人在实现个人价值的同时，还需要--有他必须承担的义务和责任！也许，有一点您说对了，我的确是一个家奴，我的出身，使我今生无法和丛惠真正的结合。”
Chu wept. “You have no right to reproach me, honored sir. I admit it—I love freedom! I love equality! And I love Cong Hui too! Cong Hui gave me many beautiful dreams; in her I glimpsed even the harmony of a family yet to come, a goal worth striving toward. But I am not a selfish man. To speak frankly, I spent eight years abroad, and over those eight years the education I received brought light into my thinking—that is not a thing achieved in a single moment, nor is it a gift bestowed by your sister! If I have wavered, if I have hesitated, it is because I believe that even as a man realizes his own worth, he must also—shoulder the duties and the responsibilities that fall to him! Perhaps in one thing you are right: I am indeed a house servant, and my origins make it impossible, in this life, for Cong Hui and me to be truly joined.”

> 丛锋说：“你错了，丛惠和我，从来没有轻视过你。”
Cong Feng said, “You are wrong. Cong Hui and I have never once looked down on you.”

> “这句话就说对了。”阿初平静了。“为什么说'从来没有轻视过我'，难道我应该被人轻视吗？如果我不是这样的出身，您恐怕一辈子都不会有轻视我的念头。你们有意无意地淡化彼此的阶级，但是，无形的压力无所不在。”
“Now that is rightly said.” A-Chu had grown calm. “Why say you have ‘never once looked down on me’—as though I were someone who ought to be looked down upon? Had I not been of such origins, the thought of looking down on me would never in your whole life have crossed your mind. You make light of the difference in your stations, whether you mean to or not; but the pressure of it, unseen, is everywhere.”

> 雨还在下，人却已经麻木了。
The rain still fell, but the man had gone numb to it.

> “阿初，如果我刚才的话，对你造成了伤害，请您原谅我。”丛锋将马灯递到了阿初手上，“她会给你写信的。”丛锋说，“如果你们真正相爱，海是隔不断恋人的。”
“A-Chu, if what I said just now has wounded you, please forgive me.” Cong Feng put the storm lantern into A-Chu’s hand. “She will write to you,” Cong Feng said. “If you two truly love each other, the sea cannot keep lovers apart.”

> 天快亮了。
Day was near breaking.

> 被荣升的病折腾了一宿的丽水，恨不得把阿初千刀万剐了。敲门声响起来，丽水怕是夏医生来复诊了。她急急忙忙对着镜子拢了拢头发，搽搽口红，整理整理衣襟，幽幽雅雅地去把门打开。“是夏先生吗？”
Lishui, worn out by a whole night of Rong Sheng’s illness, could have cut A-Chu to pieces a thousand times over. A knock came at the door. Fearing it was Dr. Xia come to see the patient again, Lishui hastily smoothed her hair before the mirror, dabbed on lipstick, tidied the front of her dress, and went with the gentlest of airs to open the door. “Is that Mr. Xia?”
