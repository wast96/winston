## H2 Chapter 8. Young Yang of Former Days Comes Once Again

> 红色的地板，红得直让人感到晕眩。一双素花蝴蝶结的高跟鞋不停地走在红漆楼板上，发出沉闷的脚步声。
The red floor, so red it made one giddy. A pair of high-heeled shoes with plain floral bows paced ceaselessly across the red-lacquered floorboards, giving off a dull tread.

> 乳白色的灯泡发出柔和的光亮，投射在整齐的书架上。
Milk-white bulbs cast a soft light over the neatly ordered bookshelves.

> “华美书店”的霓虹灯招牌在夜色中分外耀眼。
The neon sign of the Huamei Bookstore blazed all the more brilliantly in the night.

> 荣华穿着一身粉红色的旗袍，站在窗帘内不断地窥视着街面上的情况，也许是站久的缘故，她感觉自己有些吃力，于是，点燃一支烟，深吸了一口，稳定自己焦虑的情绪。
Ronghua, in a pink qipao, stood behind the curtains, peering out again and again at the goings-on in the street. Perhaps from standing so long, she felt herself flagging; so she lit a cigarette, drew a deep breath of it, and steadied her anxious nerves.

> 墙角处放着一口箱子，里面装的是一台简易发报机，她正等待组织上最后的结果，随时准备转移。
In the corner stood a case, and inside it a simple wireless transmitter. She was waiting for the organization's final word, ready to move at any moment.

> 时钟指向九点钟，荣华不再犹豫了，她掐灭了手中的烟，拎起皮箱，向楼下走去。此刻，电话铃声大振。
The clock pointed to nine o'clock. Ronghua hesitated no longer; she stubbed out the cigarette in her hand, picked up the case, and started downstairs. At that very moment the telephone rang out sharply.

> 荣华迟疑了两、三秒，还是拿起了电话听筒。
Ronghua wavered for two or three seconds, then picked up the receiver after all.

> “喂。”荣华的声音刚传过去，电话里就传来了她最熟悉的声音。“喂，林表妹啊……大表哥出车祸了，已经……被送进医院了，幸亏……抢救及时，不然就……惨了，你不用来了，就在家里看家吧。”
“Hello.” No sooner had Ronghua's voice gone through than a most familiar voice came over the line. “Hello, cousin Lin… your elder cousin's been in a motor accident, he's already… been taken to the hospital; luckily… they got to him in time, or else it would have been… a disaster. You needn't come; just stay home and mind the house.”

> “先生，你打错了。”荣华挂了电话，长长地出了口气，上级暂时脱险了，自己的联络站得以保全。她回身放下皮箱，脱了外套。窗外划过刺耳的警笛声……
“Sir, you have the wrong number.” Ronghua hung up and let out a long breath. Her superior was out of danger for now, and her contact station was saved. She turned, set down the case, and took off her coat. Outside the window a shrill siren went slicing past…

> 荣华，中共特科联络员，代号：浮尘，专门负责建立上海与延安的空中通讯，为了掩护自己的秘密工作，她利用家族资金，开办了一家中型书店，设为地下党的秘密联络站。上个星期，由于特科的一个机要员被俘叛变，组织遭受了重创，她的上级老余也被迫暴露了身份，由于荣华直接受老余的领导，她和组织上的其他人没有任何横向关系，所以，只要老余安全，她就绝对安全。不过，老余现在究竟在哪里？荣华想。自己能否帮他彻底脱险呢？
Ronghua—liaison agent of the CCP's Special Branch, codename Floating Dust—was charged with establishing wireless communication between Shanghai and Yan'an. To cover her secret work she had used family money to open a middling bookstore, set up as the underground Party's clandestine contact station. The week before, when one of the Special Branch's confidential clerks was captured and turned traitor, the organization had suffered a heavy blow, and her superior, Lao Yu, had been forced into the open. Because Ronghua took her orders directly from Lao Yu and had no lateral tie to anyone else in the organization, so long as Lao Yu was safe, she was absolutely safe. But where in the world was Lao Yu now? Ronghua wondered. Could she help him get clear of danger for good?

> 夜幕笼罩着长街，星光底行人稀落，老余今夜会在哪里藏身呢？荣华突然灵光一闪，他会不会躲在“财经新闻报”报社的地下室里？那里是“灯下黑”。一想到这里，荣华决定冒险去试试，她快速披上外套，带着手电筒，拎着一个小巧玲珑的皮包，匆匆走下楼去。
Night's curtain lay over the long street, and beneath the starlight the passersby were few. Where would Lao Yu take shelter tonight? All at once a flash of inspiration struck Ronghua—might he be hiding in the basement of the Financial News offices? That was the darkest spot right beneath the lamp. The thought once formed, Ronghua resolved to chance it. She threw her coat on quickly, took a flashlight, picked up a small, dainty handbag, and hurried down the stairs.

> 她有一辆私家车，是大太太送给她的生日礼物，平常停在车库里，她不常用，今天，她把车开了出来，趁着月色，沿着马路，向城西开去。
She had a private car, a birthday present from First Madam, usually kept in the garage; she seldom used it. Today she drove it out and, under the moonlight, went along the road toward the west of the city.

> 三太太和杏儿绕着回廊，走到“梨云阁”大太太的居所。蝉儿正端着盆“兰草”到院子里放下，大太太亲自站在雕花栏下弯着腰浇花，三太太两步并做一步走，拉开细鸭嗓子就咋呼起来了。
Third Madam and Xing'er came round the covered walk to the Liyun Pavilion, First Madam's dwelling. Chan'er was just carrying out a pot of orchid-grass to set down in the courtyard, and First Madam herself stood beneath the carved balustrade, bent over watering the flowers. Third Madam quickened her step, two strides at a time, and pitched up her thin, quacking voice in a great clamor.

> “哟，大太太，您可真清闲，在这里浇花养草的，修身养性。您可没有瞧见那西洋景，真该去开开眼呢。”
“Aiya, First Madam, what a life of ease you lead—here watering your flowers and tending your plants, cultivating body and mind. You haven't seen the foreign spectacle over yonder; you really ought to go and open your eyes to it.”

> “什么西洋景？”大太太气质悠闲地问。
“What foreign spectacle?” First Madam asked, her manner unhurried.

> “您自己去瞧瞧啊。四太太院里可热闹了，又是西洋乐，又是抱着跳舞，还脸对着脸。嗬哟，说出来都丢人。”
“Go and see for yourself. There's quite a to-do in Fourth Madam's courtyard—Western music, and dancing all clasped together, cheek to cheek besides. Ugh, it's a shame even to speak of it.”

> “四太太从来就喜欢这些洋玩意，何况阿初刚回来，他们母子跳跳舞，说说话，有什么稀罕。”大太太风平浪静地说。“你呀，你跟我都是抱残守缺的人，你看不惯他们的做派，你就眼不见为静嘛。何必去干涉他们，讨人厌呢？”
“Fourth Madam has always been fond of these foreign playthings; and A-Chu has only just come home. Mother and son have a dance, a chat—what is there so strange in that?” said First Madam, calm as still water. “You and I, now, we're both of us people who cling to the old broken ways. If you can't abide their manners, then out of sight is out of mind. Why go interfering with them and making yourself disagreeable?”

> “是呀。”蝉儿接话，说：“又要骂，又想看。”
“Just so,” Chan'er put in. “She wants to curse it and to watch it both.”

> 三太太正想骂蝉儿，被大太太截了话。“蝉儿。说话怎么没有规矩。”大太太回头对三太太笑笑。“你来得正好，帮我一起整理整理花圃。”
Third Madam was on the point of scolding Chan'er when First Madam cut in. “Chan'er. Where are your manners, speaking so?” First Madam turned back and smiled at Third Madam. “You've come just in time—help me put the flower beds in order.”

> 三太太把话噎下去，陪了笑说：“我哪里懂这些，横竖陪着大太太乐呗。”
Third Madam swallowed her words and said with a forced smile, “What do I know of such things? At any rate I'll keep First Madam company for the fun of it.”

> “你有这个心就不错了。”大太太说。
“It's well enough that you have the wish,” said First Madam.

> “不过，大太太，有句话我不知当讲不当讲。”
“Still, First Madam, there's something I don't know whether I ought to say or not.”

> 杏儿知道三太太要说些不入耳的话，直扯她衣袖。三太太拿眼瞪她。“干吗不让我说话。”
Xing'er, knowing Third Madam was about to say something unpleasant to hear, tugged at her sleeve. Third Madam glared at her. “Why won't you let me speak?”

> 杏儿赔笑说：“大太太正忙着修剪花草，三太太一唠叨，大太太该剪错了。”
Xing'er said with a placating smile, “First Madam is busy trimming her flowers; if Third Madam prattles on, First Madam is bound to make a wrong cut.”

> “花草剪错了有什么要紧，人要是看错了，麻烦可就大了。”
“What does it matter if a flower is cut wrong? But if one reads a person wrong, the trouble is great indeed.”

> “有话就说吧，不必拐弯抹角的。”大太太说。
“Say what you have to say; there's no need to beat about the bush,” said First Madam.

> “我说的是这个阿初，到底是个来路不明的人。他现在吃的、喝的、用的，哪一样不跟大少爷一样？他还有个博士头衔。我们大少爷是什么人啊？大少爷心地善良，不争这个名啊利的。有朝一日，他阿初得了志，还不把荣家给活生生地硬吞了？”
“What I mean is this A-Chu—when all's said, he's a man of unknown origins. What he eats, what he drinks, what he uses—in which of them is he not now the equal of the eldest Young Master? He's even got a doctor's title. And what sort of man is our eldest Young Master? Kind-hearted, and not one to contend for name or gain. One fine day, when this A-Chu comes into his own, won't he swallow the Rong family whole, alive and struggling?”

> 大太太不说话，眼瞅着花草，微微一笑。
First Madam said nothing; her eyes on the flowers, she gave a faint smile.

> “大太太，您笑什么？”三太太很是诧异。
“First Madam, what are you smiling at?” Third Madam was quite taken aback.

> 大太太说：“花再俗气，那也是花。草再名贵，终究是草。人常说：花草花草，花永远都排在草前面。你懂吗？”
First Madam said, “However common a flower may be, it is still a flower. However precious a grass, it is grass in the end. As the saying goes: flowers-and-grass, flowers-and-grass—the flower always comes before the grass. Do you understand?”

> “懂？懂，懂！可不是这个理吗。”三太太笑得脸上的肉都颤起来。
“Understand? Understand, understand! Isn't that just the way of it.” Third Madam laughed until the very flesh of her face quivered.

> 杏儿和蝉儿都低了头，再不吭气了。
Xing'er and Chan'er both lowered their heads and said not another word.

> “红梨阁”里的阿初和四太太并没有听见大太太的高论，他们坐在院子里喝茶，茶水是新沏的“龙井”，四太太的贴身丫鬟红儿站在檐前伺候。
In the Hongli Pavilion, A-Chu and Fourth Madam had not heard First Madam's lofty discourse. They sat in the courtyard drinking tea—freshly steeped Longjing—while Fourth Madam's own maid, Hong'er, waited on them before the eaves.

> 四太太告诉阿初，清明节去“慈云寺”烧香，要阿初一道去寺庙谢菩萨，敬祖宗，阿初虽然不迷信，不过，四太太要自己去，自己一定是去的。正说话间，忽听一阵爽朗的笑声，四太太就知道谁来了。稀罕地说：“大小姐今天怎么没出门？”
Fourth Madam told A-Chu that at the Qingming Festival she would go to the Ciyun Temple to burn incense, and she wanted A-Chu to come along to give thanks to the Bodhisattva and pay respects to the ancestors. Though A-Chu was not superstitious, if Fourth Madam wished him to go, go he certainly would. As they were talking, all at once a peal of hearty laughter was heard, and Fourth Madam knew at once who had come. “How is it,” she said, marveling, “that the eldest young lady hasn't gone out today?”

> “哟，我还没进门，四姨娘就打算往外撵我了。我是不速之客，闻茶香一路追踪而来，这么好的茶，四姨娘怎么没准备我一份？”荣荣一边说，一边走进来。红儿立即替她拿了帽子和披肩。
“Aiya, I haven't even stepped through the door and Fourth Aunt's already thinking of driving me out. I'm an uninvited guest—I followed the scent of tea all the way here. Such fine tea, and Fourth Aunt hasn't set aside a share for me?” So saying, Rongrong came in. Hong'er at once took her hat and shawl for her.

> “大小姐好。”阿初站起来给荣荣请安。
“Good day, young mistress.” A-Chu rose to pay his respects to Rongrong.

> “民国了，都民国了。”荣荣嚷嚷着。“没什么可拘礼的。你还以为是前清啊？让我细瞧瞧，我们家也能出个博士？”
“It's the Republic now, the Republic,” Rongrong clamored. “No call to stand on ceremony. Do you still fancy it's the old Qing? Let me have a good look at you—so our family can turn out a doctor too?”

> 阿初被她说的不好意思，倒也不回避她挑剔的目光，站直了说：“大小姐，几年没见了，还这么急风暴雨的？”
A-Chu was made bashful by her words, yet he did not shrink from her critical gaze; standing straight, he said, “Young mistress, some years since we last met, and still such wind and rainstorm as ever?”

> “天生的脾气，改不了。”荣荣笑得很灿烂。“简直不敢相信，阿初活像是洋画里的绅士。就算我告诉别人，他是荣家大少爷，别人也会信的！”
“It's the temper I was born with; there's no changing it.” Rongrong laughed radiantly. “I simply can't believe it—A-Chu looks the very image of a gentleman out of a foreign painting. Even if I told people he was the eldest Young Master of the Rong house, they'd believe it!”

> “大小姐，这种话可不能乱讲。被人听去，添油加醋的告诉大太太，我日子就难过了。”
“Young mistress, that's not a thing to say lightly. If someone overheard and carried it to First Madam, all embellished, my days would be hard.”

> “我还以为你闻风相悦呢，这么没胆色？亏你还留洋呢。”荣荣奚落阿初。
“And here I thought you'd be delighted at the very hint of it—so timid as that? And you a returned student, too.” Rongrong mocked him.

> “一码归一码。”阿初坐下来说：“狸猫换不了太子。”
“One thing is one thing, another is another,” said A-Chu, sitting down. “A leopard-cat can't be passed off for the crown prince.”

> “还说，你自己就是个狸猫换太子的典故。”此言一出口，荣荣立马掌了一下自己的嘴，说：“该死，该死。四姨娘，我无心提及你的伤心事。”
“And you still say it—you're a living instance of the leopard-cat-for-the-crown-prince tale yourself.” The words no sooner out than Rongrong clapped a hand over her own mouth. “Wicked of me, wicked. Fourth Aunt, I didn't mean to bring up your sorrow.”

> 四太太淡然一笑。“过去的事情，我忘得差不多了。你难得露面，今天为什么有兴趣过来？”
Fourth Madam smiled faintly. “Things past—I've all but forgotten them. You show yourself so seldom; what's put you in the humor to come today?”

> “没事还不许我串串门啊？”荣荣靠在四太太椅背上坐下。
“Mayn't I drop in for a visit without some errand?” Rongrong leaned against the back of Fourth Madam's chair and sat.

> “一定有事。说吧，看我能不能帮忙？”
“You've certainly some errand. Out with it—let's see whether I can help.”

> “当然能帮忙。明天晚上，我要去参加一个豪华舞会。我想借四姨娘的洋装礼服穿，还有，你的那双水晶舞鞋。”
“Of course you can help. Tomorrow evening I'm to go to a grand ball. I'd like to borrow Fourth Aunt's Western evening gown—and those crystal dancing shoes of yours as well.”

> 四太太打趣地说：“你想当'灰姑娘'啊？”
Fourth Madam said teasingly, “You want to play Cinderella?”

> “我原本就是贵族小姐，不，是尊贵的王子殿下。'灰姑娘'嘛，就由阿初来扮吧。”
“I'm a noble young lady to begin with—no, a noble prince, His Highness. As for Cinderella, let A-Chu play that part.”

> “干吗？拉我一起疯？”阿初抗议。
“What? Drag me into your madness?” A-Chu protested.

> “你敢说，你从小到大没和我疯过吗？”荣荣不依。
“Dare you say you never once ran wild with me, all the years we were growing up?” Rongrong would not let it go.

> “你不是有好几个舞伴吗？要阿初去做什么？他又不习惯那种场合。”四太太帮阿初推脱。
“Haven't you several dancing partners already? What do you want A-Chu for? He isn't used to that sort of occasion.” Fourth Madam made excuses on A-Chu's behalf.

> “舞伴嘛，多多益善。”荣荣开始跟四太太撒娇。
“Dancing partners—the more the merrier.” Rongrong began to wheedle Fourth Madam.

> “去吧，去吧。我怕了你了。”四太太松了口，叫丫鬟去拿礼服和舞鞋。红儿应声去了。
“Go, then, go. I give in to you.” Fourth Madam relented, and told the maid to fetch the gown and dancing shoes. Hong'er went off at her word.

> “到底是哪一家举行豪华舞会啊？我认不认识？”阿初问。
“Whose house is giving this grand ball, after all? Would I know them?” A-Chu asked.

> “你听说过上海金融界的杨家吗？”
“Have you heard of the Yang family of Shanghai finance?”

> “杨家？好像是开银行的吧？”阿初的话还没说完，只听见“啪”的一声，四太太手里的茶碗盖摔碎了。茶水直扑到素袍上，水淋漓地浸染着袍上绣得莲花。
“The Yangs? Bankers, aren't they?” Before A-Chu had finished speaking, there came a sharp crack—the lid of the tea bowl in Fourth Madam's hand had shattered. The tea splashed straight onto her plain gown, and the water soaked, dripping, into the lotus flowers embroidered upon it.

> 红儿胳膊上挂着礼服，手里拎着一双华丽的水晶鞋，满面愕然地站在院子里。
Hong'er, the gown over her arm and a pair of splendid crystal shoes in her hand, stood in the courtyard, her face all astonishment.

> “怎么了？”阿初上前问四太太。“您不舒服吗？或者，明天我不去了？留在家陪您。”
“What is it?” A-Chu came forward to ask Fourth Madam. “Are you unwell? Or—shall I not go tomorrow, and stay home to keep you company?”

> 四太太缓过神来，说：“为什么不去？我已经病怏怏二十几年了，现在也该换换别人不舒服了。”
Fourth Madam collected herself and said, “Why should you not go? I've been ailing these twenty-odd years; it's high time someone else was made uneasy for a change.”

> 阿初和荣荣没听懂四太太的话，只当她累了，需要好好地休息。
Neither A-Chu nor Rongrong grasped Fourth Madam's meaning; they took it only that she was tired and needed a good rest.

> 荣华的汽车在一片柳荫底熄了火，这里离“财经新闻报”报社不到一百米。她下了车，锁好车门，低着头从报社后门穿了进去。
Ronghua's car came to a stop in a stretch of willow shade, less than a hundred meters from the offices of the Financial News. She got out, locked the door, and, head bowed, slipped in through the back entrance of the newspaper.

> “财经新闻报”的报社是和几家报馆联租的一座大楼，楼层不高，但空间宽阔。由于报馆人多嘴杂，又没什么特值钱的东西，所以，整栋大楼只请了两个护卫人员，一个白班，一个夜班。基本上他们都呆在一楼护卫室休息，不大轻易走动。荣华熟门熟路地摸进去，没有惊动护卫员，她很快进入到大楼的地下室，她和老余曾经利用这里发过报。
The offices of the Financial News were in a building leased jointly with several other newspaper houses—not many stories high, but broad within. Since the press houses were full of people and loose tongues, and there was nothing of great value about, the whole building kept only two guards, one on the day shift and one on the night. For the most part they stayed resting in the guard room on the ground floor and did not stir about much. Ronghua, who knew the way well, felt her way in without alerting the guard, and was soon down in the building's basement, where she and Lao Yu had once used the place to transmit.

> 她娴熟地启动了密门，打开手电筒，轻轻地走了进去。潮湿阴暗的空气蕴涵着腐草的气味，她顺着弯曲的巷道前行，她闻到了血腥味……
With practiced ease she worked the hidden door, switched on her flashlight, and stepped softly in. The damp, dark air held the smell of rotting grass; she went forward along the winding passage—and caught the reek of blood…

> 荣华停住了脚步，辨别了一下方向，从精致的提包里取出了手枪，并熟练地将子弹上了膛。
Ronghua halted, made out her bearings, drew a pistol from her dainty handbag, and deftly chambered a round.

> “是谁？”一个及其虚弱的声音问。
“Who's there?” asked a voice, extremely weak.

> “老余？”荣华准确地判断出老余的声音。她跑过去。“你怎么样？”
“Lao Yu?” Ronghua knew his voice at once. She ran over. “How are you?”

> 老余浑身是血躺在地上。

> “荣荣，你的鞋子很精美啊，这种样式真不常见。”思桐暗讽荣荣穿的鞋子样式土气。
“Rongrong, your shoes are exquisite—one really doesn't often see this style.” Sitong was slyly mocking the shoes as countrified.

> “怎么？你也对我的鞋子感兴趣？这可是真水晶制作的。”
“Oh? You're interested in my shoes too? These are made of real crystal.”

> “与其说我对你的鞋感兴趣，不如说我对你的舞伴更感兴趣。你不觉得你应该介绍给我们认识认识？”
“It's not so much your shoes I'm interested in as your dancing partner. Don't you think you ought to introduce him to us?”

> “这好办，阿初……”等荣荣回头叫阿初的时候，阿初不见了。失踪了。
“That's easily done—A-Chu…” But when Rongrong turned to call A-Chu, A-Chu was gone. Vanished.

> 有一种冲动，莫名其妙的冲动，促使阿初盲目地向“秘密”的边缘走过去。随着梦中常见的景物一步一步推进，他控制不住兴奋的血液在身体中潜滋暗长。他穿过狭窄的竹道，看到一座年久失修的佛堂。黑色的两扇门虚掩着，门上长满了青苔，门环被露水润湿了。这里蕴涵着一种独特的幽静，是任何喜气的氛围都渲染不到之处。阿初不知为什么，有一种强烈的好奇心迎面袭来，驱使自己去推开这两扇门。不，不仅仅是好奇心，仿佛是一种欲望，是他一踏进杨家大门就想要知道的一种不可思议的欲望。他想要知道些什么呢？或是得到些什么？他不知道。他推开了门--
An impulse—an inexplicable impulse—drove A-Chu blindly toward the edge of the “secret.” As the scenes so often seen in dreams advanced upon him step by step, he could not master the excited blood quickening and stirring within him. He passed through the narrow bamboo path and came upon a Buddhist shrine hall long fallen into disrepair. Its two black doors stood ajar, overgrown with moss, the door-rings moistened with dew. The place held a stillness all its own, one no festive air could ever tinge. For some reason A-Chu felt a powerful curiosity rush upon him, driving him to push open those two doors. No—not curiosity alone, but something like a desire, an unfathomable desire he had longed to satisfy from the moment he set foot inside the Yang family gate. What was it he wished to know? Or to gain? He did not know. He pushed the doors open—
