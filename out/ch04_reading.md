# China's King of Assassins: Wang Yaqiao

## Chapter Four: Facing Down the Three Big Bosses of Shanghai

### 11. Wang Yaying Says: "Better to Die on Your Feet Than Live on Your Knees!"

"Idiot! The fire hasn't reached the roof yet — what are you in such a panic about? Or is there someone left on the Shanghai waterfront who dares treat Wang Jiuguang as less than a man?"

Wang Yaying wanted the riverside news faster than that. "Never mind the panic — get it out plainly for the Ninth Master. We are Hatchet Gang men; we are not the sort who lose our nerve. Why should a breath of wind through the grass leave you unable to hold yourself together?"

"Ninth Master — madam — what I mean is, the Jiang'an has fallen into Du Yuesheng's hands." The junior chief's face was still wild with it. "We got to the bank and looked, and the whole crew of the Jiang'an had been changed out for Du Yuesheng's people. Deckhands, captain, every one of them Du Yuesheng's disciples. They say Li Guojie promised the Jiang'an to Du Yuesheng long ago. So Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai flew into a rage, and since neither side would give an inch, they've come to blows on the Wusong wharf!"

"What? Li Guojie dared marry one daughter to two husbands?" Wang Yaqiao took the report with a hidden start; the color drained from his face and left it white, and the anger came up in him like a jet of flame. Though it was less that he hated Li Guojie for double-dealing and stirring the trouble deliberately than that he had never been able to stomach Du Yuesheng — the great personage who lorded it over Shanghai on the strength of Chiang Kai-shek's power.

From the junior chief's account Wang Yaqiao learned what had happened barely an hour before. When Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai led a dozen-odd Hatchet Gang men to the Wusong wharf to take delivery of the Jiang'an — Li Guojie's return gift for the killing of Zhao Tieqiao — they found to their astonishment that the freighter, which had lain at the bank for weeks, had steam up and was ready to sail. Taking the men aboard for the crew Li Guojie had sent to hand the ship over, Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai led their wholly unprepared disciples happily up the gangway. And then, before they had their feet under them, came the coarse bawling: "What kind of turtle-spawn bastards are you, that you dare climb aboard our Master Du's ship as you please? What are you staring at? And you still dare swagger about on Master Du's deck with your noses in the air?"

Only then did Xuan Jimin understand that the ground had shifted. The man coming at them full of bluster was that famous Shanghai waterfront rough, Liu Ada — a fixture at Du Yuesheng's gate for years, a piece of low cunning everybody knew. Xuan Jimin knew better than to provoke him, and stepped forward to explain, pleasantly enough, the whole history of Li Guojie's transfer of the ship. But Liu Ada would hear none of it, and answered him with his chin in the air: "Li Guojie wrote your Wang Yaqiao a deed of transfer, did he? He wrote our Master Du the same deed, and earlier. Understand: Li Guojie made this Jiang'an over to our Master Du first. You came a step too late. Keep your hands off!"

Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai had no wish to blow the thing up; they wore their throats out urging Liu Ada to yield the Jiang'an. But Liu Ada did not count Wang Yaqiao's men as worth a glance. He crowed at them: "His mother's! When I, Liu Ada, was making my way on the Shanghai waterfront, you Anhui bumpkins were still hoeing dirt in the countryside. And now you dare set up court against the great and famous Master Du? Tell Wang Yaqiao to piss and look at his own reflection — what is he? This Jiang'an has been Master Du's for certain, and if any one of you dares come up here running his mouth, don't blame Liu Ada for dropping his manners!"

So the two sides stood locked. And though neither Xuan Jimin nor Wu Hongtai wanted an open break with Du Yuesheng's people, the men who had come at their backs were a crowd of Anhui bruisers who would kill at a word. Out of that crowd sprang a bare-chested giant they called Pig-head San Leng, who back in the Anhui days had been the most willing fighter Wang Yaqiao had; when the Hatchet Gang first made its name in Shanghai, Pig-head San Leng had been one of the founding hands. Through all the years since, though Wang Yaqiao had pulled the bulk of his men back to Anhui, Pig-head San Leng had held his post at the Anhui Guildhall. He knew the Shanghai ground well. And seeing Du Yuesheng's man Liu Ada dare hold the Hatchet Gang's own chief at nothing, he went up like dry grass, rushed forward, took Liu Ada by the front of his coat and roared into his face: "Liu Ada! A dog trading on his master's power! Don't imagine that leaning on a Du Yuesheng makes us less than men! If this Jiang'an isn't cleared out for us today, this deck is where you die!"

But Liu Ada had Du Yuesheng at his back, and would not count a Pig-head San Leng as anything. He gave a great shout, flung himself forward, and laid a backhanded blow across the man's face that brought the blood into his mouth. The fire went up in Pig-head San Leng; no urging from Xuan Jimin or Wu Hongtai could reach him now, and the two of them went at each other with fists and boots on the deck of the Jiang'an. At that the ship's crew closed in, and stood off against the disciples Wang Yaqiao had sent to take delivery. Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai wore themselves out trying to stop it and could not. And when the men saw Pig-head San Leng with the blood spraying from his mouth and nose under Liu Ada's fists, which of them could stand and watch coolly? Even Wu Hongtai could bear it no longer; with a great shout he threw himself in, and the brawl went up hotter from there.

Wang Yaqiao sat on his couch, stunned, thinking how a good piece of business had gone and had a Cheng Yaojin leap out of the roadside at it. Had it been anyone else contesting the Jiang'an with him, he would have ordered it taken back on the spot; but with Du Yuesheng's people doing the taking, the decision came hard. In strength and in numbers he knew perfectly well the Hatchet Gang was no match for Du Yuesheng. Especially since these last years, when in Bo Wenwei's service he had pulled most of the Hatchet Gang back to Anhui, his disciples in Shanghai numbered only a few dozen. And yet Wang Yaqiao could not swallow it. If he ordered the brothers already aboard the Jiang'an withdrawn, then not only would he never have the ship that was rightly his — worse by far, the Hatchet Gang's name in Shanghai would be swept into the dirt, and its days of standing tall would be finished. Between what his gang's future required and what the present humiliation demanded, the fire in Wang Yaqiao ran ten thousand feet high, and he found he could neither fight Du Yuesheng nor withdraw from him. And while he stood on that knife-edge, more footsteps sounded outside — and this time it was Xuan Jimin who burst in.

"Ninth Master — we've met a strong enemy! Pig-head San Leng should never, never have gone at Liu Ada without a thought for what came after. He was one man against a crowd; Du Yuesheng's roughs swarmed him and a boot caught him in a vital place. He's dead — dead on the Jiang'an!"

"What—?" In all these years of knocking about the world Wang Yaqiao had met dangerous enemies and vicious men many times over, and had always found a way through by his own wits and nerve. Today's business left him, for once, with nothing in his hands. Had he known that this freighter of Li Guojie's would trespass on Du Yuesheng, he would sooner have refused it than let a trifle bring him into collision with the man. But nothing could be called back now — and Pig-head San Leng's wretched death had pushed Wang Yaqiao onto ground with no road behind it. The choice in front of him was ugly: either fight it out to the death with the vast power of Du Yuesheng, or take the old counsel that a wise man does not eat the loss in front of him — swallow the insult, sound the gongs and recall his men, and slink back to Hefei at the head of a beaten remnant. And if he did that, the Hatchet Gang might keep its strength for the moment; but he would have lost forever the ground it stood on in Shanghai.

"Yaying — tell me what to do now." Wang Yaqiao, with no decision in him, turned to his wife for counsel.

Wang Yaying said nothing.

He paced the room with his mind in disorder. "If we fight Du Yuesheng now, we are certainly the weaker. But if we run, the consequences are worse than I can think about — who would ever count the Hatchet Gang as men again?"

Xuan Jimin, thinking of the men sent by Du Yuesheng to take the ship, said with real worry: "Li Guojie deserves to die. If he hadn't married the one daughter to two husbands, where would this collision have come from? Either he had some difficulty of his own, or he meant the Jiang'an to set us and Du Yuesheng at each other's throats, so that in the end our two houses would cut each other to pieces and both come away ruined. It seems Li Guojie was not only Zhao Tieqiao's enemy but the common enemy of us all! Ninth Master — Du Yuesheng has years and deep roots in Shanghai, he has Chiang Kai-shek behind him politically, and Zhang Xiaolin and Huang Jinrong are close friends of his. You tell me: these few dozen Hatchet Gang men of ours — even if every one were Sun Wukong himself, how do we hold out against numbers like his?"

"Meaning," said Wang Yaqiao, "… we run?"

"Not run, exactly. I mean a wise man doesn't eat the loss in front of him. Rather than let Du Yuesheng trample the Hatchet Gang into ruin, better to step aside from his edge for now and go back to Anhui to build our strength. When our feathers have grown in again and we have our power back, it won't be too late to come down on Shanghai and claim the ship."

Wang Yaqiao made no answer. Plainly the trouble had backed him into a dead-end alley with no way forward and no way out. And then he turned his eyes on the wife who had been silent so long. "Yaying — don't tell me you too favor running back to Anhui?"

"No, Jiuguang!" Nobody had expected a woman, at a moment hanging by a single hair, to say what she said next, and it astonished Wang Yaqiao. "A mere woman I may be, but I know the principle of it: better to die on your feet than live on your knees. Du Yuesheng has pressed his way right up to our heads. What use is being afraid of him now? And if you really do run back to Anhui, you will not only never come back to Shanghai — I doubt you'll be able to hold your head up in Anhui either!"

"Good! Well said! That is what my wife should say!" The spirit came back into Wang Yaqiao at a stroke; whatever shred of fear had still been in him, Wang Yaying's words scoured it away. He sprang to his feet, set his hands on his hips, and said to Xuan Jimin: "Jimin — are we to have less nerve between us than one woman? It is not the Hatchet Gang making trouble for Du Yuesheng. It is that Du who is using his weight to crush men! I, Wang Yaqiao, would rather fight and die than lose at his feet. Get word to the brothers in Anhui, now — if they still count Wang Jiuguang as worth anything, let them all come to Shanghai at once! Damn it, this time I will take back the Jiang'an if it costs me my life!"

So Xuan Jimin and Wang Yaying divided the work and wired the Hatchet Gang disciples scattered through Hefei, Anqing, Jiujiang, Nanjing, Changzhou, Suzhou, Wuxi and Wuhu, calling them to Shanghai overnight to the rescue. That same night, several hundred Hatchet Gang men reached the city on the news, and the Anhui Guildhall, still and quiet for years, filled inside and out with a tide of people and a noise like thunder. The show of strength shook the city. By first light the guildhall was crowded past holding; six or seven hundred disciples at the least had come in from every quarter. And in Anhui, General Bo Wenwei, hearing that Wang Yaqiao was in trouble in Shanghai, hurriedly ordered a number of his own officers and men to dress as Hatchet Gang disciples and cross the Yangtze after them. For a moment the Hatchet Gang's forces gathered roaring on the Shanghai waterfront, a presence to shake the sky — bearing straight down on Du Yuesheng's mansion on Rue Wagner.

### 12. Zhang Xiaolin Urgently Offers a Plan: "Fight Poison with Poison"

For two days running, the great Shanghai boss Du Yuesheng found each day as long as a year.

For when Li Guojie first set out to have his enemy Zhao Tieqiao murdered, the first power he had thought to use was Du Yuesheng's. And then too he had offered the China Merchants freighter Jiang'an as the bait for Du Yuesheng's help in removing his enemy. But Du Yuesheng was not Wang Yaqiao, who reckoned nothing of consequences. When Li Guojie declared his intention of killing Zhao, Du Yuesheng agreed to it readily enough at first — and then, having consulted his friends Zhang Xiaolin and Huang Jinrong about the killing, quietly changed his mind.

Both Zhang Xiaolin and Huang Jinrong were firmly against Du Yuesheng murdering Zhao Tieqiao.

Huang Jinrong said: "Zhao Tieqiao may have only just found his feet in Shanghai, but the fat post of Supervisor of the China Merchants company came to him for selling the Hatchet Gang's secrets to Dai Li. Never forget that his supervisorship is a gift from Mr. Chiang. If you send men to murder Zhao Tieqiao, elder brother, that is the same as removing a disciple of Mr. Chiang's — and then how do you ever look Chiang Chung-cheng in the face again?"

Zhang Xiaolin felt much the same, and added his warning: "This absolutely will not do. We can hardly injure Mr. Chiang for the sake of one Li Guojie. Weigh which is the lighter and which the heavier, Master Du, and you will see it at once."

Du Yuesheng sighed. "But I have already given Li Guojie my word — and he handed me that Jiang'an. The ship may not be worth much money, but a convenient carrier is a convenient carrier!"

Huang Jinrong gave a dry laugh. "You take it all far too seriously. On the Shanghai waterfront, why be so serious about what you say and do? And with a man like Li Guojie, still less need to weigh your promises so heavily. When the time comes, simply have his Jiang'an brought round — I guarantee Li Guojie won't dare let out so much as a squeak."

And so Li Guojie, at the end of every road, went to Wang Yaqiao instead. He ran the same play a second time, offering the same public vessel, the Jiang'an, as the fee for a hired killing. Wang Yaqiao, knowing nothing of the underside of it, walked into Li Guojie's trap.

"Master Du — Wang Yaqiao has started up. One word from him and seven or eight hundred disciples, senior and junior, have come down from Anhui at a stroke. The Anhui Guildhall is packed with Anhui men inside and out." Disciples had already carried into the Du mansion on Rue Wagner exact intelligence that Wang Yaqiao meant to turn Shanghai upside down.

"Master Du, it grows more serious." Another spy came in with what he had just heard, and reported it to the unsettled Du Yuesheng. "Wang Yaqiao is preparing to wash the Jiang'an in blood. He is putting it about that he will kill every hand aboard her, and that if Master Du does not surrender the murderer Liu Ada he will bring the Hatchet Gang down on Master Du's mansion and smash it to pieces!"

"And they mean to carry Pig-head San Leng's corpse through the streets of the city in a demonstration, demanding that the municipal authorities punish the killers."

"Some say Wang Yaqiao doesn't care whether he lives through this — that he means to have it out with Master Du at the mouth of the Wusong, one way or the other!"

"And there's more. Wang Yaqiao says—"

"Enough! Get out, all of you!" At the beginning Du Yuesheng had not held this Wang Yaqiao out of Anhui as worth a glance — which was exactly why Liu Ada had dared come to blows with the men sent to take the ship. Now, hearing that at a single word six or seven hundred Anhui men had converged on Shanghai from every quarter, ready to stand against his own Green Gang, and that a corpse was to be carried through the streets besides, Du Yuesheng began to feel the weight of it. That would be blacking his own face in front of the city. For Du Yuesheng was not the obscure Wang Yaqiao; he was a personage of Shanghai now, and in the eyes of the Nationalists' senior military and political men he counted for something considerable. He cultivated elegance, played the rough part in a refined key, and worked to thin out his old hoodlum reputation with good works and open-handed charity. Should Wang Yaqiao shake out the business of the seized ship and the dead man until every street and lane in Shanghai knew it, the damage hardly needed imagining. Thinking that far, Du Yuesheng could sit still no longer. He sent a man with his card, at speed, to fetch his closest friend Zhang Xiaolin to the house, so that they might work out together how to meet the Wang Yaqiao who was coming to kill at his door.

"Brother Xiaolin — it was because I listened to you and to elder brother Jinrong that I never sent anyone to kill that Zhao Tieqiao. And now his mother's Li Guojie has made me wrong on the inside and wrong on the outside." Du Yuesheng hurried to meet Zhang Xiaolin as he came into the reception room, sighed with his nerves showing, and rubbed his hands. "Who could have guessed Li Guojie would buy Wang Yaqiao out of Anhui? And promise him that same ship! Now the thing has grown past all bounds. In the fight over the Jiang'an, one of his men — a fellow called Pig-head San Leng — was thrown into the Huangpu to feed the fish by my man Liu Ada. So there's a death on the books, and Wang Yaqiao has called up half of Anhui to come and kill their way into Shanghai. Tell me how I am to settle this. Had I known the day would come to this, why did I ever begin? Better never to have wanted the ship at all."

"Master Du — in the old days those of us making our way on this waterfront all honored you as our teacher. That was because we all saw a man of nerve and judgment." Zhang Xiaolin took his time about it. He had known for some while about Wang Yaqiao sending to Hefei and Anqing for men to bring pressure on Du Yuesheng, and had not lost his composure over it as Du Yuesheng had. He sat himself down by the Eight Immortals table, took the fragrant tea the serving girl offered, sipped, and said with a cold edge: "So why is it that Master Du has come to be afraid of a few Anhui hoodlums?"

"You are the great man of the Shanghai waterfront. What is Wang Yaqiao? A little biesan, no more. Where is the sense in fearing him?" Zhang Xiaolin had lorded it over Shanghai for years; he and Du Yuesheng and Huang Jinrong were called the three great bosses of the waterfront, and he did not count a Wang Yaqiao so recently arrived as worth a thought.

"But Wang Yaqiao has called all those disciples into Shanghai, and gives out that he means a fight to the death with me. If I don't produce some emergency plan, won't something terrible come of it? You tell me how to make him withdraw. I can hardly pour oil on the fire while Wang Yaqiao is in a rage!"

"Why can't you pour oil on the fire?" Zhang Xiaolin plainly had his own view already formed, and seeing Du Yuesheng frantic and empty of ideas, he laughed his dry laugh. "I say the more the fire scorches your eyebrows, the less you should give way to him. What sort of villain has Master Du not seen in Shanghai? With a man like Wang Yaqiao, of course you take the method of fighting poison with poison, meeting hard with hard. That is the only thing that answers!"

Du Yuesheng's eyes came alight. "Fight poison with poison — let me hear your plan."

"Wang Yaqiao has called a mass of Anhui men into Shanghai," said Zhang Xiaolin, "and Master Du need not care a straw for it. That is a rabble he has gathered to stiffen his own courage. Someone told me just now that Wang Yaqiao wants those Anhui men to carry Pig-head San Leng's corpse through the streets in a demonstration. That is pure fantasy. This is Shanghai, not Hefei! Let them dare carry a corpse into the street, and the foreign police of the Concession will have them in a cage before the hour is out. Do you think Wang Yaqiao dares?"

"True — true! There's reason in it!" Du Yuesheng pressed him eagerly. "Go on, go on — what way is there to settle this business?"

Zhang Xiaolin smoked his cigarette without hurrying. "And I hear Wang Yaqiao has sent that Anhui rabble down to the wharf to have it out with Liu Ada, who killed their Pig-head San Leng — yes? Master Du need not be alarmed at that either. The art of war has said it long since: when the enemy advances, we withdraw. Since they have come to the wharf to fight us to the death, why doesn't Master Du send an order out to Liu Ada aboard the ship to take her outside the Wusong bar, into open water? Those Anhui men who mean to board and settle it life for life — give them distance instead. How would that do?"

"Excellent — excellent!" Du Yuesheng's nerves eased a little; then his brows drew together again. "To withdraw and decline the fight, and avoid a ruin of both sides with Wang Yaqiao, is of course the better policy. But flight, brother Xiaolin, is no long-term plan. When Wang Yaqiao finds us withdrawn from the field, he will trumpet it all over the press: Du Yuesheng is beaten. He will say I was afraid of him and had to run that great ship outside the Wusong bar for shelter. And then Wang Yaqiao wins in public opinion just the same. What does that make Du Yuesheng?"

Zhang Xiaolin, who had the whole shape of it in his chest already, laughed his cold laugh again and spoke with precise emphasis: "Master Du is not wrong! But after we avoid his edge there is a second move on the board — we seize the moment and raid Wang Yaqiao's own nest; we give him the turned-horse thrust. This is what the war books call attacking where he is unprepared, appearing where he does not expect us!"

Only then did Du Yuesheng grasp why Zhang Xiaolin had pressed him so hard to give way before the oncoming Wang Yaqiao, and his eyes lit. "Brother Xiaolin, you are a very Zhuge Liang! Who would have thought it, with you keeping so quiet all these years — a whole cosmos up your sleeve! Quickly — tell me how to work this unpreparedness on Wang Yaqiao."

And now Zhang Xiaolin brought out the fine plan from his brocade bag, leaning to Du Yuesheng's ear and dropping his voice. "It is this way, Master Du. Wang Yaqiao is a man of many tricks. In his previous killings in Nanjing and Shanghai, as a rule, he sends his own disciples out to show their faces while he stays snug behind the curtain and watches the weather. This time he has called all those Anhui men to Shanghai to go down to the wharf and call your Liu Ada to account — and he will certainly not take the field himself. And if he is sitting in the Anhui Guildhall waiting for good news, that is our chance to put an arrow into him from the dark!"

Du Yuesheng started. "You mean — we send men to the Anhui Guildhall while the moment holds, and assassinate Wang Yaqiao?"

"That is the plan exactly!" Zhang Xiaolin brought his hand down hard on the table and said it out loud: "Master Du — what is there to startle you? This is called treating a man by his own method! Since Wang Yaqiao means to rule the Shanghai waterfront by assassination, then we have the better answer for him: put a few holes in that Wang of his. Kill him, and those Anhui men down at the wharf making trouble for Master Du — do you think they will dare stay in Shanghai? By then Master Du's difficulty unravels without a battle."

Du Yuesheng sat stunned in his chair for several minutes and said nothing. The idea Zhang Xiaolin had put in front of him moved him more than he could say. And yet, for reasons he could not have named, the nerves came back into his face. "Xiaolin — your plan is a fine one. Once it succeeds, it not only lifts the fire from our eyebrows, it removes a standing danger to our Green Gang. Only I can't quite lay hold of it. Good as the scheme is, if the hand slips — or if Wang Yaqiao sees through it — won't the consequences be worse still?"

Zhang Xiaolin came up out of his chair, struck his own chest, and said: "Master Du — how could Wang Yaqiao see through a stroke made into empty space? His whole mind is on the wharf. He thinks we will surely send a mass of strong-arm men and top fighters down there to face the Anhui crowd he has summoned. Why would he be guarding his own back against assassins? So there is no need for creeping care. Just send a few killers with nerve enough, and get them into the Anhui Guildhall quickly. Once we have Wang Yaqiao's head in our hands, I guarantee you that rabble making trouble at the riverside will scatter without a shot fired!"

Du Yuesheng turned Zhang Xiaolin's plan over and over, and felt that with the fire at his eyebrows there was nothing for it but to carry out this "fight poison with poison." He set his teeth suddenly and said, hard: "A small measure makes no gentleman; without venom, no true man! There is only this one move left now!"

### 13. Cleverness Undone by Cleverness: The Assassins Become Hostages

Having sent several hundred Anhui brothers to the Wusong wharf to demand the blood debt from the killer Liu Ada and take back the Jiang'an that Li Guojie had sent him in payment, Wang Yaqiao settled down with his wife Wang Yaying and a few close guards in the Anhui Guildhall, the headquarters, to wait quietly for news.

"Set out the wine! Let us drink while we wait for the good news. As I see it, inside of two hours Du Yuesheng will come to the door of his own accord to apologize. And then he will not only return my Jiang'an — from that day Du Yuesheng bows at the feet of Wang Jiuguang. Hmph. This Shanghai waterfront never belonged to Du Yuesheng alone; now Wang Jiuguang has come, and naturally there is a seat here for me too!" The serving girls set several plates of good Anhui dishes on the Eight Immortals table. Wang Yaqiao's spirits ran high; he drank off cup after cup of the strong wine, and a flush not seen for many days began to rise in his thin face.

"Yaqiao — better to drink less, I think." Wang Yaying watched him drinking hard beside her, and an uneasy premonition rose in her.

That shrewd woman had a mind fine as silk thread and a coolness in trouble, and when she thought of Du Yuesheng's deep-rooted power and the tangled net of his connections, the wine cup in her hand shook, and the drops fell on her moon-white skirt. The stain spread slowly through the cloth, and to Wang Yaying's eye it was a drop of blood widening and widening, pricking straight into her.

Wang Yaqiao went on drinking in great mouthfuls, and the red rose higher in his face. "Why shouldn't I drink? Yaying — inside of two hours I'll show you what Du Yuesheng is made of. Our Anhui army is on his border. If he won't yield, what road is left to him?"

Wang Yaying was a woman of fine looks and a grave bearing, who had lived through the Wuchang rising; her nature was inward and still, she did not startle in a crisis, she thought well, and she had a head and a mind of her own. So she sat before a table of food and wine with no appetite for any of it, and grew more and more uneasy. She understood that beating a Du Yuesheng with years of foundation under him was no simple matter in Shanghai. "Jiuguang, Du Yuesheng is nothing like Zhao Tieqiao, nor like the Chen Diaoyuan you once wanted to kill. In Shanghai he is a man whose hand and eye reach everywhere. You may have right on your side and a death on his — but if you think that alone will bring Du Yuesheng down, I am afraid it cannot be done."

"Cannot be done? Hmph — there is no 'cannot be done' in Wang Jiuguang's dictionary!" With several cups of wine in him, his face flushed dark, he struck himself hard on the chest. "Du Yuesheng came to Shanghai a few years before me, that is all. In ability and nerve he is not a fraction the better man. And besides — right is in my hand now. Am I to believe I can't beat him?"

"Not so, not so. Raw force is no way to manage this." The further Wang Yaying thought, the worse the ending looked to her, and she said, weighing it: "If right could truly be judged in this world, then perhaps Du Yuesheng would surrender to you. But in this great Shanghai it has never been right that wins — and a magnate like Du Yuesheng least of all. You imagine that because he killed one of your men he'll feel himself in the wrong and give in? Not at all. Killing a man is no more to him than crushing an ant."

"Nonsense — a human life, and he treats it as straw? Am I to allow it?" With the wine in him Wang Yaqiao slammed the table till it rang. "Yaying — if Du Yuesheng refuses to yield, then I will wash the Shanghai waterfront in blood again!"

Seeing he would take no honest counsel, Wang Yaying sat there staring, thinking hard — and then an idea came to her, and she resolved to prick him with it. "Yaqiao, I think this is not the moment for you to be calling Du Yuesheng to account. It may well be that he is about to put a knife hard into your back!"

"Nonsense. He dares stab me in the back?" Wang Yaqiao was furious.

"If I have not guessed wrong, Du Yuesheng will certainly move against you — and he will send his men at once."

"A joke! He is shut in by my people now like a man in an iron cask — has he any strength left to strike back? And besides, I am inside the guildhall. Who would dare come for me here?" Wang Yaqiao's laugh was cold and dismissive. "Yaying, don't try to frighten me, and don't puff up Du Yuesheng to scare people with. He is in his worst confusion just now. How would he dare put a hand on me?"

Wang Yaying's voice did not rise, but every word came cold. "You — you truly are a reckless man. When was anything in this world ever so simple? Have you thought: if Du Yuesheng really were what you take him for, if he really would kneel at your feet over one ship and one dead man, he would be the laughingstock of the whole Shanghai waterfront, a proven weakling. As I see it, even if he wants peace with you, he will never simply give in at once!"

That sobered him. His wife's words were unwelcome, but they found the vital spot at one needle-stroke. He said, as if waking: "True — true. A man holding the whole power of the Green Gang, how would he bow easily to some obscure Anhui man? And yet I can't believe that Du would dare come killing at my door at a time like this — wouldn't that fan the fire higher still?"

"Yaqiao, think it further through. Of course Du Yuesheng doesn't want the fire fanned higher. But do you imagine his only way to put it out is surrender?"

Wang Yaqiao looked at his quiet wife, puzzled. "Yaying — you mean he'll take some more savage way to put it out?"

Wang Yaying gave her judgment of the thing. "I think Du Yuesheng is a hero-villain of a chaotic age, and he will certainly see that killing you is the best way of clearing an obstacle. So I say: sober up now, and make ready — quickly — against Du Yuesheng coming to kill at our door!"

The wine went out of Wang Yaqiao. Hearing his wife spell out what an assassination might mean, the cold sweat started out on him. It was precisely having a shrewd woman at his side that saved his life. He sent at once for Wang Ganting and Niu Anru and gave them their orders: "Put a guard detail on for me immediately, inside the guildhall and out, against an attack!"

And it fell exactly as Wang Yaying had foreseen. Not long after Wang Ganting and Niu Anru had brought in a detail of Anhui men and laid them in cover through the guildhall, several figures of suspicious appearance came slipping out one after another from behind the plane trees. Niu Anru knew they could only be Du Yuesheng's assassins, and did not disturb them. He watched the killers slip quietly into the passage toward the front courtyard, turn into a covered gallery, and feel their way straight toward the front court where Wang Yaqiao lodged. Wang Ganting and Niu Anru exchanged a knowing smile and waved a hand at their men hidden in the undergrowth — and dozens of them came flashing out of the shadows with sharpened hatchets in their fists and flew at the front courtyard.

At that hour, in his house on Rue Wagner, Du Yuesheng was waiting for news with his heart on the fire.

Since sending the killers to the Anhui Guildhall he had felt not a shred of relief; rather he had grown steadily more tense and disordered. Zhang Xiaolin, seeing him unable to keep his soul in his body, spread a chess board on the round table and set to a game with him. It would be truer to say that Zhang Xiaolin had suddenly conceived an interest in loosening Du Yuesheng's nerves than in playing chess. But Du Yuesheng's mind was nowhere near that half-finished board with its river between the camps. A serving girl came in to report: "Master, a telephone call." And presently a manservant: "Master, men have come from the riverside wharf; they say they have urgent intelligence to report to the Master in person."

"Enough, enough — this is chaos. The Jiang'an is long since outside the Wusong bar; are we afraid those Anhui savages will fly to her across the water? Useless creatures, all of them!" Du Yuesheng cursed viciously, thinking that once good news came from the Anhui Guildhall — the Hatchet Gang's chief Wang Yaqiao removed — the gang's forces at the riverside preparing their raid on the Jiang'an would scatter like birds and beasts, exactly as Zhang Xiaolin had predicted. But what disturbed Du Yuesheng and Zhang Xiaolin beyond measure was that the assassination party sent against Wang Yaqiao had gone and left not a word behind it.

About two hours later a strong-arm man called Black-head A San came stumbling in from outside, covered in mud from head to foot. At the sight of Du Yuesheng and Zhang Xiaolin he dropped to his knees with a thud and cried out hoarsely: "Boss Du — it's bad! The brothers we sent to make the killing have all been taken by Wang Yaqiao's people. Master Du — that Hatchet Gang was ready and waiting! We walked straight into his trap!"

"What—?" The color went out of Du Yuesheng's face; the hand holding the chess piece trembled and would not stop. The Boss Du who summoned wind and rain on the Shanghai waterfront, whose single word weighed as nine tripods, sat frozen there with nothing to say.

Zhang Xiaolin was the cooler of the two, and pressed Black-head A San urgently. "A San — tell it. What exactly happened?"

Black-head A San, badly shaken, had no choice but to tell the whole course of it: how they had slipped quietly in through the rear courtyard of the Anhui Guildhall, and how the entire party had been destroyed. They had got smoothly up to the window of the room where Wang Yaqiao and his wife were lodged, and found the pair sitting there drinking and talking. And just as they made ready to fire in through the window, a crowd of Hatchet Gang bruisers already laid in the courtyard rushed them from all sides and took every one of the dozen-odd assassins, and trussed them like sacrifices. Then Wang Yaqiao, who had been drinking inside, came out at a measured pace, faced these killers shaking with terror, and said with a cold smile: "I reckoned all along you would try exactly this. It seems my life is not fated to end yet — my wife foresaw days ago that you would come to the guildhall today to kill me! Very well. Since you are all Boss Du's killers, don't blame Wang for showing you no mercy: I mean to have you dragged out one at a time and beheaded in public! Let the people of Shanghai see what an upright and honorable man Du Yuesheng is!"

"Boss Wang — spare us!" The black-clad assassins trussed by the Hatchet Gang men, seeing the dense crowd closing around them with bright hatchets in their hands and fury in their faces, hauling them off toward the outer courtyard, fell to their knees one after another and begged. And Wang Yaqiao, though he went on cursing them, had no real wish to kill Du Yuesheng's men lightly. At this Wang Yaying stepped in to give them a way out: "Letting you go is easy enough — but only if your Boss Du comes to the guildhall himself to collect you!"

Wang Yaqiao agreed. "Yes. Let your Boss Du come himself — and then I will have him explain why, with right nowhere near his hand, he sends assassins in the dark to murder men. A fine great personage of Shanghai!" He ordered Black-head A San's ropes cut and told him: "Take my letter to Boss Du at once. If he agrees to my conditions, the lot of you can go back; and if not, don't blame Wang Jiuguang for showing no mercy!" He wrote a letter there and then and sent Black-head A San back to the Du mansion to report.

When Du Yuesheng and Zhang Xiaolin had heard Black-head A San out and read the letter in Wang Yaqiao's own hand, they felt more than ever like men riding a tiger they could not dismount. The letter ran:

Mr. Du: On the rivers and lakes it has always been said that an honest man does no work in the dark. When I, Wang Yaqiao, demanded the Jiang'an, it was a thing promised me in Li Guojie's own hand, and may fairly be called come by honestly. Yet you, a personage of Shanghai, not only withheld the ship but came to blows and took a life. Where is public right, where is conscience? And now, so far from knowing your error and mending it, returning the Jiang'an and surrendering the killer, you have raised a fresh complication and sent assassins. I now require that on reading this letter you come at once to my guildhall to apologize in person, hand over the freighter, and punish the murderer Liu Ada. Should you dare defy these three conditions, every brother of my Hatchet Gang will come killing to your door and take off your head. In that hour your name in Shanghai will not merely fall — you will be a ghost under Wang Yaqiao's blade. Which road you take, decide quickly; otherwise do not blame my Hatchet Gang for its lack of feeling. To sweep across this great Shanghai would cost us no more effort than blowing away ash. — Wang Yaqiao of Hefei, Anhui

Du Yuesheng had never dreamed that after all his years of lordship in Shanghai he would take a run of falls in front of one Anhui man. Not only had the Jiang'an cost a life; he had lightly taken Zhang Xiaolin's advice, sent assassins into the Anhui Guildhall, missed — and the assassins were now hostages in Wang Yaqiao's hand, which was beyond question handing an enemy the handle. And considering the three conditions in the letter, they plainly amounted to forcing his surrender. The more Du Yuesheng thought about how passive his position had become, the more his agitation passed all bounds.

"Brother Xiaolin — it seems this time a strongman has me by the handle, and I doubt I can get free of it soon!" Du Yuesheng ground his teeth; he could have wished Wang Yaqiao dragged in front of him that instant so he might bite through his throat and ease his heart.

"Master Du — don't panic." Zhang Xiaolin, by contrast, offered his counsel unhurried. "Pay no mind to Wang Yaqiao's letter. He is not making this quarrel over one ship and one life; he means to be the great man of Shanghai, and that is why he invents trouble and comes at us. That he dares demand you, Boss Du, go to his guildhall and apologize in person — the man does not know how high heaven is or how deep the earth. As I see it, we should simply put every man of our Shanghai Green Gang into the field and settle it with Wang Yaqiao to the death. Whatever it costs, his arrogance has to be beaten down!"

"No, brother Xiaolin — matters have gone too far for any more wild moves!" Du Yuesheng, having already eaten one loss, would not lightly take Zhang Xiaolin's word again. He knew he was now entirely at Wang Yaqiao's mercy: over the seizing of the ship he had killed the Hatchet Gang's Pig-head San Leng, and once a life was taken, right lay in the other man's hand — and then, of all things he should never have done, he had sent assassins to the Anhui Guildhall. Now a dozen and more of his own disciples were held by Wang Yaqiao. Should the business be shouted abroad, public opinion would be in an uproar and his reputation gravely damaged — and Wang Yaqiao would be made by it. Weighing the advantage coolly against the harm, Du Yuesheng shook his head and vetoed Zhang Xiaolin's plan for a general Green Gang battle with the Hatchet Gang, and said reasonably: "Think: we killed Wang Yaqiao's man first, and sent assassins after him second. If this business ever startles the press, public right is on Wang Yaqiao's side as well. Rather than go on grappling with the Hatchet Gang — better still than moving is standing still — let us simply do as his letter says. I will go to his Anhui Guildhall myself and apologize. How would that be?"

Zhang Xiaolin jumped. "Has Master Du gone mad? What kind of man are you, and what kind of man is Wang Yaqiao? How can you go in person into that Anhui bandits' den?"

"You are afraid Wang Yaqiao would dare harm me?" Du Yuesheng shook his head with self-assurance. "I judge he hasn't the nerve for that yet."

Zhang Xiaolin too now felt how serious the situation was, and felt that the Wang Yaqiao they had once despised was no ordinary man. But he would not have Du Yuesheng run the risk, and urged him again: "Master Du — it isn't that I fear he would dare poison you. How many heads has he, to make an enemy of Master Du? What I mean is that if you go to his guildhall in person you have plainly conceded the right of it. And if the business gets talked about, Master Du's loss of face is the small matter; Wang Yaqiao's swaggering in Shanghai from that day on is the large one. When even Master Du can't hold him down, who will Wang Yaqiao fear then?"

Du Yuesheng felt the difficulty of it both ways. He knew Zhang Xiaolin's fears were not without reason; yet if he did not do as the three conditions required, not only could the dozen-odd brothers held there not be released, but Wang Yaqiao would go on directing those Hatchet Gang disciples gathered into Shanghai from Hefei and Anqing to carry corpses through the streets. With no good plan to be had, Du Yuesheng paced the floor rubbing his hands for a long while before he made up his mind, and said to Zhang Xiaolin: "There is an old saying on the rivers and lakes: a wise man does not eat the loss in front of him. Winning and losing are ordinary things, after all. Brother Xiaolin — since we intend a long reckoning with Wang Yaqiao, why haggle over one moment's gain or loss? I say this should not be carried on with him. Rather than go on facing each other down and killing, let us turn the spears and shields into silk and jade."

"With the arrow already on the string, how will you turn it aside?"

Du Yuesheng thought a long time, and at last set his teeth and waved a hand. "Negotiate!"

Zhang Xiaolin shook his head. "Negotiate with a rogue like that? How would you ever come to terms?"

"I'll simply give Wang Yaqiao a little face and go to his door myself. How would that be?" Du Yuesheng was out of expedients by then, and with his heart tied in a hundred knots he could only choose surrender.

"No, Master Du — that would cost you far too much of your standing!" Zhang Xiaolin turned it every way and could not agree with the decision; and then a compromise came to him. "Since Master Du is broad-minded enough to concede a step to this Wang for now, well and good. But whatever you do, don't go to the Anhui Guildhall yourself. If it must be negotiation, then send a man there with the message and choose neutral ground, and have Wang Yaqiao come to it. That way, Master Du's going to meet him is a face-saving expedient."

Du Yuesheng, seeing that Zhang Xiaolin had found a way that spared his face, agreed at once with pleasure, nodding again and again. "Very well. Then let it be the Old Shanghai Restaurant on Rue Fuyou — we have people there, and the talk will be safe."

Zhang Xiaolin felt this was now the only workable course. So Du Yuesheng sent Black-head A San to the Anhui Guildhall with a reply for the waiting Wang Yaqiao, appointing three o'clock that afternoon at the Old Shanghai Restaurant.

### 14. Du Yuesheng Sets the Table; Huang Jinrong Makes the Apology

Before two in the afternoon Du Yuesheng and Zhang Xiaolin, ringed front and back by a crowd of Green Gang disciples with loaded guns, left the Du house on Rue Wagner in several cars for the century-old Jiangsu-Zhejiang restaurant on Rue Fuyou. The proprietor, hearing that Du Yuesheng was entertaining there, dared not be slow: the private room was made ready, aged wines and famous dishes brought up, everything prepared well in advance.

Du Yuesheng had his hopes, too, of settling matters over wine and talk with the Wang Yaqiao who had been his enemy so long. But he and Zhang Xiaolin sat in the private room from three in the afternoon until dusk, and no shadow of Wang Yaqiao appeared. It had not occurred to Du Yuesheng that this Anhui Hatchet Gang chief would put on such airs. Several times Zhang Xiaolin cursed Wang Yaqiao at the table for a man who knew nothing of human feeling, while Du Yuesheng, at a loss, only paced back and forth. Not until the sky had gone quite dark did Black-head A San, whom they had sent with the letter, come running upstairs with his face tight, weeping out his news: "Master Du — Master Zhang — Wang Yaqiao won't give us the courtesy. He won't come to the wine, whatever's said!"

For when Black-head A San delivered Du Yuesheng and Zhang Xiaolin's invitation to the Old Shanghai Restaurant on Rue Fuyou, Wang Yaqiao had laughed aloud. "I knew it. Even ghosts fear a hard man. So Boss Du is no better than the rest of them — he knows now where the right lies, does he? Or why would he come out and lay wine for me? Well then. Since he knows himself in the wrong, let us simply make a friend of him."

But nobody had expected Wang Yaying to object on the spot. "Yaqiao — to be reconciled with Boss Du is a good thing in itself; the whole world says enmity is better untied than knotted. But the letter you wrote Mr. Du set out three conditions that had to be accepted before there could be any negotiation. And it stated that he must come here in person to apologize before the old bad blood could be washed away. Otherwise — with Mr. Du having killed our brother first — if we negotiate with him in this muddled fashion, how do we account for it to the brothers of the Hatchet Gang?"

"Just so — if my wife hadn't reminded me, I'd have come near to walking into Du Yuesheng's trap!" On Wang Yaying's reminder alone, the Wang Yaqiao who had already accepted the invitation abruptly changed his mind. He told Black-head A San: "Go straight back with this message. Tell Boss Du that Wang will meet him soft or hard, as he likes. But if he sincerely wants good relations with Wang Jiuguang, he shouldn't sit in a restaurant putting on airs. I said it first and I say it again: Boss Du killed my brother, seized a ship belonging to our Hatchet Gang, and has now sent killers to my door. Everything Boss Du has done is plainly beneath his own station. If he still has any sense, let him come to this one acre and three tenths of mine. And tell him this too: there is no Hongmen banquet waiting here. So long as Boss Du is willing to come, Wang Jiuguang will certainly give him his face."

Du Yuesheng sat there staring while the sky beyond the restaurant windows went darker and darker. Never in his dreams had he thought a petty gang chief up from Anhui would put on greater airs than his own, and refuse even a banquet he had set. The flush rose in Du Yuesheng's face and ears, and several degrees of his majesty went out of him.

"His mother's — this Wang won't drink the wine of courtesy and will have the wine of punishment instead." Zhang Xiaolin, hearing Black-head A San's report, went up in a fury and struck the table. "Master Du — I told you long ago the man is no good. His sort understands nothing of human feeling or reason; they say that in his early years at Wuhu he was a petty creature who understood killing and nothing else. Now that things have come to drawn swords and bent bows, if we don't take the hard course — blade for blade and tooth for tooth — and beat this loathsome Hatchet Gang down, then even a settlement now will only brew a later disaster. That being so, better to spend a little money and buy the Concession police, add our own men to them, and I refuse to believe we can't scatter Wang Yaqiao like flowers on the water. And then the question of who is in the right settles itself without a word."

"Not so, not so. Now that both sides favor a settlement, how can we stoop to that? Brother Xiaolin, you must see that if we go on facing each other down like this, the consequences will be even harder to gather up." Du Yuesheng was out of ideas by then. He wanted a settlement with Wang Yaqiao badly, and yet found the other man's conditions too harsh to swallow. Caught between, he thought of Huang Jinrong. "Since neither of us has a good plan, why not go straight to elder brother Huang's house and ask his advice?" Zhang Xiaolin came down off his position readily enough and stopped insisting. "Very well. Let's hear what elder brother Huang says — and I believe he'll approve my plan too, and beat that Anhui bumpkin's swagger out of him. Otherwise one day he'll be squatting on our brothers' necks to relieve himself!"

Neither argued further; they got into the car and drove to Huang Jinrong's mansion. At the Huang house a man had already gone in to announce them. Huang Jinrong had just finished his dinner and lay against the smoking couch attended by his concubines, drawing on the water-pipe. Hearing that Du Yuesheng and Zhang Xiaolin had come together, and knowing it must be the business with the Hatchet Gang that had them at odds, he told his people: "Show them in, quickly — I want to hear how they have handled this matter."

By the time he had spoken, Du Yuesheng and Zhang Xiaolin were seated in the reception room. Maids brought in osmanthus-and-green-bean tea in turn, and Huang Jinrong came out to meet them. "Yuesheng — Xiaolin — don't tell me you really mean to fight Wang Yaqiao? I told you long ago: with a man of that sort, you must never take the hard line."

Du Yuesheng, seeing that Huang Jinrong had understood his position before even leaving his room, admired him quietly. "Elder brother speaks reason. Only the thing is already sitting on my head. This Wang of theirs is like a smear of dog dirt I can neither wipe off nor scrape away. How am I to put out a fire at my eyebrows?"

Zhang Xiaolin said: "Elder brother Huang — we are all men who walk the Shanghai waterfront. Consider: even if right lies on Wang Yaqiao's side, we cannot let him take the advantage. If we go and make concessions to Wang Yaqiao as Master Du proposes, how do we hold our position afterward? As I see it, the hard way is better. With the standing and the forces we have built in Shanghai over the years, and Chiang Kai-shek's official backing from Nanjing besides, are we afraid we can't beat a few Hatchet Gang men out of Anhui?"

"Not so, not so!" Huang Jinrong, having smoked his fill, roused himself. While Du and Zhang were laying out the course of the confrontation with Wang Yaqiao, Huang Jinrong had already settled on the way to quiet it. The matter touched him only indirectly, but his friendship with Du Yuesheng ran deep. The two had been making their way in Shanghai together as far back as 1911, and later, in the opium smuggling, Du Yuesheng had won Huang Jinrong's world for him and earned his share of the credit. Now, knowing Du Yuesheng was astride a tiger he could not dismount, was he to watch him die and not save him?

Huang Jinrong waved the women away from him, and only then spoke to Zhang Xiaolin. "Xiaolin, your fears have some reason in them. But the old methods will no longer answer with Wang Yaqiao. I have heard of this man for a long time. He is by nature a man who believes in nothing sacred — they say he doesn't hold even Mr. Chiang in awe, so why should he care about local godlings like us in Shanghai? Besides, Yuesheng's men were indeed in the wrong. How can you kill a man over a ship? And you, Xiaolin, were muddled too. We had already lost the right of it, and you proposed sending men to assassinate Wang Yaqiao — with the result that several more of our brothers have been thrown in after. Consider: if we take the hard course and the hand slips a second time and there is a murder case in the courts, the consequences hardly bear thinking about. Who cleans up that mess? And say we really could drive Wang Yaqiao out of Shanghai: he would not accept it in his heart, and sooner or later he would be a great calamity. Because the man's hand is black and his heart is hard, and sooner or later he would come back with the dust of his return rolling behind him!"

Zhang Xiaolin, hearing Huang Jinrong put it that way, knew his own hard-fighting plan had no ground left under it, and said no more. Du Yuesheng, finding Huang Jinrong's words exactly to his own mind, nodded and agreed. "Elder brother's view is the higher one. Only — I can't fight, and I can't make peace either! We laid a table for Wang Yaqiao at the Old Shanghai Restaurant just now, and the man put on airs; we couldn't get him to come. Elder brother, only now do I see the Anhui Hatchet Gang is not to be provoked. Wang Yaqiao won't even grant me the courtesy. Making peace is a hard business too!"

"In fact it is not hard. Not the least bit hard!" Huang Jinrong sat in his master's chair with the look of a man who has the whole bamboo drawn in his chest. He smiled and said to Du Yuesheng: "Why do I say it isn't hard? Because from what I observe, Wang Yaqiao is no blockhead either. I hear this Wang Yaqiao sets great store by honor and by feeling. Why won't he come to your banquet? First, because he said it beforehand: you must come to his door and apologize in person before the spears and shields can be turned to silk and jade. But instead you put on the airs of a Shanghai personage and expected an Anhui man with a grievance to come and drink your wine of his own accord — of course he wouldn't. And second, Wang Yaqiao half doubts your arrangements: what if you had an ambush laid at the Old Shanghai Restaurant — wouldn't that be another Hongmen banquet? Think: if Wang Yaqiao had made such an arrangement, would you have walked into it?"

Du Yuesheng listened to the analysis with quiet admiration, but sighed with the difficulty still on his face. "Elder brother's words are gold and jade, and they cut to the bone. But I can hardly go to his Anhui Guildhall in person. You know it was my man who killed that fellow of Wang Yaqiao's, the one they call Pig-head San Leng. With that old grudge before it and my sending assassins after it, might Wang Yaqiao not set a trap in the guildhall and lure me there to my death?" Zhang Xiaolin took it up. "Elder brother — Yuesheng truly cannot go. Who will guarantee that Wang Yaqiao hasn't a machine set to kill a man? Everyone knows Wang Yaqiao made his name by killing; back in Anhui he already had the evil name of 'King of Killers.' To send Boss Du there in person now can only mean much ill and little luck — which is exactly why we thought of a dinner at the Old Shanghai Restaurant, as a way of dissolving the old quarrel. Who knew this Wang wanted no settlement at all?"

Huang Jinrong mused a moment, and then said suddenly to the two of them: "In that case, there is nothing for it but that I go myself. Wang Yaqiao and I have never met and have no quarrel of any kind. Surely he would not harm me?"

Du Yuesheng and Zhang Xiaolin would not hear of it, and pressed him hard against it. Du Yuesheng said: "Elder brother must not go. You may never have had a quarrel with Wang Yaqiao, but the man kills without blinking, and he must know that you and I are sworn brothers of many years, head-knocking and card-exchanging. If you go and an evil thought takes him and he harms you, how could I ever be easy?" Zhang Xiaolin urged every argument as well: "Wang Yaqiao's evil name has traveled; even Mr. Chiang knows there is a killing madman in Anhui. Caution is the better policy — you must on no account walk into that Hatchet Gang trap!"

But Huang Jinrong, hearing them, threw back his head and laughed, and after a while said: "Where have your minds got to? No wonder you dare not go to the Anhui Guildhall — you still don't know what sort of man Wang Yaqiao is. From what I hear, Wang Yaqiao is not the villain you make him. He has killed, yes; but his nature is thoroughly upright. Think: if he were not upright, would he have sent men to Nanjing to kill Chen Diaoyuan? If Wang Yaqiao had no sense of justice in him, would he have killed that Zhao Tieqiao who sold his own friend? I hear that this same Wang Yaqiao has a good deal of kindness in him toward the poor and the common people. As for my going to him — don't trouble yourselves. Wang Yaqiao will assuredly do me no harm."

"Why?" Du Yuesheng and Zhang Xiaolin still half believed there was a trap laid.

Huang Jinrong was, after all, a deep and long-headed schemer, and he laid it out point by point. "Wang Yaqiao has right in his hand at the moment, and a dozen of our brothers besides. But understand: he is also beating a wolf with a hemp stalk — afraid at both ends. Because he knows he is an outsider, with no wharf of his own in Shanghai and no web of connections. He has had to call all those men down from Hefei to give himself courage. But the outsider never beats the local snake in the end. Why, knowing he cannot come out on top, does he still insist on his face and demand that you, Boss Du, come to his guildhall? Because he wants a settlement and won't lose face getting it. So if I go in person as your representative, Wang Yaqiao gets his face and gets his Jiang'an back — tell me, why would he still want to kill me?"

Du Yuesheng and Zhang Xiaolin, hearing the sense of it, had nothing left to say. Only Du Yuesheng still feared for Huang Jinrong's safety. "If some accident befell elder brother, how could I be easy in my heart?"

Huang Jinrong swept his hand. "It will not, it will not. I guarantee Wang Yaqiao dares do nothing to me. Consider: if he dared lay a hand on me, would he ever dare make his way in Shanghai again? It is no boast — let him touch a hair of mine, and one day he pays for it with his life."

Zhang Xiaolin said: "There is reason in what elder brother says. Only if elder brother means to go, tonight will not do. It is dark. Leave it to tomorrow."

But Huang Jinrong would have none of that either. He called the maids to dress him, and at the same time had a man telephone Wang Yaqiao's Anhui Guildhall to say he was coming at once. Seeing Zhang Xiaolin about to obstruct him again, he said: "Tonight I must go. Never mind the safety of the dozen brothers still held in that guildhall — Wang Yaqiao's men are at this moment still camped at the riverside wharf making an uproar, and I can delay no longer. If they see no concession from us tomorrow, Wang Yaqiao may really rouse those Hatchet Gang men who know nothing of Shanghai's rules to carry that dead Pig-head San Leng through the streets. And if I go to see Wang Yaqiao after that, haven't I thrown away my face entirely?"

Seeing how far ahead Huang Jinrong had thought, and on what grounds he spoke, Du Yuesheng and Zhang Xiaolin stopped urging and escorted him out of the gate. Huang Jinrong got into a car, and with several American jeeps carrying Du Yuesheng's men as escort, drove for the Anhui Guildhall. Every car had its headlamps blazing, and the black road lit up as they flew away into the distance. About half an hour on, someone reported to Huang Jinrong in the car: "Boss Huang, the Anhui Guildhall is just ahead. Look — Wang Yaqiao's people are already posted at the gate, and a fierce-looking lot they are!"

Huang Jinrong looked quickly out of the window. Before the great guildhall a black mass of men already stood packed — bare-chested bruisers, every one with a blade or a gun in his hand, every face angry, all of them watching the approaching convoy like tigers eyeing prey. Seeing those Anhui men with their weapons out and every appearance of being about to rush the cars and fight it out, Huang Jinrong felt the start of alarm. He had summoned wind and rain in Shanghai and worked the wharves for many years, and had seen every kind of dangerous scene; but he had never seen anyone marshal men as Wang Yaqiao had. In Huang Jinrong's mind the Hatchet Gang had been so many peasant thugs who would never amount to weather — and here in front of him was a formation of this severity and this authority. It shook him considerably. And at that moment came a shout from the guildhall gate: "Make way — the Ninth Master comes to greet his guest!"

Huang Jinrong knew that Ninth Master meant Wang Yaqiao. A stir went through the Hatchet Gang men at the gate, and several bare-chested fellows with bright hatchets in their fists came out escorting a short, spare, unremarkable-looking man of middle years. It was Huang Jinrong's first sight of Wang Yaqiao, and he had not expected this Hatchet Gang chief, famous the length of Anhui and Shanghai, to be a small thin man in black-rimmed crystal spectacles — with something of the scholar in a plain gown about him.

While Huang Jinrong sat in the car studying the approaching figure, the servant with him said in his ear: "Master Huang — the man in the spectacles is the great and famous Wang Yaqiao!" Huang Jinrong dared not be slow. He came out of the car at once, went forward a few steps, and bowed with cupped hands to the Wang Yaqiao who stood with his hands clasped behind his back, taking his ease. "Heavens — Mr. Wang's great name has long been thunder in my ear, and today I have the good fortune to meet him. A fine figure of a man indeed! Huang pays his respects!"

At this a deafening shout went up from the crowd guarding the guildhall: "Give us back our man! A blood debt is paid in blood!" "Let Du Yuesheng come and apologize himself!" "Give back the Jiang'an, or we wash Shanghai in blood!" "Get out — the Huang one, get out!" Huang Jinrong took a private fright; he had never imagined that in condescending to come to the door himself he would meet a scene as awkward as this. Only now did he feel there had been something in Zhang Xiaolin's warnings. But he had come, and there was nothing for it but to walk forward with a hardened scalp.

"You do me too much honor!" Seeing how well Huang Jinrong knew the etiquette of the rivers and lakes, several degrees of Wang Yaqiao's anger went out of him at once. When the furious cursing of the Hatchet Gang men behind him rose again, Wang Yaqiao rounded on them sharply: "Silence! Do you know who has come? This is the first man of the Shanghai waterfront in virtue and standing — the great personage Huang Jinrong, Elder Huang! Whatever wrongs our Hatchet Gang has suffered from Du Yuesheng, however many of our men have died — with Mr. Huang's golden face here, every grievance can be dispersed! Hear me, all of you: Elder Huang is Wang Yaqiao's guest, and whoever dares be discourteous, let him not blame me for showing no mercy!"

The clamor stopped dead. Huang Jinrong was still looking blankly about him when he heard Wang Yaqiao say: "Boss Huang — please!"

Only then did Huang Jinrong notice that dozens of lanterns great and small had been lit, at some point, inside and outside the Anhui Guildhall, dazzling him. His heart beat hard; he had no idea what kind of play Wang Yaqiao had arranged for him. But he was the first magnate of Shanghai, and could hardly show a shred of fear in that forest of blades. He simply kept close behind Wang Yaqiao, and with a crowd of Hatchet Gang disciples before and behind, crossed the paved way and passed hurriedly through several linked courtyards, deeper and deeper into the guildhall.

How many courts they crossed he did not know; then Wang Yaqiao said: "Please — go in!" Huang Jinrong looked up at a great tiled hall with several large red lanterns hung before the door. He stepped carefully over the threshold and found himself in the Hatchet Gang's headquarters: a gilt board reading COUNCIL HALL hung across the entry. Inside, the lamps were brilliant, and on the far wall hung a great scroll of a tiger. Huang Jinrong was shown to a seat at the Eight Immortals table — and before he could open his mouth Wang Yaqiao said: "Boss Huang, if I have not guessed wrong, you have come on Boss Du's behalf to negotiate with our Hatchet Gang?"

"No, no — that is a mistake." Huang Jinrong laughed his dry laugh, and said in the manner of a man who wants trouble settled: "This old fellow has come expressly to apologize!"

"To apologize?" Wang Yaqiao started; he thought his own ears had failed him. In his picture of the world, the great Green Gang boss who ruled Shanghai would never have said such a thing. Since the collision over the Jiang'an with Du Yuesheng's man Liu Ada, Wang Yaqiao had been at the highest pitch of guard. So when Du Yuesheng sent his card inviting him to drink at the Old Shanghai Restaurant on Rue Fuyou, Wang Yaqiao took Wang Yaying's advice on the spot and firmly refused, demanding instead that Du Yuesheng come to his door and apologize — or he and the Hatchet Gang would carry a corpse through the streets of Shanghai to put pressure on him. When the Huang mansion telephoned to say Huang Jinrong was coming in person, Wang Yaqiao took it for one of Du Yuesheng's delaying moves — a use of Huang Jinrong's prestige and swagger to press the Hatchet Gang down. So to hear Huang Jinrong say he had come to apologize was startling. For the Huang Jinrong of Wang Yaqiao's mind, with his multitude of disciples and his authority on every side, to come and apologize to a raw Anhui gang chief just out of the reeds was naturally hard to believe.

"Yes — Huang Jinrong has indeed come to apologize." Huang Jinrong knew he must show entire sincerity or he would not easily leave the Anhui Guildhall that night, and he bowed over his cupped hands. "I apologize to Mr. Wang not only for my brother Yuesheng but on behalf of every brother of our Green Gang. In the dispute over the freighter Jiang'an, public right and men's hearts are truly on your Hatchet Gang's side. What is infuriating is that Yuesheng's men not only would not listen to reason and would not return the Jiang'an, but struck down one of your men and killed him. Tell me — ought I not to come and apologize to Mr. Wang?"

Wang Yaqiao sat frozen. Huang Jinrong's coming in person had already surprised him considerably, and now, hearing this speech from the man's own mouth — that Du Yuesheng and Huang Jinrong should apologize so gravely to him and to the Hatchet Gang — he was moved more than a little.

"And there is more. Brother Yuesheng went somewhat too far. The world's ground belongs to the brothers of every road in common; the Shanghai waterfront is not our Green Gang's alone. Why should there be no place in it for Mr. Wang's Hatchet Gang?" Seeing Wang Yaqiao sit silent in the lamp-shadow, Huang Jinrong felt his words had reached the man's heart. The light of tears standing in Wang Yaqiao's eyes confirmed that the estimate he had formed of this evil-famed Hatchet Gang chief before coming had not been wrong. Huang Jinrong knew Wang Yaqiao was a man who could be moved by softness but never by force, and that was exactly why he had decided to come and talk the Hatchet Gang round, and so pull back the raging tide and dissolve a conflict on the very edge of blades. Huang Jinrong went on: "It was little enough worth doing, to wound the brothers' good feeling over one ship; but of all things Yuesheng should never, never have listened to a small man's counsel and sent assassins to your guildhall to do wickedness. Happily Mr. Wang's clear eye knew the traitors and had those trouble-making disciples taken. Otherwise, who knows what terrible thing might have happened, and the consequences beyond bearing. Stirring up disaster is one thing; what I fear is that such an affair would not merely have set Mr. Wang and Boss Du still harder against each other but given fresh matter for laughter to certain people who enjoy laughing at us brothers. Mr. Jiuguang — in taking those troublemakers, did you do anything but right?"

Hearing Huang Jinrong speak so earnestly, Wang Yaqiao felt something like shame instead, and said quickly: "Say no more, Boss Huang. I will release them at once."

"No, no — Mr. Wang should not release those guilty disciples! They richly deserve to die!" Huang Jinrong waved a hand and cursed them roundly. "As I see it, every one of them should be cut down with those great hatchets of yours — it would teach a lesson to the fools who fear only that the world may not be in chaos."

This made Wang Yaqiao more uncomfortable still, and he said hastily: "If Boss Huang talks like that, then I must release them at once. Those disciples came under another man's orders; they have no quarrel with Wang Yaqiao. On what ground should I kill them? Besides, they came with evil intent, true — but they never harmed one hair of Wang's head, and if I killed them, every man of judgment on the Shanghai waterfront would laugh at me. And after all, killing a man goes no further than his head touching the ground. Boss Du went much too far — but tonight, with Boss Huang's golden face here, we could dissolve a grievance the size of heaven!"

"No, no — these men must be killed. They have ruined the rules and laws of our own house!" The more solemnly Huang Jinrong insisted, the more improper Wang Yaqiao felt it, until he rose in haste and bowed low to him. "Boss Huang, say no more of it. From this day forward, on one word from you, Wang Yaqiao will never again make trouble for Boss Du's people. That Jiang'an is the root and seed of the whole quarrel — let it simply be handed to Boss Du and be done with!"

Huang Jinrong would have none of that either, and stood firm. "Since the Jiang'an was Li Guojie's gift to the Hatchet Gang, Boss Du ought by rights to yield it up. Let us do it this way. The heroes of every road under heaven meet as wind and cloud meet, and a grievance is better untied than knotted. So — tomorrow at noon I will lay a simple table at the Lao Banzhai. I ask you and Boss Du both to come and take a cup of thin wine. Friends who meet face to face dissolve every misunderstanding between them. How would that be?"

Seeing Huang Jinrong dissolve a conflict on the very edge of eruption in three or four sentences, Wang Yaqiao was naturally pleased, and agreed.

The next day Wang Yaqiao duly came to the famous Lao Banzhai restaurant and met Du Yuesheng and Zhang Xiaolin with full courtesy. After the wine had gone three rounds, the whole affair was flattened out under Huang Jinrong's management. Du Yuesheng dared not brush aside Huang Jinrong's face: he agreed on the spot to bring the Jiang'an back inside the Wusong bar to her wharf and hand her over to Wang Yaqiao entirely; he promised a solemn and generous burial for the dead Pig-head San Leng; and he agreed to deliver the killer Liu Ada to the French Concession police for detention and prosecution on a grave charge, to lay the hatred in the hearts of the Hatchet Gang brothers.

Seeing Du Yuesheng accept these conditions, Wang Yaqiao released the assassins who had gone to the Anhui Guildhall. A conflict that had come within an inch of a great murder case melted away like ice and snow under Huang Jinrong's mediation.

Wang Yaqiao had his Jiang'an, and had forced the three great personages of Shanghai to concede defeat and apologize besides; his delight knew no bounds. Only Du Yuesheng and Zhang Xiaolin, whenever they thought of the business, nursed a private grievance — for they had after all been beaten by an outsider from Anhui. One day Du Yuesheng went round again to Huang Jinrong's house to pour out his sense of injury. Huang Jinrong said: "Yuesheng, this is where you are wrong. Wang Yaqiao got the Jiang'an, and it was rightfully his to begin with. Why do you keep chewing it over?"

Du Yuesheng said: "Elder brother, one broken ship is neither here nor there. What I mean is that we three brothers have been making a noise in Shanghai for years — and in the end we were brought down by the chief of a hatchet gang. Looking back on it in time to come, won't it be a humiliation past all bearing?"

Zhang Xiaolin egged him on: "It truly does stick in the throat!"

Huang Jinrong said angrily: "If there is anger in your hearts, it shouldn't be laid at Wang Yaqiao's door. All this came of Li Guojie's mischief in the dark. If he hadn't married one daughter to two husbands at the start, would there have been this fight to the death between Boss Du and Wang Yaqiao? Every wrong has its author, every debt its debtor. That Li fellow should not be let off so lightly." Du and Zhang heard him, and nodded with understanding.

About a month later, in the depth of a night so black a man could not see his own five fingers, a black motorcar came fast along the road near the Waibaidu Bridge, heading for Suzhou Creek. It was past midnight and the road was nearly empty of walkers. And in that hour of ten thousand silences, the car braked quietly at the creek's edge.

The doors opened in the darkness and two furtive figures got out. They lifted out a stiffened corpse and threw it into the water; there was a heavy splash, and the dark ripples of Suzhou Creek flickered. In a moment the mysterious car had driven back the way it came, unseen by spirits, unknown to ghosts, and the creek returned to its usual calm.

Some days later, on a report from passers-by, the police dragged the body up — and it proved to be none other than Li Guojie, whose disappearance the papers had been carrying for days.

But for reasons nobody could explain, neither the Shanghai police nor the Concession force paid much attention to Li Guojie's death, and no one investigated or hunted for the killers. With the passage of time the assassination of Li Guojie was gradually forgotten. As for who murdered him, that had long since become a riddle of the ages that no one troubled to ask about.
