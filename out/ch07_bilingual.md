## H2 Chapter 7. I Half-Suspect the Spring Has Gone Next Door

> 荣家大门口，张灯结彩，布置的喜气洋洋。台阶上下的青石条被水冲洗的能照见人影子，朱漆大门上铜钉金灿灿的眩目。随着时间的推移，主人昔日赫赫的权势已不复存在，可是荣府门前那两个娇慵的石狮子依然荡漾着华贵的风采。那种从骨子里浸透出来得贵族气息弥漫着十足的优越感。
At the gate of the Rong house, lanterns and colored streamers had been hung, and everything was arranged in festive joy. The bluestone slabs of the steps, above and below, had been washed with water until they could mirror a person’s shadow, and the brass studs on the vermilion-lacquered gate dazzled the eye with gold. With the passing of time the master’s once-blazing power was no more; yet the two languid stone lions before the Rong mansion still rippled with an air of noble splendor. That aristocratic aura, steeped in the very marrow, spread a thorough sense of superiority.

> 昂首痴望的佣人们分散站在两边街口，连间壁的街道也打扫的干干净净。
The servants, heads raised and gazing eagerly, stood scattered at the street corners on either side, and even the neighboring street had been swept spotlessly clean.

> 低眉凝注的丫鬟们穿得一色的新春装，莲花条子的坎肩，碎花布的长裤，红扑扑的脸，齐眉的刘海，一条松软软的大辫子，个个都像年画上贴了统一标签的广告女孩。她们整整齐齐站了一排，迎候着即将回府的少爷。容光焕发的大太太和盛装以待的四太太在府门前徘徊了好一阵子了。
The maidservants, eyes downcast and intent, were dressed alike in new spring clothes—lotus-striped waistcoats, floral-print trousers, ruddy cheeks, fringes cut level with the brows, and a single soft, thick braid—every one of them like the advertisement girls on a New Year print, stamped with the same label. They stood in a neat row, awaiting the young masters soon to return home. First Madam, her face aglow, and Fourth Madam, waiting in full dress, had been pacing before the mansion gate a good while.

> 三太太推说自己肚子痛，躲在屋里不出来。懒得看他们上演“母子大团圆”的活话剧。谁叫自己不争气，没给荣家生一个儿子。两个女儿，没有一个是省心的。
Third Madam, pleading a stomachache, hid in her room and would not come out—she could not be bothered to watch them stage this living drama of “mother-and-son reunion.” Whose fault was it but her own that she had proved unequal to it, and borne the Rong house not a single son? Two daughters, and not one of them a comfort.

> 荣荣大学毕业后，眼高手低，一直没找着一个好婆家，成天没心没肺的和一帮少爷、小姐们吃喝玩乐，过着纸醉金迷的“夜生活”。说她一句不好听的话，她有十句话等着回你，夹枪带棒地说：“大太太还没厌弃我呢，关三太太什么事？我吃的、喝的都是荣家的钱，败光了，也轮不到姨奶奶教训。可怜我没从大太太屋里出来，不然，何至于二十多岁了还窝在家里碍眼。”言下之意，自己如果不是庶出的，早嫁到豪门去当少奶奶了。这些话没有一句不戳到三太太的心窝里的痛处，气得三太太再也不管她。
After Rongrong graduated from university, her sights set high and her gifts modest, she never did find a good family to marry into; all day long she ate, drank, and made merry, heartless and carefree, with a pack of young gentlemen and young ladies, living a “night life” of gilded dissipation. Say one unpleasant word to her, and she had ten words waiting to fling back, thrusting and jabbing: “First Madam hasn’t wearied of me yet—what business is it of Third Madam’s? What I eat and drink is all the Rong family’s money; even if I squander it all, it’s not for a concubine-aunt to lecture me. Pity me for not having come out of First Madam’s room—else why should I be past twenty and still stuck at home, an eyesore?” Her meaning being that, had she not been born of a concubine, she would long since have married into some great house as a young mistress. Not one of these words failed to stab Third Madam in the tenderest spot of her heart, and Third Madam, in a fury, gave up minding her altogether.

> 荣华的性格很内敛，大学还没毕业就跟大太太商量着自己要筹办一个小书局，大太太也舍得钱拿给她去折腾，折腾来折腾去，小书局改办了两层楼的书店。荣华隔三岔五的不回家，就在书店里睡。三太太要找她说个话，也不容易。更别说替她找婆家了。
Ronghua was reserved by nature. Before she had even finished university she was discussing with First Madam her wish to set up a small bookshop, and First Madam was willing to part with the money for her to fuss over it; fuss upon fuss, and the small bookshop became a two-story bookstore. Every few days Ronghua would not come home, but slept at the store instead. For Third Madam to get even a word with her was no easy thing—let alone to find her a husband.

> 还好，三太太身边有个伶俐的使唤丫头叫：杏儿，干活手脚麻利，心眼也多。不过，此刻杏儿的心，也不在三太太这里，她也惦记着到前面去看热闹呢。
Luckily, Third Madam had at her side a clever little maidservant called Xing’er, quick and deft at her work and full of wiles. Just now, though, Xing’er’s heart was not with Third Madam either; she too was thinking of going up front to see the excitement.

> 三太太也看出来了，拿话挤对她，说：“你要想去外面看那红头发、绿眉毛的西洋景，我也不拦你。只是，不要痴心妄想谁给你好脸子看。”
Third Madam saw it too, and needled her with words: “If you want to go out and gawk at the red-haired, green-browed foreign spectacle, I won’t stop you. Only, don’t fondly imagine that anyone will give you a kind face.”

> 杏儿说：“我才不指望谁给我好脸子看，我只想看看大少爷长什么样，还有那个阿初少爷。我呀，不是个男孩子，我要是大少爷的书童，跟着大少爷留洋，没准现在也是个什么博士了。哪像跟着您啊，做到死也是个丫头。”
Xing’er said: “I don’t count on anyone giving me a kind face at all; I only want to see what the eldest Young Master looks like, and that Young Master A-Chu too. Me—if only I weren’t a girl. If I’d been the eldest Young Master’s study-boy and gone abroad with him, I might be some sort of doctor by now. Not like serving you, where I’ll be a maid till the day I die.”

> “哟，哟，哟。委屈死你了。那你怎么不去啊？去啊！”三太太说。“我不稀罕你伺候。”
“Oh, oh, oh. Aren’t you hard done by. Then why don’t you go? Go, then!” said Third Madam. “I don’t prize your waiting on me.”

> “算了吧。”杏儿笑着说：“等我去了，你还不哭天抹泪的难过。你不稀罕我伺候，我稀罕伺候您啊。”说得三太太也笑了。
“Never mind,” Xing’er said with a smile. “The moment I left, you’d be weeping and wailing with grief. You may not prize my waiting on you, but I prize waiting on you.” It made Third Madam laugh too.

> 正说话间，就听见外面放鞭炮，炸响炸响，三太太想，一定是大少爷到家了。
As they were talking, they heard firecrackers going off outside, bang after bang, and Third Madam thought it must be that the eldest Young Master had reached home.

> 阿福的汽车一驶进弄堂，就听得一串鞭炮声炸响。阿初略向外一看，冷不防地吃了一惊，他看见大太太和四太太都站在外面迎接。这可使不得，礼遇太过隆重。他连忙叫阿福停车。
The moment A-Fu’s car drove into the lane, a string of firecrackers went off with a crash. A-Chu glanced out and was caught off guard with a start: he saw both First Madam and Fourth Madam standing outside to welcome them. This would not do—the courtesy was far too grand. He hastily told A-Fu to stop the car.

> 车直开到丫鬟们的面前，还没停稳，阿初就下来了。
The car drove right up before the maidservants, and before it had even come to a stop A-Chu was out of it.

> “大少爷您好！”丫鬟们异口同声地鞠躬问安。
“Greetings, eldest Young Master!” The maidservants bowed and asked after him with one voice.

> 阿初笑着解释说：“姐姐们误会了，我不是大少爷，我是阿初。大少爷还没到……”
A-Chu explained with a smile: “You’ve mistaken me, sisters—I’m not the eldest Young Master, I’m A-Chu. The eldest Young Master hasn’t arrived yet…”

> 他还没解释完，丫鬟们又齐刷刷地鞠躬，说：“阿初少爷，你好！”
Before he had finished explaining, the maidservants bowed again in unison and said: “Greetings, Young Master A-Chu!”

> 阿初还没答话，四太太已经含着泪奔过来了。“阿初！”
Before A-Chu could answer, Fourth Madam had already rushed over with tears in her eyes. “A-Chu!”

> “干娘。”阿初本打算见了四太太，好好地给她行个大礼，没想到四太太一走过来，就把他紧紧抱住，大哭起来。阿初劝也不是，不劝也不是，只好跟着四太太难过。
“Godmother.” A-Chu had meant, on seeing Fourth Madam, to make her a proper deep bow; but the moment she came over she clasped him tightly and burst into tears. To comfort her was awkward, and not to comfort her was awkward, so A-Chu could only grieve along with her.

> 这时，丽水兴高采烈地迎着大太太跑去，抱着大太太又哭又笑，一五一十地跟大太太讲荣升的事情。说荣升在码头遇见一个晕倒的姑娘，执意送她去了医院。荣升的脾气大家都是知道的，谁也拘束不了他，大约要等一会才能回家，叫大太太不必担心。大太太这才略舒了一口气。
Just then Lishui, in high spirits, ran to meet First Madam, embraced her, and wept and laughed at once as she told her, point by point, all about Rong Sheng. She said Rong Sheng had met a girl who had fainted at the dock and had insisted on taking her to the hospital. Everyone knew Rong Sheng’s temper; no one could hold him back, and it would likely be a while before he came home, so First Madam need not worry. Only then did First Madam breathe a little easier.

> 四太太哭了一会，猛想起大太太来，自觉有失礼数。赶紧叫阿初给大太太磕头。阿初到大太太跟前行礼，大太太笑着说：“现在不同从前了，好歹阿初也是一个留洋的博士了，旧规矩不用因循了。”四太太揩了揩泪，说：“大太太慈悲，没有大太太，哪有我们阿初今天的出人头地，旧规矩不用因循，恩情须是要铭记的。”说着，说着，四太太自己先跪了下去，阿初赶紧随四太太跪了，一同给大太太磕头。“罢了，罢了。我知道你们的心。”大太太一把将四太太扶了，吩咐阿初起来，说，等大少爷回来，一家人给祖宗上香去。
When Fourth Madam had wept a while, she suddenly remembered First Madam and felt she had been remiss in courtesy. She quickly told A-Chu to kowtow to First Madam. A-Chu came before First Madam to make his bow, and First Madam said with a smile: “Things are not as they were before; A-Chu is, after all, a doctor trained overseas now—there’s no need to hold to the old rules.” Fourth Madam wiped her tears and said: “First Madam is merciful; without First Madam, how could our A-Chu have risen in the world as he has today? The old rules need not be kept—but kindness must be graven on the heart.” As she spoke, Fourth Madam knelt down first herself, and A-Chu hurriedly knelt with her, and together they kowtowed to First Madam. “Enough, enough. I know what is in your hearts.” First Madam raised Fourth Madam up with one hand, and bade A-Chu rise, saying that when the eldest Young Master came home the whole family would go to burn incense to the ancestors.

> 一场热热闹闹的迎归大戏，因为主角的缺席，而显得虎头蛇尾。直到掌灯时分，荣升才回来。大太太自然也免不了喜极而泣的俗套，母子二人一阵欢喜、一阵伤心。给祖宗上了香后，母子二人促膝长谈，说不完几年来得悲欢离合，人世沧桑。大太太见荣升声音清朗，形容也不憔悴了，暗暗感谢上苍，总算是合浦珠还，称心如意。
A lively grand drama of homecoming, for want of its leading man, began with a flourish and trailed off to nothing. Not until lamp-lighting time did Rong Sheng return. First Madam of course could not escape the cliché of weeping for very joy; mother and son knew now a spell of gladness, now a spell of sorrow. After incense had been burned to the ancestors, the two of them talked knee to knee, an endless telling of the partings and reunions, the joys and griefs of these several years, and all the vicissitudes of the world. Seeing that Rong Sheng’s voice was clear and his looks no longer haggard, First Madam thanked heaven in secret: the pearls had returned to Hepu at last, and her heart’s wish was fulfilled.

> 天气仿佛还是早春，冷飕飕的。窗外的空气特别新鲜，一大片郁郁葱葱的绿叶在窗沿外招摇，春烟润着绿油油的叶子，系恋着生命的趣味。
The weather still seemed to be early spring, chill and biting. The air beyond the window was uncommonly fresh; a great spread of lush green leaves swayed outside the sill, and the spring haze moistened the glossy foliage, clinging to the delight of life.

> 和雅淑醒来得时候，既恍惚，又迷茫。她睁着一双忧惧的眼睛，先是痴望着头顶上粉白粉白的天花板，然后，目光缓缓移动到输液瓶上。她不敢轻举妄动，她觉得身体很疼，浑身乏力。她看见有一个清瘦的男子正坐在病房里的椅子上看报纸，那人的头发梳得一丝不苟，穿着一件蓝缎子长衫，身上弥漫着淡淡的茉莉花茶的香气，虽然报纸挡住了那人的面目，她从那人的坐姿和穿着上也能感觉到这个人的温文尔雅，格调不俗。
When He Yashu awoke, she was at once dazed and bewildered. Opening a pair of frightened eyes, she first gazed blankly at the pinkish-white ceiling overhead, then let her eyes move slowly to the drip bottle. She dared not stir rashly; her body ached, and she was weak all over. She saw a lean, spare man sitting in a chair in the sickroom, reading a newspaper. His hair was combed without a strand out of place, and he wore a blue satin gown; a faint fragrance of jasmine tea hung about him. Though the newspaper hid his face, from his posture and his dress she could feel that this was a man of gentle refinement and no common taste.

> 她想着自己百事乖违，落魄无靠，珠泪儿滚滚而下，哽咽了起来。
Thinking how all her affairs had gone against her, how she was cast down and without support, she let her pearl-tears roll down and began to sob.

> “你醒了？”她的悲伤，换来了荣升的问候。荣升也不知道为什么，自己看见和雅淑晕倒的一瞬间，身体上反而有了重生一次的幻觉。
“You’re awake?” Her sorrow was answered by Rong Sheng’s greeting. Rong Sheng himself did not know why, but in the instant he had seen He Yashu faint, his body had felt, on the contrary, the illusion of being born once again.

> “你感觉怎么样？你已经昏睡了三天了。”
“How do you feel? You’ve lain unconscious for three days now.”

> “我感觉很难……”和雅淑难过地说不下去。
“I feel it’s very hard…” He Yashu, in her grief, could not go on.

> “很难受吗？”
“Is it very hard to bear?”

> “很难，活下去。”雅淑说。
“Very hard—to go on living,” said Yashu.

> 荣升沉默了。
Rong Sheng fell silent.

> 面对一个不想活下去的人，使他想起从前的自己。
Faced with someone who did not want to go on living, he was reminded of his own former self.

> 他温柔地看着躺在床上，脸色苍白的雅淑，说：“你失去了你一生中最心爱的人吗？”
He looked tenderly at Yashu, lying on the bed with her pale face, and said: “Have you lost the person you loved most in all your life?”

> 雅淑摇头。
Yashu shook her head.

> “那，你为什么不想活？”
“Then why do you not want to live?”

> “我家里人逼我嫁人。”
“My family is forcing me to marry.”

> “你宁死也不肯嫁？”
“You’d rather die than marry?”

> “是。”
“Yes.”

> “为什么？”
“Why?”

> “我们没有感情。”
“We have no feeling for each other.”

> “感情是可以培养的。”荣升说。当年他和妻子结婚的时候，也是互不相识的。
“Feeling can be cultivated.” said Rong Sheng. When he had married his own wife in those days, they too had been strangers to each other.

> “他在外面养小妾。”雅淑的声音有些激动。“他好逸恶劳，他还抽鸦片。”
“He keeps a concubine outside.” Yashu’s voice grew somewhat agitated. “He loves ease and loathes work, and he smokes opium besides.”

> 荣升的脸阴沉起来，他突然站直了身子，脸冲着墙，不说话。雅淑感觉到了他的不快，问他：“你怎么了？”
Rong Sheng’s face darkened; he suddenly stood up straight, turned his face to the wall, and said nothing. Yashu sensed his displeasure and asked him: “What’s the matter with you?”

> “我也吸过鸦片。”荣升的声音很沉很冷。
“I have smoked opium too.” Rong Sheng’s voice was very low and very cold.

> 雅淑长叹了一口气。说：“戒了吧。”
Yashu heaved a long sigh. “Give it up,” she said.

> “已经戒了。”荣升说：“但是，很想再抽。精神上很不容易控制。”
“I’ve already given it up.” said Rong Sheng. “But I badly want to smoke again. It is very hard to master in the mind.”

> “是先生救了我。”雅淑突然把话岔开。“我却没有偿还先生恩情的能力。”
“It was you, sir, who saved me.” Yashu turned the talk aside abruptly. “Yet I have no means to repay your kindness, sir.”

> “我没有救你。”荣升淡淡地说：“你不必偿还。”他走到病房门口，又转过身，说：“你的医药费我已经提前预付了，等你身体康复了，你就自行出院吧。”
“I didn’t save you.” said Rong Sheng mildly. “You need not repay anything.” He walked to the door of the sickroom, then turned back and said: “I’ve paid your medical fees in advance; when you’ve recovered your health, you may discharge yourself.”

> “等一下。”雅淑支撑着坐起来。“先生您还来吗？”
“Wait.” Yashu propped herself up to sit. “Will you come again, sir?”

> “如果，我来，不带给你任何压力的话……”
“If my coming brings you no pressure of any kind…”

> “那么，先生请再来。”雅淑表明了态度。
“Then please come again, sir.” Yashu made her feeling plain.

> “好，那么，再会。”荣升走出病房，关上了门。
“Good. Until we meet again, then.” Rong Sheng walked out of the sickroom and closed the door.

> 雅淑看见椅子上翻落的《上海白话报》上一张引人触目的大照片，那是自己昏倒在地的惨相和一条消息：上海药业大家族掌门人荣升大少爷与一神秘女子于三天前秘密入住同一家病房。该女子疑为荣大少的秘密情人……
Yashu saw, on the Shanghai Vernacular News that had fallen open on the chair, a large, striking photograph—the pitiful sight of herself fainting to the ground—and a news item: Rong Sheng, eldest Young Master and head of the great Rong family of the Shanghai medicine trade, checked secretly into the same hospital room three days ago with a mysterious woman. The woman is suspected to be Young Master Rong’s secret lover…

> “梨云阁”的雕花栏杆下，荣府的丫鬟蝉儿和杏儿正给红嘴绿鹦鹉喂食，阳光暖暖地映在鹦鹉架上，特别惬意。两个人一边嬉笑，一边说着闲话。
Beneath the carved railing of the “Liyun Pavilion,” the Rong household’s maidservants Chan’er and Xing’er were feeding a red-billed green parrot, the sunlight falling warm on the parrot’s perch, wonderfully pleasant. The two of them chattered and joked as they gossiped.

> “大少爷还歇在大太太房里吗？”杏儿问。
“Is the eldest Young Master still resting in First Madam’s room?” Xing’er asked.

> “可不。大太太想了这么多年儿子，眼泪集了几大筐。好容易盼到大少爷完好无损的回来，那还不得宝贝似的供着。”
“Of course. First Madam has longed for her son so many years, she’s gathered whole basketfuls of tears. Now that she’s at last waited out his safe and sound return, wouldn’t she keep him like a treasure?”

> “算算大少爷回来有大半个月了吧？”
“Reckoning it up, the eldest Young Master’s been back a good half-month now, hasn’t he?”

> “是啊。”蝉儿的手被鹦鹉啄了一下。“哎呀，真该打。饿死鬼投胎啊你。”蝉儿用金匙敲了敲它的头。
“That’s right.” Chan’er’s hand was pecked by the parrot. “Ai-ya, you deserve a beating. Born of a starved ghost, you.” Chan’er rapped its head with a gold spoon.

> 鹦鹉叫起来：“少爷回来了，少爷回来了。”
The parrot cried out: “The Young Master’s back, the Young Master’s back.”

> “少爷回来了，一样打你。”蝉儿傲气地说。
“Young Master’s back or not, I’ll beat you just the same.” said Chan’er haughtily.

> “你没听说吗？二少爷也来了。”
“Haven’t you heard? The second Young Master’s come too.”

> “还不是来伸手要钱的，大太太烦着他呢。”
“Come to hold out his hand for money, no doubt—First Madam’s vexed with him.”

> “听府里老人说，老爷在世的时候，最疼二太太。”
“The old folk in the house say that when the Master was alive, he doted most on Second Madam.”

> “胡说。老爷最疼的是四太太。”蝉儿说。“不然，阿初少爷能出国？”
“Nonsense. The one the Master doted on most was Fourth Madam.” said Chan’er. “Else how could Young Master A-Chu have gone abroad?”

> “也是。”杏儿想想，说：“阿初少爷真是前世修来得好福气，一个沿街乞讨的小叫花子硬叫四太太拣回来做了干儿子。怎么没有人认我回家去做大小姐。”
“True enough.” Xing’er thought, and said: “Young Master A-Chu really has good fortune earned in a former life—a little beggar-child cadging along the street, and Fourth Madam would have him back to be her godson. How is it no one claims me home to be a young mistress?”

> “同人不同命嘛。”蝉儿忽然神秘地笑起来。“你知道吗？大太太想给大少爷纳妾呢。”
“Same folk, different fates.” Chan’er suddenly gave a mysterious smile. “Did you know? First Madam means to take a concubine for the eldest Young Master.”

> “纳妾？为什么不给大少爷娶妻呢？”
“A concubine? Why not marry the eldest Young Master a wife?”

> “大少爷眼界高着呢，可这香火得续吧。不知那个丫头命好，能飞上枝头当凤凰。”
“The eldest Young Master’s sights are set high, but the family line must be carried on. Who knows which lucky girl will fly up to the branch and turn phoenix.”

> “你看着我干吗？”杏儿被蝉儿看得心慌。
“What are you looking at me for?” Xing’er grew flustered under Chan’er’s gaze.

> “你不就盼着嫁个少爷吗？”
“Isn’t it a young master you’re hoping to marry?”

> “得分是谁。要是大少爷，八抬大轿抬我进门，我还不肯呢。”
“That depends on which. If it were the eldest Young Master—even carried in through the door in an eight-bearer sedan, I still wouldn’t have him.”

> “你失心疯了。大少爷都不肯嫁，想嫁谁？”
“You’ve lost your wits. If you won’t marry the eldest Young Master, who would you marry?”

> “嫁阿初。”杏儿欢快地笑起来。
“A-Chu.” Xing’er laughed merrily.

> “做梦吧你。”蝉儿推攘着杏儿，赶巧阿初从侧门进来，杏儿刚好撞到他怀里。阿初抱歉地往后退了几步，蝉儿大笑起来，杏儿不服气地赶过去打蝉儿，蝉儿趁机跑到阿初背后，说：“杏儿丫头想嫁人想疯了，阿初少爷替她找个婆家吧。”
“Dream on.” Chan’er gave Xing’er a shove—and just then A-Chu came in by the side door, and Xing’er fell smack into his arms. A-Chu stepped back a few paces in apology; Chan’er roared with laughter, and Xing’er, refusing to take it, chased over to hit Chan’er, who seized the chance to duck behind A-Chu and said: “The girl Xing’er’s gone mad to marry—Young Master A-Chu, find her a husband, won’t you?”

> “你还嚼舌头！”杏儿叉了腰示威。
“Still wagging your tongue!” Xing’er put her hands on her hips in defiance.

> 阿初笑着说：“开玩笑呢。姐姐们看我面子吧，不要闹了。”
A-Chu said with a smile: “They’re only joking. For my sake, sisters—don’t quarrel.”

> “她正是要看你的面子呢。”蝉儿还在笑。
“It’s your face she’s after, right enough.” Chan’er was still laughing.

> 杏儿真有些生气了，掉转了头要走，阿初忙叫住她。“杏儿姐姐，我正有事找你呢。”杏儿停住脚步，心下有点得意，问：“什么事啊？”
Xing’er was truly a little angry now, and turned her head to leave; A-Chu quickly called her back. “Sister Xing’er, I’ve been meaning to find you about something.” Xing’er halted, a touch pleased at heart, and asked: “About what?”

> “我回来这么久了，一直没瞧见二位小姐，我想，总要打个招呼才好。烦姐姐费心替我留意，哪天她们回来了，叫我一声。”
“I’ve been back so long and still haven’t seen the two young ladies; I think I really ought to pay my respects. Might I trouble you, sister, to keep a watch for me, and give me a call whichever day they come home.”

> “大小姐是个夜猫子，白天见不着。二小姐成天泡在书店里，不爱回家，你要去她的书店，准能遇见她。”
“The eldest young lady’s a night owl—you won’t see her by day. The second young lady’s forever steeped in her bookstore and doesn’t like coming home; if you go to her store, you’re sure to meet her.”

> “哪家书店？”阿初问。
“Which bookstore?” A-Chu asked.

> “华美书店。”杏儿答。
“The Huamei Bookstore.” Xing’er answered.

> “阿初少爷，你这会就去吗？”蝉儿问。“你要去呀，把杏儿也带去，她做梦也想跟少爷们一同出去满大街逛……”
“Young Master A-Chu, are you going now?” Chan’er asked. “If you’re going, take Xing’er along—she dreams even in her sleep of going out with the young masters to roam the whole city…”

> 杏儿把头仰起来。“你以为我不敢啊，只要阿初少爷愿意？”
Xing’er lifted her head. “You think I wouldn’t dare—so long as Young Master A-Chu is willing?”

> 十八、九岁的丫鬟们最是纯情的，她们急于表达自己的爱慕之情，取悦于自己心仪的男子，却没有丝毫的“杂念”和“媚态”，这让阿初感觉到她们朴素的美丽。
Maidservants of eighteen or nineteen are the most innocent of hearts; eager to express their adoration and to please the man they fancy, they yet had not the least “ulterior thought” or “coquetry,” and this let A-Chu feel their plain, unadorned beauty.

> “我不急。我来看看大少爷在不在大太太屋里？”阿初说。
“I’m in no hurry. I’ve come to see whether the eldest Young Master is in First Madam’s room.” said A-Chu.

> “大少爷，一大早又出去了。这会子二少爷正在大太太屋里呢。”蝉儿做了个鬼脸。
“The eldest Young Master went out again first thing this morning. Just now it’s the second Young Master who’s in First Madam’s room.” Chan’er made a face.

> “二少爷来了？”阿初准备进去。蝉儿一把拖住阿初。“不要进去。大太太不喜欢二少爷，你现在进去，自讨没趣。”
“The second Young Master’s come?” A-Chu made to go in. Chan’er grabbed hold of him. “Don’t go in. First Madam doesn’t like the second Young Master; if you go in now, you’ll only be asking for a snub.”

> “那我先回书房去。”阿初知道这些丫鬟是最能揣摩主人心思的，依着她们的话做，没错。
“Then I’ll go back to the study first.” A-Chu knew that these maidservants were the best at reading their masters’ minds, and that to go by their word was never wrong.

> 不过，不相见要碰见的事是常有的。
Still, it often happens that those who would not meet, meet all the same.

> 阿初回自己住的“墨菊斋”没多久，就发现院子里站着一个人，东张西望地四处看。这个人二十多岁年纪，穿了一身洗得发白的青布衫，头发梳得很整齐，脚上穿着一双质量较好的布鞋，浑身上下收拾得很干净。阿初第一个反应就是二少爷荣归来了。
A-Chu had not been back long in his own quarters, the “Moju Studio,” when he noticed someone standing in the courtyard, glancing this way and that. The man was a little past twenty, dressed in a blue cloth gown washed pale, his hair combed very neat, and on his feet a pair of fairly good cloth shoes; he was clean and tidy from head to foot. A-Chu’s first thought was that the second Young Master, Rong Gui, had come.

> “是二少爷吗？”阿初问。
“Is it the second Young Master?” A-Chu asked.

> “是。”荣归有些拘谨地回答。
“Yes.” Rong Gui answered with some constraint.

> 得到肯定答案后的阿初，赶紧笑着出来，说：“您来了，也不知会我一声，您请进来坐吧。”
Having got his answer, A-Chu hurried out with a smile and said: “You’ve come, and never let me know—please come in and sit.”

> 阿初内穿着崭新的衬衣，外套一件熨贴的西背，金色的领带夹泛着光，足下是一双雪亮的皮鞋。荣归很是自惭形秽，低着头，还没讲话，脸先红了。“我，我找我大哥。”
A-Chu wore a brand-new shirt beneath a smoothly pressed Western waistcoat, his gold tie-clip gleaming, and on his feet a pair of brightly polished leather shoes. Rong Gui, keenly ashamed of his own shabbiness, hung his head; before he had even spoken, his face reddened first. “I—I’ve come to see my elder brother.”

> “他一大早出去了，您进来坐吧。”
“He went out first thing this morning—please come in and sit.”

> “怕，怕打扰你了。”荣归的脖子不由自主地往后缩了缩。
“I’m afraid—afraid of disturbing you.” Rong Gui’s neck shrank back in spite of himself.

> “打扰什么？”阿初笑地十分阳光。“我一个人正无聊呢。您来了，正好说说话。大少爷昨天还跟我提起二少爷。”
“Disturbing what?” A-Chu’s smile was full of sunshine. “I was just bored here on my own. You’ve come at the right time for a talk. Only yesterday the eldest Young Master was speaking to me of the second Young Master.”

> “我大哥提起我了？”
“My elder brother spoke of me?”

> “是啊，还说过了清明节，专程去看您。”
“Yes, and said that after the Qingming festival he’d make a special trip to see you.”

> 荣归突然有些感动。“大哥真这么说？”
Rong Gui was suddenly a little moved. “Did my elder brother really say so?”

> “我骗您做什么？”阿初心底自始至终都很同情这位二少爷。“您还是进来等吧，他就快回来了。”
“What would I gain by deceiving you?” From first to last A-Chu felt deep sympathy for this second Young Master. “Do come in and wait; he’ll be back soon.”

> 荣归在阿初热情地邀请下，局促地走进了大哥的书房。
At A-Chu’s warm invitation, Rong Gui walked awkwardly into his elder brother’s study.

> 书房摆设异常雅致，虽然布置的简单明了，但是，明眼人一看就知道，这间房子里所有的东西都“价值不菲”。所谓富贵人家，“富贵”逼人。
The study was furnished with rare taste; simple and plain though its arrangement was, any discerning eye could see at a glance that everything in the room was “worth no small sum.” In a house of wealth and rank, as they say, the wealth and rank press in upon you.

> “您喝什么？咖啡？还是茶？”阿初问。
“What will you drink? Coffee? Or tea?” A-Chu asked.

> “不，不麻烦你了。随便，喝杯水就行了。”
“No—don’t trouble yourself. Anything; a cup of water will do.”

> “麻烦什么？您来了就是客人。”说到这，阿初的话突然打住了。阿初觉得自己的话有些唐突，他如果是客人，自己算什么人？自己也不是主人，倒说他是客人？于是，敷衍地笑笑。
“What trouble? Now that you’ve come, you’re a guest.” Here A-Chu’s words suddenly broke off. He felt what he had said was a little abrupt—if the man was a guest, then what was he himself? He was not the master either, and yet he called the other a guest? So he smiled it off perfunctorily.

> 一杯香浓可口的咖啡端了上来。
A cup of fragrant, mellow coffee was brought in.

> “二少爷在哪里公干？”
“Where does the second Young Master work?”

> “在乡下教书。”
“Teaching in the countryside.”

> “教哪门课？”
“Which subject do you teach?”

> “中国历史。”荣归坐得很规矩，答得很认真。“听说，初，初先生是留洋的博士？”
“Chinese history.” Rong Gui sat very properly and answered very earnestly. “I hear that—that Mr. Chu is a doctor trained overseas?”

> 阿初点点头。
A-Chu nodded.

> 荣归十分羡慕地说：“可惜，我没有出国深造的机会。”
Rong Gui said, with great envy: “A pity I never had the chance to study abroad.”

> “二少奶奶身体怎么样？”阿初巧妙地把话题拉开。
“How is the second young mistress’s health?” A-Chu deftly drew the talk aside.

> “还好，她最近快生了。我，我就是为了这件事来找大哥的。想，想。”
“Well enough—she’s near her time now. I—it was for just this that I came to see my elder brother. I wanted—wanted…”

> 阿初望着他。荣归的额头上冒出汗来，他掏出手绢来擦了擦汗。“我想找大哥要点奶粉钱。”话终于说出口了，荣归反而不紧张了。“就是这件事。”
A-Chu looked at him. Sweat broke out on Rong Gui’s forehead; he drew out a handkerchief and wiped it. “I wanted to ask my elder brother for a little milk-powder money.” Once the words were out at last, Rong Gui was, on the contrary, no longer nervous. “That’s all it is.”

> “您跟太太讲过了吧？”
“You’ve spoken to the madam about it?”

> “是。”
“Yes.”

> “太太怎么说？”
“And what did the madam say?”

> “太太说，毕竟是一家子骨肉，原也应该帮忙的。只是，大家子有大家子的难处，要等到秋后收了乡下的租子，才有现钱呢。我，我想，我是可以等，但是孩子不等人啊。所以，所以到大哥这里来，碰碰运气。”
“The madam said that, being one flesh and blood after all, we ought of course to help. Only, a great house has a great house’s difficulties; not until the autumn rents are gathered in from the country will there be ready cash. I—I thought, I can wait, but a child won’t wait. So—so I came to my elder brother here, to try my luck.”

> “您需要多少钱？”
“How much money do you need?”

> “三百块。”
“Three hundred.”

> “您请等一下。”阿初转身进了里屋，过了一会，阿初从里面拿了钱和一支装潢精美的钢笔出来。“三百块钱您先收着。这支笔是大少爷从英国带回来，送给二少爷的礼物。”
“Please wait a moment.” A-Chu turned and went into the inner room, and after a while came out with money and a beautifully finished fountain pen. “Take this three hundred first. This pen is a gift the eldest Young Master brought back from England for the second Young Master.”

> “怎么好拿你的钱？”
“How can I take your money?”

> “权当我孝敬二少爷和二少奶奶的。改天得了空，我去府上给二少奶奶请安。”
“Count it as my token of respect to the second Young Master and the second young mistress. Some day when I have time, I’ll call at your home to pay my respects to the second young mistress.”

> 荣归真的感动了，满口的谢谢，就差给阿初作揖了。目的达到了，荣归又急着回去，怕出门晚了，赶不上回去的末班车。阿初也不强留了，于是，送出门来。荣归又反复的千恩万谢，急急地去了。
Rong Gui was truly moved, thanks pouring from his mouth, all but bowing to A-Chu with clasped hands. His end achieved, Rong Gui was in a hurry to get back, afraid that if he left too late he would miss the last bus home. A-Chu did not press him to stay, and saw him out. With thanks past all counting, over and over, Rong Gui hurried away.

> 晚上，荣升回来，阿初淡淡地跟他提了几句荣归的事，荣升漠不关心地“哼”了几声，倒是丽水过来，唠唠叨叨说荣归缺钱，问荣升手上有没有现钱，毕竟是一家人。荣升只淡淡说了一句。“我这里住着一个观音菩萨，惯会修桥铺路，你还怕他空着手回去？”
In the evening, when Rong Sheng came home, A-Chu mentioned Rong Gui’s affair to him in a few offhand words. Rong Sheng gave a few indifferent grunts; it was Lishui, rather, who came over and nagged on about Rong Gui being short of money, asking Rong Sheng whether he had any ready cash on hand—they were one family, after all. Rong Sheng only said, mildly, a single sentence: “I’ve a Bodhisattva Guanyin living here with me, forever fond of building bridges and mending roads—do you really fear he’d let anyone go home empty-handed?”

> 四月的天气，有些阴冷。不过，对阿初来说，工作的热情远胜过天气的冷淡。回国不久，他就在“同济”医院找到了一份工作，本来，大太太和四太太要阿初留在荣家药行里干，可是阿初说，先到外面去历练历练，对将来更好的为荣家工作有帮助，大太太也就顺水推舟的同意了。
The weather of April was somewhat gray and cold. But for A-Chu, his ardor for work far outdid the coldness of the weather. Not long after returning home he found a post at the “Tongji” hospital. First Madam and Fourth Madam had wanted A-Chu to stay and work in the Rong family’s medicine business; but A-Chu said that to go and season himself outside first would help him serve the Rong house better in future, and First Madam, going with the current, agreed.

> 清风如许，皓月当空。在四太太居住的“红梨阁”里，传来阵阵优雅的外国古典音乐，那是阿初在摆弄从英国带回来得留声机，四太太情不自禁地跟着音乐哼了起来，阿初十分好奇地问：“四太太也会跳舞？”
A fresh breeze, a bright moon high in the sky. From the “Hongli Pavilion” where Fourth Madam lived came strains of elegant foreign classical music—it was A-Chu playing with the gramophone he had brought back from England—and Fourth Madam could not help humming along. A-Chu asked, very curious: “You can dance too, Fourth Madam?”

> “你小看我吧？想当年我跟着太后老佛爷……”四太太突然不说话了。
“You think little of me, do you? In my day, when I was in attendance on the Empress Dowager, the Old Buddha…” Fourth Madam suddenly fell silent.

> “是啊，您是谁啊？您是阿初的干娘啊。”阿初背对着她拨弄唱片，看不见四太太的脸，美妙的音乐划过四太太的耳膜。“有什么能难倒您的？”阿初转过身子，和着音乐的节奏做出一个无比幽雅地邀请姿势。说：“尊贵的夫人，我谨以诚挚的心，邀您月下共舞一曲--”四太太不知不觉地被阿初牵引到中庭，阿初笑盈盈揽着四太太的腰，四太太轻盈盈扶着阿初的肩，踏着温柔的节拍，翩翩起舞。月光下，阿初的面容更加清晰，四太太甚至不敢平视他的眼睛，可是她紧紧贴近他的胸膛，一瞬间，眼中蕴涵的泪珠像无数钻沙的爬虫，很快冲破眼眶的最后防线，四太太哭了。
“Indeed—and who are you? You are A-Chu’s godmother.” With his back to her, A-Chu fiddled with the record and could not see her face, the lovely music brushing across her eardrums. “What is there that could ever get the better of you?” A-Chu turned about and, keeping to the rhythm of the music, struck an inexpressibly graceful attitude of invitation. He said: “Honored lady, with a sincere heart I beg to invite you to a dance beneath the moon—” Without knowing it, Fourth Madam was drawn by A-Chu into the central court; smiling, A-Chu took her about the waist, and lightly she rested her hands on his shoulders, and to the tender beat they danced. In the moonlight A-Chu’s face was clearer still; Fourth Madam dared not even look levelly into his eyes, yet she pressed close against his breast, and in an instant the tears held in her eyes, like so many burrowing sand-mites, broke swiftly through the last defense of her lids, and Fourth Madam wept.

> “干娘。”阿初试探性地问：“您，是不是我亲娘？”
“Godmother.” A-Chu asked, testing: “Are you—are you my own mother?”

> 四太太惊愕。“为什么会有这种想法？”
Fourth Madam was startled. “What makes you think such a thing?”

> “您，只需要回答：'是'、还是'不是'。”阿初认真地看着四太太的表情。
“You—you need only answer: ‘yes,’ or ‘no.’” A-Chu watched Fourth Madam’s expression closely.

> “不是！”四太太回答。
“No!” Fourth Madam answered.

> 阿初的舞步戛然而止。他看得出四太太讲的是真话，因为是真话，反而使阿初有些失望。
A-Chu’s dancing steps came to an abrupt halt. He could see that Fourth Madam spoke the truth—and because it was the truth, it left A-Chu, on the contrary, somewhat disappointed.

> “我虽然不是你的亲娘，但是，我可以肯定地告诉你，我是你的亲人。”四太太把头再一次埋到阿初的胸口上，让泪水尽情地淌下来。
“Though I am not your own mother, I can tell you for certain that I am your own kin.” Fourth Madam buried her head once more against A-Chu’s chest, and let her tears flow freely.

> 阿初再次迈开舞步，引领着四太太进入曼妙的音乐世界。阿初觉得只有在这个音乐的幻想世界，自己可以拥有那一份失去的母爱。
A-Chu took up the dance again, leading Fourth Madam into the enchanting world of the music. A-Chu felt that only in this dreamworld of music could he possess that lost portion of a mother’s love.

> “呸！什么东西！”三太太隔着雕花窗子狠狠地唾了一口。“横竖都是两个来历不明的贼王八！”
“Pah! What creatures!” Through the carved window Third Madam spat viciously. “Both of them nothing but two thieving bastards of unknown origin!”

> “三太太，不要这样讲嘛。”杏儿皱着眉头说。
“Third Madam, don’t talk like that.” Xing’er said with a frown.

> “为什么不讲？一个男的、一个女的，也不想想是什么辈分、什么身份？主子、奴才就这样搂着转圈圈……”
“Why shouldn’t I? A man and a woman, and never a thought for what generation, what station they are? Mistress and slave, clasped together turning round and round like that…”

> “什么搂着转圈圈，那是交际舞，我听大小姐说过，现在外面最流行、最时髦的莫过于此，你不懂就不要讲嘛。”杏儿的眼光始终没有离开雕花窗子。
“What ‘clasped together turning round and round’—that’s ballroom dancing. I’ve heard the eldest young lady speak of it; nothing is more in fashion, more the mode outside now than this. If you don’t understand it, then don’t talk.” Xing’er’s gaze never once left the carved window.

> “你还看！”三太太恶声恶气地拉着杏儿走过回廊。
“Still watching!” In a nasty, snarling voice Third Madam dragged Xing’er off along the covered walk.
