### Garden Bridge

In the opening years of the Xianfeng reign of the Qing, an Englishman built a great bridge over the Wusong River; a man was posted at the bridgehead, and every person crossing paid two cash in copper, carts and sedan-chairs double, so that the foot-passenger, though he grudged the bother of it, had no help for the matter. When the guiyou year of the Tongzhi reign came round, the bridge was bought out by the Municipal Council, and as the Tianhou Temple Bridge, the Inner Baidu Bridge and the Pentang Lane Bridge were one after another thrown up besides, from that day on no toll was taken of anyone who passed. This bridge is the very one that now spans Suzhou Creek — that lofty, mighty structure, its deck laid with steel rails, over which the trams run day and night: the Garden Bridge.
