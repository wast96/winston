## H2 Chapter 18. Drawing the Silk, Climbing the Vine, a Single Thread

> “您说什么？”
“What did you say?”

> “我说什么？”阿初揶揄一笑。“我说的是被你们刻意抹杀的真相。让我们来回顾一下从前、过去、曾经流逝过的光阴吧。那些深思熟虑的谎言、循环不已的虚构，不仅没有扰乱我的视线，正相反，重重疑窦，为我开启了故事的机关。故事真正的情节，其实非常简单。”
“What did I say?” A-Chu gave a teasing smile. “I spoke of the truth you people took such pains to blot out. Let us look back a while over the former days, the past, the time that once slipped away. Those carefully considered lies, those fictions spun round and round without end, not only did not cloud my sight — on the contrary, doubt heaped upon doubt threw open for me the very workings of the story. The real plot of it is, in fact, exceedingly simple.”

> 阿初神态祥和，语气平静，正是这反常的祥和与平静，令人不寒而栗，杀气在他忧郁的眉宇间弥漫，离奇的故事从阿初的唇间舌底得以展开……
A-Chu’s manner was serene, his tone calm; and it was precisely this unnatural serenity and calm that set a man shivering though he was not cold. A killing air spread across his melancholy brow, and the strange tale unfolded from between his lips and the root of his tongue…

> 我从来没有怀疑过姐姐对我的爱，至亲至纯，致美致善。
Never once had I doubted my sister’s love for me — closest of kin and purest, most beautiful and most good.

> 直到你的出现。
Until you appeared.

> 我对家庭曾经发生的惨剧，原先是一无所知的，直到姐姐告诉我“全部的真相”，我权且把它称为第一个故事。
Of the tragedy that had once befallen the family I had known nothing at all, until my sister told me “the whole truth” — which for the moment I shall call the first story.

> 第一个故事，充满了黑暗，兄弟相残，叔嫂通奸，灭门惨祸等等。第一个故事是我姐姐亲口讲述的，如同目遇。是我在茫然而不知所措的情况下被动地接受的。也是，第一个为我所认同的。你来了，带来了第二个故事，模糊的，不清晰的，残缺的。因为你来得匆忙，没有充裕的时间准备，不可避免的迷乱了故事的情节。而听故事的我，此刻却是清醒的，冷静的，绝对主动的。所以，尽管故事纷杂，我还是从纷杂凌乱的线索里梳理出主题，并给它下了定义，两个字：背叛。
The first story was full of darkness: brother destroying brother, uncle and sister-in-law in adultery, the calamity of a whole house wiped out, and the rest. The first story my sister told me with her own mouth, as though I had seen it with my own eyes. I took it in passively, bewildered and at a loss. And it was the first that I accepted as my own. Then you came, and brought a second story — blurred, unclear, full of gaps. Because you came in haste, with no ample time to prepare, you could not but muddle the plot of it. But I, who now listened, was clear-headed, cool, wholly in command. And so, tangled as the story was, still I drew its theme out of the tangled and disordered threads and set a definition upon it, in two words: betrayal.

> 反过来说，第一个故事就相当于一个实验室，第二个故事中的所有细节都将在第一个故事的框架内，鉴别真伪。
To put it the other way about, the first story served as a kind of laboratory: within its frame every detail of the second story would be tested, true against false.

> 于是，障眼法无以蔽日遮天了。
And so the sleight of hand could no longer hide the sun or blot out the sky.

> 真正的“真相”渐渐浮出水面。
The real “truth” rose slowly to the surface.

> “第三个故事”呼之欲出了。
A “third story” was all but ready to step forth at a call.

> 到底是什么呢？
And what was it, after all?

> 四个字：色欲无边。
Four words: lust without bound.

> 第三个故事应该从我的生母谈起，也就是“徐玉真”，我父亲的小妾。
The third story ought to begin with my birth mother — that is, “Xu Yuzhen,” my father’s concubine.

> 我记得，姐姐是这样描绘我的生母的，她说：我们的父亲因为精通洋务，很受太后的赏识和宠爱，所以，我曾蒙恩诏在金门献舞，我十六岁那年，母亲生下了一对双胞胎，我亲爱的弟弟们，你和阿次……来到了这个充满光明、又暗藏黑暗的世界。
I remember how my sister painted my birth mother for me. She said: Because our father was deeply versed in foreign affairs, he was much esteemed and favored by the Empress Dowager, and so I was once granted by gracious decree to dance before her at the Golden Gate; and in the year I turned sixteen, mother gave birth to a pair of twins — my dear little brothers, you and A-Ci — who came into this world so full of light and yet with darkness hidden within it.

> 还是那一年，太后心血来潮，赐给父亲一个十八岁的宫女做小妾，父亲不能拒绝太后的美意盛情，就将这个宫女领回了杨家，给了她偏房的名分，使她成为了我们杨家的新姨娘。
In that same year, on a sudden whim, the Empress Dowager bestowed upon father an eighteen-year-old palace maid to be his concubine. Father could not refuse the Dowager’s kind and lavish favor, and so he led the maid home to the house of Yang, gave her the standing of a secondary wife, and made her the new concubine-aunt of our household.

> 第一个疑问来了。
Here came the first question.

> 太后为什么要心血来潮，赐给父亲一个十八岁的宫女做小妾？而且是在母亲刚刚生下一对孪生兄弟的时候？这是不大合乎人情的。
Why should the Empress Dowager, on a sudden whim, bestow on father an eighteen-year-old palace maid for a concubine — and just at the time when mother had newly borne a pair of twin brothers? It did not much accord with human feeling.

> 一个女人在经过痛苦的分娩过程后，她需要的是男人的亲切关怀和问候，而不是眼睁睁地看着属于自己的男人纳妾。看着他在月光下搂着另一个女人进入香甜的梦乡，而自己孤零零对着月色和嗷嗷待哺的孩子。
A woman, after the pain of childbirth, needs a man’s tender care and solicitude — not to watch with her own open eyes as the man who is hers takes a concubine; to watch him under the moonlight gather another woman in his arms and drift into sweet dreams, while she is left alone with the moonlight and her hungrily crying children.

> 太后是一个女人。
The Empress Dowager was a woman.

> 一个女人用这样一种近乎残忍的方式去虐待另一个女人的心灵，只有一种解释，她们之间有“深仇大恨”。
That one woman should torment the heart of another woman in a way so near to cruelty admits of only one explanation: that between them lay some “deep and bitter hatred.”

> 除此之外，还有一种解释。
And besides that, there is one other explanation.

> 我的姐姐撒谎了。
My sister lied.

> 太后之所以要赐父亲一个小妾，那是因为中国人的古训“不孝有三，无后为大。”太后赐的这个宫女，是去给杨家续香火的。
The reason the Empress Dowager bestowed a concubine on father was the old Chinese teaching: “There are three unfilial things, and to be without heirs is the greatest.” The palace maid the Dowager bestowed was to carry on the incense-fire of the house of Yang.

> 她做到了。
And this she did.

> 她跨进杨家大门的第二年的春天，我和我的弟弟，来到了这个美丽繁杂的世界。
In the spring of the second year after she crossed the threshold of the house of Yang, my brother and I came into this beautiful, tangled world.

> 我是徐玉真的儿子，一个庶出的孩子。但是，我是杨家的长子，杨家的合法继承人。
I am the son of Xu Yuzhen, a child of a concubine’s line. But I am the eldest son of the house of Yang, the lawful heir of the house of Yang.

> 第二个疑问来了。
Here came the second question.

> 我的姐姐为什么要撒谎？
Why did my sister lie?

> 目的是什么？
To what end?

> 她的目得很显然。
Her end was plain enough.

> 她曾经亲口对我说过：我为什么要处心积虑的这样做呢？因为，我要报复！我要你亲手杀死他们！亲手杀死他们！！我要和你，看着他们这对狗男女在眼前化为泡沫，挫成灰烬。
She once said to me with her own mouth: Why do I go to such scheming lengths to do this? Because I want revenge! I want you to kill them with your own hands! To kill them with your own hands! I want, together with you, to watch that shameless pair of dogs turn to froth and be ground to ashes before our very eyes.

> 你也曾经告诉我，你说：大小姐曾经亲口对我说过，杨家的事，一定要由杨家的人来完成。我知道，你们一定都活着，二十年来你们一定朝着预订的轨迹在行走，我没有权利去干预你们的复仇计划。
And you too once told me — you said: The eldest young mistress once said to me with her own mouth, the affairs of the house of Yang must be finished by the people of the house of Yang. I knew that you must all be alive, that for twenty years you must have been moving along a preordained track, and that I had no right to meddle in your plan of revenge.

> 到底是什么样的复仇计划？什么样的复仇计划要策划整整二十年？
And what kind of revenge was this, after all? What kind of revenge takes a full twenty years to plot?

> 让儿子亲手杀死自己的母亲！
To have a son kill his own mother with his own hands!

> 的的确确是一个非常解恨，但是必须耐心等待的复仇方法，带有明显的极端性和残忍性。
It was, most assuredly, a way of revenge that slaked the hatred utterly, yet had to be patiently waited out — a way plainly marked with the extreme and the cruel.

> 女性往往喜欢选择这种复仇的方式，为了这一分钟得到来，她们可以忍耐、等待，在漫长的岁月里期盼“黑暗”的瞬间，一旦，梦想成真，她们就会渴饮仇人的血，再告诉杀死仇人的人，这个人是你的亲人，她曾经给了你生命！你却凶狠地结束了她的生命！这就是命运！造化弄人。这种打击方式是毁灭性的！
Women are apt to choose this kind of revenge. For the sake of that one minute’s coming, they can endure and wait, longing through the long years for the instant of “darkness”; and once the dream comes true, they will drink down the blood of their enemy with a parched thirst, and then tell the one who has killed that enemy: this person was your own kin, she who once gave you life! And you have savagely ended her life! This is fate! Destiny makes sport of men. Such a way of striking is annihilating!

> 可是，计划没有变化快。
But the plan could not keep pace with the changes.

> 姐姐精心策划了二十年的阴谋，出错了。
The conspiracy my sister had so carefully plotted over twenty years went wrong.

> 首先是我不肯就范。因为我天性善良，受过良好的教育，当然，这也是拜我姐姐所赐。其次，我的姐姐太善良了。她违背了自己残酷的初衷，她对我这个仇人的“孽种”自始至终恨不起来，她内心的慈爱关怀是真诚的，绝非伪装。在最后关头，她选择了放弃。
First, I would not be brought to heel. For I am by nature kind, and was well brought up — which too, of course, I owe to my sister. Second, my sister was too kind. She went against her own cruel first purpose; from beginning to end she could not bring herself to hate me, the “spawn of sin” of her enemy; the loving care within her was sincere, in no way feigned. And at the last moment she chose to give it up.

> 正因为她选择了放弃，以往所有潜藏在她内心的狰狞、恐惧、毒焰都消失于无形，善恶两极，瞬间分离。
And because she chose to give it up, all the ferocity, the terror, the venomous flame that had lurked within her before vanished into nothing; the two poles of good and evil, in that instant, drew apart.

> 她要杀徐玉真，是因为，这个女人杀死了她的父亲，毁了她的一生。
She wanted to kill Xu Yuzhen because this woman had killed her father and ruined her whole life.

> 她放弃了酝酿二十年的复仇计划，是因为，她不舍得毁了我，她认我是她的亲人，她的亲兄弟，她爱我。
She gave up the revenge she had brewed for twenty years because she could not bear to destroy me; she owned me as her own kin, her own brother; she loved me.

> 正因为她选择了放弃，所以，我将终生怀念她、尊敬她、爱她。
And just because she chose to give it up, I shall cherish her, honor her, and love her, all my life long.

> 这样一个善良的女人，却死得异常惨烈。
And yet a woman so kind died a death of rare and dreadful violence.

> 徐玉真杀了她。
Xu Yuzhen killed her.

> 她杀了一个不再威胁她的人，一个手无寸铁的人，一个曾经与她亲如姐妹的人。
She killed one who no longer threatened her, one who bore not a weapon in her hand, one who had once been as dear to her as a sister.

> 可是，杀人的过程中，又出错了。徐玉真派人炸的是我的诊室，也就是说，她想杀的人是我，而不是其他什么人。
But in the course of the killing something again went wrong. What Xu Yuzhen sent men to blow up was my consulting room — which is to say, the one she meant to kill was me, and no one else.

> 第三个疑问如期而至了。
The third question arrived on schedule.

> 一个母亲会杀死自己亲生的骨肉吗？
Would a mother kill the flesh and blood of her own body?

> 我开始费解了。
I began to be baffled.

> 对于一个做母亲的人来说，二十年，二十年失子之痛，痛不欲生；二十年，二十年忆子之苦，苦不堪言。可是，她却要执意杀死一个离开自己整整二十年，失而复得的亲生儿子，为什么？
For a woman who is a mother, twenty years — twenty years of the pain of a lost son, a pain that makes life not worth the living; twenty years — twenty years of the bitterness of remembering a son, a bitterness beyond all telling. And yet she was set on killing an own son parted from her a full twenty years and only now regained. Why?

> 上不合天理，下不符人情。
It accorded neither with the way of Heaven above nor with the feeling of men below.

> 这个故事讲到此处，无论如何，都走不通了。
Told to this point, the story, do what one would, could go no further.

> 好比一篇内容精彩、题名隐讳的纪实文章，刚一露面，就获得读者的关注，一时间注家蜂起，众说纷纭，越说越玄，最后纪实文学演变成了科幻小说。
It was like some piece of reportage, splendid in its matter and veiled in its title, that no sooner shows its face than it wins the readers’ notice; commentators swarm up all at once, opinions buzz and clash, the telling grows more and more fantastical, until in the end the documentary tale has turned into a work of science fiction.

> 到底是哪里出了问题呢？
Where, in the end, had it gone wrong?

> 我在姐姐的遗物里找到了答案。
I found the answer among my sister’s effects.

> 姐姐有一个习惯，她把自己认为是最珍贵的东西都一一保存起来，她的遗物里有一本小册子，是父亲闲暇时作的诗。有些是父亲的笔迹，有些是徐玉真用小楷誊写的。字迹清秀，字如其人。
My sister had a habit of keeping, one by one, all the things she held most precious; and among her effects was a little booklet of poems father had composed in his hours of leisure. Some were in father’s own hand, and some were copied out by Xu Yuzhen in a fine small regular script. The hand was clear and graceful — the writing was like the woman herself.

> 这些誊写的诗歌，直到出事前的半年里，突然停止了。她没有再为父亲誊写了。是习惯改变了？还是字迹变了？
These copied-out poems stopped abruptly in the last half-year before the thing happened. She copied for father no more. Had the habit changed? Or had the hand changed?

> 如果是因为字迹变了，不敢写，那么，人也就变了。
If it was that the hand had changed, so that she dared not write, then the person too had changed.

> 弃之不写，也就无迹可循了。
To leave off writing was to leave no trace to be followed.

> 我又回忆起了姐姐的话，她说：她初来时，非常本分，也很活泼开朗，我和她因为年龄相近，很快成了最要好的朋友。我们彼此分享父亲不同的宠爱，同时也承担起照顾多病母亲的责任。
Again I called to mind my sister’s words. She said: When she first came she was very proper, and lively and bright as well; and she and I, being near in age, soon became the closest of friends. We shared between us father’s differing favors, and took upon ourselves together the care of our often-ailing mother.

> 直到有一次，父亲从德国经商回来，他送给姨娘一双美丽的水晶鞋。我很嫉妒，我几次向她讨要，她却不肯给我，她说这是“爱”的礼物，她说她要永远珍藏。
Until once, when father came back from trading in Germany, he gave the concubine-aunt a beautiful pair of crystal shoes. I was very jealous, and asked her for them several times, but she would not give them to me; she said they were a gift of “love,” and said she would treasure them forever.

> 我们的感情和友谊有了嫌隙。
A rift came into our affection and our friendship.

> 此时的姨娘宁肯得罪姐姐，也不肯将父亲的爱分流。从这个角度去看，她的内心深处对父亲充满了爱。
The concubine-aunt of that time would sooner give offense to my sister than let father’s love be drawn off elsewhere. Seen from this angle, in the very depths of her heart she was full of love for father.

> 可是，好景不长，姐姐说：姨娘为了讨好我，居然把那一双水晶鞋送给了我。她忘了自己曾经爱过的一切。她丢弃这双鞋子的同时，也丢掉了父亲的给予她的“爱”。
But the fair days did not last. My sister said: To curry favor with me, the concubine-aunt actually gave me that very pair of crystal shoes. She had forgotten all that she had once loved. In casting away these shoes she cast away as well the “love” that father had given her.

> 是不是仿佛一夜之间有了一百八十度的大转变？事实不然。
Was it not as though, overnight, she had turned clean about, a full hundred and eighty degrees? The fact was otherwise.

> 事实告诉我，我的生母已经遇害了。现在这个徐玉真，是一个彻头彻尾的赝品。
The fact told me this: my birth mother had already been murdered. This present Xu Yuzhen was a counterfeit through and through.

> 第四个疑问需要你来解答了。
The fourth question is one that you must answer.

> 这个假的徐玉真是从哪里冒出来得？
Where did this false Xu Yuzhen spring up from?

> 我的生母是何时遇害的？
And when was my birth mother murdered?

> 在阿初咄咄逼人的目光下，韩正齐快要崩溃了。“先生，我可以理解您的心情，可是，所有的这一切，都是您的推断。我发誓，我没有参与罪恶。我发誓，我从来就不认为夫人是假的。直至今日。”
Under A-Chu’s overbearing gaze, Han Zhengqi was near collapse. “Sir, I can understand how you feel; but all of this is your own inference. I swear I had no part in the crime. I swear I never once thought the mistress was false — not until this very day.”

> “不，你知道，你知道所有的一切。”
“No — you knew. You knew all of it.”

> “我不知道，我不可能知道。”
“I did not know; I could not possibly have known.”

> “你不可能不知道！一夜之间，一夜之间真夫人变成了假夫人，仿佛真古董换成了舶来品，天长日久，能骗过我的父亲吗？不可能。最亲密不过夫妻，容貌可以通过医学手段来完成，她可以整容，整到以假乱真。可是，只要父亲一碰她，真假立辨。怎么办呢？他们来得时候早就酝酿好了整个犯罪过程。他们需要一个帮手，不，应该是需要一个帮凶。他们选中了你。这个假'徐玉真'的确给你下了药。她要跟你做交易。用她肮脏的肉体来交换你对杨家的忠诚。她成功了。”
“It is impossible that you did not know! Overnight — overnight the true mistress became a false mistress, as though a genuine antique had been swapped for some imported thing; and over long days and years, could that deceive my father? Impossible. There is no intimacy closer than that of husband and wife. Her looks could be managed by medical means — she could have her face remade, remade until the false passed for the true. But father had only to touch her once, and true and false were told apart at once. What was to be done? When they came they had long since brewed the whole course of the crime. They needed a helper — no, they needed an accomplice in the killing. They fixed upon you. This false ‘Xu Yuzhen’ did indeed drug you. She meant to strike a bargain with you: to trade her filthy body for your loyalty to the house of Yang. And she succeeded.”

> “先生。”韩正齐想分辩。不过，阿初没有给他讲话的机会。“毋庸置疑的是，你上了她的床！家养的猎犬，从此迷失了方向。白布落染缸，面目全非了。你不知所措了，以沉默换取生存，忘了恩情，忘了自己的责任。于是，更大的悲剧无可避免的发生了……”
“Sir.” Han Zhengqi tried to argue his case, but A-Chu gave him no chance to speak. “What is beyond doubt is that you got into her bed! The house-reared hound lost its bearings from that day on. A white cloth dropped into the dye-vat comes out changed past all recognition. You were at a loss; you traded silence for survival, forgot the kindness done you, forgot your own duty. And so a still greater tragedy could not but come to pass…”

> “先生。我真的不知道夫人……可能是假的，如果我知道夫人被他们害了。我不会沉默到现在。我真的以为是……夫人一时失志，夫人曾经救过我的命，就因为这个原因，我放弃了自己做人的原则。”
“Sir. I truly did not know the mistress… might be false. Had I known the mistress had been done to death by them, I would not have kept silent all this while. I truly believed it was… that the mistress had lost her way for a moment. The mistress had once saved my life, and for that reason alone I gave up my principles as a man.”

> “所以你真的该死！”阿初怨毒地说。
“And so you truly deserve to die!” A-Chu said, venomous with rancor.

> “你在穷途末路之际，我的母亲救了你。我不去研究任何有关她救你脱难的细节，但是，我相信，是我的生母，她给了你机遇，给了你重生。而你对她也不仅仅是单纯的心怀感恩，你一定暗恋过她，你不用否认！
When you were at the very end of your road, my mother saved you. I will not go inquiring into any of the particulars of how she saved you from your peril; but this I believe — that it was my birth mother who gave you your chance, who gave you a second life. And what you felt for her was not simple gratitude alone; you must have loved her in secret. You need not deny it!

> 你的确是一个十分优秀的军人，健壮的体魄，服从的天性，坚韧的意志，让你成为了我家庭里必不可少的成员。
You were indeed a most excellent soldier: a strong and healthy frame, an obedient nature, a tough and tenacious will — all of it made you an indispensable member of my household.

> 我那心地纯良、涉世不深的姐姐爱上了一个英俊体贴的军人，一点也不奇怪。因为，你给了她安全感，还有，一朵温室里的娇花所需要的恒温花棚施与她的温暖。你做到了。
That my pure-hearted sister, so little tried by the world, should fall in love with a handsome and attentive soldier was no wonder at all. For you gave her a sense of safety, and the warmth that a delicate hothouse flower needs of its even-heated glass shelter. This you did.

> 我不知道你有多爱我姐姐？但是，我知道，你一定很爱我的母亲！你爱她，却无法得到她，你接近她，却不能亲近她。你的内心一定很痛苦，很受煎熬。
How much you loved my sister I do not know. But this I know — that you must have loved my mother very much! You loved her, yet could not have her; you drew near her, yet could not be intimate with her. Your heart must have been in great pain, sorely racked.

> 我的姐姐反而在不知不觉中成为了你心目中”最爱“的代替品，做了你的”次爱“。
My sister, without your ever knowing it, became instead the substitute in your heart for your “best-loved,” and served as your “second-best love.”

> 终于，有一天，有一夜，你突然得到了你想拥有的一切。当你睁开睡意惺忪的双眼，看见温香软玉抱满怀的瞬间，你是怎样的惊诧？怎样的惶恐？怎样的紧张？
At last, one day, one night, you suddenly gained all that you had wished to possess. When you opened your drowsy, sleep-blurred eyes and saw, in that instant, a warm fragrance and soft jade filling your arms, what astonishment was yours? What dread? What agitation?

> 她一定是预先准备好了一套动情的动作，哀怨缠绵的情话，她渴求你的爱，你的温暖，她一切一切的表演正好满足了你的虚荣心和潜藏在体内的欲火。
She must have prepared beforehand a whole set of moving gestures, of plaintive and clinging words of love; she craved your love, your warmth, and every last part of her performance answered exactly to your vanity and to the fire of desire hidden within your body.

> 你告诉自己，是她可怜，是她一时失志，是她行为不轨，是她”逼迫“自己上了她的床。
You told yourself: it was that she was pitiable, that she had lost her way for a moment, that hers was the improper conduct, that it was she who “forced” you into her bed.

> 道德和良心在谴责你，可是，同情心和爱情在帮助你，强硬的道德与脆弱的同情心之间的张力发生了微妙的变化，所谓：柔能克刚，水能灭火。于是，你认定了你”背叛“有情，行为合理。你心安理得地原谅了自己。
Morality and conscience reproached you; but pity and love came to your aid, and between rigid morality and fragile pity the tension shifted in some subtle way — what they call: the soft can conquer the hard, and water can quench fire. And so you settled it that your “betrayal” was a thing of feeling, your conduct reasonable. With an easy heart you forgave yourself.

> 你再度沉溺。这一回，就不再是无意识的世界了，而是有高度刺激的狂欢，无度的狂欢，你为此而沉醉。
You sank into it again. This time it was no longer a world of the unconscious, but a highly charged revel, a revel without measure, and by it you were drunk.

> 我姐姐的乳娘，岳嬷嬷告诉我，姨奶奶在杨家'出事'的前几个月，曾经在慈云寺住过一段日子，据说是去吃斋还愿，回来之后，性情大变。
My sister’s wet-nurse, the amah A-Yue, told me that in the months before the “disaster” befell the house of Yang, the aunt-mistress had stayed a while at the Ciyun Temple — to keep a fast and fulfill a vow, it was said — and that after she came back her temper was wholly changed.

> 我并不知道，这个假徐玉真来自何方？但是，我能想到，她极有可能来自东瀛。杨羽桦曾经留学日本，他们之间一定发生过什么故事，他们达成了某种默契，他们的阴谋也许并非只是图谋家产这么简单。
Whence this false Xu Yuzhen came I do not know. But I can well imagine that in all likelihood she came from Japan. Yang Yuhua once studied in Japan; between the two of them some story must have passed, they reached some tacit understanding, and their conspiracy was perhaps not so simple a thing as a mere scheme after the family fortune.

> 他们利用了我生母信仰佛教的善念，在我生母虔心敬佛的时候，他们动了杀机！
They made use of my birth mother’s pious devotion to the Buddhist faith, and in the very hour when she worshipped the Buddha with a devout heart, they set their murderous design in motion!

> 就是这段在慈云寺的日子里，我的生母遇害，假冒的徐玉真正式登场了，你和杨家的姨太太有了奸情，我母亲的声名从此被践踏、被凌辱，她的灵魂至今忍受着痛苦和煎熬。
It was during this spell of days at the Ciyun Temple that my birth mother was murdered, and the counterfeit Xu Yuzhen made her formal entrance; you fell into an adulterous affair with the concubine of the house of Yang, and from then on my mother’s good name was trampled and defiled, and her soul suffers pain and torment to this very day.

> 而我的姐姐依然被蒙在鼓里，幻想着她纯真的爱情。
And my sister was still kept in the dark, dreaming her dream of pure and innocent love.

> 你很乐意和这个假的徐玉真保持这种主仆之间的浪漫爱情、危险游戏，这种浪漫让你有了征服的快感，当然，我不否认你曾有过负罪感，也许曾经深深忏悔过。不然，你怎么会同意和我姐姐双双私奔呢？
You were very glad to keep up, with this false Xu Yuzhen, this romance between master and servant, this dangerous game; the romance of it gave you the thrill of conquest. To be sure, I do not deny that you once felt the weight of guilt, that you perhaps once repented deeply. Else how should you have agreed to run away together with my sister, the two of you?

> 但是，负罪和忏悔在整个阴谋形成的过程中已经显得微不足道了。
But guilt and repentance had already come to count for next to nothing in the whole shaping of the conspiracy.

> 因为浪漫，你已经把自己的身份和杨家的关系彻底搅乱了，当你突然发现徐玉真与杨羽桦之间隐藏着暧昧的'情人'关系后，你迷茫了。
Because of the romance you had thrown your own standing and your ties to the house of Yang into utter confusion; and when you suddenly discovered the shadowy “lovers’” bond hidden between Xu Yuzhen and Yang Yuhua, you were lost.

> 徐玉真在床上、月下，对你说过的所有情话，全部在瞬间颠覆了。就像是临时搭建起来得简陋工棚，经不得推敲。
All the words of love Xu Yuzhen had spoken to you in bed, under the moon, were in an instant overturned — like some crude shed thrown up on the spur of the moment, that will not bear a moment’s scrutiny.

> 你从来没有想到我父亲的安危，这个时候的你，极力想逃，跟我的姐姐一起逃。
You never once thought of my father’s safety; you at this time wanted with all your might to flee — to flee together with my sister.

> 结果呢？我父亲死了，死于谋杀！我的姐姐嫁人了，嫁做妾妇，犹如服鸩自毒！我呢，寄人篱下，为人奴婢！我弟弟，认贼作父！你逃了，全身而退！
And the upshot? My father died — died by murder! My sister married — married as a concubine, as though she had drunk poisoned wine to poison herself! And I? I lived under another man’s roof, a bond-slave! My brother — took a thief for his father! And you fled, and drew off whole and unharmed!

> 不过，今天，我还是要谢谢你，谢谢你亲口承认我姐姐是你最心爱的女人，尽管我不能判断你话里的真伪。我仍然感谢你，谢谢你今天的选择。
And yet today I must still thank you — thank you for owning with your own mouth that my sister was the woman you loved best, though I cannot judge the truth or falsehood of your words. Still I thank you; thank you for the choice you made today.

> 你说，我不能选，二十年前你让我选，你用我最心爱的女人的命胁迫我；你用你的身体、你的美色勾引我，你逼我选择；你制造杀人现场，陷害我，你强迫我选；现在，二十年都过去了，你依然要我选。不，我不会选，不要说我现在手上还有权利，就算我如今是一个凡夫走卒，我也绝不再选。大不了，鱼死网破，你死我活！
You said: I cannot choose. Twenty years ago you made me choose; you held the life of the woman I loved best over my head; you seduced me with your body, with your beauty; you forced me to choose. You staged a murder scene and framed me, you compelled me to choose. Now, twenty whole years gone by, and still you would have me choose. No. I will not. Never mind that I still hold some power in my hands — even were I now the meanest of common wretches, I would never choose again. At the worst, let the fish die and the net be torn; it is you or me!

> 看起来，二十年前，他们曾经制造了杀人现场，陷你入局。杀人重罪，你是背不起的。不用说给你高官厚禄，光是替你移灾解罪，转祸为福，你就感激涕零了。
It appears that twenty years ago they staged a murder scene and drew you into the trap. A capital charge of murder was a thing you could never shoulder. Never mind their giving you high office and a fat salary — merely to have your calamity shifted and your guilt dissolved, your disaster turned to fortune, was enough to move you to tears of gratitude.

> 故事讲完了，有什么出入，您可以补充。如果，没有什么大的出入，你知道该怎么做！”
“The story is told. If there is anything amiss in it, you may add what is wanting. And if there is nothing greatly amiss — you know what you must do!”

> 韩正齐已经完全丧失了抵御的能力，当他听到徐玉真是一个冒牌货的时候，在精神上，他完全缴械投降了。他并不是屈服于阿初的黑势力，而是诚心服法。
Han Zhengqi had wholly lost the power to resist. When he heard that Xu Yuzhen was a counterfeit, in spirit he laid down his arms and surrendered outright. It was not that he yielded to A-Chu’s dark power, but that he submitted, in true sincerity, to justice.

> 他劣迹斑斑，严重地乖背了江湖道义。他已经跨越了江湖规矩的最底线。阿初的身影在他眼前无疑是一座难以飞跃的山峰，整个茶室安静极了，只剩下一个声音，那就是死亡的脚步。
His record was stained through and through; he had gravely broken the code of honor of the gangs. He had crossed the lowest line the rules of that world allowed. A-Chu’s figure before his eyes was, beyond doubt, a mountain peak past leaping over. The whole tearoom was utterly still, and only one sound remained: the footfall of death.

> “怎么办呢？”韩正齐喃喃自语。
“What am I to do?” Han Zhengqi murmured to himself.

> “你说呢？”阿初走到窗前，看着天上的云彩。
“What do you say?” A-Chu walked to the window and looked at the clouds in the sky.

> “二十年前，如果我向您的父亲坦诚一切，他会原谅我吗？”
“Twenty years ago, if I had confessed everything honestly to your father, would he have forgiven me?”

> “我不知道。”
“I do not know.”

> “他会杀了我。因为，他把家族荣誉看得比命还重要。”
“He would have killed me. For he held the family’s honor dearer than life itself.”

> “也许，他会这样想。”
“Perhaps he would have thought so.”

> “他会这样做。而且，他曾经做过，我亲眼目睹。”
“He would have done so. And he had done so before — I saw it with my own eyes.”

> “于是，我父亲死总好过你死，是吗？”
“And so, better my father should die than you — is that it?”

> “我从来没有想让谁去死。我想让这件丑闻死掉，永远，永远死掉。我是应该受到您的谴责，并得到应有的惩罚。”
“I never wanted anyone to die. I wanted this scandal to die — forever, forever to die. I do deserve your reproach, and the punishment that is my due.”

> “我没有谴责你，也无力纠错。我只想告诉你，一个人应该为他的行为负责任。”
“I do not reproach you, nor have I the power to set the wrong right. I only wish to tell you this: that a man ought to answer for his own deeds.”

> “您能指一条生路给我吗？”
“Can you point me to a way to live?”

> “如果没有生路呢？”阿初发出的死亡的讯号。
“And if there is no way to live?” It was the signal of death that A-Chu gave.

> “您肯原谅我吗？”
“Will you forgive me?”

> “求人原谅，原是求己心安。”阿初淡淡一笑。“一个背信弃义的人，永远不会重获新生。除非他死一次，死一次，我既往不咎！这是你毁约背誓的代价，你理该偿还。你除了以死谢罪外，别无救赎之道。”阿初的话讲得和风细雨，但是眼目嚣张。
“To beg another’s forgiveness is really to beg one’s own heart’s peace.” A-Chu gave a faint smile. “One who betrays faith and honor will never be born anew — unless he die once. Die once, and I hold nothing of the past against you! This is the price of your broken pact and broken oath, and it is only right that you repay it. Save by atoning with your death, there is no road of redemption for you.” A-Chu’s words came soft as gentle wind and fine rain, but there was arrogance in his eyes.

> 怎一个“悔”字了得。
How could the one word “regret” ever suffice?

> 杨慕莲优雅与婀娜的影像，隐约又飘浮在眼前，既疏远，又亲近。二十年了，她并没有色谢容衰，她很年轻。她用期待的眼神看自己，没有忧伤，仿佛只有宁静的等待。韩正齐知道，这是死亡的感觉。
The image of Yang Mulian, graceful and willowy, floated dimly again before his eyes, at once far off and near. Twenty years, and her beauty had not faded, her looks had not withered; she was very young. She looked at him with expectant eyes, without sorrow, as though there were only a tranquil waiting. Han Zhengqi knew that this was the feeling of death.

> “你自裁谢罪，我就当你二十年前，忠于旧爱，蹈难赴死。怎么样？”阿初竟有些残忍地愿他继续保持这种即将丧失生命的感觉，并从中调剂出过世的姐姐对他寡情负心的恨意。
“Take your own life in atonement, and I will reckon it as though, twenty years ago, you had stayed true to your old love and gone to your death through peril. Well?” A-Chu, with something cruel in it, wished the man to keep this feeling of a life about to be lost, and to draw out of it, and temper away, his dead sister’s hatred for the man’s coldness and faithlessness.

> 韩正齐掏出了枪，对准自己的太阳穴，面对阿初双膝跪下。“请先生照顾韩禹。”
Han Zhengqi drew out his gun, held it to his own temple, and knelt down on both knees facing A-Chu. “I beg the gentleman to look after Han Yu.”

> “您跟我讲条件？”
“You would strike a bargain with me?”

> “不是。”
“No.”

> “那就行。我不搞株连。”
“Then it is well. I do not visit the guilt of one upon his kin.”

> “我愿意用我的血来洗清罪孽。”话音未落，扳机扣响。
“I am willing to wash my sin clean with my own blood.” The words had not yet died away when the trigger fell.
