### Two Characters for Sixty Cash

"Two characters, sixty cash. A knotty trouble — try it and see." This refrain is the cry raised by the fortune-telling friends who set up their character-splitting stalls against the wall; for the sacrifice of a mere six coppers, a man's knottiest troubles can be resolved. Such a bargain is a rare thing indeed — though whether, once split, the character proves true or false, the character-splitting gentleman is of course not to be held answerable, a point I will make plain on their behalf.

When it comes to character-splitting, there are wide distinctions within the trade. The man who works the teahouses and sets up a stall is of the humblest sort, and so his two characters go at the great-cheap-and-extra-cheap rate of a bare sixty cash. Then there is a higher class of friend, who rents a shopfront, or takes a room in some grand hotel, hangs out his character-splitting sign, and to split two characters wants four mao in small silver at the least. And there is besides a certain worthy Yan Fusun, whose line goes by the name of "gourd character-divining," and who charges a fee of two dollars and two jiao for two characters — dearer, set against "sixty cash, try it and see," by some tens of times over!
