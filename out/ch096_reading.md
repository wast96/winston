### The Wonton Carrying-Poles

There are two kinds of carrying-pole for selling wonton. One is the tall-legged sort, knocked and struck as the man goes, with a *bo-bo* sound; the other is the low, squat sort, which strikes not a bamboo tube but a bamboo clapper, knocking with one hand and crying out with the other, "Shrimp-and-pork wonton and noodles!" Since these poles all sell noodles too, the wonton filling is minced shrimp and pork worked together, and the wontons are very large, so they are called "big wonton"; a bowl costs at least a *mao* in small silver, and the noodles the same. The tall-legged pole has the longest history; it only knocks its bamboo tube and does not cry out, and its wontons are all of the small kind, a bowl costing at least a hundred cash (that is, ten coppers), though nowadays a few of the poles sell noodles as well. The squat wonton pole was the invention of Cantonese men, whose cry is "Shrimp wonton for sale!"; of late, besides the Cantonese, there are no small number of Zhenjiang men and Yangzhou men in the trade too.
