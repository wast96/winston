### Superior Chinese

As analyzed through foreign eyes, a distinction is drawn between what are called "superior Chinese" and "common Chinese"; but which sort is the superior Chinese and which the common—that is worth looking into a little.

It is said to be the comprador class and a portion of the well-connected idle rich sojourners—men who can speak a few words of pidgin, who can wear a Western suit and leather shoes, who can play the fox borrowing the tiger's dread, who can borrow foreign power to bully their own countrymen, who can lick the boil and suck the piles: those who have these several qualifications may deserve to be called "superior Chinese." The rest—the honest common folk who labor with mind and body, keep to their place, do not fawn on foreigners, and so pass their bitter lives—must, one supposes, be the "common Chinese."

How glorious, the "superior Chinese"! How splendid, the "superior Chinese"! But should the nation one day have the ill luck to fall, then all these superior Chinese, so high of nose and so full of themselves—can they be sure of not becoming the slaves of a conquered land?
