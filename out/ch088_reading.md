### The Grand Funerals

When some great and famous personage of society stretches out his legs, gives up the ghost, and lets go his hold on the world, there must be a grand funeral held for him ("grand funeral" meaning simply the funeral procession); and the more lavish the display, the greater the stir it makes for a season, until word of it carries several hundred li away and the townsfolk of distant places come from afar, all with one voice crying, "Come see the grand funeral, come see the grand funeral!" On the day of the procession the crowds throw over their proper business like things possessed and come on purpose to watch the grand funeral. Along the several roads it passes there is a very mountain and sea of people, shoving before and crushing behind, packed so tight that not a drop could trickle through; and the hotels and eating-houses and teahouses along the way have all set out premium seats on their balconies, doing a brisk one-time trade.

In former days both Sheng Xingsun and Zhu Baosan had grand funerals, which people to this day recall with clicks of the tongue and envy. When Huang Chujiu died the year before last, the crowd that loves a spectacle said gaily once more, "We shall have a grand funeral to watch again!"—but on account of his debts Huang Chujiu's grand funeral quietly came to nothing, to the great disappointment of the multitude.

When a great house has money and has a name, and one day loses one of its own, it becomes a matter of face: nothing but a grand funeral will serve to show off its wealth and parade its consequence, and though the waste be vast it minds not at all. Money they have in plenty, and squander it as they will it costs them not a hair, while they win besides the loud admiration of the common herd—and so they are grandly and singularly turned out.

To swarm like bees after a spectacle is, at bottom, an inborn bad streak in the Chinese character, and gawking at excitement will often enough gawk up a calamity (as when a bridge collapses at a temple festival and lives are lost, and suchlike horrors)—yet they will never wake to it, I daresay!
