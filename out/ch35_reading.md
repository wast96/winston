## 5

Day was just breaking when Yang Qun got back to the security bureau, and the secretary to the director of the Police Administration Department was already waiting outside his office door. But when he was shown into the director's small private receiving room, the man he found was a young officer.

"This is Adjutant Yan of the Zhongtong." With that brief introduction the secretary hurried out, carefully drawing the door shut behind him.

"And who is Adjutant Yan's superior?" Yang Qun stood a moment, then asked it straight out.

"You'll know when you see him." With that Adjutant Yan walked straight over, pulled the door open, and made a respectful gesture of invitation.

On the way to the Zhongtong the smoke over Chongqing had not yet cleared, and everywhere there were military police and hired hands cleaning the streets. Uneasy in the car, Yang Qun made conversation for its own sake and asked a great many questions. Adjutant Yan answered each of them politely, but not one was the answer Yang Qun wanted. As the car passed the East Sichuan Normal School on Zhongshan Second Road, Yang Qun could not help saying, "Isn't this your headquarters? Where exactly are you taking me?"

"The place everyone knows is only a signboard." Adjutant Yan turned round from the front passenger seat with a smile. "Chief Yang, please don't trouble yourself."

After they got out and wound through several long lanes, Yang Qun was led into a courtyard with no house number, and up the stairs; at a glance he saw the Chaotianmen Wharf beyond the window.

"Chief Yang is an Anxi man, I believe?" Guo Bingyan did not introduce himself, but with a genial laugh ushered him to the seat of honor and poured his tea with his own hands. "Spring water and autumn fragrance—this is the season's Tieguanyin from your own home country."

Just then Yang Qun was somewhat dazed, and not only from the scent of home in his nose. He had overseen public order in Chongqing for three years, and yet he had never known there was such a nameless mansion at the Chaotianmen Wharf, nor had he ever seen it on any edition of the city map.

Guo Bingyan, all ease, as though sipping tea and reminiscing with an old friend, cup in hand let fall the mention of a shop in Shapingba called Longsheng, dealing in ginseng and deer-antler: before the war it had been a secret liaison post of the Japanese Foreign Ministry, and was now assigned to the Army Ministry, but it still handled the receipt and forwarding of intelligence. They also had a high-power wireless set, installed in an earth-god shrine at Sanshuiwan outside the city. "Chief Yang may send men at any time to pull out this nail," Guo Bingyan said, "but take care—these are professional agents, they are armed, and they will very likely fight to the last."

Yang Qun made himself smile as easily as he could. "I am only a policeman. Killing spies and rooting out traitors both fall outside the authority of the Police Administration Department."

"Every countryman bears the duty to defend the land and resist the enemy." Guo Bingyan was still all smiles. "Not far from the Longsheng ginseng shop is Lotus Lake, and there too you will fish up a fish that slipped the net—with a surgical scalpel in the breast pocket of his coat . . . Chief Yang may take this as a small personal token of my regard."

When Yang Qun had arrested Jiang Yongnan, he had found on him not only a pistol but credentials of the Central Party Headquarters. He picked up the teacup from the table, took a sip, and said, "If the Zhongtong wishes to hand a man over to the Police Administration Department, a single official dispatch would do."

"If a dispatch could solve the problem, what would the Party-State want with those secret departments?" Guo Bingyan drew the smile from his face. "An American diplomat assassinated by a Japanese spy—that too is the result the American side hopes to have from you."

At this Yang Qun grew calm instead. He drank off the last of the tea in his cup in one go. "But why is it that you strike me more as the Japanese spies?"

Guo Bingyan smiled again, took out a pen and dashed off a line on a slip of paper, then pressed his seal lightly to it and handed it to Yang Qun. "The answer Chief Yang wants is all in the file. You may go at any time to the Zhongtong's confidential archive at the East Sichuan Normal School and consult it."

When Yang Qun made out the seal at the foot of the note, his face turned grave on the instant. He had seen the name years ago in the textbooks of the police officers' special-training class, and had heard it in many an astonishing rumor. Yang Qun rose respectfully, laid the note before Guo Bingyan with both hands, bowed his head, and said, "I would not presume. I obey Director Guo's command."

Guo Bingyan waved a modest hand. "Sit, please sit."

That night, when Jiang Yongnan was brought to a ferry moored at the edge of the Jialing River, Guo Bingyan, who never smoked, held a cigarette between his fingers. Not until it had nearly burned down to them did he throw it away with a hard flick. "All right. We'll turn this page."

Jiang Yongnan could not believe his ears. He lifted his head at once. "Sir . . ."

Guo Bingyan said, "Forget Chongqing. You leave tomorrow."

Jiang Yongnan lowered his head. "Yes."

"If you can't bear to part from her, you may take her with you."

Jiang Yongnan raised his head again, and looked at his superior in astonishment.

"We lick blood from the knife's edge. If a man can't so much as have a woman of his own, then what are we defending this country for?" A smile came to Guo Bingyan's face at last. He rose, clapped Jiang Yongnan on the shoulder, and the two walked together to the boat's rail, gazing at the scattered lights on the far shore. After a long while Guo Bingyan said, with deep feeling, "But a woman's heart? Sometimes it's a needle at the bottom of the sea."

Yang Qun drove Tang Ya to the apartment where the two of them had once lived together. Opening the door, he said, "All your things are here. Just as they were when you left, so they still are."

In the light that came on, the furnishings of the room were as before, their photographs still hanging on the wall, without a speck of dust.

A year earlier, when Tang Ya had decided to leave this place, Yang Qun had not been in the least surprised. He had only said, with a touch of pain, "You don't need to degrade yourself just to spite me."

"And why would I be degrading myself? This is simply who I am." What Tang Ya could least bear was that fatherly look in an old man's eyes. To get away from this man she had insisted on a transfer to the bailiffs' corps and had volunteered to become an executioner of the condemned. Sometimes she would even bring strange men home. She wanted only to see this bowl of lukewarm water flush with rage and shame, to have it out with him in a screaming, hysterical row, and then to sweep out in a flood of tears.

But Yang Qun seemed to have seen through her heart long before. He sat up out of the rocking chair. "Here's another way, then: I'll first arrange to send her back to her old home, and then we'll marry." As he spoke he walked slowly to the dressing mirror, picked out a white hair on his head and plucked it, and went on, "What else do you want? Anything within my power, you have only to say."

Tang Ya was a long time stunned. "How is it you make everything into a transaction?"

"Without transactions, would there have been those two years of ours?" Yang Qun turned to look at her. "When you've lived to my age you'll understand—life is nothing but one transaction after another."

Tang Ya remembered clearly that the sun over Chongqing that day had blazed like fire. Afterward she had shut herself in her mother's bedroom and stood the whole afternoon before the portrait of the dead, not once coming out.

Now Yang Qun turned on the lights in the several rooms one by one, then came and took the satchel from her hand and hung it on the coat rack; and, exactly as with a husband and wife home late, he said, "It's late. Wash up and get some sleep."

Only then did Tang Ya come to herself. She looked at him hard. "How did you know it was him?"

Yang Qun thought a moment. "Who else in this world knows you better than I do?" And seeing Tang Ya still staring blankly at him, he came around behind her, steadied her by the shoulders with both hands, and added, "It's all over now. Just take it for a dream."

Tang Ya was all but pushed to the door of the washroom. She spun around suddenly. "Doesn't it disgust you?"

"It doesn't." Yang Qun shook his head lightly, let his hands fall, thought again, and said, "People are strange things sometimes. There are places you've already been, and still you want to go back to them."

Early the next morning, coming out the front gate of the apartment, Tang Ya saw Jiang Yongnan standing across the road. In his gray cloth robe he looked so downcast and worn.

As he pulled the car door open Yang Qun said, "Why not go and say goodbye to your doctor?"

Tang Ya said nothing and ducked into the car, her eyes on the rearview mirror, until Jiang Yongnan's figure vanished swiftly in the roar of the engine. Then all at once Tang Ya turned her head. "Goodbye? Why do you say goodbye?"

"Not goodbye, then—or would you rather sit and talk over old times?"

"How did you know he was a doctor?"

This time Yang Qun did not answer. He drove Tang Ya to the front of the courthouse, hesitated, and said, "If you truly want to take it back, I won't hold it against you."

Tang Ya kept her lips pressed shut, sat a while in the passenger seat, then without a word pushed the door open, got out, and walked briskly up the steps.

Near noon the gatekeeper brought her a neatly folded note, saying a young man had just now begged him to be sure to pass it on. Tang Ya's heart leapt into her throat, and a long time passed before it would settle.

But when she came as arranged to the teahouse and walked into the private room, the person she found was a solemn-faced middle-aged man.

Guo Bingyan put the melon seeds in his hand back into the dried-fruit dish and looked at her coldly. "You took your time getting here."

"Who are you?" Tang Ya had meant to turn and leave, but she held back, and meeting that icy gaze she asked it as if in challenge.

Guo Bingyan sat up straight in the bamboo chair. "I am the one who gave the order to have you silenced."
