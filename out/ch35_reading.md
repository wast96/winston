## The Huangpu River

At eight in the morning, at the gate of Gujiazhai Park, Chen Qianli was indeed waiting for Wei Dafu.

Lu Zhongde was perfectly composed; he knew that in the car across the road the headquarters' well-trained marksmen were watching this way with staring eyes. Let Wei Dafu make the least suspicious move, and a bullet would go straight through his skull. Just before they came out, Ye Qinian had wavered once more. In that hidden room at the Zhengyuan Hotel he had said to Lu Zhongde that to use Haohan as bait, to fish out Chen Qianli's secret boarding place, was to stake too much on this one hand.

Lu Zhongde thought his teacher might truly be growing old. Let this thing but succeed, and they might seize a whole host of the Chinese Communists' upper ranks. This was a decisive battle with the underground Communist organization; no stake was too great to lay on it.

“Rest easy, Teacher; we have set our men and we hold the rendezvous, and Chen Qianli can play no trick that will get him anywhere.”

So Lu Zhongde spoke, but his heart was not as settled as in former days. At first he had not reckoned Chen Qianli a true adversary; after the meeting at the racecourse he had never understood why Teacher Ye should fear the man so. He had seen so many of the underground Party — seasoned hands like Long Dong — and made them all his beaten foes. Yet that affair of the bank vault had, in truth, wrung from the calculating Lu Zhongde a secret admiration; though he still felt that this reserved, self-contained opponent owed the most of it to luck. These last few days, for all the men You Tianxiao had led out to hunt him down, Chen Qianli had undone them one by one — which did indeed show a nimbleness and skill beyond the common. Now Lu Zhongde comforted himself again: secret-service work was not a matter of having learned a few grappling holds and turning peril to safety by luck.

Chen Qianli wore still that unfathomable look, and showed no surprise at the sight of Lu Zhongde: “Your business is done?”

“Old Yi has a thing to report to you —” Wei Dafu was on the point of throwing out the bait plainly, then recalled Lu Zhongde's charge to him: he must not be hasty, but first hear what Chen Qianli would say.

“Then speak, the two of you, first.”

Wei Dafu looked hesitantly at Lu Zhongde.

Lu Zhongde did not look at Wei Dafu; he addressed Chen Qianli: “This is a matter Old Fang charged me with before he died — before the meeting at the market, in fact.”

To talk at the gate of the park brought always a certain unease. A servant out marketing hurried past with her basket, cast a wary glance at them, and made off again. A pedicab came swaying and lurching up, laden with slatted, partitioned flat crates full of empty milk bottles, the bottles jouncing in their wooden compartments with an endless clink and clatter. A few foreign schoolchildren rushed toward the park gate, ‘hunting each other down’ as they ran; one of them, shot in the back just behind Lu Zhongde by a water pistol, fell in a slovenly, halfhearted way, then sprang up to return fire.

They watched the children scamper by at their play, their faces each turned a different way, and Chen Qianli said low: “Let us talk somewhere else; here is not convenient. There's a motorcar across the road that has stood there half the day.”

Lu Zhongde hardened his resolve and agreed to move. They went in at the park gate, followed the tree-lined avenue to the edge of the pond, and halted by a long bench that faced the Vallon Monument. Chen Qianli and Lu Zhongde sat down; Wei Dafu stood before them, and because the ground sloped he moved a little to the right, so as to lean against the small tree at the water's edge.

Now Lu Zhongde meant to let out the bait in form; but before that he must first tell a story about Old Fang.

“The morning of the meeting at the market, it was not yet seven when someone rapped at the shutters of the painting-and-scroll shop. The door opened: it was Old Fang. On so cold a day his head was all sweat — he must have walked a long way — his color bad, looking as though he had not slept the whole night. I went to the breakfast stall next door and got fried dough-sticks and soy milk, and we talked as we ate. In a few hours more we were to go to the meeting; I told him to rest a while in the back, and I would call him to go with me later, but he said he could not — he must be off at once, there was an important rendezvous come up, and after it he would go on to the market for the meeting.

“But that day he did not come; the meeting was held up ten-odd minutes on his account, and then the agents burst in. It was well it was held up that little while; otherwise, once Comrade Lin Shi had passed on the task at the meeting, Cui Wentai would have known the secret, and then the agents might well not have let us go.

“Old Fang died on the twenty-first of the last lunar month. At that time I was still shut up in the Longhua detention house; not until the twenty-second was I let out, and the moment I was free I learned of his death from an informant inside the Municipal Police. My heart then was full of grief and bitter anger; I had no mind at all for anything else, and it was only the next day that I remembered a certain thing.

“Old Fang and I had, in fact, a secret contact-box — a letterbox, we called it, though it was really a seat in the auditorium of the New Stage on Penglai Road. Under the panel behind the seat-back was a crack, just wide enough to slip in a scrap of paper. Push the paper in and pinch it once with the fingers, and nothing whatever showed. But if you knew of it, you had only to sit in that back row, and when the play began, to bend down and feel for it; a gentle prise with the hand, and the scrap of paper in the crack of the boards would fall out.

“Old Fang had long since arranged with me that in any urgent case he would tuck a secret message in here. Only the next day did the thing come back to me; I hastened to buy a ticket and go into the theater, and feeling there, sure enough, I found a scrap of paper. The moment I saw the writing on it my tears fell like rain: it was Old Fang's hand.

“In the message Old Fang bade me, once the New Year was past, on the fourteenth of the first month, place an advertisement in the Shen Bao. He had written out the whole of its wording on the paper, requiring that it be printed in the paper without the change of a single character. The advertisement was a secret contact signal; and the comrade to receive that signal — guess who? Comrade Haohan. The moment I saw the name I understood the whole of it.

“Why could Old Fang not come to the meeting? Why did he die? It was to shield Comrade Haohan that he died. The advertisement had to carry a telephone number; seeing it, Comrade Haohan would ring that number to make contact. So when you bade me leave the painting-and-scroll shop, I could not agree, for Haohan was to call that number, and I had to keep by the telephone. Yet though I made contact with Comrade Haohan, I had no way to arrange the withdrawal. By Old Fang's written instructions I should have settled the route of withdrawal with Comrade Lin Shi — but he too has died. I thought and thought, and could only come to you to take counsel.”

“Where have you placed Comrade Haohan in hiding?”

Lu Zhongde had meant to say that he had not yet met Haohan face to face, but had only fixed a rendezvous by telephone; but the words on his very lips, he changed them: “I have already received Comrade Haohan and placed him in a secret lodging. The spot is perfectly safe; not a soul knows of it.”

“Old Fang left you no word of the means of withdrawal?”

“He said only that when the time came I was to consult ‘Old Kai.’”

Chen Qianli thought a long while, and only after a good pause said to him: “I will arrange the withdrawal. Where have you put Comrade Haohan?”

Lu Zhongde shook his head: “It is not that I cannot tell you. Old Fang gave charge that Comrade Haohan is far too important, and the fewer who know, the surer his safety. This is the task Old Fang gave me before he died; I must see it through. It is a mission to be carried out though it cost my life.”

Lu Zhongde spoke with some feeling. Chen Qianli said no more; he thought quietly a while: “In truth it is not Comrade Haohan alone. Some of the Central leadership are also to withdraw from Shanghai before long; the higher levels gave this task to the Shanghai action group long ago. Comrade Lin Shi came to Shanghai for the very purpose of carrying it out.

“I reckon that when the higher levels told Old Fang to make contact with Haohan on the day of the meeting, it was a decision that the Shanghai group should take charge of moving Comrade Haohan.

“These last days the route of withdrawal has been arranged; indeed, this very evening a party of comrades will leave by ship. We have hired a freighter; at midnight tonight she will lie waiting at the Wusong bar, and we will use a steam-launch to carry the leading comrades out to her.

“The assembly point is in Pudong; the steam-launch will put in there at ten in the evening.”

“Where in Pudong?”

“The launch's landing place is not yet fixed — this is to guard against the water-police patrols, and against a leak of the news. This afternoon Chen Qianyuan will board the launch ahead of time and direct the little craft in a slow passage up the river along the Pudong shore. After dark he will have her choose a wharf and put in, and will himself go ashore at Tangqiao in Pudong to receive the comrades waiting there to board. Before seven tonight you are to hire a sampan at Dongjiadu and cross the river; Li Han will wait for you on the far bank and escort you to the assembly point at Tangqiao.”

“Assemble at Tangqiao.” Lu Zhongde turned it over. “So the launch puts in at a wharf nearby?”

“It may be near, it may not. Chen Qianyuan is aboard the launch; he will decide.”

“And if it is too far from Tangqiao, how is the way to be managed?”

“Only on foot.”

As they parted, Chen Qianli said to Wei Dafu: “I am about to go aboard that freighter and wait at the Wusong bar. You might come along with me and ride the great steamer.”

Wei Dafu smiled at Chen Qianli, and said that Old Yi here had the greater need of him.

Night; at the Dongjiadu crossing the river wind blew bleak and chill. Along this stretch of the Huangpu the boats were few. There being no lights, the shoreline of Tangqiao opposite could no longer be made out. On the outer road at Dongjiadu a motorcar drew up. After a while someone opened its door; the first to get out was Lu Zhongde.

Chen Qianli, hidden on the flat roof of a timber-yard warehouse, a pair of field glasses in his hands, watched Lu Zhongde from afar. By the light at the roadside he made out Comrade Haohan, who got out after him.

He began to run swiftly, and under cover of the dark reached the riverbank and flung himself into the cold water of the Huangpu. He had reckoned it out at length, and judged that Ye Qinian must by now have posted a great force of soldiers and police at Tangqiao. Here on the western bank of the Huangpu, calm and still as it seemed, many an enemy too must be hidden in the dark.

At this hour the oar-worked ferry boats should long since have stopped running, and the boatmen had withdrawn into the wooden hut at the crossing to drink and eat meat. Not yet five in the afternoon, a man had come to the crossing saying he was of the Garrison Command's detective squad, and had them ready a ferry boat, the boatmen to stand by, for someone would want the boat about seven. Moreover, once off work not one of the boatmen might leave the crossing; whoever broke this would be dealt with as consorting with the Communists, and taken to Longhua to be shot. That said, the man sent to a Muslim eating-house on the inner road for several hotpots, and stood them all an evening of wine and boiled mutton at the crossing.

The night lay thick; Lu Zhongde led Haohan onto the jetty, where someone waited at the far end — a man Ye Qinian had had him meet that afternoon at the Zhengyuan Hotel.

“Is everything arranged?” Lu Zhongde asked him from a distance.

The man waved a hand toward the wooden boat moored at the wharfside.

Tangqiao, on the far bank, lay now in utter dark. You Tianxiao sat in his car with his eyes fixed on the ferry wharf by the river. He saw figures appear on the jetty at the crossing — that would be Li Han; he could hardly wait to seize him at once. At the Maochang coal yard, Li Han and Chen Qianli had killed several of his men.

A subordinate come over from Tangqiao town with word told him that several strangers had entered the town, knocked open the door of a small eating-house, and sat down to their supper. They had not gone up for a close look, for fear of startling them; but from afar there were a good many at the table, and they had made out Dong Huiwen. The moment You Tianxiao had this news he had a man signal to the far bank with a lamp. When Lu Zhongde reached the crossing, the Shanghai-station agents waiting there would quietly signal to him that all was ready, and let him cross.

Section Chief Mu Chuan of the Judge Advocate's office was at the Zhengyuan Hotel for the first time, and learned for the first time that the Special Operations Headquarters kept such a secret sub-station in Shanghai. He knew he was soon to be transferred to Nanjing, and guessed that Deputy Director Ye's invitation to look in here might mean the man had heard something. And Deputy Director Ye had his reasons, to be sure: this seizure of the underground Communists' leading figures was to be a joint operation of the Special Operations Headquarters and the Judge Advocate's office of the Songhu Garrison Command.

He and Ye Qinian were both of the jianren grade, of like official rank, and meeting needed stand on no great ceremony. The two sat on the sofa and had drunk tea a while when the telephone rang. It was You Tianxiao. He reported that a fair number of Communists had indeed come to Tangqiao town, several of whom he had recognized; they were all sitting in a small eating-house, which the Shanghai-station operatives and the military police of the Judge Advocate's detective squad had thrown a cordon around. They waited only for ‘Xi Shi’ to let out the bait, hook Chen Qianli into telling the launch's landing place, and there and then make the arrests and take the whole haul at a cast.

“Has Lu Zhongde reached the crossing?” Ye Qinian pressed.

“We cannot see how things stand on the far bank. We have signaled to the operatives lying in wait at the crossing opposite; all is in readiness.”

“And where have the two ships got to?” Ye Qinian set down the telephone and turned to ask Secretary Ma.

“The Lintai Shipping freighter has just finished loading and still lies at the China Merchants' North Wharf. The Gongmao Transport launch, towing no lighter, left the wharf alone this afternoon and has been cruising the Huangpu, now and again drawing near the small wharves along the shore, perhaps to spy out the situation on the banks. Chen Qianyuan is aboard.”

“Can it be kept in view?”

“Section Chief Mu has borrowed a patrol boat from the Garrison Command; but the river is quiet, the engine too loud, and they dare not draw too near for fear of alarming them. The boat is in the Huangpu; they cannot get away.”

Ye Qinian shot a glance at Mu Chuan: “Where is Chen Qianli?”

“Our men followed him all the way from Gujiazhai Park; this afternoon he went to the China Merchants' wharf. After he came out from the wharf, the tail lost him.”

Ye Qinian nodded, and Secretary Ma withdrew.

Mu Chuan gazed at the map on the opposite wall — a military map at one to ten thousand, the ground of both banks of the Huangpu drawn out in detail, with the positions of the garrison posts and police bureaus marked. Mu Chuan had brought it expressly from the Garrison Command as a gift for Deputy Director Ye. After the fighting at Zhabei ended last year, the Garrison Command had resurveyed the terrain of the defense zone; the map was a military secret, and though there was little harm in giving it to Deputy Director Ye of the Special Operations Headquarters, it was still a favor neither small nor large.

In Nanjing it was rumored that Mu Chuan was soon to be transferred to the Military Commission's secret investigation group, and would then be in the same line of work as Ye Qinian.

“I hear Section Chief Mu is also to take charge of investigation work?”

“Until the order comes down from above, I am Judge Advocate still, and go on giving Director Ye and Captain You my best cooperation.”

“You Tianxiao, in the Judge Advocate's detective squad, owes much to Section Chief Mu's care; there have always been those at headquarters who say that of all the investigators sent to the various posts, cooperation with the Songhu Judge Advocate's office is the pleasantest.”

“Director Ye is too kind; I am slack by nature, and these years it is Captain You who has borne the toil.”

Ye Qinian thought a moment and said, smiling: “It would seem Section Chief Mu has some views on investigation work?”

“Investigation work is a great affair of the Party and the state; the Generalissimo leans heavily on it and holds it his eyes and ears. Formerly I did not understand it — it seemed to me too murderous a business; but seeing how Director Ye racks his very heart, holds the Communists a personal hatred, and must root them out and kill them, I too have been won to the cause.”

On the far bank, at the Dongjiadu crossing, Lu Zhongde had Haohan board first. When the two had settled themselves, the boatman cast off the mooring line, and the little boat drew slowly away from the wharf.

This little eating-house in Tangqiao town, of an evening, would ordinarily have no custom at all; today, though, a whole party of guests had come. The place had nothing ready, but the guests were not in the least particular: they had the owner stir-fry a plate of greens as he pleased, steam a piece of salt pork, and add a pot of rice. Having seen his guests settled, the owner too went off to his own family's table at the back.

The guests spoke little. They knew that outside the little eating-house was a great force of agents, many an enemy hidden in that darkness. They knew that before long they would be seized, and perhaps would die. Qin Chuan'an, Dong Huiwen, Tian Fei — every one of them had come, and even Liang Shichao had come. Chen Qianli had once said to him: “Old Liang need not go to the riverside; they believe you have left Shanghai.”

But Liang Shichao said that he too would go. By night, let him but alter his look and dress a little, and the enemy could not tell from afar who he was. He was needed here: the more people who showed themselves in Tangqiao town, the more the agents would believe their ‘bait’ was working. Willingly, gladly, the comrades walked into the ‘trap’ the enemy had laid, their hearts full of a high ardor, and afraid of nothing.

Chen Qianli swam fast, and in the mid-channel of the Huangpu he caught up with the ferry boat. Lu Zhongde had not taken his advice to hire a separate sampan for the crossing, but used the ferry still; on its stern hung an oil lamp, and Lu Zhongde, most cautious, kept a flashlight burning besides. This made things rather awkward.

He swam to the side opposite where the boatman stood and put his head above water to look. It was the ebb now, and along this reach the water ran north; to bring the oar-boat true to the wharf on the far bank, the boatman first drove her hard to the southeast, and at midstream her bow began to bend around to the northeast.

Diving underwater to the boat's side, Chen Qianli laid a hand soundlessly on the planking and waited a while without a stir, his body wholly slack, letting himself drift along with the boat. When he heard talk begin aboard, he gripped the planking hard, sprang up, and rolled himself over onto the boat. He had marked his direction well beforehand, and the moment he was aboard he knocked the oil lamp down into the Huangpu. Lu Zhongde started, and swung the flashlight round upon him.

Chen Qianli did not rise, but lunged from where he lay at Lu Zhongde, driving the dagger in his hand toward him. In his confusion Lu Zhongde warded it off with the flashlight, and the flashlight too fell into the water. Chen Qianli knew he could not delay; he must give his man no room to draw breath, for he did not know whether, if Lu Zhongde cried out, the soldiers and agents lying in ambush on either bank would hear. The light gone out, he sprang up, and with a single kick sent Lu Zhongde into the Huangpu. On the instant he leapt into the water after him and flung himself bodily upon him. He seized Lu Zhongde's clothes and dragged him under. He meant to finish Lu Zhongde with the dagger, but at a struggle from his man the dagger only grazed the collar, slipped from his grasp, and sank to the riverbed. Chen Qianli held his breath, dived to the bottom, gripped Lu Zhongde by both legs, and dragged him down, hard and fast, toward the bed of the Huangpu. He held on a minute or two, until he felt Lu Zhongde's body struggle no more. He drew Lu Zhongde's head up close, and underwater gave the man's throat, at the Adam's apple, a savage pinch with his knuckles; then he let go, and watched the agent drift, on the current of the Huangpu, farther and farther away.

Chen Qianli gazed at the right bank of the Huangpu, and heaven and earth grew darker still. He knew that those comrades would be taken by the enemy at any moment — Qianyuan among them. For the ‘A Thousand Li of Rivers and Mountains’ plan they had gone forward without a backward glance, bravely making themselves the ‘bait’; and to lay that bait straight to the mouths of Ye Qinian and Lu Zhongde, Wei Dafu had let the agents take him on purpose and feigned a turncoat's part. At the gate of Gujiazhai Park something had stirred suddenly in Chen Qianli's heart, and he had told Wei Dafu not to go back with Lu Zhongde — he had meant to pull Wei Dafu out of the devils' clutches; but Wei Dafu, with a smile, had refused what was perhaps his one chance of escape.

But he could not go to their rescue; his charge was to bring Comrade Haohan safe to Ruijin. That evening of New Year's Day, setting the comrades their tasks at the Maochang coal yard, he had long since arranged two sets of tasks, one open and one hidden. While Ling Wen and Lu Zhongde went to Guangzhou, he himself had taken Liang Shichao to Shantou, and opened up, besides, another top-secret liaison line.

Chen Qianli rolled himself up onto the boat once more, wiped the water from his face, cast a glance at the cabin, and ordered the boatman to turn the ferry toward Suzhou Creek.

Completed at Sinan, Shanghai, March 2022.
