### Fortune-Telling

The fortune-tellers who go hurrying up and down the streets are of about two kinds. One is a half-blind man or woman, carrying a little gong and a fiddle, and striking the one and plucking the other as they go; being half-blind, they walk with no great ease, and so must always have another to lead them along. The other kind carries nothing in the hand and is not blind, and cries as it goes, "Long life reckoned here...!" and "Two hundred cash!" The first sort come from every quarter, but the second are all of the Ningbo group. To have a fortune told to the fiddle and the little gong costs rather more than the Ningbo gentlemen ask: two mao of small silver a reading at the outside, and one mao at the very least.

But their real object is not this trifling fortune-teller's fee. Should they take you for one of the dull and gullible, they will often make a great mystery of their patter, falsely declaring that this year your stars run against you, that in such-and-such a month the constellations are ill-set and you had best have a care, and that if you would turn calamity into blessing you must have a propitiation performed, for only so can the disaster be dispelled and the misfortune escaped. When they have rambled on at random for a while and talked you into a flutter, their patter has done its work. Offerings to the stars, a rite to drive off the bad luck—they will see to the whole of it single-handed, and the fee is bound to come out at either just under ten dollars or just over.
