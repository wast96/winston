### New Rules at the Yao-er Houses

The rules of the yao-er houses — the second grade, that is — have always been kept to the letter, hidebound and never altered: a girl called out to a party, two dollars; a tea-visit, a dollar; the first night in the room, six dollars (whence the name "the six knock-down"), and from the second night on, four. This old scale had been in force for scores of years, unlike the changsan houses, which had long since changed it once, and twice, and a third time; so the common saying goes, "Slack changsan, rigid yao-er, iron-hard pheasant" — a phrase that praises the yao-er houses for their rigid, ruled-off sameness.

Of late years, though, even this has shifted a little: the room has gone up to eight dollars, and from the second night on it too is five, so that the "six knock-down" ought by rights to be renamed the "eight knock-down." A call to a party, which used always to be two dollars, may now be had for one if a regular sends for her — though it must still be paid in cash on the nail, as with the Cantonese girls. Such are the new rules of the yao-er houses.

The yao-er houses used all to cluster together in the International Settlement, about Qipan Street, Cross Street, and Chicken-and-Duck Lane; but ever since prostitution was outlawed by the drawing of lots, they long ago removed themselves, bag and baggage, to the French Concession.
