### Opening a New Chapter (Part 3)

### The Songhu Garrison Command

In Shanghai, the chief organ by which the Kuomintang reactionaries made a business of wrecking the organizations of the Chinese Communist Party and slaughtering Communists and progressives was the Songhu Garrison Command; it was an enemy the Central Special Section had to reckon with in earnest.

At that time the Kuomintang's Songhu Garrison Command stood north of the Longhua Temple, the ancient building at Longhua town. This place had first been the Longhua Arsenal, set up in the fourth year of the Qing Tongzhi reign (1865); by the second year of the Republic (1913), Yuan Shikai, to suppress the revolutionaries of the south, specially dispatched a trusted man as Songhu garrison commissioner and stationed a division of troops within the Longhua Arsenal. Afterward the garrison here changed hands again and again, now called the "Defense Command," now the "Martial Law Command," all of them tools by which the reactionaries suppressed the people. In March 1927, after Zhou Enlai led the third armed uprising of the Shanghai workers to victory, Bai Chongxi, front-line commander-in-chief of the Eastern Route Army of the Northern Expeditionary Army, led his troops into Shanghai and set up a Kuomintang garrison command within the Longhua Arsenal, appointing Yang Hu commander (on April 9 renamed the Shanghai martial-law commander); Bai Chongxi took part in the April Twelfth counterrevolutionary coup and supported Chiang Kai-shek in setting up the Nanjing regime. In September of the same year, Chiang Kai-shek had Bai Chongxi set up in Longhua, in place of the old command, the Songhu Garrison Command, and take its command himself. In January 1928 Bai Chongxi led his troops out of Shanghai, and Chiang Kai-shek sent Xiong Shihui, deputy commander of the Kuomintang's Thirteenth Army and commander of its First Division, to serve as acting Songhu garrison commander. On March 30 of the same year it was renamed the Songhu Garrison Command, with Qian Dajun, commander of the Thirty-second Army, serving concurrently as its commander; in September, Qian Dajun was transferred to be commander for "bandit suppression" in the Jiangnan area, and Xiong Shihui again served concurrently as commander. In this period the Central Special Section's chief adversaries were Qian Dajun and Xiong Shihui.

Qian Dajun, styled Muyin, was born in 1893 in Kunshan, Jiangsu. In 1916 he entered the Baoding Army Officers' Academy to study artillery. In 1924 he served as instructor and in administrative posts at the Whampoa Military Academy, and took part in the First and Second Eastern Expeditions; in January 1926 he rose to command the First Division, and in July was made Guangzhou garrison commander, among other posts. In 1927, in concert with the April Twelfth counterrevolutionary coup Chiang Kai-shek launched in Shanghai, he joined with the Guangdong warlords to engineer the "April Fifteenth" coup in the Guangdong region, slaughtering Communists and revolutionaries on a great scale. In August 1927, on the southward march of the Nanchang Uprising army, Qian Dajun led his troops to intercept the uprising army in the Ruijin and Huichang area, "to good effect," and was stationed at Meixian in Guangdong; in October he rose to command the Thirty-second Army. In early 1928 he led his troops north, and on March 30 was made Songhu garrison commander; before long he was also made a standing member of the Kuomintang's Shanghai special municipal party headquarters and a member of the Jiangsu provincial government. In September his troops were reorganized as the Third Division, and he served as its commander while concurrently commander for "bandit suppression" in the Jiangnan area; in October he became a member of the military commission of the Nanjing National Government… In the end he attained the "rank of lieutenant general with the honorary title of full general."

Xiong Shihui, who twice served as Songhu garrison commander in this stretch, was styled Tianyi, a native of Anyi county, Jiangxi, and also born in 1893. In 1915 Xiong Shihui graduated from the Baoding Army Officers' Academy; in 1920 he studied in Japan, entering the Japanese Army War College. At the start of the Northern Expedition in 1926 he was party representative of the First Division, and afterward held in turn the posts of party representative of the Fourteenth Army, commander of the Independent First Division, deputy commander of the Thirteenth Army with concurrent command of the First Division, and commander of the Thirty-seventh Division. From January 1928 he served concurrently as Songhu garrison commander until the end of March; on April 1 he led his troops north with Chiang Kai-shek's Northern Expedition, in June was transferred to command the Fifth Division, and on September 7 again became Songhu garrison commander, and afterward also commander-in-chief for "bandit suppression" in Song-Hu-Hang. In the summer of 1931 he was transferred to be chief of staff of the Nanchang field headquarters… In the end he attained the rank of "full general, second class."

The Kuomintang's Songhu Garrison Command had crimes beyond counting: "From 1927, after Chiang Kai-shek launched the April Twelfth counterrevolutionary coup in Shanghai, the Kuomintang garrison command, the defense command, and the Songhu Garrison Command that were set up one after another at Longhua slaughtered no one knows how many Communists and revolutionaries. By incomplete count, in the years from the 'April Twelfth' counterrevolutionary coup of 1927 to 1933 alone, the victims came to several thousand. Among the martyrs imprisoned or killed there were many famous early Communists, such as Sun Bingwen, Luo Yinong, Peng Pai, Yang Yin, Yan Changyi, Xing Shizhen, Chen Yannian, Chen Qiaonian, Zheng Futa, Xu Baihao, Xuan Zhonghua, and Huang Jingxi. All of them endured every kind of cruel torture in prison, and in the end most died heroically there; the martyr Xuan Zhonghua was even cut in two at the waist by the enemy on that spot."

### "Political Investigator No. 4"

The Songhu Garrison Command was a mortal foe of the Chinese Communist Party, forever threatening the safety of the Party organization. How to get intelligence from there in time and grasp its movements was a question the Central Special Section constantly turned over. The Special Section's famous legendary figure "Old Song" (Song Qirong, alias Song Zaisheng) was the first important intelligence agent Chen Geng sent into the Kuomintang's Songhu Garrison Command after he took charge of the intelligence section's work. Close behind, planting himself inside, was Song's younger brother "Young Song" (Song Qihua). In 1929, "Old Wang" too, through Yang Dengying's connection, planted himself in the inspection office of the Songhu Garrison Command as an inspector. They supplied the intelligence section with much valuable intelligence, and the one who did best of all was Song Zaisheng, who got hold of the credentials of "Political Investigator No. 4" of the Songhu Garrison Command.

Song Zaisheng, originally named Song Qirong, also styled Huaiqing, was born in 1897 in Zhuji, Zhejiang. In the winter of 1926 he joined the Chinese Communist Party in Luoyang, Henan. After joining he took part in the military commission work of the CCP Luoyang district committee, and was then appointed by the Wuhan National Government commander of the Fifth Route of the National Revolutionary Army. On taking up the post he went secretly into the various parts of western Henan to organize armed forces, and in May 1927 led his troops into Luoyang. But because the enemy's strength was too disproportionate to his own, he could not hold, and Song Qirong was arrested by Han Fuju and sentenced to death. Then Xuan Xiafu, director of the political department of the Northwest Army and a Communist, hurried through the night to Xingyang county and asked Fang Zhenwu and Sun Liangcheng to go to Luoyang and intercede with Han Fuju, so that the sentence was commuted to life imprisonment. Not long after the retrial, Han Fuju's forces moved to Xinyang. Because Song Qirong had once saved Niu Yongjian's younger brother Niu Yingu, and had read in the papers that Niu Yongjian had become chairman of the Jiangsu provincial government, he had the CCP Luoyang district committee write to Niu Yingu to find a way to rescue him. Niu Yongjian soon wired Feng Yuxiang, commander-in-chief of the Northwest Army, to ask for his release, and interceded as well through Feng Yuxiang's wife Li Dequan; once released, Song Qirong took the name "Zaisheng." Before long, because a traitor gave up the address of the CCP Luoyang district committee, two of its leaders were arrested. Song Zaisheng then went by way of Kaifeng to Nanjing, and after finding the Party organization worked in the military commission of the Nanjing municipal committee. After May 1928 he was transferred by the Party organization to the Central Special Section, and stayed to work at Chen Geng's side.

Through Chen Geng's careful and thorough arrangements, Song Zaisheng soon made connections with the relevant quarters in Shanghai. On September 7 that year, Chiang Kai-shek appointed Xiong Shihui Kuomintang Songhu garrison commander, and then had him also serve as "commander-in-chief for bandit suppression in Song-Hu-Hang." One day Song Zaisheng ran into his fellow townsman Chen Datong on the street, and the man, the moment they met, said to him cheerfully, "Old Song, your luck has turned!"

Song Zaisheng smiled deferentially and asked in surprise, "What is it?"

"Xiong Tianyi"—Xiong Shihui's given name—"has become garrison commander. He is a student of old Mr. Jiang Fangzhen; you had better hurry and take Jiang as your master!" Chen Datong, well pleased with his own foresight, urged him on: "Once you take him as master, he will recommend you. Go by this door, and Xiong Tianyi is bound to give you a respectable post. With a strong backer like the garrison command, need you worry about rising in office and making your fortune?"

"Yes, that's a fat post, but—" Song Zaisheng nodded and asked thoughtfully, "Are there any conditions?"

Chen Datong said with a grin: "Old Mr. Jiang is fond of good works and glad to give; presenting a disciple's card is not so hard. The main thing is that you must get ready a generous gift, and lay out some money for a banquet besides. Later, when you really have a way to make your fortune, you must let that crowd share in the good things too."

Song Zaisheng waved a hand, feigning hesitation, and said slowly: "Don't be in a hurry. Let me go back and think it over carefully first, all right?"

"Fine!"

What Chen Datong had said about the teacher-and-student tie between Jiang Fangzhen and Xiong Shihui was no lie. Jiang Fangzhen, styled Baili, called Danning, was a native of Haining, Zhejiang. He had served as head of the Baoding Army Officers' Academy and as acting head of the Nanjing Army War College, and after his death was posthumously granted the rank of full general by the National Government. Xiong Shihui was a native of Anyi, Jiangxi, a graduate of the second class of the infantry course at the Baoding Army Officers' Academy. When Xiong Shihui entered the Baoding academy in 1913, Jiang Fangzhen was then its head. In those days officialdom set great store by the teacher-and-student bond, a thing Zhou Enlai and Chen Geng naturally knew very well.

After Song Zaisheng parted from Chen Datong, he turned the matter over and over, thought it a good chance, and on his return reported it to Chen Geng. Chen Geng, hearing it, was delighted, and told him to try the method and work to plant himself in the Songhu Garrison Command. So Song Zaisheng presented a disciple's card and took Jiang Fangzhen as his master. Sure enough, not long after Jiang Fangzhen accepted his card, he introduced him to Xiong Shihui's mansion on Avenue du Roi Albert. Xiong Shihui, seeing that Song Zaisheng came recommended by old Mr. Jiang, received him warmly. When Song Zaisheng came back from Xiong Shihui and had just stepped into Jiang Fangzhen's parlor, Jiang Fangzhen told him with great pleasure: "Tianyi has already telephoned; he appoints you political investigator of the Songhu Garrison Command."

Song Zaisheng soon received the credentials of Political Investigator No. 4 of the garrison command. Then, following Chen Geng's instructions, he used this political investigator's status to make contact with the British and French police stations and the inspection and detective offices of the Shanghai municipal police. He soon came to know Fan Guangzhen, a detective officer of the French police station, and set up an exchange of intelligence with him. Fan Guangzhen, also called Bofu, was a native of Shaoxing, Zhejiang, of policeman's origins; he had served ten years in the investigation section of the political office of the French Concession. The investigation section, also called the investigation squad, was charged specially with helping the Kuomintang government hunt Communists, progressives, and anti-Chiang figures within the Kuomintang in the French Concession. Its squad leader was a Frenchman, Ma Muya, and Fan Guangzhen was one of the three Chinese detective officers. At this time the intelligence Fan Guangzhen gave Song Zaisheng came in part from traitors, in part from the yellow labor unions, and in part from the police station's informers. Song Zaisheng carried it back and gave it to Chen Geng, then handed Fan Guangzhen some true-and-false intelligence the organization had made up. Afterward Song Zaisheng also struck up a connection with You Agen, detective inspector of the Laozha station of the British Concession, and his fame grew and grew. Song Zaisheng's younger brother Song Qihua ("Young Song") was a famous legendary figure too, and here he served as "Old Song's" helper.

One day at the end of the twelfth lunar month that year, a man named Wang Tiezheng came to Song Zaisheng and introduced him to a young man surnamed Huang. In a heavy Hunan accent this Huang said to Song Zaisheng: "I can help you catch Luo Mai, an important Communist!"

"Luo Mai?" Song Zaisheng's heart tightened, and he could not help repeating it. Now Luo Mai was an alias of Li Weihan, who was then serving as secretary of the Jiangsu provincial committee in Shanghai, one of the responsible comrades of our Party the Kuomintang reactionaries were bent on hunting down. When the enemy put out a warrant for Li Weihan, they had once set a reward of 50,000 yuan on his head.

Before Song Zaisheng could speak again, Huang nodded and pressed on: "Do you know whether there's a cut in the 50,000-yuan reward for catching Luo?"

Song Zaisheng raised an eyebrow, gave a faint smile, and said: "Paid in full, not a bit docked. But the thing must be real, and sure."

The fellow Huang thumped his chest and said, full of swagger: "Absolutely guaranteed."

"Good then!" Song Zaisheng then fixed a date with this Huang: they would meet on the fifth of the first lunar month at the Changle Teahouse. And at Huang's earnest request, he first paid him 30 yuan in cash for his "urgent New Year needs."

After sending this fellow Huang off, Song Zaisheng at once reported the matter to Chen Geng. Chen Geng thought a moment and said: "All right! When the time comes, bring Huang to the Dadong Hotel. I have my own arrangements; as for how you are to play along, I'll tell you in a while."

The fifth of the first month came round in a flash. That morning Huang did indeed come to the Changle Teahouse, and met the political investigator of his imaginings — Song Zaisheng.

When Song Zaisheng brought this Huang to the Dadong Hotel, Chen Geng, dressed in a khaki wool uniform, every inch a high Kuomintang officer, was "waiting respectfully" in a lavishly furnished room. Song Zaisheng led the money-mad informer into the room, put out his hand, and made the introduction with great ceremony: "This is our command's chief of staff, Colonel Wang; you go with him to see the commander!"

Chen Geng then took the man into a car and drove to a shikumen house at 805 Weihai Road. Waiting for them there were Gu Shunzhang, then in charge of the Central Special Section's day-to-day work, and three other Red Squad men.

Gu Shunzhang, seeing Chen Geng leading in this stranger of no great height who was just about to enter, hurried to the door with a smiling face and said cheerfully: "Good day, Chief of Staff Wang! And this must be Mr. Huang!"

"Just so!" Chen Geng answered with a nod.

Gu Shunzhang invited the two of them in, saying as he went: "Come, come, let us talk it over first, and then go to see the commander." And he at once ordered fine wine and food set out to entertain the guest.

The wine now brought out was called "Thousand-Li Fragrance." Though the men keeping the guest company kept filling his cup, they showed not the least sign of drunkenness; but this honored guest, the moment it touched his lips, at once fell dead drunk. From then on, this Huang was never seen to walk out the door again. It turned out that Gu Shunzhang, this master magician of "Hua Guangqi," had worked his magic: that wine-pot was no ordinary thing, but a magician's prop that could "treat each man differently" according to who he was.

One day, strolling on Nanjing Road, Song Zaisheng walked to the door of the Sanyou Industrial Company and ran into Fan Guangzhen, the French-concession detective officer, with a young man behind him. On introduction, this young man was surnamed Gu, but what exactly he did Song Zaisheng did not know. Yet when the young man heard that Song Zaisheng was a political investigator, he took to him like a fish to water, would not leave, and followed all the way to Yingchun Lane on Zhejiang Road, Song Zaisheng's lodging. All along the way the young man fawned and flattered, and no sooner was he in the room than he asked: "How much is the reward for catching Li Lisan?" When he heard "a reward of 50,000," he begged Song Zaisheng to take him to see Xiong Shihui. The next day the man came to the appointed place, bringing a photograph of Comrade Li Lisan taken abroad. Song Zaisheng then reported the matter to Chen Geng. Chen Geng quickly found out that this man surnamed Gu was a secretary of the railway workers' union, who saw Li Lisan morning and night and was on no shallow terms with him! Now, money-mad, he had set about a monstrous, heaven-defying business. In this Shanghai steeped everywhere in the White Terror, such a degenerate who had betrayed the revolution could not be borne; and so Chen Geng took decisive measures and, for the Party and the people, did away with this menace.

Song Zaisheng was arrested by the enemy in August 1930 when a fund-raising errand went wrong. The Kuomintang, working by turns hard and soft, pressed him hard to surrender; "Old Song" would not yield and was sentenced to life imprisonment. Later the Central Special Section engaged the lawyer Gu Jirong to defend him, and through legal rescue his sentence was reduced to four years' imprisonment; his term ran out and he was released in 1934. By Song Zaisheng's account to Hong Yangsheng: after he came out of prison, the organization decided to send him to the Soviet area, but since he had a household of five, old and young, and no livelihood at all, he asked the organization for a loan to settle them before he went; this was not approved, and instead, on the ground of "refusing to obey assignment," he was expelled from the Party. Song Zaisheng was "unwilling at heart, with no way to appeal." He had meant to raise funds and go to Chaohu to wage guerrilla war, but it came to nothing. Later, by way of trade, he passed through the white areas and went by Vietnam and Yunnan to Chongqing to find the Party organization; he did not find it, but was fixed on by agents. At his wits' end, he could only return to Shanghai. After Liberation he served as head of the first group of the intelligence section of the Shanghai municipal public security bureau, and in 1951 as manager of the Tongyuchun Teahouse on Nanjing Road in Shanghai. Li Qiang said in October 1980: when Shanghai had just been liberated, "Old Song" once picked out two baskets of honey peaches and sent them to Chen Geng, and Chen Geng gave part of them to him. Li Weihan, in a letter to Li Yimang dated May 7, 1982, said: "When Song Zaisheng worked in the Shanghai municipal public security bureau after Liberation, I went to Shanghai on business, and he learned of it and came to see me, and spoke of this matter" (meaning the time that year when a certain Huang informed to Song Zaisheng about the address of "Luo Mai" and sought the reward, and it was afterward found that the one informed against was not Li Weihan but someone else, "so he did not ask me to move house"). "Afterward he retired to his home in Ningbo, and we had letters back and forth several times."

"Young Song," that is Song Qihua, was arrested in Shanghai after Gu Shunzhang's defection and sentenced to life imprisonment. In 1941 he won a reduced sentence and release, and lived in hiding in Xuzhou. After Xuzhou was liberated in 1949, he greatly wished to see Chen Geng, his old leader from the days of the Central Special Section, and knowing that Chen Geng set great store by fellowship, he ran a "Missing Persons Notice" in the Xuzhou Ribao under the names Song Qirong and Song Qihua, which read: "General Chen Geng: since we parted in '31, when Shunzhang turned traitor and we were held in prison and our family fell on hard times, we now seek a living in Xuzhou and long to see you once." In early February 1949, Chen Geng saw this notice at his troops' station in Luohe, Henan, and sent someone to fetch Song Qihua to Luohe, where he stayed some days at Chen Geng's headquarters to talk over old times. At the time the troops were being reorganized and preparing to cross the Yangtze, and the work was busy. After a long talk with him, Chen Geng sent someone to escort him to the CCP Central Plains Bureau to be suitably placed.

Song Qirong, during the cadre-vetting movement of the early 1950s, was once arrested by the Shanghai municipal public security bureau on the ground of being a "diehard of the Yang Fan clique," and held for nineteen months. On examination there was no evidence at all; Chen Geng, coming to Shanghai and learning of it, intervened, and he was released.

### The British and French Concession Police

The organs of the CCP Center in Shanghai were housed in the British and French Concessions, and the Concession police stations always gravely threatened the safety of the Party organization. For this reason the Central Special Section attached great importance to intelligence work on the British and French Concession police as well. Besides drawing intelligence from the British and French Concession police through Yang Dengying, Chen Geng used the methods of "planting inside" and "winning over" to obtain intelligence on the Concession authorities directly.

After Song Zaisheng planted himself in the Songhu Garrison Command and found his footing, the main task the Special Section gave him was to build connections with the British and French police stations and the inspection and detective offices of the Shanghai municipal police. As recounted earlier, in his standing as an investigator of the Songhu Garrison Command he first built a connection with Fan Guangzhen, a detective officer of the French police station, and they exchanged intelligence. The intelligence Fan Guangzhen supplied came in part from traitors, in part from the yellow labor unions and from informers. The intelligence Old Song handed the other side was all drawn up by the organization and given to him by Comrade Chen Geng, and then passed by him to Fan Guangzhen. Old Song also built a connection with You Agen, detective inspector of the Laozha station of the British Concession, which played a certain part. Fan Guangzhen, though he "exchanged intelligence" with us for a good while, was reactionary to the core. By the account of Xue Gengxin, who from 1930 was a policeman in the French Concession alongside Fan Guangzhen and later rose to be a special-grade chief inspector of the political office: "Fan Guangzhen, also called Fan Bochuan, a native of Shaoxing, Zhejiang, of policeman's origins, served more than ten years in the investigation squad. Every time he took part in an arrest or the seizure of documents, he made copies, and together with the arrested person's name, address, photograph, and fingerprints had them condensed into a file, and asked Yao Daren, a detective officer of the political office, to copy them out by hand and keep them. In 1939 he sold this set of copies to the puppet secret service 'No. 76.' In 1933, on the introduction of Wang Zhaohuai, head of the detective squad of the Kuomintang's Songhu Garrison Command, this man took a monthly stipend of 60 yuan from Juntong; in 1942, his identity exposed, he left the post and went to the interior. After the victory over Japan he returned to Shanghai and served as head of the second group under Mao Sen of Juntong, under Zhu Cenlou, who in 1964, for having performed meritorious service, was dealt with leniently by the central Ministry of Public Security and sentenced to life imprisonment."

In the British Concession police station, there was also a connection with the detective officer Lu Liankui. Chen Geng sent someone to serve as an "apprentice" under Lu Liankui and gather some intelligence. Through Lu Liankui's connections he also placed a Party member, one Wang, as a detective in the police station, who did no little work. For instance, after a traitor had given up information on our Party, this man, on the pretext of forcing a confession, beat the traitor to death. Zhou Guisheng, detective inspector of the Laozha station of the British Concession's Municipal Council, also had ties with the Central Special Section and often brought intelligence. After national liberation he died of illness at his old home in Yancheng, Jiangsu.

In the French Concession police station, the Communist Chen Pengnian, in his standing as a detective, did many things for the Party. For instance, he passed intelligence, got hold of blacklists, and used his position to let our comrades go. When one of our organs was broken into, he would keep watch at the door, and when someone came he would deliberately hail them: "Hey! Are you a Communist? No? Then be off!" And our comrade would walk away just so. Chen Pengnian was a Chinese laborer who had studied in France and a student of the Communist University of the Toilers of the East in Moscow. (He later joined the Red Army and was killed on the Long March.) In the French police station there were two or three other Chinese laborers who had taken part in the First World War in France and won medals, and who after returning to Shanghai served as head of the traffic police in the French station; they too often passed us word and did some covering work.

In this period the Central Special Section went on widening its gains on the positions already taken. After a stretch of effort, the Central Special Section's intelligence network developed greatly: intelligence agents were set up in the Shanghai municipal police, the Kuomintang's Shanghai special municipal party headquarters, the Shanghai telegraph office, and some news agencies and hospitals, and in Nanjing's Kuomintang municipal party headquarters, public security bureau, and Nanjing defense command, and in Zhenjiang's Jiangsu provincial government and Suzhou's county government, and elsewhere. "Pockmarked Cai" even went to the Northeast to develop a "Manchuria intelligence network." In time, among the Concession police stations and the Kuomintang's secret-service organs, police bureaus, garrison commands, gendarmerie, and party offices, and among society's secret societies, local ruffians, and hoodlums, and the detectives and police of the Concessions, everywhere there were our own people, or sympathizers, or connections that could be used, active on every hand, able at any time to supply intelligence, most timely and valuable, on political, military, economic, and even international matters. Because word came through so well, when the enemy meant to break into one of our organs, we usually knew of it beforehand. With so many connections, not only did word come through, but often things could be managed that were hard to imagine.

Toward the figures it was in touch with at the time, the Special Section took different attitudes according to the different objects. If they were sympathetic to the Party, contact was made in the Party's own name; some were only kept up as ordinary friends, and were not told the comrade's own identity. So there were some who, though they did much for the Party, did not themselves know the inside of it.

In this way the clandestine work of the Party in Shanghai at last broke free of pure terror tactics and turned to political struggle, winning victory after victory, and the Party's organs in Shanghai were thoroughly protected. This was the success of the line and policy that Comrade Zhou Enlai had drawn up with his own hand; and Chen Geng, Li Qiang, Liu Ding, and the other comrades of the Special Section, who resolutely carried out Zhou Enlai's instructions, played an extremely important part in bringing this change about.
