## H2 Chapter 30. Brothers in Life and Death Alike

> 千钧一发之际，慕次眼到手到，大叫一声“卧倒”，直扑过来，抱住阿初，猛力扎向青砖塌陷处，连人带砖都直落深渊，而那根致命的横梁被还没有来得及塌陷的部分青砖支撑住，摇晃了几下，耷拉下狰狞的面孔，无力地滚落在大殿上，溅起灰尘。
In that hair’s-breadth instant, Muci’s eye and hand moved as one; with a great shout of “Down!” he flung himself forward, seized A-Chu, and drove hard toward the place where the grey bricks had given way, so that men and bricks together plunged straight into the abyss. The deadly crossbeam, caught on the bricks that had not yet collapsed, swayed a few times, let its savage face droop, then rolled feebly onto the floor of the hall, throwing up a spray of dust.

> 氤氲泛白的烟灰袅袅升腾在潮湿的空气中。
A pale, curling haze of dust rose slowly through the damp air.

> 一片寂静。
Utter silence.

> 窗外，轻飘飘地纸衣滑落在地，一双雪青色的绣鞋轻轻地踩在纸衣上，一个披头散发的女人把早已准备好的炸药放在所需的炸点上，连好引线，她细心地把导火索牵引至殿门外，她伸手关紧了大殿的门，月色下，那双手显得像蛇一样邪恶和妖媚，她用这双柔媚的手，凶残地点燃了引线，然后，转身离去。
Outside the window, a paper garment slid lightly to the ground; a pair of snow-blue embroidered shoes stepped softly onto it. A woman with dishevelled hair set the explosives she had long since prepared at the points where they were needed, joined up the wires, and carefully drew the fuse out beyond the door of the hall; she reached out and shut the hall doors fast. In the moonlight those hands looked evil and bewitching as a snake’s, and with those soft, alluring hands she cruelly lit the fuse, then turned and walked away.

> 她像鬼魅一般走出山门，就在她迈出山门的瞬间，“轰”的一声巨响，大殿内发生了剧烈的爆炸，整个大殿坍塌下来，山门也因爆炸的波及而摇摆。
Like a phantom she passed out through the mountain gate, and in the very instant she stepped beyond it, with a thunderous roar a violent explosion tore through the hall; the whole hall came crashing down, and even the mountain gate rocked in the blast.

> 万籁俱静，一团漆黑。
Every sound stilled; all was pitch-black.

> 剧烈的震荡之后，杨慕次睁开双眼，他的视线有些模糊不清。但是，他很清楚自己目前的处境，他被死死的困在了泥潭。
After the violent shock, Yang Muci opened his eyes; his sight was blurred and dim. But he understood his situation clearly enough: he was pinned fast in a bog of mud.

> 自己没有死，那么，阿初应该还在。因为临落地的瞬间，慕次将阿初紧拽胸前，自己用血肉之躯替他支撑了一个平安软垫。所幸的是，泥潭的泥沙救了自己的命。
He was not dead, then A-Chu must still be alive; for in the instant before they landed Muci had clutched A-Chu tight against his chest and, with his own flesh and blood, made a soft cushion of safety for him. Mercifully, the mud and sand of the bog had saved his own life.

> 他听见了咳嗽声，那是阿初的声音，就在他附近。
He heard coughing, A-Chu’s voice, close beside him.

> “你怎么样？”慕次问。
“How are you?” Muci asked.

> “我的膝盖陷在淤泥里，拔不出来。”阿初回答。“你怎么样？受伤了吗？”
“My knee is sunk in the silt; I can’t pull it free,” A-Chu answered. “And you? Are you hurt?”

> “我跟你情形差不多，我泡在水沟里。”
“Much the same as you. I’m soaking in a water-ditch.”

> 他们彼此寻声，找到对方的影子，慕次艰难地向阿初的方向移动，他先把阿初的腿拽出来，扶着他沿石而上，一股股熏人的霉气直窜向阿初的脑门，阿初喘息了几声。
Following each other’s voices, they found each other’s shadowy shapes. Muci worked his way with difficulty toward A-Chu, pulled his leg free first, and helped him up along the rocks; wave upon wave of a choking, mouldy stench shot straight up into A-Chu’s head, and he gasped a few times.

> “这里怎么会隐藏着一个地下岩洞呢？”慕次说。
“How does an underground cavern come to be hidden here?” Muci said.

> “这不奇怪，从前的寺庙啊，大家族啊，都挖掘了一些地道，用于躲避土匪、灾难。”阿初说。“不过，这岩洞像是天然的，有人利用了这个天然的洞穴，做不法勾当。”
“There’s nothing strange in it. In the old days temples, and great families, all dug out tunnels of a kind, to hide from bandits and disasters,” A-Chu said. “But this cavern looks natural; someone has made use of a natural hollow to carry on unlawful business.”

> “谢天谢地，我们没砸在石头上，拣了条命。”阿次爬到阿初身旁坐下。
“Thank Heaven we didn’t come down on the rocks; we’ve picked up our lives again.” A-Ci crawled over and sat down beside A-Chu.

> 阿初还在咳嗽。
A-Chu was still coughing.

> 慕次说：“这里又湿又滑，你往上坐一点，安全。”
Muci said, “It’s wet and slippery here. Sit up a little higher; it’s safer.”

> “跟你在一起，没法安全。”阿初说。
“There’s no safety to be had with you around,” A-Chu said.

> 慕次笑起来，他知道阿初在责怪自己的莽撞，所谓的“专业人士”判断出现了严重偏差和失误。
Muci laughed. He knew A-Chu was blaming him for his recklessness, that the so-called “professional” had made a grave error of judgment.

> “还笑。”阿初嗔怪了一句。“身上有伤吗？”
“Still laughing.” A-Chu chided him. “Are you injured anywhere?”

> “旧伤口，有点撕裂的疼。”
“An old wound, a bit of a tearing pain.”

> “要紧吗？”
“Is it serious?”

> “不要紧，你呢？”
“Nothing serious. And you?”

> “我没事。”阿初说。“怪了，这么高掉下来，居然没受伤。”
“I’m fine,” A-Chu said. “Strange, a fall from such a height, and yet not a hurt on me.”

> “其实高度并不高，主要是黑暗，黑暗令人恐惧。”
“The height wasn’t really so great. It was the dark, mostly; the dark is what frightens.”

> “你怎么确定高度？”
“How did you gauge the height?”

> “声音。当时地下的所发出的声音，那歌声。她离我们很近。”
“By sound. The sound that came from underground just then, that singing. She was very close to us.”

> “你还认为那个鬼在这里？”
“You still think that ghost is down here?”

> “是啊。我不否认？”
“I do. I won’t deny it.”

> “那么，上面发生的爆炸是怎么回事？”
“Then what was the explosion up above?”

> “上面？上面还有一个鬼！”慕次下结论。“上面的鬼和下面的鬼，没有直接联系，所以，上面的鬼封死了出口，把下面的鬼和我们一起置于死地。”
“Up above? Up above there’s another ghost!” Muci concluded. “The ghost above and the ghost below have no direct link, so the ghost above sealed the way out and consigned the ghost below, and us with her, to death.”

> “那下面的鬼呢？”
“And the ghost below?”

> “走啦。”
“Gone.”

> “走？”阿初四面望望，四面全是石壁。“往哪里走？”
“Gone?” A-Chu looked all around; on every side were walls of stone. “Gone where?”

> “从何处来，往何处去。”阿次合掌做参禅状。
“Whence one comes, thither one goes.” A-Ci pressed his palms together in a posture of Zen meditation.

> “施主何处来？”阿初问。
“Whence comes the patron?” A-Chu asked.

> “来处来？”
“From the place of coming.”

> “何处去？”
“And whither goes he?”

> “去处去。”
“To the place of going.”

> “十二时如何行走？”
“How does one walk through the twelve hours?”

> 慕次模仿坐禅开悟，答：“小弟是步步踏着。”
Muci mimicked a monk awakening in meditation and answered, “This humble brother treads with every step.”

> 阿初被他假模假势的样子逗乐了，止不住“噗哧”一声，笑出来。
A-Chu was so tickled by his mock-solemn airs that he could not help bursting out with a “pfft” of laughter.

> “这就对了，笑笑多好。”阿次坐直身，跟阿初靠得更近。
“That’s more like it. How good a little laughter is.” A-Ci sat up straight and drew closer to A-Chu.

> “我在荣家长大成人，所有的上下规矩，将我死死地扼制在封建大家庭的制度下。我从来没有大声笑过，或者放肆地哭过。一直都是小心翼翼地做人。”
“I grew to manhood in the Rong household, where all the rules of high and low bound me fast within the order of a feudal great family. I never once laughed aloud, or wept without restraint. Always I lived carefully, warily.”

> “一直？持续了多久？”慕次问。
“Always? For how long did that last?” Muci asked.

> “出国以后吧，阅历丰富了，开了眼界。在英国的时候，我有一段很开心的日子，恣情地享受人生的快乐。”
“Until I went abroad, I suppose; my experience widened, my eyes were opened. In England I had a spell of very happy days, giving myself up freely to the pleasures of life.”

> “有过爱情吗？”
“Was there ever love?”

> “有过，流星般的爱情。”阿初说到“流星”时，眼角挂着温馨地暖意。
“There was, a love like a shooting star.” As A-Chu said “shooting star,” a tender warmth hung at the corners of his eyes.

> “现在呢？”
“And now?”

> “有女人。”
“There is a woman.”

> “仅此而已？”
“And that is all?”

> “仅此而已。”
“That is all.”

> “我为嫂夫人感到难过。”慕次从口袋里掏出打火机，打燃火苗，从石头上站起来，仰望四周和坍塌的洞口。他发现了墙体夹缝间斜插着一个废弃的松油火把，他顺着碎石走过去，点燃火把，地下道有了光明。
“I feel sorry for your good lady.” Muci drew a lighter from his pocket, struck a flame, and rose from the rock, looking up all around and at the caved-in mouth of the hollow. He spotted a discarded pine-oil torch wedged aslant in a crack of the wall; picking his way over the rubble, he lit the torch, and light came into the underground passage.

> “你童年生活怎样？”阿初问。
“What was your childhood like?” A-Chu asked.

> “很压抑。”阿次说。他开始敲击墙体。
“Very stifled,” A-Ci said. He began to tap at the wall.

> “是吗？”阿初神情很奇怪地看着他。“你的性格并不是很反叛啊？而且个性也并不张扬。”
“Is that so?” A-Chu looked at him with a strange expression. “Your nature isn’t so very rebellious, though, and you don’t make a show of your character either.”

> “也不见得，我上中学的时候，盛气凌人，锋芒毕露。老师和同学都不太喜欢我。”
“Not necessarily. In my middle-school days I was overbearing, all my edges showing. Neither teachers nor schoolmates cared much for me.”

> “大学生活呢？”
“And your university years?”

> “很美好。”慕次微笑，笑意很深沉。“在你眼里，我是怎样一个人？”
“Very fine.” Muci smiled, a deep smile. “In your eyes, what kind of man am I?”

> “至柔至刚。”阿初下了极好的评语。
“Utterly soft and utterly hard at once.” A-Chu gave him the highest of judgments.

> “这四个字，像是评价你，而不是我。”慕次一副不敢当的面孔。
“Those four words sound more like a verdict on you than on me,” Muci said, with a look of not daring to accept them.

> “至刚易折。”阿初说。“我是一个很有韧性的人。”
“What is utterly hard breaks easily,” A-Chu said. “I am a man of great pliancy.”

> 墙体很牢固，慕次重新坐下来。
The wall was solid; Muci sat down again.

> “有一个问题，一直很想问问你。”阿初说。
“There is a question I have long wanted to ask you,” A-Chu said.

> “请讲。”
“Please, ask it.”

> “你和你现在的父亲感情怎么样？”
“How do you feel toward your present father?”

> “不错。”
“Well enough.”

> “不错？不错是什么意思？好？还是不好？”
“Well enough? What does ‘well enough’ mean? Good? Or not good?”

> “好。”
“Good.”

> “你住院的时候，他表现如何？”
“When you were in the hospital, how did he behave?”

> “他坐在我床头哭，哭得很伤心。”
“He sat at my bedside and wept, wept bitterly.”

> “鳄鱼的眼泪。”
“Crocodile tears.”

> “也不尽然，我们也是二十几年的父子了。”
“Not entirely. We have been father and son these twenty-odd years.”

> “你爱他吗？”
“Do you love him?”

> “爱。”慕次回答地毫不犹豫，这让阿初非常失望。
“I do.” Muci answered without hesitation, which disappointed A-Chu greatly.

> “你爱一个杀死了你父亲的人？爱一个杀父仇人？你不觉得你的回答非常可悲吗？”
“You love the man who killed your father? You love the enemy who slew your own father? Do you not find your answer utterly pitiable?”

> “正确地说，应该是很矛盾。”慕次低下头。“你口中的父亲，我很生疏，而他在我的心目中是一位慈父。”
“To put it exactly, it is deeply contradictory.” Muci lowered his head. “The father you speak of is a stranger to me, while in my heart the other is a loving father.”

> “一个凶手！”
“A murderer!”

> “你爱荣家的四太太吗？”慕次反问。
“Do you love the Fourth Madam of the Rong family?” Muci countered.

> “爱。”
“I do.”

> “她养你的目的，也是想利用你。”
“She raised you meaning, too, to make use of you.”

> “我知道。”
“I know it.”

> “你知道，你还爱，你跟我不是一样矛盾吗？”
“You know, and still you love; are you not caught in the same contradiction as I?”

> “我跟你不一样！”阿初站起来。
“I am not the same as you!” A-Chu stood up.

> “哪点不一样？”
“In what way not the same?”

> “本质不一样！四太太养育的是仇人的孩子！杨羽桦却亲手杀死了自己的哥哥！”阿初激动地说。“姐姐本性善良，以至于对仇人的孩子也无法施展仇恨，最终放弃了复仇。”
“Not the same in essence! The Fourth Madam raised the child of her enemy; but Yang Yuhua killed his own elder brother with his own hands!” A-Chu said with agitation. “My sister was good by nature, so much so that she could not bring herself to wreak her hatred even on the enemy’s child, and in the end she gave up her revenge.”

> “你能保证四太太没有欺骗你吗？你所有的推测，本身就来自她半真半假的谎言。常言说得好：假作真时真亦假。”
“Can you swear the Fourth Madam has not deceived you? All your conjectures rest on her half-true, half-false lies. As the old saying has it: when the false is taken for the true, the true becomes false as well.”

> “你怀疑她？”
“You doubt her?”

> “我怀疑一切。”
“I doubt everything.”

> “那你也怀疑我？！”
“Then you doubt me too?”

> 慕次不说话了，因为他知道阿初的情绪开始焦灼。
Muci said no more, for he knew A-Chu’s feelings were beginning to fret and burn.

> “关于这个问题，我们出去后再讨论吧。”慕次取下火把，示意阿初跟他走。
“Let us discuss that question once we’re out of here,” Muci said, taking up the torch and signalling A-Chu to follow him.

> 阿初余怒未息。
A-Chu’s anger had not yet subsided.

> 慕次跟他也接触过一段时间，知道他脾性了，过去拉他。“走啦，要打，要骂，出去再说。”慕次以柔克刚地把阿初拽住了。
Muci had been in his company a while now and knew his temper; he went over and took hold of him. “Come on; if you want to hit me or curse me, save it for outside.” With softness overcoming hardness, Muci pulled A-Chu along.

> “放手。”阿初口气软下来。“走不稳，两个人一起滚下去。”
“Let go.” A-Chu’s tone softened. “If you lose your footing, we’ll both roll down together.”

> “那才好呢。”阿次笑着说。“有缘共死，不枉同生。”这一句话巧妙地将阿初的心再次拉拢。
“So much the better,” A-Ci said, smiling. “To die together is a bond well met; it would not wrong the life we shared.” That single line drew A-Chu’s heart to him once again.

> 两人漫步踏道，沿着幽暗的地道缓行，不多久，他们发现一条及其狭窄的入口，慕次走过去，用手触摸入口处的青苔，很干净，没有长年淤积的绿泥。
The two walked the path, moving slowly along the gloomy tunnel, and before long they found an extremely narrow opening. Muci went over and touched the moss at its mouth: it was clean, with none of the green sludge of long years’ silting.

> “就是这里，有人时常进出过。”慕次说。他把火把递给阿初，自己准备先进去探路。
“This is the place; someone has passed in and out often,” Muci said. He handed the torch to A-Chu and made ready to go in first and scout the way.

> “嗳，小心点。”阿初说。
“Hey, be careful,” A-Chu said.

> “放心。”慕次攀缘而上，进入到狭小的空间，他尽量蜷缩身体，向前爬行，他越往里前行，感觉脊背上的凉气越渗，甚至呼吸都感觉困难，他的身体被潮湿和黑暗所包围，等他爬到尽头时，他发现出口竟是一堆青砖，显然，这是慈云寺大殿的某一个不起眼的角落。他正要有进一步的动作，没有任何征兆前，顶上突然有碎裂的青砖落下，慕次赶紧用手背护住头和脊椎，砖头砸在他手背上，他立即做出了“撤退”的决定。
“Don’t worry.” Muci climbed up and entered the cramped space, curling his body as small as he could and crawling forward; the deeper he went, the more the cold seeped into his spine, until even breathing came hard, his body wrapped round in damp and darkness. When he reached the far end, he found that the exit was a heap of grey bricks, plainly some out-of-the-way corner of the Ciyun Temple’s great hall. He was about to go further when, without the least warning, shattered bricks came falling from above; Muci hastily shielded his head and spine with the back of his hand, the bricks struck it, and he decided at once to “retreat.”

> 慕次从入口处下来，异常狼狈，血迹污了衣领，阿初很紧张，扶了他一把，问：“怎么了？”
Muci came back down from the opening in a wretched state, blood staining his collar. A-Chu, tense, steadied him with a hand and asked, “What happened?”

> “这里一定有两个空间。”慕次说。
“There must be two spaces here,” Muci said.

> “什么？”
“What?”

> “两个通道口，一实一隐，我们需要找到那个隐蔽的出口。”
“Two passage-mouths, one real and one hidden. We need to find the hidden way out.”

> “你的意思是，这个入口，出不去了？”
“You mean this opening is no way out?”

> “对。这个洞口被废墟淹没了，我们没有这个力量去掀开通往自由的门。”
“Right. This mouth is buried under the rubble; we haven’t the strength to heave open the door to freedom.”

> “那么，另一个出口在哪里呢？”阿初目光呆滞，自言自语。“让我想一想。”
“Then where is the other way out?” A-Chu’s gaze was dull; he spoke as if to himself. “Let me think.”

> “你说什么？”慕次很诧异。“你的意思是？你曾经……来过？”
“What did you say?” Muci was startled. “You mean, you have been here… before?”

> “我觉得自己脑子有问题。”阿初面色苍白地说。“你上去以后，我就开始祷告，向上帝祈祷，向上帝忏悔。可是，我闭上眼以后，我的头很疼。我每次摔跤以后，或是跌落，我都会产生幻觉……”
“I think there’s something wrong with my mind.” A-Chu spoke, his face pale. “After you climbed up, I began to pray, to pray to God, to confess to God. But once I closed my eyes, my head ached terribly. Every time I’ve taken a fall, or dropped from a height, I have hallucinations…”

> 幻觉？慕次凌乱不堪的思路一下触到了兴奋点。
Hallucinations? Muci’s tangled train of thought struck all at once on a point of excitement.

> “不要抗拒，你感觉到什么？说出来。”
“Don’t resist it. What do you feel? Say it aloud.”

> “那恐怖的铁锹声，还有黑屋子，黝黑弯曲的道路，那里面有灯，有床，有一个女人……”
“That terrible sound of an iron spade, and a dark room, a black winding passage; inside there is a lamp, a bed, a woman…”

> “还看见什么？继续，继续想，不要停。”慕次忽然从阿初迷惘的眼神里看见了揭开谜底的希望。
“What else do you see? Go on, keep thinking, don’t stop.” In A-Chu’s dazed eyes Muci had suddenly glimpsed the hope of lifting the riddle’s veil.

> “看见，看见有吃的东西。”
“I see, I see there’s something to eat.”

> “什么？”慕次继续追。
“What?” Muci pressed on.

> “海蜇、有鱼……酒。”
“Jellyfish, and fish… wine.”

> “门，门在哪里？”
“The door, where is the door?”

> “在里面。”
“Inside.”

> “在哪里？”因为慕次站的方位本身已没有退路了。“你指给我看。”
“Where?” for the very spot where Muci stood had no way back. “Point it out to me.”

> 阿初抬起手指向慕次的脸，慕次下意识地回头看，背后是坚固的石壁，他贴着墙走过去，脚下踩着了一些亮晶晶的碎渣子，他正欲俯下身去，阿初突然喊了一句：“是镜子！镜子很宽、很亮。”
A-Chu raised a finger and pointed at Muci’s face. Muci instinctively turned to look behind him: there was only solid rock wall. He walked along it, and underfoot he trod on some glittering fragments; he was just bending to look when A-Chu suddenly cried out, “It’s a mirror! A mirror, very wide, very bright.”

> “镜子？”慕次指了指墙壁。“如果你从镜子里看见门，那么门的方向应该在……”他的手指向阿初的脸。
“A mirror?” Muci pointed at the wall. “If you saw the door in the mirror, then the door should lie in the direction of…” His finger pointed at A-Chu’s face.

> “我不知道。”阿初说。
“I don’t know,” A-Chu said.

> “不着急。”慕次倒走几步，以镜子悬挂为中心视线，退到阿初背后的墙角。阿初没有回头，他整个身子陷入记忆的沼泽。他很难受。
“No hurry.” Muci walked backward a few steps, taking the hung mirror as the centre of his sightline, and drew back to the corner of the wall behind A-Chu. A-Chu did not turn his head; his whole body was sunk in the swamp of memory. He was in great distress.

> “你怕吗？”慕次继续问，因为他怕阿初记忆的锁链突然中断。
“Are you afraid?” Muci went on asking, for he feared the chain of A-Chu’s memory might suddenly break.

> “怕得要命。”
“Frightened to death.”

> “你感受到恐惧？”
“You feel the terror?”

> “是，被幽闭，很恐怖的幽闭。”
“Yes, shut in, a terrible shutting-in.”

> “你看见自己有多大？”
“How old do you see yourself as being?”

> “很小，三、四岁左右，不，四、五岁，不太清楚。”
“Very small, three or four, or thereabouts; no, four or five; I can’t quite tell.”

> “你身边有人？”
“Is there someone beside you?”

> “是，一个女人。”
“Yes, a woman.”

> “她在干什么？确切地说，那女人在干什么？”
“What is she doing? To be exact, what is that woman doing?”

> “给我吃药。”
“Giving me medicine.”

> “看得见她的脸吗？”
“Can you see her face?”

> “看不见。”阿初很沮丧。
“No.” A-Chu was dejected.

> “你再想想，她身上有什么东西？她身上一定会有某种特别的东西，你想想。”
“Think again, what is she wearing, what is on her? There must be something particular about her; think.”

> “带子！”
“A sash!”

> “什么？”
“What?”

> “有一根带子，很特别。”
“There’s a sash, a very unusual one.”

> “颜色？什么颜色？”
“The colour? What colour?”

> “青红二色，筒状。”
“Blue and red, two colours, tube-shaped.”

> “有花纹吗？”
“Is there a pattern?”

> “看不见。很艳丽。”
“I can’t see. Very bright and gaudy.”

> “名古屋带！”慕次的脑海里跳动起了这种日本桃山时代，女性常用的色彩艳丽的和服腰带。“还有什么？”
“A Nagoya obi!” Into Muci’s mind leapt the image of that brilliantly coloured kimono sash, worn by women of Japan’s Momoyama age. “What else?”

> “看不见了。”无情的记忆在挤压阿初的神经。
“I can’t see anymore.” The pitiless memory was pressing on A-Chu’s nerves.

> “再想想！”
“Think again!”

> “你不要逼我！”阿初无法忍受了。一瞬间，幻觉像旋风般消失了。阿初的身体瘫软下来，慕次抱住他。
“Don’t force me!” A-Chu could bear no more. In an instant the vision vanished like a whirlwind. A-Chu’s body went limp, and Muci caught him in his arms.

> “好了，没事了。”慕次低低地安慰。“没事了。”
“There, it’s all right now.” Muci comforted him low. “It’s all right.”

> “我想我患了妄想症。”阿初说。
“I think I’ve a delusional sickness,” A-Chu said.

> “没事的，你很正常。”慕次扶阿初坐定，他感到阿初的身体在湿润的风中战栗，他脱下外套，又迟疑了一下，因为外套湿漉漉的，他索性把贴身的棉背心脱了，给阿初穿上。自己再穿上那湿漉漉的外套。
“It’s nothing; you are perfectly normal.” Muci settled A-Chu down and felt his body shivering in the damp wind. He took off his coat, then hesitated, for the coat was soaked through; so he simply pulled off the cotton singlet next to his own skin and put it on A-Chu, and drew the sodden coat back over himself.

> 风怎么会如此湿润呢？甚至带着一点新鲜的泥土味。
How could the wind be so moist? It even carried a faint smell of fresh earth.

> 慕次检查过坚固的墙壁后，没有发现一丝的破绽，没有空心砖的踪影，他又重新回到了起点。
Having examined the solid wall, Muci found not the least flaw, no trace of any hollow brick, and he came back once more to where he had started.

> 门在哪里？
Where was the door?

> 他的手上捏着粉碎的玻璃碴，这些碎渣子，不是玻璃镜片，而是水晶制作的饰品，也许是女人头上戴的水晶珠花。那么阿初所说的，宽而亮的镜子在何处呢？
In his hand he held the crushed glass shards; these fragments were no mirror-glass, but ornaments made of crystal, perhaps a woman’s crystal hair-flower. Then where was the wide, bright mirror A-Chu had spoken of?

> 慕次的眼睛从岩石上，回顾到水潭底。
Muci’s eyes went from the rocks back to the bottom of the water-pool.

> 奇怪的事情发生了。
A strange thing happened.

> 水珠漾起了波纹，水面溅显花蕾，水是活的！静静的深水潭，粼粼涟漪，水底流淌着一条通往新生的门。
The water rippled into little waves; buds of spray broke on its surface; the water was alive! The still, deep pool shimmered with ripples, and along its bed there flowed a door leading to a new life.

> 慕次站起来，因潮湿和寒冷，他打了一个冷战。但是，他的心不冷了。
Muci stood up and, in the damp and cold, gave a shiver. But his heart was no longer cold.

> 镜子，阿初口中的镜子，不在石壁上，他应该指的是水！二十年前的水潭，也许是宽而晶莹透明的。
The mirror, the mirror A-Chu spoke of, was not on the stone wall; what he must have meant was the water! Twenty years ago the pool may well have been wide and crystal-clear.

> 慕次俯身就水，试了试水温，水温冰凉，表面浮有碎雪渣。
Muci bent to the water and tested its temperature: icy cold, with broken shards of snow floating on the surface.

> “你发现什么了？”阿初关心地问。
“What have you found?” A-Chu asked with concern.

> “镜子。”慕次回眸淡淡一笑。
“The mirror.” Muci glanced back with a faint smile.

> “镜子？”虚弱的阿初，神情依旧很恍惚。“什么镜子？”
“The mirror?” The weakened A-Chu still looked dazed. “What mirror?”

> “等一下告诉你。”阿次脱掉皮鞋和外套。
“I’ll tell you in a moment.” A-Ci took off his leather shoes and coat.

> “你干什么？”
“What are you doing?”

> “我去探探路。”
“I’ll go and scout the way.”

> “你知道哪里水深水浅？”
“Do you know where the water’s deep and where it’s shallow?”

> “凭感觉吧。”慕次说。
“By feel, I suppose,” Muci said.

> “你是专业人士，你应该下判断，而不是凭感觉。”
“You are the professional; you should make a judgment, not go by feel.”

> “你是权威人士，你曾经从这里走出去。”慕次说。“是你的幻觉，引发了我的直觉。相信我，没事的。”慕次潜水而下，他的脚踩到了水草，水下静谧而又安宁，飘过一个岔口，他发现了水下的岩石洞口，岩石洞是天然的，洞里堆积的石块阻塞了水流的前行，成功的分流而下，洞里应该没有积水。他爬上岩石洞的天然石阶后，发现了血迹……
“You are the authority; you once walked out of this place,” Muci said. “It was your vision that set off my intuition. Trust me, it will be all right.” Muci dived down; his feet touched the water-weeds. Below the surface all was silent and at peace. He drifted past a fork and found the underwater mouth of a rock cave; the cave was natural, and the stones piled within had blocked the water’s forward flow, splitting it off and sending it down, so that inside there should be no standing water. Climbing the cave’s natural stone steps, he found bloodstains…

> 他看见了微弱的光亮和一扇开启的木门。
He saw a faint light, and a wooden door standing open.

> 慕次相信自己找到了真正的出口。
Muci believed he had found the true way out.

> 他深呼吸一次，两次，心态平和，石阶上的点点血迹，滴滴答答地引领着慕次走向木门，木门的把手上有一个清晰的血手印。血是腥的，证明有人刚刚路过。
He drew a deep breath, once, twice, his mind calm; the drops of blood on the stone steps led him, drip by drip, toward the wooden door, on whose handle was a clear bloody handprint. The blood was raw and fresh, proof that someone had just passed by.

> 慕次想，深不见底的谜底就要被揭开了。
Muci thought: the riddle, deep beyond all sounding, was about to be laid bare.

> 自信敢于决疑。
Confidence dares to resolve doubt.

> 慕次不急不缓地推开了门。
Neither hurried nor slow, Muci pushed the door open.

> 阿初坐在岩石上，看着慕次堆放在岩石上的外套和皮鞋，注视着水潭里不时泛起的浪花，他隐约感到内心的忧郁和恐惧，正无休止的在黑暗中放散，弥漫。
A-Chu sat on the rock, looking at the coat and leather shoes Muci had piled there, gazing at the spray that broke now and then on the pool, and he felt, dimly, his own inner melancholy and fear spreading and diffusing without end through the dark.

> 阿初一直很自信，他认为自己能够有效地控制自己的情绪，可是，此时此刻，他的心却向神灵祈祷，他感到神的威慑，他甚至想到自己父母的亡魂应该出来救阿次，他第一次看到自己内心的懦弱，他怕失去阿次，也怕自己枉死在此！
A-Chu had always been self-assured, believing he could keep his emotions well in hand; yet at this moment his heart was praying to the spirits. He felt the awe of the divine; he even thought that the departed souls of his own parents ought to come forth and save A-Ci. For the first time he saw the cowardice within him. He feared to lose A-Ci, and feared to die here himself for nothing.

> 人间和冥界只有一步之遥。
Between the world of the living and the world of the dead lay but a single step.

> 水面激荡起数朵浪花，他看见阿次浮出水面。阿初的心一下踏实了。
The surface heaved up several sprays of foam, and he saw A-Ci break the water. A-Chu’s heart settled at once.

> “怎么样？”
“Well?”

> 慕次浑身是水地爬上来，他甩了甩湿润的头发，口里呼出白色的气，从腰间取下一个白色塑料包。
Muci climbed up dripping wet, shook out his soaked hair, breathed out white vapour, and took from his waist a white plastic bundle.

> “什么东西？”
“What is it?”

> “防水布。”慕次答。“特制的。给你用。”
“A waterproof sheet,” Muci answered. “Specially made. For you to use.”

> “我会游泳。”
“I can swim.”

> “我知道，底下太冷，你听我的，跟我来。”阿次言语简捷，语气却很有分量。
“I know. It’s too cold down there. Do as I say, come with me.” A-Ci’s words were terse, but his tone carried weight.

> 慕次把防水布拉开，像是一个透明的小睡袋，阿初在阿次的授意下，睡了进去。阿初没有跟慕次谦让，一切都仿佛事先演练过一样，阿初相信慕次有能力把自己顺利带出绝境。
Muci pulled the waterproof sheet open, like a small transparent sleeping-bag, and at A-Ci’s bidding A-Chu lay down inside it. A-Chu did not stand on ceremony with Muci; it was all as though rehearsed beforehand, and A-Chu trusted that Muci had the power to bring him safely out of this dead end.

> 慕次把自己的的皮鞋和外套，也塞进了防水布袋的下方，然后他涉水而下。慕次在水底全力托举着阿初，游向目的地-岩石洞口。
Muci tucked his own leather shoes and coat into the bottom of the waterproof bag as well, then waded down into the water. On the pool’s bed he bore A-Chu up with all his strength, swimming toward their goal, the mouth of the rock cave.

> 很快，他们到达了洞口的石阶。两个人爬上石阶后，慕次扶阿初小坐。
Soon they reached the stone steps at the cave mouth. When the two had climbed the steps, Muci helped A-Chu to sit a while.

> “我想，我也许找到了出口的捷径。”慕次说。
“I think I may have found a shortcut to the way out,” Muci said.

> “谢谢。”阿初在喘息。
“Thank you.” A-Chu was breathing hard.

> “谢谢逝去的亡灵吧。”慕次低头说。
“Thank the departed souls, rather,” Muci said, lowering his head.

> “亡灵？”阿初的神经敏感地颤动起来。“你发现什么了？”
“Souls of the dead?” A-Chu’s nerves quivered sensitively. “What have you found?”

> “可能，我发现了谜底。”慕次穿上皮鞋。
“It may be that I’ve found the answer to the riddle.” Muci put on his shoes.

> “在哪里？”
“Where?”

> “在木屋里。”慕次说。
“In the wooden room,” Muci said.

> 阿初站起来，很严肃。
A-Chu stood up, very grave.

> “你看见了什么？”
“What did you see?”

> “一副骸骨。”阿次说。
“A skeleton,” A-Ci said.

> 阿初沿着石阶前行，走到木门边，他清晰地看见了血手印，血很腥，味很重，他推开了木门，里面很窄，很冷。他走进去，一步一个寒颤，只觉得四周阴霾重重，鬼影绰绰，不似人间。
A-Chu went forward along the stone steps to the wooden door, and clearly saw the bloody handprint; the blood was raw, its smell heavy. He pushed the door open; inside it was narrow, and cold. He walked in, a shudder at every step, feeling only the thick gloom pressing on every side and the flitting of ghostly shadows, as though this were no place of the living.

> 逝去的光阴重现，黑色的帷幕撕裂开……
The vanished years came again; the black curtain tore open…

> 阿初看到有一张床，床头上挂着一件日本和服，大约是粉红色的，很喜气，虽然岁月的痕迹将和服的色彩磨灭，却依然有某种暧昧的欲念在和服上流动。仿佛冥冥中有人暗示，暗示这件衣服的主人，是一个日本女人。
A-Chu saw a bed, and at its head hung a Japanese kimono, pink, most likely, a festive colour; though the marks of the years had worn away its hues, still some ambiguous longing seemed to flow across it. As though, in the unseen, someone were hinting, hinting that the owner of this garment was a Japanese woman.

> 床下有一个被废弃的铁皮桶，桶里有一个空酒瓶。
Beneath the bed was a discarded tin pail, and in the pail an empty wine bottle.

> “是日本清酒。”慕次说。
“Japanese sake,” Muci said.

> 床上有一副凄凉的骸骨，孤零零地躺在冰冷的床上，阿初不知怎的，忽感一股分辨不清的莫名哀怨扑面而来，泪水夺眶而出。
On the bed lay a desolate skeleton, all alone on the cold bedstead; and A-Chu, he knew not how, felt of a sudden an indistinct, nameless grief rush upon him, and the tears burst from his eyes.

> 杨慕次不说话，他的心底大约描画出了二十年前的某个细节，他用手按住了阿初抖动不止的肩膀，说：“不要太难过。”
Yang Muci said nothing; in his heart he had roughly sketched out some detail of twenty years past. He laid a hand on A-Chu’s ceaselessly trembling shoulder and said, “Don’t grieve too much.”

> “你知道我为什么要难过？”阿初哽咽。
“Do you know why I grieve?” A-Chu choked on the words.

> “你猜测到了母亲遇害的真相。”
“You have guessed the truth of how our mother was killed.”

> “说来听听。”
“Let me hear it, then.”

> “这件和服想必就是母亲、母亲遇害时元凶所穿。一个居心叵测的日本女人，通过复杂的易容手术，悄悄来到上海。她蛰伏在慈云寺的地下室里，伺机而动。在这个阴暗、潮湿的洞穴里，她嫁给了她所爱的人。”
“This kimono must be the one worn by the culprit at the time Mother, Mother was murdered. A Japanese woman of hidden and sinister purpose came quietly to Shanghai through a complex surgery of disguise. She lay dormant in the cellar of the Ciyun Temple, biding her time. And in this dark, damp hollow she married the man she loved.”

> 阿初的头抬起来，显然，他从自己所了解的事件中，没有解读到这一段细节。
A-Chu raised his head; plainly, from the events as he knew them, he had never read out this particular detail.

> “这件和服是日本少女的花嫁服，做工精致，色彩艳丽，粉色樱花代表春天，振袖代表少女，花嫁新娘装是日本女性一生中最美丽的时刻。而她却把花嫁服丢弃在阴暗的洞穴里，她一定是在这里完成了她少女的心愿。她的情人却被她残忍地永远地留在了这里……”
“This kimono is a Japanese maiden’s bridal robe, finely wrought, brilliant in colour; the pink cherry blossoms stand for spring, the long swinging sleeves for maidenhood, and the bridal dress marks the most beautiful moment in a Japanese woman’s life. Yet she cast her bridal robe aside in this dark hollow; she must have fulfilled here the wish of her girlhood. But her lover she cruelly left here forever…”

> “你错了。留在这里的不是她的情人，而是我们的母亲，亲生母亲。”阿初情绪有些失控，他心中压抑、隐藏很久的痛楚骤然间引爆，悲苦之情一泻千里。“这副遗骨，是一名年轻的女性，她是被人用非人道的、极端残忍的杀人手段所杀害的！她是被虐杀的！她是被人腰斩的！这些变态的畜生！我要他们付出这一生最惨痛的代价！”
“You are wrong. The one left here is not her lover, but our mother, our own birth mother.” A-Chu’s feelings slipped a little out of his control; the anguish he had pressed down and hidden so long detonated all at once, and his grief poured out in an unstoppable flood. “These remains are of a young woman, killed by an inhuman, an utterly savage means of murder! She was tortured to death! She was cut in two at the waist! These perverse beasts! I will make them pay the most agonising price of their lives!”

> 阿初的瞳孔开始放大，几乎绽裂。
A-Chu’s pupils began to dilate, almost to bursting.

> 当慕次听到这副遗骨是一名年轻的女性，而且是被人惨无人道地杀害后，他的内心深深震动，无法平静，不管这女人是否是自己的生母，她都死得可怜、凄惨。
When Muci heard that these remains were a young woman, and one murdered with such savage inhumanity, he was shaken to the depths and could not grow calm; whether or not this woman was his own birth mother, she had died a pitiable and wretched death.

> “二十年前的某一个夜晚，母亲带我夜宿于慈云寺，有人密谋、策划好了一套谋杀计划，她们一定是扮作寺庙的女尼，诱骗母亲落入陷阱。然后，这个日本女人在这张肮脏的床上，与她心爱的男人云情雨意了一番，她告别了这个男人，去冒充另一个女人，进入这个女人的家庭，她剥下了母亲的衣服，从里到外，她脱下和服后，就彻底伪装起来，她穿上母亲的衣服，踏上归家的路，夺取这个女人所拥有的一切幸福人生。包括她的孩子、她的骨肉。而我们的母亲被他们残忍地杀害在这永不见天日的黑暗巢穴。这就是真相。”一直困扰在内心深处的谜团，得以霎时揭开。然而，阿初和阿次的心态再次向“怒”与“疑”之间互动、挣扎。
“One night twenty years ago, Mother took me to lodge overnight at the Ciyun Temple. Someone had plotted and laid a murder scheme complete; they must have disguised themselves as the temple’s nuns to lure Mother into the trap. Then this Japanese woman, on that filthy bed, took her fill of love with the man she cherished; she took her leave of him, went to impersonate another woman and enter that woman’s family, stripped Mother of her clothes from the skin outward, and, once she had shed the kimono, disguised herself completely, put on Mother’s clothes and set out on the road home, to seize all the happy life that woman possessed, her children and her own flesh and blood included. And our mother they cruelly murdered in this dark den that never sees the light of day. This is the truth.” The riddle that had troubled him deep within was, in that instant, laid bare. And yet the hearts of A-Chu and A-Ci moved and struggled once more between “rage” and “doubt.”

> “这只是臆断、猜测。”慕次说。“我们需要证据，更需要先从这里走出去。”
“This is only surmise, conjecture,” Muci said. “We need evidence, and more than that, we need first to get out of here.”

> 阿初冷笑。
A-Chu gave a cold laugh.

> 慕次知道，由于两个人的生活背景和成长环境相差太远，所以，他们面对过去的悲伤投影，不免会掺杂着自己的感情色彩。
Muci understood that, because the two of them came from backgrounds and upbringings so far apart, they could not, facing the sorrowful shadows of the past, help colouring them each with his own feeling.

> “她刚来过。”阿次把话题巧妙转移到“女鬼”身上。
“She was just here.” A-Ci deftly turned the subject to the “female ghost.”

> 阿初不说话。
A-Chu said nothing.

> 慕次继续说：“你觉不觉得这里空间很高，声音很空，房间的形态也很畸形。地板是木头的，为什么墙也是木头的呢？我们就像走进了一个烟囱。”
Muci went on, “Don’t you feel how high this space is, how hollow the sound, how misshapen the form of the room? The floor is wood; why should the walls be wood as well? It is as if we had walked into a chimney.”

> 忽然，慕次头顶感觉到了小水滴，他抬头望顶，顶高而黑。
Suddenly Muci felt small drops of water on the crown of his head; he looked up, the top was high, and black.

> “江南多雨啊。”阿初喃喃自语。
“It rains much in the south of the river,” A-Chu murmured to himself.

> 慕次恍然大悟。“原来如此。”慕次说：“怪不得，如此潮湿，却没有一丝霉味，空气很新鲜，知道了，花非花，雾非雾……鬼非鬼，树非树……”
Understanding broke over Muci all at once. “So that is it.” He said, “No wonder, so damp, and yet not a trace of mould; the air is fresh. Now I see it: the flower is no flower, the mist is no mist… the ghost is no ghost, the tree is no tree…”

> “想好怎么从树心里爬上去了？”阿初问。
“Have you worked out how to climb up through the heart of the tree?” A-Chu asked.

> “想好了，距离树干并不高，大约九米，徒手就能攀上去。我背你？”慕次提出建议。
“I have. It’s not far to the top of the trunk, about nine metres; I can climb it bare-handed. Shall I carry you on my back?” Muci proposed.

> “你行吗？”阿初仰望着密匝匝的奇特的枯树干。
“Are you able?” A-Chu looked up at the dense, strange, withered trunk.

> “你肯吗？”慕次眼睛里习惯地挑衅。
“Are you willing?” A note of habitual challenge was in Muci’s eyes.

> 阿初开始脱外套，慕次明白，阿初想减轻自己身体的重量，换而言之，阿初在为自己减轻负担。
A-Chu began to take off his coat; Muci understood, A-Chu wanted to lessen the weight of his own body, which was to say, to lighten Muci’s burden for him.

> “不用脱了，上面冷。”慕次说。“来吧。”
“No need to take it off; it’s cold up there,” Muci said. “Come.”

> 黑暗深处，慕次背着阿初开始徒手攀缘，阿初的气息不均匀地低喘，慕次隐约感到阿初有恐惧感。“不要往下看。”慕次温情地提示。
Deep in the dark, Muci, with A-Chu on his back, began to climb bare-handed. A-Chu’s breath came low and uneven, and Muci sensed dimly that A-Chu was afraid. “Don’t look down,” Muci prompted him tenderly.

> “你不要讲话。”阿初说。
“Don’t talk,” A-Chu said.

> 慕次低声笑笑，信任和真诚在彼此的患难中互相渗透到对方的心中。就在慕次接近树干的时候，他听到了树干的抖动声，这种抖动和风声无关。
Muci laughed low; trust and sincerity, in their shared adversity, seeped each into the other’s heart. Just as Muci neared the trunk, he heard the trembling of the wood, a trembling that had nothing to do with the wind.

> 他敏锐地嗅觉准确做出了判断，头顶上有人。
His keen sense made the judgment surely: there was someone overhead.

> 一支黑洞洞的枪口对准了慕次的头……
A black muzzle was levelled at Muci’s head…

> 杨慕次机械地抬起头，他看见了“母亲”接近扭曲的一张脸。
Yang Muci mechanically raised his head and saw the near-contorted face of “Mother.”

> 小山缨子笑起来，森然地笑起来……她的笑声远比她的哭声更可怖，活在地狱中的小山缨子重新闻到了她渴望闻到的血腥味。
Koyama Eiko laughed, a grim, chilling laugh… Her laughter was far more dreadful than her weeping, and Koyama Eiko, living in hell, smelled again the reek of blood she had so longed to smell.

> “阿次……”
“A-Ci…”

> “妈！”慕次的声音很恳切。但是，他已经将阿初转移到胸前。摸出腰际的铁钩，死死插入树皮深处。“妈，我是你带大的，你不能这样对我。”黑暗中，慕次的口气像是在哀求。
“Mother!” Muci’s voice was full of earnest feeling. But he had already shifted A-Chu round to his chest, and, feeling out the iron hook at his waist, drove it deep and fast into the bark. “Mother, you raised me; you cannot do this to me.” In the dark, Muci’s tone was almost a plea.

> 这两声“妈”，让小山缨子的手颤抖起来。“阿次，不要怪我啊，我是看着你长大的，我似乎情不自禁地喜欢过你，疼过你，我送你去日本留学，就是希望你能成为半个日本人。我这样疼爱你，你不珍惜，是你，是你自己来寻死路的。黄泉路上，不要怨我。”
Those two cries of “Mother” set Koyama Eiko’s hand trembling. “A-Ci, don’t blame me. I watched you grow up; it seems I could not help being fond of you, could not help doting on you. I sent you to study in Japan hoping you might become half a Japanese. I doted on you so, and you did not cherish it. It was you, you yourself, who came seeking the road to death. On the road to the Yellow Springs, do not hold it against me.”

> 在小山缨子说话的时候，阿次已经成功的让阿初紧紧地挂在铁钩上。
While Koyama Eiko was speaking, A-Ci had already managed to hang A-Chu fast on the iron hook.

> “妈！你疯啦！”慕次说。
“Mother! You’ve gone mad!” Muci said.

> “我不是你妈，你妈在下面。”
“I am not your mother. Your mother is down below.”

> “我不信！”阿次拖延时间，为自己脱困做准备。
“I don’t believe it!” A-Ci was playing for time, making ready to free himself.

> “你不信？你不信，你会骗我来？”小山缨子在喘。
“You don’t believe it? If you didn’t believe it, would you have tricked me into coming?” Koyama Eiko was panting.

> “我没有！”这一句理直气壮。
“I did not!” The words rang out, upright and unabashed.

> “你骗我来也就算了，你还想炸死我。”
“Tricking me into coming would be one thing, but you meant to blow me up as well.”

> “我差一点也被人炸死！”慕次抬头逼视“母亲”。“我差点被活埋了。”
“I was very nearly blown up myself!” Muci raised his head and stared “Mother” down. “I was very nearly buried alive.”

> “是你干的！我养了你二十年！”
“It was you who did it! I raised you for twenty years!”

> “你养了我二十年，你还拿枪对着我的头？！”
“You raised me for twenty years, and still you point a gun at my head?”

> “你想活是吧？”小山缨子阴冷地说。“我给你机会，你把那个人扔下去，你把他扔下去，我让你活。”
“You want to live, do you?” Koyama Eiko said icily. “I’ll give you a chance. Throw that man down, throw him down, and I’ll let you live.”

> “我要不肯呢？”
“And if I refuse?”

> “你去死吧。”小山缨子握紧了枪。
“Then go and die.” Koyama Eiko tightened her grip on the gun.

> “我死之前，要你告诉我，你到底是谁？”
“Before I die, you must tell me, who are you, really?”

> “我不会告诉你的。”
“I will not tell you.”

> “我求你告诉我！”
“I beg you to tell me!”

> “不要求她！”阿初怒吼。
“Don’t beg her!” A-Chu roared.

> “你看看，你想救的人，他利用你，他害你，他是一个魔鬼。你信任他，不然你怎么会背着他往上爬？你就跟你那该死的大哥一起去做鬼吧。”
“Look at you, this man you want to save, he uses you, he does you harm, he is a devil. You trust him; else why would you climb up with him on your back? Go and be a ghost, then, together with that cursed elder brother of yours.”

> “思桐！”慕次大叫。
“Sitong!” Muci shouted.

> 枪声响了。
The gun went off.
