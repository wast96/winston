### A Chronology of Chen Yangshan's Life
Chen Yangshan, originally named Cheng Yangshan, courtesy name Yingliu. Born on October 7, 1906, in Qianjiang village, Baiguan town, Shangyu county, Zhejiang. In the years of revolutionary struggle, being engaged in clandestine work, he used the cover names Chen Yingzhou, Chen Deqing, Gao Junshi, Lao Wang, Chen Zhongying, and Chen Mingjun, among others.
1913 (age 7)
Entered the Mingqiang Primary School in his home village.
1919 (age 13)
His family being poor, he left school and went to Wuhan to make his living, and through an introduction was taken on as an apprentice at a money-shop. When his three years' apprenticeship were up he became a clerk.
1923 (age 17)
He saw the February 7 great strike with his own eyes, and it shook his thinking. Reading the magazine China Youth stirred him, and he organized the Hankou Young Men's Self-Improvement Society to seek the truth of how to save the country and the people.
1924 (age 18)
In May he wrote to make contact with Yun Daiying. In July, at Yun Daiying's invitation and under his guidance, he made contact with the Party organization. In October, introduced by Liu Bowen, he joined the Chinese Socialist Youth League.
1925 (age 19)
In January, after a talk with Chen Tanqiu, secretary of the Party's Wuhan district committee, he was declared to have passed over into membership of the Chinese Communist Party. In February the secretary of the Youth League's Wuhan district committee, Lin Yunan, named him special commissioner of the League's Wuchang special branch. In May he was moved to the League's central leadership in Shanghai as a courier, and in that time took part, under Ren Bishi, in organizing the May Thirtieth movement. In July he was made acting secretary of the League's Wuhan district committee. In September, sent by the organization and under the cover name Chen Yingzhou, he set up the Kuomintang's Wuhan municipal headquarters in the guise of a Kuomintang member, serving as a standing committee member and head of its organization department. In November he was recalled to Shanghai to help Yun Daiying set up the Shanghai Communications Bureau of the Kuomintang Central Propaganda Department, serving as head of its distribution section.
1926 (age 20)
In February, assigned by the Jiangsu provincial committee, he took part in leading the strike of the printing workers of Shanghai's Zhabei district. In this period, entrusted by Wang Yifei, secretary-general of the Central Military Commission, he carried three bombs from Shanghai to the Central Military Commission in Wuhan. In the winter of the same year he was transferred to the Kuomintang's Zhejiang provincial headquarters in Ningbo to do united-front work.
1927 (age 21)
In March he was transferred to Hangzhou to build the Party's organization for Zhejiang province. In April Chiang Kai-shek launched the April 12 counter-revolutionary coup; the White Terror in Hangzhou was too fierce for him to hold out in, and he returned to Shanghai to find the organization. Shanghai was then reordering its bodies, and he was assigned as propaganda chief of the Shanghai Fa'nan district committee. In November the Party Center charged him with shielding He Long. In the winter of the same year he was transferred to Wuhan for shop clerks' union work, but too many people there knew him for secret work in Wuhan to be safe. Back in Shanghai he could not for a time re-establish his organizational contact, and having nowhere to live had to return to his home in Shangyu, where in this period his parents arranged his marriage to Zhang Suzhen.
1928 (age 22)
In January he organized a peasant uprising in Haimen county, Zhejiang; betrayed by an informer, he was hunted by soldiers and police, and with Zhang Suzhen's help escaped into hiding and made his way back to Shanghai. Unable for a time to re-establish his organizational contact, he lodged for a while at the house of the Kuomintang member Bao Junfu. In April, for having recommended Bao Junfu, he was moved to the intelligence section of the Central Special Branch, and under Zhou Enlai took part in building the Central Special Branch.
1931 (age 25)
In June, sent by the Party Center, he went with Chen Geng to Tianjin to look into the doings of traitors and to rescue arrested comrades of our Party. In September, the Center's task done, he returned to Shanghai and stayed on in the Central Special Branch.
1935 (age 29)
In September the Central Special Branch was wound up, and the Shanghai Provisional Central Bureau sent him to Chongqing and Chengdu in Sichuan to open up new intelligence ground. With Chen Chang and Chen Kehan he ran the New Sichuan News Agency in Chongqing as a cover for intelligence and united-front work, and helped lead the anti-Japanese national-salvation movement in Chongqing and Chengdu.
1936 (age 30)
In July he was summoned from Chongqing to Xi'an for united-front work, taking part in liaison with the Northwest Army. After the Xi'an Incident broke out in December, on Zhou Enlai's instructions he set up the Xi'an branch of the Red China News Agency, and when that task was done was moved to the Xi'an intelligence station.
1940 (age 34)
In June he was summoned back to the Central Department of Social Affairs in Yan'an, serving as chief of its local-work section and later as head of the intelligence-cadre training course.
1941 (age 35)
In November, sent by Kang Sheng, head of the Central Department of Social Affairs, he set out with his wife Zhang Suzhen for Hong Kong to help Pan Hannian with intelligence work. But on reaching Baoji in Shaanxi he was held up by Kuomintang obstruction and was ordered to wait at the Eighth Route Army office in Xi'an.
1942 (age 36)
In February he was summoned back to the Central Department of Social Affairs in Yan'an. The Seventh Congress delegates were then gathered at the Central Party School for the Rectification movement, and as a Seventh Congress delegate himself Chen Yangshan was brought into the School's First Section. In October, when the Central Party School reorganized its structure, Chen Yangshan was recommended to serve as secretary of its Seventh Branch.
1943 (age 37)
In October, as the Rectification movement entered its cadre-screening phase, the Central Party School reorganized its leadership: Mao Zedong became principal, Peng Zhen deputy principal, Huang Huoqing secretary-general, and Zhang Xiushan dean of studies; Chen Yangshan was appointed chief of the School's cadre section, in charge of the political vetting of the Seventh Congress delegates. He was put into the senior-cadre study group discussing "The Question of the Two Lines."
1944 (age 38)
In December, on He Long's proposal, the Central Intelligence Department appointed Chen Yangshan director of the investigation bureau of the Jin-Sui Military Region. In January 1945 the Jin-Sui Military Region handed the investigation bureau over to the direct leadership of the Party's Jin-Sui Sub-bureau, and Chen Yangshan became director of the investigation bureau of the Jin-Sui Sub-bureau. In April he returned to Yan'an to attend the Party's Seventh National Congress.
1946 (age 40)
In January the investigation bureau of the Party's Jin-Sui Sub-bureau merged with the Jin-Sui Border Region public security bureau to form the Jin-Sui Border Region General Public Security Bureau, with Tan Zhengwen as director and Chen Yangshan as deputy director in charge of intelligence. In June 1947 Tan Zhengwen was transferred to central work, and Chen Yangshan became director.
1949 (age 43)
In March the Party Center appointed Chen Yangshan a member of the Party's Northwest Bureau and head of its social-affairs department, in charge of intelligence and of the takeover of cities in the Northwest. In May, when Xi'an was liberated, Chen Yangshan was named a member of the Xi'an Military Control Commission, head of its public security office, and director of the public security bureau of the Xi'an municipal people's government.
In September, on notice from the Center, he came to Beiping for the First National Conference on Public Security Work.
In November he was transferred to be a member of the Party's Shanghai municipal committee and deputy director of the Shanghai public security bureau.
1950 (age 44)
In November he was transferred to be a standing member of the Party's Nanjing municipal committee, deputy head of its political-legal commission, and director of the Nanjing public security bureau and chief procurator of the Nanjing procuratorate.
1952 (age 46)
In June he was transferred to be a member of the Party's North China Bureau, a member of the North China Administrative Commission, deputy head of its political-legal commission, and director of the North China public security bureau.
1954 (age 48)
In August he was transferred to be vice-minister of justice of the Central People's Government and deputy secretary of the ministry's Party group.
1956 (age 50)
In August he was elected a delegate to the Eighth National Congress of the Chinese Communist Party and attended the Congress.
1958 (age 52)
In June, at the Fourth National Conference on Political and Legal Work, under the influence of "Left" thinking, he was wrongly criticized and disciplined, stripped of his posts, demoted, and sent down to grass-roots work.
1960 (age 55)
In October, on Li Kenong's proposal, the Central Organization Department transferred Chen Yangshan to the Central Investigation Department, and Chen Yangshan returned once more to the hidden front, assigned to cadre-training work. In July 1961 Li Kenong, head of the Central Investigation Department, named Chen Yangshan head of the group compiling materials on the Central Special Branch, charged with investigating, gathering, sorting, and writing up the draft Historical Materials on the Central Special Branch of the Central Special Committee, and materials for Biographies of the Martyrs.
1962 (age 56)
In October the State Council appointed Chen Yangshan deputy president of the branch college of the College of Foreign Affairs (that is, the Institute of International Relations). At this time the Ministry of Justice Party group's "anti-Party clique" case was reviewed by the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection, and part of Chen Yangshan's punishment was rescinded.
1966 (age 60)
In February the Central Organization Department appointed Chen Yangshan a vice-chairman of the government of the Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region, sitting in on the regional Party committee's standing-committee meetings. Not long after he took up the post in Yinchuan, the "Great Cultural Revolution" began, and because Chen Yangshan had criticized the rebel factions for disrupting the normal work of Party and government organs, he was struggled against by the rebels and stripped of his post.
1967 (age 61)
In February the Center directed the Ningxia Military Region to form a Production Command to lead the Party and government work of the Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region, and named Chen Yangshan to join the Production Command as a veteran cadre. In October a preparatory group for the Revolutionary Committee of the Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region was set up, and Chen Yangshan was chosen as one of its members.
1968 (age 62)
In April the Revolutionary Committee of the Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region was established; Chen Yangshan was elected a standing member and before long was raised to deputy head.
In May Zhou Enlai brought Chen Yangshan to Beijing to handle a special case, screening Bao Junfu's historical record with Li Qiang. Early in 1970 Bao died of a cerebral hemorrhage. At this time Chen Yangshan was suffering from colon cancer, and after treatment in hospital was permitted, by Premier Zhou Enlai's leave, to convalesce in Beijing.
1972 (age 66)
In July Chen Yangshan, recovered, returned to work in Ningxia. The Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region Party committee was then established, and Chen Yangshan was elected a standing member, given charge of the work of the culture-and-education, science, health, and sports departments.
1975 (age 69)
In January he was chosen as a Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region deputy to the National People's Congress and attended its session.
1978 (age 72)
In February he was elected a Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region deputy to the National People's Congress; at the same time he was invited to attend the Fifth Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference and was elected a member of the standing committee of its Fifth National Committee. At this time the Ministry of Justice Party group's "anti-Party clique" case was reviewed and reversed by the Central Organization Department, the Ministry of Public Security, and the Supreme Court, and Chen Yangshan was transferred to be deputy procurator-general of the Supreme People's Procuratorate and a member of its Party group.
1980 (age 74)
In April a medical check-up found that Chen Yangshan had stomach cancer; after an operation in hospital he was left weak, and asked to resign as deputy procurator-general of the Supreme Procuratorate and retire from office. In 1982 the Center approved his resignation as deputy procurator-general and his retirement.
1985 (age 79)
In December Zhang Suzhen, Chen Yangshan's wife of fifty-eight years, died of illness. Chen Yangshan was then in hospital with lung cancer; as soon as he was a little better he left the hospital and organized and led the former intelligence and public-security cadres of the Jin-Sui Border Region in investigating, gathering, sorting, and compiling the Draft Historical Materials on Intelligence Work in the Jin-Sui Border Region. At the same time he set to writing up historical materials himself.
1987 (age 81)
In August, at the invitation of the Zhejiang provincial department of state security, he went to Hangzhou, Ningbo, and elsewhere to lecture on the history of intelligence.
1988 (age 82)
In May, entrusted by Liu Fuzhi, procurator-general of the Supreme People's Procuratorate, he went to Shandong on an inspection. On his return to Beijing he wrote an inspection report and reported to the Party group.
1989 (age 83)
In August his lung cancer suddenly worsened and he was hospitalized; in the end, the malignancy having spread, treatment failed, and he departed this life on February 22, 1991, at the age of eighty-five.
