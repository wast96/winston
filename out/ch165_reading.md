### Fake Calligraphy and Paintings

Your common petty hucksters who deal in forgeries have their ways of imitating the calligraphy and painting of the ancients. The colour of the paper, the vermilion seals and marks, the mounting and the format—all can be copied, so that seen from a little way off the thing is not a hair's breadth from a genuine work of the old masters. These they hawk about the market and pass off among the true, and the buyer whose eye is not keen is again and again taken in by them. Only when one meets a real connoisseur, an expert in bronze and stone, can the genuine be told from the false.

Calligraphy and painting are by nature things of refined and deep feeling; who would have thought that here too all manner of dark dealings lie hidden? Could the ancients know of it in the world below, they would surely sigh and grieve over it!

So too with the noted calligraphers and painters of our own day: there are those who copy their brushwork and their seals and pass the copies off in the market, and people with no eye for it are all deceived. To go and buy at a fan shop is the one way not to be gulled; the shop has a byword that runs, "genuine guaranteed, good not guaranteed"—meaning that the piece is truly by the master's hand, only that they will not vouch whether it is one of his good ones or his poor.
