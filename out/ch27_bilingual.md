## H2 Chapter 27. Treading Through Ice and Fire, the Ninefold Heaven

> 房间里的空气异常沉重，紧接着是垂死挣扎声混杂着蹬腿声和行刑者的喘气声，随着声音的消逝，房间里沉闷下来。
The air in the room was unusually heavy; then came the sounds of a dying struggle mingled with the drumming of kicking legs and the panting of the executioner, and as the sounds died away, the room sank into a dull hush.

> 阿初亲自确认了“铆钉”死亡，他的心里如释重负。
A-Chu confirmed the “Rivet’s” death with his own eyes, and a weight lifted from his heart.

> “得尽快处理尸体。”阿初说。
“The body must be dealt with as fast as possible,” A-Chu said.

> “这个容易。我来想办法。”韩正齐说。
“That’s easy. I’ll think of a way,” Han Zhengqi said.

> “其实，我知道不该在这里动手。是我过于急切，连累你了。”阿初言语诚恳。“谢谢你。”
“In truth, I know I shouldn’t have done it here. I was too much in haste, and I’ve drawn you into it,” A-Chu said with sincere feeling. “Thank you.”

> “先生？”韩正齐觉得自己现在真正的和阿初站在了同一条战线上。
“Sir?” Han Zhengqi felt that he now truly stood with A-Chu on the same battle-line.

> “不过，我坚信，一个杀死了手无寸铁的老妇人的人，决非善类。”阿初说。“他刚才说得对，有人知道他进来过。所以，他必须从这里走出去。”
“But I firmly believe that a man who could kill an unarmed old woman is no decent sort,” A-Chu said. “He was right just now: someone knows he came in here. So he must walk out of here.”

> “放心吧，先生，他会从这里走出去的。”韩正齐微笑着保证。
“Set your mind at ease, sir; he will walk out of here,” Han Zhengqi promised with a smile.

> 半个小时后，一个穿着“阿春”衣服，戴着旧毡帽的男人大摇大摆地领着“雪狼”走出了警察局的大门。
Half an hour later, a man in “A-Chun’s” clothes and an old felt hat swaggered out through the front gate of the Police Bureau, leading “Snow Wolf.”

> 当然，“阿春”从走出警察局的那一刻起，就无声无息地“人间蒸发”了。
And “A-Chun,” of course, from the moment he walked out of the Police Bureau, silently and without a trace “vanished from the face of the earth.”

> 他生命的痕迹像暮秋的残叶，被秋风扫得一干二净。
The traces of his life were like the withered leaves of late autumn, swept clean away by the autumn wind.

> 出了狱的“雪狼”很快和钟云迪取得联系。没过多久，侦缉处的人才发觉他们的“铆钉”失踪了，不仅如此，三天后，在黄浦江里，他们找到了白云的尸体。
“Snow Wolf,” out of prison, soon made contact with Zhong Yundi. Not long afterward the men of the Detective Division discovered that their “Rivet” had gone missing—and, three days later, they found Bai Yun’s body in the Huangpu.

> 李沁红的神经却异常敏感地推断出，中共特委的会议召开在即了，就在上海，就在租界，就在最为繁华的地段，就在自己的眼皮底下……
Li Qinhong, with her unusually keen nerves, deduced that the conference of the Communist special committees was about to convene—right here in Shanghai, right here in the Concession, right here in the busiest quarter of all, right here under her very eyes…

> 这一段时间里，阿初的心境大好，他一方面与雅淑保持亲密的交往，另一方面密切地关注棉纱市场的股指交易，他恢复了一个普通商人的正常生活，所有的天风海雨、惊心动魄都化做云烟飘散了。
Through this stretch of time A-Chu’s mood was excellent. On the one hand he kept up his intimate dealings with Yashu; on the other he watched the cotton-yarn market’s share dealings closely. He had resumed the ordinary life of a common merchant, and all the tempests of heaven and sea, all the heart-stopping perils, had dissolved into drifting cloud and smoke.

> 这天，汤少和夏跃春很早就来了，阿初陪他们聊天饮茶，岳嬷嬷做了一桌丰盛的午餐，让汤少吃得赞不绝口。
This day Young Tang and Xia Yuechun came very early; A-Chu kept them company chatting and drinking tea, and Amah A-Yue laid on a lavish luncheon that had Young Tang praising it without cease.

> 不过，细心的跃春发现岳嬷嬷用餐盘盛着番茄炒鸡蛋和酥肉果饼上了二楼。这两样菜是丛锋最爱吃的。
The careful Yuechun, however, noticed that Amah A-Yue carried up to the second floor a tray bearing tomato-fried eggs and crisp meat pastries. These two dishes were Cong Feng’s favorites.

> “叫他下来，一起吃吧。”跃春对阿初说。
“Call him down to eat with us,” Yuechun said to A-Chu.

> 阿初脸上的笑意泛起一丝不悦，不过，他假装什么也没听见。汤少正吃在兴头上，顾不上分析他们的神态。
The smile on A-Chu’s face gave way to a trace of displeasure; still, he pretended to have heard nothing. Young Tang was eating at the very height of his relish, and had no leisure to weigh their expressions.

> “跃春，你这个人什么都好，就是观察力太敏锐了。”阿初说。
“Yuechun, everything about you is fine—it’s only that your powers of observation are far too keen,” A-Chu said.

> “我想见见他。”跃春很平静。
“I want to see him,” Yuechun said, very calm.

> 阿初不说话了。
A-Chu fell silent.

> “就五分钟。”跃春坚持。
“Just five minutes,” Yuechun insisted.

> “见谁？想见谁？”汤少终于问话了。
“See who? Want to see who?” Young Tang finally spoke up.

> “想见……”跃春的目光在阿初和汤少的脸上回扫之后，说：“我想见见阿初的女人。”
“I want to see…” Yuechun’s gaze swept back and forth across the faces of A-Chu and Young Tang, and then he said, “I want to see A-Chu’s woman.”

> 阿初淡淡地一笑。“你怎么知道我有女人了？”
A-Chu smiled faintly. “How do you know I have a woman?”

> “凭直觉。”
“By instinct.”

> “阿初有女人了？”汤少来了兴趣。“什么样的女人？阿初看上的女人一定是个木头美人。”
“A-Chu has a woman?” Young Tang was all interest. “What sort of woman? A woman A-Chu fancies is bound to be a wooden beauty.”

> “那不一定。”夏跃春的眼光一直锁定在阿初的眉间眼底。“有的人表面很道学，其实骨子里透着轻浮。阿初，你说我说的话，对不对？”
“Not necessarily.” Xia Yuechun’s eyes stayed fixed on the space between A-Chu’s brows. “There are people who look every inch the moralist and are frivolous to the bone. A-Chu, tell me—am I right or not?”

> 阿初感觉到他话里辛辣的味道，低头说：“我想你误解我了。”
A-Chu caught the pungent flavor in his words and said, head bowed, “I think you’ve misunderstood me.”

> “跃春，你不必这样认真。”汤少出来解围了。“女人也分很多种。她们就像鲜花一样，早晚市价不同。”
“Yuechun, you needn’t take it so much to heart.” Young Tang came to the rescue. “Women are of many kinds, too. They’re like fresh flowers—the price differs from the morning market to the evening.”

> “是吗？”跃春口气很不善。
“Is that so?” Yuechun’s tone was far from friendly.

> “当然啦。打个比方，一朵牡丹养在深闺，富贵绝伦，男人要摘了它，得捧在心窝里供养着；可是，一旦它落在尘埃里，沾了灰，哪怕是一丁点尘迹，男人再把它捡起来，很可能就随意地插在西装口袋里，做了装饰品。柔媚的体态，花样的年华，可怜巴巴地成了取悦男人的资本……就像当年的和氏姐妹。”
“Of course. Take an example: a peony reared in the deep inner chambers, matchless in wealth and rank—if a man would pluck it, he must cradle it against his very heart and keep it enshrined. But once it falls into the dust and takes on a smudge of soil—even a speck—should a man pick it up again, very likely he’ll just tuck it carelessly into his suit pocket for an ornament. A tender, alluring form, years fresh as flowers, pitifully reduced to a stock-in-trade for pleasing men… like the He sisters in their day.”

> “怎么，你对和家两姐妹依然耿耿于怀？”阿初探问。
“What, are you still nursing a grudge over the two He sisters?” A-Chu probed.

> “岂止耿耿于怀，简直于心耿耿。”
“Not merely nursing a grudge—it goes to the very quick of my heart.”

> “你是不是还打算娶她？”阿初截住汤少的话。
“Do you still mean to marry her?” A-Chu cut across Young Tang’s words.

> “娶她？娶谁？”
“Marry her? Marry whom?”

> “和雅淑。”
“He Yashu.”

> “我疯啦！”汤少瞪眼。
“Am I mad?” Young Tang goggled.

> “那从今往后，在雅淑的事情上，请您保持缄默。可以吗？”
“Then from this day on, in the matter of Yashu, kindly hold your peace. Will you?”

> “凭什么？”汤少不服气。
“On what grounds?” Young Tang would not have it.

> “因为，我、要、娶、她！”阿初几乎是一个字一个字递送到汤少耳里的。
“Because I—am—going—to—marry—her!” A-Chu all but delivered the words to Young Tang’s ear one syllable at a time.

> 汤少手里的刀叉掉到盘子里。他尖叫：“你疯啦！”
The knife and fork in Young Tang’s hand clattered onto his plate. He shrieked, “You’re mad!”

> 阿初不说话，夏跃春依旧进餐，其实，阿初和跃春心里都明白，他们说的是丛锋，不得已话锋中途转道了。
A-Chu said nothing; Xia Yuechun went on with his meal. In truth, both A-Chu and Yuechun knew in their hearts that they had been speaking of Cong Feng, and had, for want of any other course, turned the drift of the talk aside midway.

> “什么时候的事？”汤少的好奇心又来了。
“When did this happen?” Young Tang’s curiosity was roused again.

> “关你什么事？”
“What business is it of yours?”

> “上床了？”汤少狡猾地笑起来。“道学家也要吟风弄月？”
“Been to bed?” Young Tang gave a sly laugh. “Even a moralist must sing to the breeze and toy with the moon?”

> “道学家也要吃饭。”阿初笑着堵了他一句。
“Even a moralist must eat,” A-Chu retorted with a laugh, stopping his mouth.

> “会娶她吗？”汤少想知道雅淑在爱情旅途上最后的结局。正在此时，岳嬷嬷慌慌张张地跑下来。
“Will you marry her?” Young Tang wanted to know the final outcome of Yashu’s journey through love. Just then Amah A-Yue came running down in a fluster.

> “先生，先生……”
“Sir, sir…”

> “怎么了？”阿初和跃春异口同声地问。
“What is it?” A-Chu and Yuechun asked with one voice.

> “那位先生，不见了……”
“That gentleman—he’s gone…”

> 夏跃春和阿初几乎是同时往楼上跑去，跃春的速度显然比阿初还要快捷，汤少一脸茫然，看着他们的背影。
Xia Yuechun and A-Chu bolted upstairs almost at the same instant; Yuechun was plainly the quicker of the two. Young Tang, his face all bewilderment, watched their backs recede.

> 楼上的房间里，床铺凌乱，窗户半开着，书桌上的咖啡，还有余温。
In the upstairs room the bedding lay in disorder, the window was half open, and the coffee on the desk was still faintly warm.

> “他没走远。”阿初说。
“He hasn’t gone far,” A-Chu said.

> “他会去哪？”跃春想。“去医院？”
“Where would he go?” Yuechun wondered. “The hospital?”

> “有可能。”阿初和跃春同步往楼下跑。“跃春你去医院，我去梅花巷，务必截住他。”
“Possibly.” A-Chu and Yuechun ran downstairs in step. “Yuechun, you go to the hospital; I’ll go to Plum Blossom Lane. We must cut him off at all costs.”

> “你为什么不早告诉我？”
“Why didn’t you tell me sooner?”

> “他就像颗定时炸弹，我不想再连累你担惊受怕。”
“He’s like a time bomb. I didn’t want to drag you into fresh dread and alarm.”

> “是炸弹，就一定会炸……你以为你藏得住？”
“If it’s a bomb, it’s bound to go off… did you think you could keep it hidden?”

> “什么事啊？”汤少还在吃，张着嘴问他们。
“What’s going on?” Young Tang was still eating, mouth agape, and asked them.

> “没你的事。”阿初一步不停地往外走。
“None of your business.” A-Chu walked out without breaking stride.

> 跃春紧跟着出去。
Yuechun followed close behind him out the door.

> 汤少继续吃，叫岳嬷嬷坐下来陪自己。
Young Tang went on eating, and told Amah A-Yue to sit down and keep him company.

> 春和医院，杨慕次的病房里，暖意浓浓。杨思桐和荣初双双来到慕次的病房，探望他。思桐在上海最昂贵的陈氏温室花房里购买了一束富贵牡丹。牡丹色泽艳丽，娇美无双。思桐把花插在宝蓝色的花瓶里，让花上的露自然地滴落，花香和水汽带给慕次新鲜的气息。
At the Chunhe Hospital, Yang Muci’s sickroom was full of warmth. Yang Sitong and Rong Chu came together to his room to visit him. Sitong had bought, at the costliest hothouse florist’s in Shanghai, the Chen family’s, a bunch of opulent peonies. The peonies were gorgeous in hue, tender and matchless in beauty. Sitong set the flowers in a sapphire-blue vase, letting the dew on them drip of itself, so that the fragrance of blossom and the vapor of water brought Muci a breath of freshness.

> 杨慕次坐起来，兄妹俩亲热地靠在一起。
Yang Muci sat up, and brother and sister leaned affectionately together.

> 思桐银铃般的笑声，驱除了慕次心底的寒气。他喜欢思桐，她调皮撒娇、任性胡闹，这个妹妹在哥哥面前，永远纯如白纸，晴朗如月。
Sitong’s silver-bell laughter drove the chill out of Muci’s heart. He was fond of Sitong; she was mischievous and coaxing, willful and full of madcap fun, and this younger sister was, before her elder brother, forever pure as white paper and bright as the clear moon.

> 荣初穿着价格不菲的黑色天鹅绒礼服，文质彬彬地站在慕次床前，他斯文、安静。不过，当慕次第一眼看到他时，心里就感到难以名状的不安。
Rong Chu, in a black velvet dress-suit of no small price, stood elegant and refined at the foot of Muci’s bed—cultured, quiet. Yet the moment Muci first laid eyes on him, he felt in his heart an unease he could not name.

> 他认识荣初，在去杭州的船上，他们曾经同船摆渡。
He knew Rong Chu: on the boat to Hangzhou, they had once crossed the water together.

> 荣家的小公子？英国留学生？那么？阿初又是谁呢？这个人这个时候走进思桐的情感世界，难道是偶然？
The young master of the Rong house? A returned student from England? Then—who was A-Chu? That this man should walk into Sitong’s world of feeling at this very moment—could it be mere chance?

> 尽管思桐介绍荣初的身份是英国华侨，可是慕次在荣初身上只闻见了大上海的市井气，丝毫没有欧洲的贵族气。
Though Sitong introduced Rong Chu as an overseas Chinese from England, Muci caught on Rong Chu only the market-street air of greater Shanghai, and not the least breath of European nobility.

> “喜欢我送的花吗？”杨思桐问。
“Do you like the flowers I brought you?” Yang Sitong asked.

> “你送的，我都喜欢。”慕次笑着说。他刻意回过头来看荣初，目光温煦地问：“你帮她选的？”
“Whatever you bring me, I like.” Muci smiled. He turned his head deliberately to look at Rong Chu, and asked, his eyes warm, “Did you help her choose them?”

> “哦，不是。”荣初腼腆地笑笑。“牡丹是富贵之花，锦屏人所爱。我生性淡泊，倒喜欢寒松翠竹。”
“Ah, no.” Rong Chu smiled shyly. “The peony is the flower of wealth and rank, beloved of those behind brocade screens. I am of a plain and quiet nature; I favor the cold pine and the green bamboo, rather.”

> “你错了。”杨思桐说。
“You’re wrong,” Yang Sitong said.

> 荣初愕然。“哪里错了？”
Rong Chu was startled. “Wrong where?”

> “牡丹虽是富贵之花，却也是天生傲骨。传说唐朝则天皇上曾于冬夜醉酒颁诏，命百花盛开。百花神主不敢违抗天子之令，于雪夜盛开春秋之花，唯有这牡丹不从圣命，抗拒天威，被则天皇帝发配洛阳，到了洛阳以后啊，牡丹盛开，千里花香，才留下这：洛阳牡丹甲天下的千古佳话。你说，这牡丹比及寒松翠竹来，有何逊色？”
“The peony may be the flower of wealth and rank, but it has a proud spine by nature. The tale goes that Emperor Zetian of the Tang, drunk one winter night, issued an edict commanding all the flowers to bloom. The gods of the hundred flowers dared not defy the Son of Heaven’s command, and on a snowy night set the flowers of spring and autumn blooming—all save this peony, which would not obey the sacred order but withstood the imperial might, and was banished to Luoyang by Emperor Zetian. And after it reached Luoyang, the peony bloomed, its fragrance stretching a thousand li, and left behind that everlasting saying: the peony of Luoyang is first under heaven. Tell me—set beside the cold pine and the green bamboo, wherein is the peony their inferior?”

> 慕次和荣初看她一脸认真的天真模样，都禁不住笑起来。“是我才疏学浅，实在是不堪小姐一驳。”荣初说。
Muci and Rong Chu, seeing the whole earnest, innocent look on her face, could not help laughing. “It’s my poor learning; I really am no match for the Young Miss in a debate,” Rong Chu said.

> “思桐啊，麻烦你请护士小姐把轮椅推来，我想去楼下晒晒太阳。”慕次说。
“Sitong, would you trouble the nurse to bring the wheelchair? I’d like to go downstairs and sun myself,” Muci said.

> “好啊，一会我推你。”思桐笑嘻嘻地去了。
“Of course—I’ll wheel you in a moment.” Sitong went off, all smiles.

> 病房里只剩下荣初和慕次。
Only Rong Chu and Muci were left in the room.

> “荣先生，还记得我们初次见面吗？”慕次神情忽然严肃起来。
“Mr. Rong, do you still remember our first meeting?” Muci’s face turned suddenly grave.

> “记得。在船上。”
“I remember. On the boat.”

> “当时，你的身份并不是什么英国华侨。”
“At the time, your identity was no overseas Chinese from England.”

> “当时，您告诉我，您是一名失业的职员。”荣初微笑地看着慕次。
“At the time, you told me you were an out-of-work clerk.” Rong Chu looked at Muci, smiling.

> “也许我们彼此都有彼此的秘密。不过，我要告诉你，你跟我妹妹交往，是要花很多钱的。”
“Perhaps we each have secrets of our own. But I’ll tell you this: to keep company with my sister will cost you a great deal of money.”

> “物有所值。”
“It’s worth the price.”

> “你倒一点也不避讳。”慕次说。“不过，我可以告诉你，我妹妹曾经交往过很多男朋友，不消三、两月，她就对他们厌倦了。所以，你也不必抱太大希望。”
“You don’t shrink from it in the least,” Muci said. “But I can tell you: my sister has kept company with a good many young men before, and in less than two or three months she wearies of them. So you needn’t pin too much hope on it.”

> “事事岂能尽如人意。不过，两、三个月的时间，足够了。”
“Not everything can go just as one wishes. Still—two or three months is quite enough.”

> “足够什么？”慕次眼底射出寒光。
“Enough for what?” A cold light shot from Muci’s eyes.

> “足够我这一生对'爱'的回忆。”
“Enough to be the memory of ‘love’ for the whole of my life.”

> 慕次从荣初的眼睛里读出了“复杂”的心绪。
Muci read in Rong Chu’s eyes a “complicated” cast of feeling.

> “我越看你，越觉得你像一个人。”
“The more I look at you, the more you seem to me like someone.”

> “如果我告诉你，我是荣华的弟弟，你信吗？”
“If I told you I was Ronghua’s younger brother, would you believe it?”

> 慕次摇头。“我倒觉得你跟我很……很相像。”
Muci shook his head. “I feel, rather, that you are very… very like me.”

> 荣初借助慕次的推断，说：“那么，如果我说，我是你的外甥，您信吗？”
Rong Chu, borrowing on Muci’s inference, said, “Then if I said I was your nephew, would you believe that?”

> “无论你是谁，如果你的所作所为伤害到了我的妹妹，我绝不饶你。”慕次说。“直觉告诉我，你被某些居心叵测的人所操纵。”
“Whoever you are: if your doings bring harm to my sister, I will never forgive you,” Muci said. “My instinct tells me you are being manipulated by certain people of hidden purpose.”

> “被血缘所操纵。”荣初补充了一句。“您忘了，我们是同一个血型。连枝连叶，打断骨头连着筋。”
“Manipulated by blood,” Rong Chu added. “You forget—we are of the same blood type. Branch joined to branch, leaf to leaf; break the bone and the sinews still hold.”

> 荣初优雅的气度，令阿次难以情测。
Rong Chu’s elegant bearing left A-Ci unable to read his feeling.

> 门被推开了，一束暖光刺入眼帘，慕次突然意识到了什么，有人在用望远镜窥视自己的一举一动。
The door was pushed open; a shaft of warm light stabbed into his eyes, and Muci suddenly sensed something: someone was spying on his every move through a telescope.

> 他异常警觉起来。
He grew unusually alert.

> 杨思桐和小护士推来轮椅，荣初帮忙扶他上去坐好。
Yang Sitong and a young nurse wheeled the chair up, and Rong Chu helped support him into it and settle him.

> 慕次看见思桐对荣初抱以甜美地微笑，感觉到妹妹的眼底荡漾着浓浓的爱意，他开始紧张了。
Muci saw Sitong give Rong Chu a sweet smile, and felt his sister’s eyes brimming with a deep, deep love; he began to grow anxious.

> 荣初亲自推了轮椅往外走，慕次低低地说：“我劝你一句话：回头是岸。”荣初笑盈盈地做俯首受教状，只在慕次耳边低声回答了一句：“小侄回长辈一句话：水到渠成。”
Rong Chu himself pushed the wheelchair toward the door; Muci said in a very low voice, “Let me give you one word of advice: turn back, and the shore is there.” Rong Chu, all smiles, made a show of bowing his head to receive instruction, and only murmured one sentence low by Muci’s ear: “Your humble nephew returns his elder one word: when the water comes, the channel forms of itself.”

> “你们嘀嘀咕咕地说什么呢？”思桐问。
“What are you two muttering about?” Sitong asked.

> 荣初直起腰来，说：“说你很可爱。”
Rong Chu straightened up and said, “I was saying how very lovely you are.”

> 杨思桐开心地笑起来，这让荣初感到了她的另一面，涉世不深。
Yang Sitong laughed happily, and this let Rong Chu feel another side of her: how little she knew of the world.

> 那束光若隐若现反射在金属轮椅的扶手上，慕次冷峻地朝窥视点看去，那是一幢靠医院住院部很近的楼房……
That shaft of light glinted, now seen, now gone, off the metal armrest of the wheelchair; Muci looked coldly toward the point it came from—a building very near the hospital’s in-patient block…

> “他发现我们了，处座。”俞晓江放下了望远镜。
“He’s spotted us, chief,” Yu Xiaojiang said, lowering the telescope.

> 杜旅宁没有讲话，他们已经在医院监视慕次三天了，据他自己估算，他早该发现他们了，不然，岂不是白做了自己的学生。
Du Luning did not speak. They had been keeping watch on Muci at the hospital for three days now; by his own reckoning, Muci should have discovered them long since—otherwise, would he not have taken his own teacher’s schooling in vain?

> “处座，我觉得阿次并没有什么值得怀疑的地方，倒是……”
“Chief, I don’t think there’s anything about A-Ci worth suspecting. Rather, it’s…”

> “说下去。”
“Go on.”

> “那位李沁红组长好像对阿次很有成见。”
“That Section-head Li Qinhong seems to bear A-Ci a real prejudice.”

> “是偏见。”
“It is a prejudice.”

> “按说他才去没多久，李组长不该这样对他持有偏见，除非是……有什么嫌隙？宿怨？”
“By rights he’s only just joined; Section-head Li ought not to hold such a bias against him—unless there’s some… some rift? Some old grudge?”

> “这话对。”
“That’s true.”

> “处座？”俞晓江诧异地望着杜旅宁。这个人从来都很隐蔽自己的内心，几乎无人能走进他的精神领域。俞晓江知道他和李沁红曾有过一段短暂的“爱情”，但是，杜旅宁每每回首往事，都会咬牙切齿。
“Chief?” Yu Xiaojiang looked at Du Luning in surprise. This was a man who always kept his inner self hidden; scarcely anyone could enter the realm of his mind. Yu Xiaojiang knew he and Li Qinhong had once had a brief “love affair”; but every time Du Luning looked back on the past, he would grind his teeth.

> “你知道吗？一个失控的妒妇远比一个出了轨的火车头还要可怕。”
“Do you know—a jealous woman out of control is a good deal more terrible than a runaway locomotive off its rails.”

> “那是因为她爱您，处座。”
“That’s because she loves you, chief.”

> “爱？你知道她曾经做过什么？”
“Love? Do you know what she once did?”

> 俞晓江摇头。
Yu Xiaojiang shook her head.

> “五年前，她曾经以'爱我'的名义，杀了我的学生。一个比丽丽更有潜质的女孩子，仅仅是因为妒忌。”
“Five years ago she killed a student of mine—in the name of ‘loving me.’ A girl of greater promise than Lili, merely out of jealousy.”

> 俞晓江无语了。
Yu Xiaojiang was silenced.

> “我不杀她，已经是仁尽义至了。”杜旅宁站了起来，在房间里踱步。“她对我的学生一直采取远而避之、用而防之的态度。事事凌人，样样占上风。”
“That I have not killed her is mercy carried to its limit.” Du Luning stood up and paced the room. “Toward my students she has always taken the line of keeping them at a distance and warding them off, using them yet guarding against them—overbearing in every matter, coming out on top in every thing.”

> “为什么呢？”
“But why?”

> “因为我曾经警告过她，我的学生再要死在她手上，我就杀了她。”杜旅宁拿起望远镜，向外观望。
“Because I once warned her: let another student of mine die at her hands, and I would kill her.” Du Luning picked up the telescope and looked out.

> 一切如常。
All was as usual.

> 杨思桐、荣初和慕次在绿荫底愉快地交谈，只不过，慕次的目光有意无意地朝监视点回荡，这让杜旅宁的嘴角泛起一丝得意的笑容。
Yang Sitong, Rong Chu and Muci were talking cheerfully in the green shade—only, Muci’s gaze drifted, now by design, now by chance, toward the watch-point, and this brought a faint smile of satisfaction to the corner of Du Luning’s mouth.

> “共党的特委会议即将召开，李组长却一味的把力量集中在医院，监视一个病人，一个自己人，这岂不是让真正的敌人安心舒意地躲过罗网。”俞晓江在暗示杜旅宁。“处座，我想我们不能这样等下去了。我建议，从今天开始撤销对阿次的监控，集中精力搜捕共匪。”
“The Communist special-committee conference is about to convene, yet Section-head Li throws all her strength into a hospital, watching one patient—one of our own—which is as good as letting the true enemy slip the net at his ease,” Yu Xiaojiang hinted to Du Luning. “Chief, I don’t think we can go on waiting like this. My advice is that from today we lift the surveillance on A-Ci and concentrate our energies on rounding up the Communist bandits.”

> “上海这么大，你从哪里下手？”
“Shanghai is so vast—where would you begin?”

> “处座。上海这么大，要集中搜捕共匪的确殊非易事。但是，开会就需要人员集中，开会就会选定一个会址。找到这个会址才是至关重要的。”
“Chief. Shanghai is vast, and to concentrate on rounding up the Communist bandits is indeed no easy matter. But to hold a meeting they need to gather people; to hold a meeting they must fix on a venue. To find that venue is what matters most.”

> “说下去。”
“Go on.”

> “如果，我是共产党特科负责保卫这次会议安全召开的人，我来选定会址，我会有三个必选的条件。第一，会址必须在租界；第二，会址必须在大上海最为繁华的地段，交通四通八达，易于隐藏，易于撤退；第三，会址必须能容纳一百多人的食宿，能达到这个要求的，有酒店、舞厅、戏院、麻将馆、私人会所。”
“If I were the man of the Communist Special Branch charged with guarding the safe convening of this conference, and I were to fix the venue, I’d set three requirements it must meet. First, the venue must be in the Concession; second, the venue must be in the busiest quarter of greater Shanghai, with roads reaching in every direction, easy to hide in, easy to withdraw from; third, the venue must be able to house and feed more than a hundred people. What can answer to that: hotels, ballrooms, theatres, mahjong parlors, private clubs.”

> 杜旅宁满意地点头。
Du Luning nodded, satisfied.

> “处座，事不宜迟，我想从今天起，开始对符合这三个条件的地方，进行大搜捕。”
“Chief, there’s no time to lose. From today, I’d like to start a great sweep of every place that meets these three conditions.”

> “我同意。”杜旅宁说。
“I agree,” Du Luning said.

> “要不要和李组长沟通一下？”
“Should we coordinate with Section-head Li?”

> “不需要。”
“No need.”

> “处座？”
“Chief?”

> “我需要一个跳梁小丑在明处吸引住共匪的目光，而你在暗处将施与对手致命一击。每一个角色、每一个位置都必不可缺。”
“I need a clown capering in the open to hold the Communist bandits’ eyes, while you, in the dark, deal our adversary the fatal blow. Every role, every position, is indispensable.”

> “您的意思是让阿次在明处？”
“You mean to keep A-Ci in the open?”

> “阿次？”杜旅宁笑起来，把望远镜递给俞晓江。“你认为，阿次会一直让李沁红监控吗？”
“A-Ci?” Du Luning laughed and handed the telescope to Yu Xiaojiang. “Do you suppose A-Ci will go on letting Li Qinhong keep watch over him?”

> 俞晓江半信半疑地接过望远镜朝外看，浓荫处，阿次依旧坐在轮椅上。杨思桐和荣初已经走了。
Half in doubt, Yu Xiaojiang took the telescope and looked out; in the deep shade A-Ci still sat in his wheelchair. Yang Sitong and Rong Chu had already gone.

> 晓江没有发现异常。
Xiaojiang saw nothing amiss.

> “他习惯单兵鏖战，这是他的优点。”杜旅宁说。
“He’s used to fighting his battles single-handed—that’s his strength,” Du Luning said.

> “可是，处座，我发现了一个更有趣的事。我们的小青蛇，不肯冬眠。”
“But, chief, I’ve found something more interesting still. Our little green snake won’t hibernate.”

> 杜旅宁的脸黑下来，他知道，一定是辛丽丽来了。
Du Luning’s face darkened; he knew it must be that Xin Lili had come.

> 辛丽丽穿着红地绣银花高领、窄袖长袄姗姗而来，她的眼波迅捷地观察着医院的地势，为了来探望阿次，她特意打扮成了一个十足的富家少奶奶。自从在学校里和阿次分手后，两人就很难见面了，辛丽丽的相思欲望愈来愈浓烈，由于军统的“家规”森严，一条被命令“冬眠”的蛇，无法在光天化日下和组织里的任何人保持任何联系，她对慕次的爱恋永远无由消解。她在得知慕次受伤的消息后，五内如焚，备受煎熬，她无论如何都得要见他一面，哪怕是远远地看他一眼。
Xin Lili came stepping daintily up in a high-collared, narrow-sleeved long jacket of red ground embroidered with silver flowers; her glance swept swiftly over the lie of the hospital. To come and visit A-Ci she had got herself up as every inch a rich young matron. Ever since she and A-Ci had parted at the academy, the two had found it very hard to meet; Xin Lili’s longing grew fiercer and fiercer, and because of the strict “house rules” of Juntong, a snake ordered into “hibernation” could keep no contact whatsoever with anyone in the organization in the light of day, so that her love for Muci could never be dissolved. When she learned that Muci had been wounded, she burned within as with fire, tormented past bearing; come what might, she had to see him once—even to look at him only from afar.

> 辛丽丽的痛楚在于她还有爱，还有梦。
Xin Lili’s anguish lay in this: that she still had love, and still had dreams.

> “阻止她。”杜旅宁说。“已经够乱的了，我不想再看见无味的儿女情长，我需要她长远的效力，她这样做，等于把自己的身份暴露给侦缉处所有的人，也包括共产党。”
“Stop her,” Du Luning said. “Things are muddled enough; I don’t want to see any more of this insipid lovers’ business. I need her long-term service. To do this is as good as exposing her identity to everyone in the Detective Division—the Communists included.”

> “丽丽是性情中人。”
“Lili is a creature of her feelings.”

> “这不是她渎职的借口。”
“That’s no excuse for dereliction of duty.”

> “我去吧，处座。”俞晓江郑重地说。“我是女人。”
“I’ll go, chief,” Yu Xiaojiang said gravely. “I’m a woman.”

> “好吧，注意分寸，小心点。”杜旅宁说。“李沁红，她不是女人。”
“Very well; mind your measure, be careful,” Du Luning said. “Li Qinhong—she is no woman.”

> 杜旅宁吩咐完后，起身出门，随从替他披上风衣，他们匆匆下楼，上了吉普车，很快消失在繁华的马路上。
His instructions given, Du Luning rose and went out; an attendant draped a windbreaker over him. They went quickly downstairs, got into a jeep, and were soon lost among the busy streets.

> 俞晓江从楼的侧门走向医院的住院部。
Yu Xiaojiang went by the building’s side door toward the hospital’s in-patient block.

> 李沁红的确不是一个简单的女人，她凭借着女人敏感的直觉，认定春和医院有问题，虽然她没有任何证据。
Li Qinhong was indeed no simple woman; on the strength of a woman’s sensitive intuition, she had determined that there was something wrong at the Chunhe Hospital, though she had not a scrap of evidence.

> 她有她的理论根据。
She had her own line of reasoning.

> “电话辨音”一事，刚刚了结，“铆钉”就神秘的人间蒸发了，这不可不谓一疑。共产党召开特委会议，必须要有一个固定的会址，去酒店，明目张胆；去舞厅、麻将馆不宜保密，更无安全可言；去私人会所，哪一个社会贤达会冒这种风险，把私人会所租给一些来历不明的人；那么，去医院呢？
The “telephone voice-matching” affair had barely been settled when the “Rivet” mysteriously vanished from the face of the earth—which could only be counted a first cause for doubt. For the Communists to hold a special-committee conference, they must have a fixed venue: to use a hotel was too brazen; ballrooms and mahjong parlors were unfit for secrecy and offered no safety at all; and as for private clubs, what man of good social standing would run such a risk as to lease his private club to persons of unknown origin? Then—what about a hospital?

> 她曾经消除过对杨氏兄弟的怀疑，可是，现在，她疑窦丛生，怀疑又起，她不肯撤回对杨慕次的监控，并以此为诱饵，孤注一掷钓大鱼。
She had once cleared away her suspicion of the two Yang brothers; but now doubts crowded thick upon her, and the suspicion rose again. She would not withdraw her watch on Yang Muci, and made it her bait, staking everything on the one throw to land the big fish.

> 事实证明，她的判断是正确的。
The facts proved her judgment correct.

> 丛锋来了。
Cong Feng had come.

> 丛锋在阿初的家里憋了将近一个多星期，他完全和地下党失去了联系，荣华已经牺牲了，唯一能联系到的同志就是杨慕次，特委会议召开在即，他如果再无法和地下党取得联系，他将无法完成使命。
Cong Feng had been penned up in A-Chu’s house for the best part of a week; he had wholly lost contact with the underground Party. Ronghua had fallen; the one comrade he could still reach was Yang Muci. With the special-committee conference upon them, if he could not make contact with the underground Party he would be unable to fulfill his mission.

> 阿初警告他，说医院里到处是特务的暗哨，如果冒险前往，很可能掉进陷阱。但是，时间紧迫，容不得自己再等了，他先是去了梅花巷，犹豫再三，没有进去，转身就坐黄包车到了春和医院，丛锋横下一条心，今天一定要找到联系人。
A-Chu had warned him that the hospital was thick with agents’ hidden posts, and that to venture there was very likely to walk into a trap. But time pressed, and he could not wait any longer; he first went to Plum Blossom Lane, hesitated long, did not go in, turned about, and took a rickshaw to the Chunhe Hospital. Cong Feng had set his heart on one thing: today, whatever came, he would find his contact.

> 纵是刀俎在前，游鱼也视死如归了。
Though the block and the chopping-knife lay before him, the roaming fish went to meet death without flinching.

> 可是，他万万没有想到，拉他的黄包车夫就是侦缉处特情组成员。丛锋同一时间段出现在两个敏感地点，他的可疑之处，已经不容置疑了。
But what he could never have imagined was that the very rickshaw man pulling him was a member of the Detective Division’s special-intelligence group. Cong Feng had appeared, within the same span of time, at two sensitive places; that he was a man to be suspected was now beyond dispute.

> 当李沁红接到特务的消息后，喜出望外，这真是一个出乎意料的收获。
When Li Qinhong received the agents’ report, she was overjoyed—here was a windfall past all expectation.

> “通知侦缉处的高队，立即到春和医院配合抓捕行动。只要这个嫌疑人跨进杨慕次的病房一步，就立即逮捕。”李沁红发布命令。
“Notify Captain Gao of the Detective Division to come at once to the Chunhe Hospital to join the arrest operation. The moment this suspect sets one foot into Yang Muci’s sickroom, arrest him,” Li Qinhong issued her order.

> “也，也包括杨副官吗？”特务问。
“And—and does that include Adjutant Yang?” the agent asked.

> “当然。”李沁红声音脆冷。“我会亲自逮捕这个共党，到时候，我要拎着他的人头，去问问他的老师，这个人该不该死！”
“Of course.” Li Qinhong’s voice was crisp and cold. “I will arrest this Communist myself; and when the time comes, I’ll carry his head to ask his teacher whether the man deserved to die!”

> 小特务一脸茫然。
The little agent’s face was all bewilderment.

> 夏跃春返回医院后，直奔慕次的病房，他没有看到病人，自然，也没有找到丛锋。他仔仔细细、认认真真地私下里把医院翻了个遍，也没有找寻到丛锋的踪迹。
Xia Yuechun, back at the hospital, made straight for Muci’s sickroom; he did not find the patient there, and, naturally, did not find Cong Feng either. Carefully, painstakingly, on the quiet, he turned the whole hospital over, and still could find no trace of Cong Feng.

> 他万分疲惫地回到院长办公室，桌子上的电话铃声响了。
Utterly worn out, he returned to the superintendent’s office; the telephone on the desk rang.

> “喂。”跃春有气无力地说。“阿初啊，我这里没人。”
“Hello.” Yuechun spoke feebly. “A-Chu—there’s no one here.”

> “你再找找，他肯定到了。”
“Look again. He’s certainly arrived.”

> 夏跃春放下电话，把院长办公室的窗帘全拉下来。他像匹骆驼躬着腰在窗子面前来回走了两圈，拿起电话：“绝对没来。我保证！……他来了。”
Xia Yuechun set down the telephone and drew all the curtains of the superintendent’s office. Bent like a camel, he paced twice before the window, then picked up the telephone: “He absolutely has not come. I guarantee it!… He’s come.”

> “什么？”
“What?”

> “他来了。”夏跃春的目光凝聚在窗外的草坪上。
“He’s come.” Xia Yuechun’s gaze fastened on the lawn outside the window.

> “截住他，我就来。”电话断了。
“Cut him off. I’m coming.” The line went dead.

> 夏跃春扔下话筒就往外跑。
Xia Yuechun flung down the receiver and ran outside.

> 此时此刻的杨慕次与丛锋的距离只有咫尺之遥，怎么办？
At this very moment the distance between Yang Muci and Cong Feng was no more than a hand’s span. What was to be done?

> 丛锋此际就像一枝风标，他走向哪里，几股风就会在瞬间合股冲袭而至。危险已然降临。
Cong Feng was now like a weathervane: wherever he moved, several winds would in an instant gather into one and come rushing at him. Danger had already descended.

> “阿次。”辛丽丽看见了慕次，她跑过去。
“A-Ci.” Xin Lili caught sight of Muci and ran to him.

> 慕次的手心沁出冷汗。
Muci’s palms broke out in cold sweat.

> “丽丽。”
“Lili.”

> “怎么了？”丽丽半蹲下来，仰视他。
“What’s wrong?” Lili crouched half down and looked up at him.

> “帮帮我。”慕次决定冒险了。
“Help me.” Muci resolved on the risk.

> “你说。”
“Tell me.”

> “你向前看……”
“Look ahead of you…”

> 丽丽抬头向前看，冷不防从身侧走来一人，扬手就是一记耳光。“叫你好好看着少爷，你没听见吗？”一个女人把慕次的轮椅来了一个一百八十度的大转弯。
Lili raised her head and looked ahead; and out of nowhere a person came up from her side and dealt her a slap across the face. “You were told to keep a proper eye on the young master—didn’t you hear?” A woman spun Muci’s wheelchair round a full hundred and eighty degrees.

> 丛锋狐疑地看着两个女人的背影，从慕次身边走过……
Cong Feng, eyeing with suspicion the backs of the two women, walked on past Muci’s side…

> 俞晓江推着轮椅走向另一僻静处，慕次的脑海里浮现出无数个问号？无数个关键词。俞晓江到了，那么，杜旅宁也就到了。丛锋现在的目标，一定是自己的病房，他去了自己的病房，自己还回得去吗？
Yu Xiaojiang wheeled the chair toward another out-of-the-way spot; in Muci’s mind rose question mark upon question mark, keyword upon keyword. Yu Xiaojiang had come—which meant Du Luning had come too. Cong Feng’s goal now must be his own sickroom; if Cong Feng went to his room, could he himself still get back there?

> 正思想，没提防，俞晓江用力一倾，慕次直接从轮椅上跌落，倒在草地上。由于地势低，树荫浓，外面根本看不到里面发生的事。辛丽丽咬着自己的手指，禁止自己出声，她也没敢去扶慕次。
While he was still thinking, without warning Yu Xiaojiang tipped the chair hard, and Muci pitched straight out of it and fell onto the grass. The ground being low and the tree-shade thick, from outside one could not see at all what was happening within. Xin Lili bit her own finger to forbid herself any sound, and dared not go to lift Muci up.

> “说，谁约的谁？”俞晓江问。
“Speak—who arranged to meet whom?” Yu Xiaojiang asked.

> “我。”慕次说。
“I did,” Muci said.

> “不是的。”丽丽欲解释。
“That’s not so.” Lili made to explain.

> “不要解释。”慕次制止丽丽。
“Don’t explain.” Muci stopped Lili.

> “那就照规矩来。”俞晓江冷酷地说。她扶正轮椅，向丽丽伸出手来：“你的枪。”
“Then we’ll do it by the rules.” Yu Xiaojiang said coldly. She set the wheelchair upright and put out her hand to Lili. “Your gun.”

> 丽丽看着慕次，慕次把左手背平放在轮椅的扶手上。丽丽从精致的皮包里取出一只镀金小手枪，她交枪的时候，恳求地说：“老师，他身上有伤。”
Lili looked at Muci; Muci laid the back of his left hand flat on the armrest of the chair. Lili took from her exquisite handbag a small gilt pistol, and as she handed it over she pleaded, “Teacher, he’s injured.”

> 俞晓江面无表情，直接把枪柄砸在慕次的手背上，这一次，丽丽喊出来了。
Yu Xiaojiang, without a flicker of expression, brought the butt of the pistol straight down on the back of Muci’s hand; and this time Lili cried out.

> 血从慕次的手背上渗出……
Blood seeped out from the back of Muci’s hand…

> “没有下次。”俞晓江把枪扔还丽丽。“别忙着出售自己，设身处地为他人想想。”
“There will be no next time.” Yu Xiaojiang tossed the gun back to Lili. “Don’t be in such haste to sell yourself; put yourself in another’s place for once.”

> 两分钟后，丽丽脸色苍白的离开了草坪。
Two minutes later Lili, her face ashen, left the lawn.

> 俞晓江却推着慕次走向住院部，慕次的病房。
Yu Xiaojiang, however, wheeled Muci toward the in-patient block—toward Muci’s sickroom.

> 慕次的衣襟下藏了丽丽的枪。
Under the flap of Muci’s gown lay Lili’s gun, hidden.

> 丛锋镇定自若地走进医生休息室，过了一会儿，他穿着白色大褂、戴着口罩，走出来。护士站内，两名护士正在低头配药水。
Cong Feng, calm and unhurried, walked into the doctors’ rest room; after a while he came out in a white gown and a mask. At the nurses’ station two nurses were bent over, mixing medicine.

> 丛锋走过去，看了看挂在护士站里病人的名字和床号，他清晰地看见慕次的床号主楼右旋处二楼十九床。
Cong Feng walked over and looked at the patients’ names and bed numbers hanging at the station; he saw clearly Muci’s bed number—second floor, to the right off the main block, bed nineteen.

> 他顺手拿了桌沿上的听诊器，继续往前走。
He picked up a stethoscope from the edge of the desk as he went, and walked on.

> 楼道里，有一名护士从房间里出来，她轻轻关上门，步履轻盈地跟上了丛锋。丛锋上了二楼，护士也不紧不慢地跟上去。
In the passage, a nurse came out of a room, gently closing the door, and fell in behind Cong Feng with a light step. Cong Feng went up to the second floor, and the nurse, unhurried, followed him up.

> 丛锋走到十七床至十九床的走廊上，突然停止了步伐，而是转过身来，向护士走来。护士礼貌地对他微笑。
Cong Feng reached the corridor between beds seventeen and nineteen, and suddenly halted; instead he turned about and walked toward the nurse. The nurse smiled at him politely.

> “您有什么需要吗？”李沁红主动开口，一脸温情。
“Is there something you need?” Li Qinhong spoke first, all warmth in her face.

> 丛锋迅速打量了护士，她大约三十七、八岁，没有化装，穿一件白色护士服，腰带平整，戴着蝴蝶结头花，仪表端庄。
Cong Feng sized the nurse up swiftly: about thirty-seven or -eight, wearing no makeup, in a white nurse’s uniform, her belt smooth and neat, a butterfly-bow hair ornament on her head, her appearance decorous.

> 是自己多心了。丛锋想。
I’m being over-suspicious, Cong Feng thought.

> 他大大方方地走到李沁红跟前，说：“你的内衣领边和袖口露在护士服外了。”
He walked frankly up before Li Qinhong and said, “The edge of your undergarment collar and cuffs are showing outside your uniform.”

> “哦。”李沁红下意识地开始整理衣襟。
“Oh.” Li Qinhong began, unconsciously, to tidy her collar.

> “护士应该给病人留下整洁、干净的印像。下次注意。”
“A nurse ought to leave a patient the impression of neatness and cleanliness. Mind it next time.”

> “是。”李沁红低下头。
“Yes.” Li Qinhong lowered her head.

> 丛锋低头看见她穿的鞋子，那是一双还没有来得及换的皮鞋，鞋皮铮亮，闪着光。
Cong Feng, glancing down, saw the shoes she wore—a pair of leather shoes she had not had time to change, the leather glossy and gleaming.

> “你应该换上护士鞋……”
“You ought to change into nurse’s shoes…”

> “我的护士鞋昨天洗了，还没晾干……”李沁红微笑。“您请……”她有礼貌地请丛锋先走。
“My nurse’s shoes were washed yesterday and aren’t dry yet…” Li Qinhong smiled. “After you, please…” She politely invited Cong Feng to walk on ahead.

> 丛锋不再犹疑。
Cong Feng hesitated no longer.

> 他走到了病房门口挂着的十九床的门牌前。
He walked up to the door-plate marked “Bed 19,” hanging at the sickroom door.

> 李沁红瞬间有一丝不祥的预感，又觉得哪里不对劲，又说不出是哪里出了问题。不过，她还是相信自己的判断，眼前这个人一定就是她要找的人。
In a flash Li Qinhong had a premonition of ill; she felt, too, that something was not right—yet she could not say where the trouble lay. Still, she trusted her own judgment: this man before her must surely be the one she was hunting.

> 她没有看错。
She had not misjudged.

> 丛锋的手已经握紧了十九床病房的门把手。
Cong Feng’s hand had already closed tight on the door-handle of the Bed 19 sickroom.

> 李沁红的手伸进了护士服的口袋，等待他推门的动作。
Li Qinhong’s hand slid into the pocket of her nurse’s uniform, and waited for him to push the door open.
