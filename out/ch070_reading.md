### The Picture-Mounter's Heaven-Head Swap

The picture-mounting shops have practised their frauds from of old. Let a painting by some famous hand be brought in for mounting, and the shop will lay out a trifle of money to have a painter — one of the sort who can paint but has made no name — copy it, drawing the gourd from the model, stroke for stroke; the very paper and seals can be imitated too, and when the thing is done the counterfeit will pass for the true, whereupon they hide away the genuine and hand the owner the fake. Though the owner would dispute it, he can come by no proof, and must swallow his anger and go. Besides this there is the method of "changing the heaven-head." What is meant by "changing the heaven-head"? Say they come upon some rare and precious old painting: they first soak it through with alum-water and peel it apart into two layers — the colour of the painting lying denser in the upper layer and fainter in the lower — then work up the lower with pigment and washes, so that the inscription and the seals too are lifted onto it; and when it is mounted, they hide the upper layer and give the owner the lower. This secret art is also called "stealing the dragon and swapping the phoenix," and it can only be set about where the paper has been backed with tribute Xuan paper. And so, when a precious painting by a famous hand is given out to be mounted, one cannot be too careful to pick an honest, trustworthy shop, if one is to escape being cheated.
