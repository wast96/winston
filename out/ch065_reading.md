### The Lavish 'Great Works'

The head office where the huahui is decided is called the "Great Works" (or else "the Big Tube"); the name "Works" is chosen, no doubt, for the great scale of the thing, as with the factories and works of the various trades. A gang of gamblers who wish to go straight to the Works themselves and try a fall do what is called "submitting a sealed packet"; there is no fixed sum to be staked in a packet — ten dollars a packet, or five, is settled as the moment dictates.

When the gamblers come up to the Great Works, an attendant of the house leads them in and carries their sealed packets first to the counting-house, where a receipt is made out. When the hour strikes, an important man of the house takes a silver casket in his hand and opens it before them all. Inside is pasted a slip of red paper, written out beforehand, naming the huahui figure (Zhankui, Bangui and the like). Suppose the tube this time has opened on Zhankui: nothing is heard but a great cry of "Zhankui!" ringing full in the ears, and every gambler whose packet-slip carries the words "Zhankui" has won, and stands laughing as he waits for the counting-house to pay him out; but if the packet holds no "Zhankui," it is an "eaten packet" (that is, the money-packet has been swallowed up — a way of saying he has lost), and out he trudges with his head hanging.

I am told that in the old days they used no silver casket but a hanging scroll, on which the huahui name was written; and when the tube was opened, the scroll would be let down from a height. In the out-of-the-way country parts about Ningbo and Shaoxing they still let the name down on a scroll to this very day!
