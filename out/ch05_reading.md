## 5

In many streets the Japanese had strung barbed wire and marked out controlled zones. By day they stopped and questioned anyone they thought suspect; by night they kept a curfew. Oddly, this made the quarters where the Japanese gathered flush with a strange prosperity. Restaurants, dance halls, brothels, and gambling dens filled the moment night fell, as if everyone lived for the day and gave no thought to tomorrow, and everywhere rose the din of pleasure-seekers snatching the hour.

Lin Nansheng, by contrast, was unusually still. On any day Zhu Yizhen had no assignment, the two of them spent it in the little garret, one embroidering, one reading, though more often than not they were in bed.

Those days ended soon enough, when Gu Shenyan came back to Shanghai. He had arranged to meet at a brothel kept by an Italian, and the moment they met he pointed at the mirrors set into all four walls and asked, idly, whether Lin had ever seen the like. Lin Nansheng shook his head. A man who is never dissolute wastes his youth, Gu Shenyan said, smiling. In France he had gone to the brothels of Paris and fallen for a golden-haired girl there; now that was a place truly alive with color and scent. As he spoke, as if calling back his lost youth, a light Lin Nansheng had never seen glittered in his eyes. He sat down on the sofa and gazed a long while at the golden liquid in his glass. "Love is like a fine wine," he said. "It can make a man drunk, and it can give him the courage to throw everything to the winds." His eyes traveled through the glass and came to rest, slowly, on Lin Nansheng's face. "But you should know, too, that even the finest wine gives no more than a moment's joy."

Something stirred in Lin Nansheng. He bowed his head. "Yes."

Gu Shenyan drained the glass at a draught and began to hand down his orders. The agents he had recruited were coming into Shanghai one after another. He wanted Lin Nansheng to work out, and fast, a wholly new way of communicating, so that an agent, once taken, could not drag the whole organization down with him.

"We must learn from our defeats," Gu Shenyan said. "I'd have you take the Communists' structure for a model."

Lin Nansheng started. "Why model it on theirs?"

"Their network may not be the most scientific," Gu Shenyan said, "but in times like these, practice has proved it beyond doubt the most serviceable."

"Yes," Lin Nansheng said.

Gu Shenyan waved him to a seat, poured himself another glass, and began to lay out the network he would spread anew across Shanghai.

Lin Nansheng broke in quickly. "Sir, you shouldn't be telling this to a subordinate."

"And do you know why I tell it to you?" Gu Shenyan smiled. "Trust is a strange thing sometimes." He turned to Lin Nansheng. "Are you worthy of my trust?"

Lin Nansheng came to his feet and stood straight before him, at a loss for the answer.

Still smiling, Gu Shenyan said, "In times like these, I have to guard against the unforeseen as well."

"That day will never come," Lin Nansheng said.

Gu Shenyan's face went grave. "I've already put it to headquarters: should it come, you are to take over my work."

All the way from the brothel Lin Nansheng's heart rose and fell, and the fear of it grew on him. He turned the thing over and over, and came home to find that Zhu Yizhen had supper ready and was sitting quietly under the lamp, waiting.

Halfway through the meal Lin Nansheng said, suddenly, "You have to leave here as soon as you can."

Zhu Yizhen was taken aback, and went on eating with her head bowed over the bowl.

"This place is blown," Lin Nansheng said again.

Only then did Zhu Yizhen set the bowl down. She rose, switched off the light, and stood a long while at the window, looking down, but saw nothing wrong. So she turned the light back on, sat and finished her rice, cleared the table, and settled at the frame, where she worked the silk for the better part of the night.

Zhu Yizhen did not speak again until she was in bed. In the dark she looked at the man on the pillow beside her. "We've come to the time when it ought to end," she murmured.

Lin Nansheng seemed to be back where he had once been, meeting Zhu Yizhen every week, sometimes once, sometimes twice, by day or at dusk, but never any longer to trade intelligence. Like all lovers in the thick of it, they ate together, went to the pictures, sat in cafés, and made love in hotel rooms. Only, every meeting was kept with the greatest care, at once a rendezvous and a secret affair, charged between them with a dangerous pleasure.

One day after the Spring Festival, Gu Shenyan sent for Lin Nansheng without warning and said he wanted to meet the man who ran the Communists in Shanghai.

"So far as I know," Lin Nansheng said, "their Jiangsu Provincial Committee has already left Shanghai."

"Their New Fourth Army office is still here." Gu Shenyan's tone left no room for question. "Find them. Quickly."

That very night Lin Nansheng burst into Zhu Yizhen's new lodging. The next afternoon he waited for her answer by the Nine-Turn Bridge at the City God Temple, and when he caught sight of her far off in the crowd, he caught sight too of the plainclothesman on her heels. By the agents' code, the one thing to do now was turn and walk away. He did not. Without a moment's hesitation he went to her, threw an arm around her in the crowd. "Come with me." Pressed together in the throng, they crossed the Nine-Turn Bridge at speed, through the shrine hall and the incense room behind it, and out of the City God Temple by a small side door off the back court. He had scouted the way beforehand; it had become second nature. But this time they were up against masters. Out of the lane, all he could do was grip her hand and run for their lives.

That was when the shots came. A bullet drove through Zhu Yizhen's chest from behind, and in the same instant bored into Lin Nansheng's spine. As if tripped headlong, his face to the flagstones, he cried out: "Zhenzhen."

Zhu Yizhen looked at him and opened her mouth, and the blood came choking from her mouth and her nose.

That night a black sedan rolled slowly through the garden gate of No. 101 Yuyuan Road. Gu Shenyan, in a felt hat and a sable coat, followed a guard into a study and sat a long while on the sofa before Ding Mocun pushed open the door and came in.

Ding Mocun, by now moved to the post of Minister of Communications, had plainly just come from his bed, wrapped tight in a padded silk gown.

Gu Shenyan smiled. "Old friends, met again. Won't you offer me a drink?"

Ding Mocun stayed where he stood, and looked at him coldly. "So far as I know, you've pulled out of Shanghai along the whole line."

"Your intelligence was never accurate." Still smiling, Gu Shenyan rose, took a bottle of brandy from the cabinet, poured himself a glass, and looked at the label. "A 1935 cognac. That year we were both at the bandit-suppression field headquarters in Nanchang, weren't we? Say what you have to say. There's no need to trade on old acquaintance here."

"I want you to pull a man out of Japanese hands for me."

Ding Mocun sat down. "What you should be weighing now is how to get out of this room in one piece."

"If Minister Ding means to keep me, he might at least let me first use the wireless set Mr. Dai installed on your premises, to send word to Chongqing."

Ding Mocun's face changed. It was a while before he spoke. "You must understand that anything on the Japanese side is very hard to arrange."

"I know you still hold the directorship of the Secret Service Headquarters as well."

"What manner of man is it you want to save?"

"A subordinate."

"For a subordinate you break into my house in the dead of night?"

"The man is in the emergency room of the Renji Hospital, this very moment."

"I can help you shut his mouth forever."

"Is this how you treat your own comrades?"

Ding Mocun laughed. "Brother Shenyan, at bottom you're still a Communist."

This time it was Gu Shenyan whose color changed. He set down his glass, stood, and glanced at his watch. "We haven't much time."

"But I'd like to know how you mean to repay me."

Gu Shenyan thought a moment. "My coming to you is itself the repayment."
