## 5

In many streets of the city the Japanese had strung barbed wire and marked out controlled zones. By day they stopped and questioned every passerby they thought suspect; by night they enforced a curfew — which, oddly, made the quarters where the Japanese gathered flush with a strange prosperity. Restaurants, dance halls, brothels, and gambling dens filled to the doors the moment night fell, as though everyone lived for today with no thought of tomorrow, and everywhere rang the clamor of pleasure-seekers snatching the hour.

Lin Nansheng, by contrast, was unusually still. Every day, so long as Zhu Yizhen had no assignment, the two of them would spend the whole day in the little garret — one embroidering, one reading, though more often than not they were in bed.

But such days ended soon enough with Gu Shenyan's return to Shanghai. He arranged to meet Lin Nansheng at a brothel kept by an Italian, and the moment they met he pointed at the mirrors set into all four walls and asked, idly, whether Lin had ever laid eyes on such things. Lin Nansheng shook his head. Gu Shenyan said with a smile that a man who was never dissolute wasted his youth; when he studied in France he had gone to the brothels of Paris, and had even fallen in love with a golden-haired girl there — now that, he said, was a place truly alive with color and scent. As he spoke, as though calling back the vanished days of his youth, a light Lin Nansheng had never seen glittered in his eyes. Sitting down on the sofa, he gazed a long while at the golden liquid in his glass and said, with feeling, Love is like a fine wine: it can make a man drunk, and it can lend him courage, make him throw all caution to the winds. His gaze traveled through the glass and came slowly to rest on Lin Nansheng's face, and he said, But you must know too that even the finest wine can give a man no more than a moment's joy.

Something stirred in Lin Nansheng; he bowed his head and said, Yes.

When Gu Shenyan had drained his glass at a draught, he began to hand down his orders, saying that the agents he had recruited were making their way to Shanghai one after another. He required Lin Nansheng to work out, as fast as he could, a wholly new system of communication, so that an agent, once captured, could not drag the whole organization down with him.

We must learn from our defeats, Gu Shenyan said. I suggest you take the Communists' organizational structure for your model.

Lin Nansheng started, and said, Why model it on theirs?

Gu Shenyan said, The system of the Communists' intelligence network may not be the most scientific, but practice has proved it, in circumstances like the present, beyond doubt the most serviceable.

Lin Nansheng said, Yes.

Gu Shenyan waved a hand, motioning him to sit, then poured himself another glass and began to speak of the intelligence network he would spread out anew across Shanghai.

Lin Nansheng hastened to break in and said, Sir, you should not be telling these things to a subordinate.

Then do you know why I tell them to you? Gu Shenyan said with a smile, Trust is sometimes just such a strange thing. He turned to look at Lin Nansheng and added, Are you worthy of my trust?

Lin Nansheng stood up at once and held himself erect before him, not knowing how best to answer.

Still smiling, Gu Shenyan said, In extraordinary times, I too must guard against the unforeseen.

Lin Nansheng said, That day will never come.

Gu Shenyan's face turned grave, and he said, I have already put it to headquarters: should that day come, you are to take over my work.

All the way from the brothel Lin Nansheng's heart rose and fell, and a dread crept over him more and more. Having turned many things over and over in his mind, he came home to find that Zhu Yizhen had supper ready and was sitting quietly under the lamp, waiting.

Halfway through the meal Lin Nansheng said suddenly, You must leave here as soon as you can.

Zhu Yizhen was taken aback, and went on eating with her head bowed over her bowl.

Lin Nansheng said again, This place is blown.

Only then did Zhu Yizhen set down her bowl; she rose, switched off the light, and stood a long while at the window looking down, but saw nothing amiss. So she switched the light on again, sat and finished the rice in her bowl, cleared the table, and settled at the embroidery frame, where she worked the silk for the better part of the night.

Zhu Yizhen did not speak again until she was in bed. Looking in the darkness at the man beside her on the pillow, she murmured, We have come to the time when it ought to end.

Lin Nansheng seemed to have gone back to how it had been before, meeting Zhu Yizhen every week — sometimes once, sometimes twice, sometimes by day, sometimes at dusk — but never now to trade intelligence. Like all lovers in the heat of it, besides eating together, going to the pictures, and lingering in cafés, they would make love in hotel rooms. Only, each of their trysts was kept with the utmost care, at once like a clandestine meeting and like an affair, charged between them with a dangerous pleasure.

One day after the Spring Festival, Gu Shenyan suddenly sent for Lin Nansheng and said he wished to meet the man in charge of the Communists in Shanghai.

Lin Nansheng said, So far as I know, the Communists' Jiangsu Provincial Committee has already pulled out of Shanghai.

Their New Fourth Army liaison office is still here. In a tone that brooked no question, Gu Shenyan said, Find them, and be quick.

That very night Lin Nansheng burst into Zhu Yizhen's new lodging. The next afternoon he waited by the Nine-Turn Bridge at the City God Temple for her reply, and when he caught sight of Zhu Yizhen far off in the crowd, he caught sight also of the plainclothesman trailing her. By the agents' code, all Lin Nansheng could do now was turn and walk away; but he did not. Without a moment's hesitation he went to meet her, threw an arm around her in the crowd, and said, Come with me. Pressed together in the throng, they crossed the Nine-Turn Bridge at speed, passed through the shrine hall and the incense room behind it, and left the City God Temple by a small side door off the back court. He had scouted the way before coming; it had become second nature to him. But this time the men they were up against were masters. Out of the lane, all Lin Nansheng could do was grip Zhu Yizhen's hand and run for their lives.

It was then that the shots rang out. A bullet drove through Zhu Yizhen's chest from behind, and in the same moment bored into Lin Nansheng's spine. As though tripped headlong to the ground, his face against the flagstones, Lin Nansheng cried out: Zhenzhen.

Zhu Yizhen looked at him and opened her mouth, and blood came choking from her mouth and nose.

That night a black sedan rolled slowly through the garden gate of No. 101 Yuyuan Road. Gu Shenyan, in a felt hat and a sable coat, followed a guard into a study, and had sat a long while on the sofa before he saw Ding Mocun push open the door and come in.

Ding Mocun, by now transferred to the post of Minister of Communications, had plainly just risen from his bed, wrapped tight in a padded silk dressing gown.

Gu Shenyan said with a smile, Old friends meet again — will you not offer me a drink?

Ding Mocun stood where he was and looked at him coldly. So far as I know, he said, you have pulled out of Shanghai along the whole line.

Your intelligence was never accurate. Still smiling, Gu Shenyan rose, went to the liquor cabinet, chose a bottle of brandy, poured himself a glass, and, looking at the label, said, A 1935 cognac — that year we were both at the bandit-suppression field headquarters in Nanchang, were we not? Say what you have to say; there is no need to trade on old acquaintance here.

I want you to help me pull a man out of Japanese hands.

Ding Mocun sat down on the sofa and said, What you should be weighing now is how to get out of this room in one piece.

If Minister Ding insists on detaining me, he might at least first let me use the wireless set Mr. Dai installed on your premises to send word to Chongqing.

Ding Mocun's face changed; it was a good while before he said, You must understand that anything on the Japanese side is very hard to manage.

I know that you still hold, into the bargain, the directorship of the Secret Service Headquarters.

What manner of man is it you want to save?

A subordinate.

For a subordinate you break into my house in the dead of night?

The man is in the emergency room of the Renji Hospital at this very moment.

I can help you shut his mouth forever.

Is this how you people treat your own comrades?

Ding Mocun laughed and said, Brother Shenyan, at bottom you are still a Communist.

This time it was Gu Shenyan's turn to change color. He set down his glass, stood, raised his wrist to glance at his watch, and said, We have not much time.

But what I want to know is how you mean to repay me.

Gu Shenyan thought a moment and said, My coming to you is itself the repayment.
