# China's King of Assassins: Wang Yaqiao

## Chapter Five: The Great Lushan Assassination

### 15. To Kill Chiang Kai-shek — Blessing or Calamity?

Having outwitted the three great bosses of Shanghai over the Jiang'an affair, Wang Yaqiao knew perfectly well that the three had not truly put away their guard against him, and knew too that Du Yuesheng was never not looking for a chance to pay him back. So he resolved to live retired in Shanghai as a private gentleman and show his face in public no longer. He bought a small foreign-style house at No. 24 Rue Pottier in the French Concession, kept indoors day after day, and watched constantly for a stroke out of the dark from the three bosses. It was, beyond question, the shrewd course.

But the tree may wish for stillness; the wind does not stop. After the Spring Festival of 1931, into that quiet courtyard on Rue Pottier there walked a rare visitor — and merely by coming, this man raised a wave in a life that had settled into calm. From that day, Wang Yaqiao was drawn once more into an assassination more dangerous than any before it.

The caller's name was Li Shaochuan. He had been a man of the Nationalist Party's upper ranks, and in the early years, when Wang Yaqiao first came from Anhui to try the Shanghai wharves and found the going hard, it was Li Shaochuan's protection and patronage that carried him. One New Year, when Wang Yaqiao could not even pay his brothers' holiday money, it was the open-handed Li Shaochuan who put out a hand and got him over the turn of the year. And later, when Wang Yaqiao was matching wits with the three great bosses over the Jiang'an, it was this same Li Shaochuan who worked the channels until Du Yuesheng and Huang Jinrong had no choice but to concede.

It was March now, and Shanghai lay under heavy cloud. Spring by the calendar, but the cold pressed in on a man, and it seemed a downpour might come at any hour. That Li Shaochuan should arrive at such a moment, unasked, gave the retired Wang Yaqiao a private start — for Li Shaochuan did not cross his threshold lightly. Wang Yaqiao dared not be slow: he called for tea, sent the servants out, and said to him: "An honored elder brother does not climb to the Three Treasures Hall without business. Is there something today you would have me do?"

Li Shaochuan and Wang Yaqiao were close, and he never talked in circles. He glanced around the room and dropped his voice. "Brother Yaqiao — do you know the man Hu Hanmin?"

"Hu Hanmin? Of course. In the days when I moved about at Mr. Sun Yat-sen's side in Guangzhou I knew Mr. Hu well. Mr. Sun was Grand President then, and Mr. Hu was secretary-general of the presidential office — how should I not know him?" Wang Yaqiao marveled privately, unable for the moment to guess why Li Shaochuan should open by speaking of a Hu Hanmin who had so little to do with either of them.

Li Shaochuan sighed. "Brother Jiuguang — do you know that Mr. Hu is now in trouble?"

"Who doesn't know it? The papers carried the news not long ago that Chiang Kai-shek has him under house arrest. Who in this country isn't outraged at Chiang's shameless conduct?" Seeing that Li Shaochuan would not leave the subject of Hu Hanmin, Wang Yaqiao felt the tension rise in him. Gang chief of Shanghai he might be, but he had never let go his connections in the Nationalist official world. He knew well enough what had happened one evening a month before: Chiang Kai-shek, on the pretext of inviting Hu Hanmin to a dinner at Tangshan outside Nanjing, had lured the president of the Legislative Yuan there and then, without warning, ordered him confined. The country had erupted. Every political faction condemned the gangster's trick, and the anger ran through court and countryside alike. As a gang leader long suppressed by Chiang Kai-shek, Wang Yaqiao naturally felt for Hu Hanmin's plight — but it had never occurred to him that he might one day do something about it. In those months he feared that a tall tree draws the wind, and had resolved to furl his banners and still his drums.

Seeing that Wang Yaqiao still could not read his purpose, Li Shaochuan opened the inside of the matter further. "Brother Jiuguang — do you know that since Chiang imprisoned Hu Hanmin, Sun Ke has stirred the anti-Chiang Cantonese officials into resigning their posts in the Nanjing government in a body, and that they have all gone off to Guangzhou in their fury?"

"That… I believe the papers printed that too." Still Wang Yaqiao could not see where this was going. He knew Chiang Kai-shek's fortunes stood nothing like as high as two years before, when he was elected chairman of the National Government at Nanjing; where the man had once been carried on everyone's shoulders, he was now hated by every official in the hall. As Chiang Kai-shek's name grew steadily fouler, Sun Yat-sen's son Sun Ke — seeing Chiang confine Hu Hanmin, who had been his father's chief minister in life — had in his anger stirred a great number of government dignitaries into flying to Guangzhou, and so formed the Southwest opposition. Among these Nationalist military and political grandees were not only the famous Republican general Li Liejun but Tang Shengzhi and Tang Shaoyi besides. Their parting of the ways had put Chiang in a very poor position. Wang Yaqiao looked at his old friend in perplexity. "I don't follow. What have any of these things to do with us? I'm a commoner when all is said. When two factions fight in the official world, men like me can only sit on the mountain and watch the tigers."

"Does my honored brother truly not hate Chiang Kai-shek?" Seeing Wang Yaqiao still looking left and right and talking of other things, Li Shaochuan went at it straight and spoke the real purpose of his visit. "I remember that Chiang Kai-shek long ago wanted to send Dai Li to murder you. Which tells you that Chiang won't merely injure one Hu Hanmin for daring to oppose his dictatorship — in time he will have no room for gang chiefs like you either. Chiang is a born king of killers; did you not say so to me yourself, long ago? And if that's so, why not stand up now and rid the people of him?"

"You want me to stand up and rescue Hu Hanmin?" At Li Shaochuan's words Wang Yaqiao felt a private jolt. Rich in a sense of justice since boyhood, how should he not hate the Chiang Kai-shek who used the power in his hands to crush the revolutionary forces? And yet, asked to ride out alone and kill Chiang Kai-shek, Wang Yaqiao found it sudden indeed. He shook his head again and again. "It won't do, it won't do. Brother Shaochuan, I take your meaning kindly, and my own hatred of Chiang is no matter of a single day. But to move against Chiang now — that's nothing like Zhao Tieqiao. Everyone knows that at Chiang's side there isn't only the Ten-Man Team gathering intelligence but a newly established Attendants' Office as well. There are dozens of sharp men in it, and most of them can put a shot through a willow leaf at a hundred paces. We of the Hatchet Gang are only rough men who hack and brawl. How should we dare go making trouble in Nanjing? Besides, Hu Hanmin's affair has little enough to do with either of us. Why should my brother ask me to run such a risk?"

"No. This risk you must run. For I too am here at another man's asking, come to beg your full strength!" Seeing Wang Yaqiao unwilling, Li Shaochuan knew that without the truth the man would never take the field. So he laid out in detail the sharp division between the two Nationalist camps since Hu Hanmin's imprisonment at Tangshan, and pleaded hard. "With Sun Ke and the rest far off in Guangdong, they're near to setting up a rival National Government and hollowing Chiang Kai-shek out. And Hu Hanmin is the spiritual leader of Sun Ke and the Southwest opposition. With two forces fighting this bitterly, Chiang Kai-shek may very well break the cauldrons and sink the boats. I've come because Hu Hanmin's kinsman by marriage, Lin Huanting, has had certain word that Chiang Kai-shek may put Hu Hanmin secretly to death very soon. Lin Huanting, as perhaps you know, has been my close friend for years. He came to my door himself to beg my help. How could I see a man die and not save him?"

Only now did Wang Yaqiao understand why his friend had come so suddenly, and what he truly wanted. Thinking of all Chiang Kai-shek had done to him over the years, and then of Li Shaochuan's long friendship, something turned over in him. "Chiang Kai-shek is indeed a criminal of the ages, pointed at by ten thousand fingers. By any reasoning, for me to kill him would be no more than right. But my brother must understand: killing Chiang is no small business. Handled badly, a man goes out to hunt the tiger and is swallowed by it! And besides, Chiang Kai-shek is no longer the little biesan scraping a living on the Shanghai waterfront. He knows now how many enemies he has, which is why he keeps so many guards about him who can use a gun. They say he never goes out with fewer than a hundred men behind him — he has simply become an emperor of our own day. Even if my Hatchet Gang men truly wished to act, I doubt we're a match for him."

Li Shaochuan heard in this that Wang Yaqiao's hatred of Chiang Kai-shek was of long standing but that he judged the hour not yet come. Knowing that a friend should not press too hard, he said: "Brother Jiuguang, I come at another's asking, and only to open the matter with you. Hu Hanmin's kinsman believes that the first good man under heaven is Wang Yaqiao, and that you alone can save Hu Hanmin. But you have your reservations, and that too stands to reason. I won't force you to act. And still I believe that you, Wang Yaqiao, will rid the country of this evil — because you're, when everything else is said, a Chinese with blood in him!"

"Very well, brother Shaochuan." With the thing put to him in those terms, Wang Yaqiao knew he could not go on refusing. He thought a long while and at last said: "This is a matter of the gravest consequence. Allow me a few days to think it through seriously before I decide. Would that do?" Delighted to have his assent at last, Li Shaochuan rose at once to take his leave. "Best so — that's a good man's answer. Only, whether the thing is done or not done, I beg my brother keep it absolutely secret."

After Li Shaochuan had gone, Wang Yaqiao sat drinking alone with his thoughts. Looking at the photograph on the opposite wall — the great Hatchet Gang assembly at Jing'an Temple — he found himself running back over all the dangers he had come through since founding the gang. Above all, the memory of the autumn of 1928, when he attended the founding assembly of the National Government at Nanjing and Chiang Kai-shek secretly sent Dai Li to kill him, still turned his heart cold. He knew that had Dai Li not fired his warning shot as the Ten-Man Team closed on his lodging in Hongwu Street, he might well have died that night at their hands. And hating Chiang Kai-shek, he could not help thinking of Dai Li — and thinking of Dai Li left a desolation in him.

"Elder brother, you're the man who saved my life. In this life we cannot be born on the same day, but I pray we may die on the same day." What rose before Wang Yaqiao's eyes was the scene in the Anhui Guildhall in 1924, when he and Dai Li smeared blood in covenant. It was not long after he had pulled Dai Chunfeng out from under the car, and the man who had come from Zhejiang to beg his living in Shanghai turned up one day at his gate. By then Dai Chunfeng had shed the beggar's rags for a new Western suit that Wang Yaqiao had given him, and he cut a strikingly martial figure. Wang Yaqiao looked up and thought for a moment he had the wrong man. Washed clean of the blood and filth, Dai Chunfeng proved to be a handsome fellow with a certain boldness between the brows. Wang Yaqiao looked him up and down and said at once: "Dai Chunfeng — by your face, you may be living under another man's roof today, but your day will come. If you don't think it beneath you, stay here in my guildhall."

"Elder brother, thank you for saving my life. If one day your good words come true and I really do rise in the world, I'll never forget what has been done for me today." Seeing himself treated so generously, Dai Chunfeng dropped to his knees on the spot and knocked his head on the floor.

From that time Dai Chunfeng regarded Wang Yaqiao as benefactor and elder brother. He acted in everything by Wang Yaqiao's wishes, and made a name for himself quickly enough in the ten-li foreign strip. Wang Yaqiao saw that Dai Chunfeng, though of low birth, was a quick-witted man with both courage and cunning, and used him everywhere. Sometimes he brought him along to banquets where men of standing gathered — and it was thus, through Wang Yaqiao's connections, that Dai Chunfeng came to know Dai Jitao and Hu Zongnan and others who would later be men of weight in the Nationalist Party. It was in this Shanghai period too that, at Dai Chunfeng's repeated urging, the friendship-loving Wang Yaqiao agreed to bind himself to him in the eight-bow brotherhood. But then, wholly against Wang Yaqiao's expectation, in 1925 Dai Chunfeng received a letter from Dai Jitao, who had gone to Guangzhou. It said: "Chiang Kai-shek is building his own strength here, and under Mr. Sun Yat-sen's direction the Whampoa Academy has been founded. Why not come and study, and look for a day of advancement…"

"Elder brother — since the gangs offer less of a future than the army, hadn't I better go to Guangzhou and join Dai Jitao?" It was about the autumn of that year that Dai Chunfeng finally told Wang Yaqiao his secret: he wanted to go to Guangdong and enter the Whampoa Academy. Wang Yaqiao knew that Dai Jitao was only the pretext and Chiang Kai-shek the destination. Yet though he despised Chiang Kai-shek in his heart, he would not use an elder brother's authority to force his sworn junior to give up the army, and told him firmly: "We're brothers, but I've never compelled any man. Every man has his own ambition; go to Guangdong if you will! Only, when you have risen high, don't forget the bond we swore. If you can weigh friendship heavily, then I, Wang Jiuguang, won't have known you in vain!"

At this Dai Chunfeng dropped to his knees with a thud, knocked his head three times before Wang Yaqiao, and swore with tears running down his face: "Elder brother, believe me — Dai Chunfeng is no ingrate. If my day truly comes, I'll never forget that you saved me."

After they parted, Wang Yaqiao kept an ear out for news of Dai Chunfeng's soldiering in the south. What came back disappointed him again and again. Dai Chunfeng did indeed enter the Whampoa Academy's artillery section on Dai Jitao's introduction — and then, in the course of the Northern Expedition, went over to Chiang Kai-shek's side. By the time the expeditionary army reached Wuhan, Dai Chunfeng had even changed his name, to Dai Li; and the name, they said, was given him by Chiang Kai-shek himself. From that time Dai Li was foreman of the Ten-Man Team, the special-service outfit that gathered intelligence and murdered the party's opponents for Chiang Kai-shek alone.

"Elder brother — do you still know me? I'm the Dai Chunfeng who nearly became a ghost under a car in Shanghai!" It was about January 1927, after Dai Li had followed Chiang Kai-shek — by then commander-in-chief of the National Revolutionary Army — from Hubei to Nanchang, that he was sent on Chiang's orders to Shanghai to make contact with the various forces there. And so he came again to the Anhui Guildhall where he and Wang Yaqiao had exchanged cards and sworn brotherhood. The Dai Li who stood before Wang Yaqiao now was a smart officer of the Northern Expedition army, uniform pressed, bayonet at his belt. He had not, it seemed, forgotten the debt he owed, and greeted Wang Yaqiao with a disciple's courtesy. Over wine they talked warmly, like brothers long parted, and Dai Li gave the Hatchet Gang's chief a fluent account of his life since leaving Shanghai, dwelling in particular on how he had come to know Chiang Kai-shek on the road north. Nor, of course, did he forget the errand that had brought him: he began actually praising Chiang Kai-shek to Wang Yaqiao — how Chiang loved talent, how he valued political men of every stripe. And at last Dai Li came out with his real purpose, and said gravely: "Elder brother, with your gifts you may summon wind and rain on the Shanghai waterfront — but in the end you're only a gang chief. One palm can't clap alone, and though you have the ambitions of a swan you will never make real weather this way. In your younger brother's view, elder brother would do better to do as I've done and go to Nanchang and join Mr. Chiang. I guarantee that once you're under his command your prospects will be without limit. Play it well, and you might even come out a general or a minister. Would that not be a hundred times better than running a little hatchet gang in Shanghai? So I urge you, elder brother…"

Wang Yaqiao thought little of it, but he did not reproach Dai Li to his face for going wherever the milk was. He only shook his head slightly. "Chunfeng, do you imagine that in this world of ours the greatest honor a man can have is some petty office in the Nationalist Party? Not so. I still hold to what I said when you went to Guangdong: every man has his own ambition, and none should be compelled. If you have the itch for office, then go and ask Chiang for one. As for me — from the day I was born I was never the stuff officials are made of. And as for that Chiang: he and I were never carts on the same road. You had better not go on canvassing for him."

Dai Li knew Wang Yaqiao's character well enough to know that high rank and rich pay would never move him. Two days of talk brought nothing, and he withdrew crestfallen. At parting he cupped his hands again and struck his chest, swearing oath on oath: "Elder brother, though our roads run apart from now on, Dai Chunfeng doesn't forget an old kindness. The day you have need of me, I'll spill my brains on the ground and not decline it!"

Later Chiang Kai-shek moved his army from Nanchang to Nanjing and, by political maneuver, secured the chairmanship of the National Government together with the chairmanship of the Nationalist Party's Military Affairs Commission — and merely because Wang Yaqiao spoke a few words from the platform of the celebratory assembly in support of Sun Yat-sen's Three Principles of the People, he brought Chiang Kai-shek's murder down on himself. Had Dai Li not helped him from the shadows, he might truly have died that night under the Ten-Man Team's guns.

Now, thinking back over everything Chiang Kai-shek had done since Sun Yat-sen's death and his own rise, Wang Yaqiao's feelings ran a thousand ways. And his friend Li Shaochuan had just come to his house to plead, on behalf of Hu Hanmin's kinsman Lin Huanting — and lit the old hatred and the new together. Kill this autocrat, this thief of the people, and rid the nation of him! But when he thought of the attendants around Chiang Kai-shek with their German weapons, Wang Yaqiao wavered again. And as he sat there unable to decide, a woman came quietly in. He looked up: his elegant, clear-minded wife, Wang Yaying.

"Yaying — you come at exactly the right moment!" Wang Yaqiao's eyes lit at once.

### 16. A Secret Room in Shanghai, Knives Sharpening in Nanjing

Seeing her husband, who had not touched wine for days, drinking alone until his face was flushed, Wang Yaying felt a private alarm. "Jiuguang — what has happened, that you drink like this today?"

With the wine to help him, Wang Yaqiao simply told her the whole of it: Li Shaochuan's coming to plead for Hu Hanmin, the request that he and his Hatchet Gang brothers rid the nation of its evil by killing Chiang Kai-shek, and every misgiving he had about it. He finished: "Yaying, if I followed the wish I've carried for years, I would long since have torn that Chiang to a thousand pieces and eased my heart. He hates me to the marrow as it's. Only — killing Chiang Kai-shek touches too much. The smallest carelessness and we're shot down by his guards. So for the moment I can't decide."

Wang Yaying understood her husband's difficulty: he wanted to purge the nation's traitor and feared every consequence of the attempt. An ordinary woman would have come forward to dissuade him. But Wang Yaying had been a woman of far-reaching ambition since her girlhood, and in the past, whenever Wang Yaqiao contemplated a great case, he had always asked her counsel before making his decision. Now, facing the question of whether to assassinate the ruling figure of the party and the state, even Wang Yaying felt her nerves tighten. She came to the table, poured herself a cup and drank it down, and her fair, full face took on the color of peach blossom. She sat, thought a moment, and then asked him suddenly: "Jiuguang, I want to ask you one thing. Do you mean to live out your days in mediocrity — or do you hope your name will stand in history?"

Wang Yaqiao was startled and for a long moment made no answer. His wife's deep gaze was fixed on him, as though she had already seen into him. He thought a long while, and then nodded, hard. "A great man stands between heaven and earth. Of course I've no wish to be a muddled wine-sack and rice-bag!"

"Then you mean to accomplish something great?" she pressed.

"To stand in history I wouldn't presume," Wang Yaqiao said gravely. "But Wang Jiuguang is certainly no spiritless coward, no soft egg. What has this to do with killing Chiang?"

The wine drew out what was in her heart. "If you truly mean to be a man who stands between heaven and earth, then what Li Shaochuan asks of you is your best opportunity."

"How so?"

"It's very simple. Consider: since Chiang Kai-shek is a traitor to the national revolution who moves against the current of history, then if you put a bullet in him, who under heaven wouldn't approve of you?"

Wang Yaqiao had not expected words like these from his wife, and after a moment he asked: "Yaying — then you don't oppose my firing on Chiang?"

"Of course not. If my husband accomplishes it, he will beyond all question be a hero of the ages. Everyone knows Chiang Kai-shek became the head of the Nationalist Party by trading on his standing as Mr. Sun Yat-sen's faithful disciple. It's precisely because he set about destroying Mr. Sun's Three Principles and butchering revolutionaries the moment he took power that the anger of ten thousand people rose against him. So in killing Chiang Kai-shek we don't act to save one Hu Hanmin — what matters is that we hoe out a great traitor for the nation!" Wang Yaqiao knew that once his wife spoke, the argument would march in order and the strategy stand a head above others. That she had now set her heart on the risk moved him further still.

"Yaying, well said!" Wang Yaqiao gazed at his startling wife with admiration; her speech had put the last of his hesitation to flight. "My wife, a mere woman, dares face a storm of bullets. Why should I, Wang Yaqiao, a grown man, go soft in the heart and weak in the hand before a strong enemy? Even to die gloriously for the killing of Chiang would be to die in the right place! Only — killing Chiang is no child's game. We must plan it in earnest. Until the plan is seamless as heaven's own robe, we don't move." Wang Yaying was composed and quick at once. She supported her husband in rising to kill Chiang at this best of hours, when the whole country hated him; and she stayed cool and exact, and reckoned the future like a diviner. Seeing his mind now made up, she turned round and urged restraint: "In any great matter, be cool. Without coolness you won't only fail to kill Chiang, you will put yourself past all escape. So there must be no haste. Let us first send several parties to Nanjing and learn the pattern of Chiang Kai-shek's movements. Only then do we act."

Wang Yaqiao said, wholly convinced: "Good. We do it by my wife's plan!"

And while Wang Yaqiao and his wife were laying their secret plans against Chiang, Sun Ke and the others, far off in Guangzhou, were debating the same killing. Wang Jingwei, member of the Nationalist Central Standing Committee, newly returned from abroad, had flown down from Nanjing to Guangzhou; and for a season the party's grandees gathered thick in the south, with the anti-Chiang forces of every road drawn up in the city — much as Sun Yat-sen had once formed a provisional government there. And while these factions were planning a provisional government at Guangzhou, in Wang Jingwei's villa at the foot of Baiyun Mountain another matter of the greatest weight was being laid in secret: the killing of Chiang Kai-shek, and quickly.

"If Chiang Kai-shek is killed at this moment, we not only preserve Mr. Hu Hanmin's life; we leave his Nanjing government a body of dragons without a head. And then Guangzhou becomes the forward post of the Chinese revolution." So Wang Jingwei spoke at the secret meeting on the killing of Chiang.

Sun Ke was naturally the most eager of them. "As for Chiang Kai-shek, that traitor to the Three Principles — were my father alive, he too would approve strong measures. The difficulty is that Chiang Kai-shek lords it in Nanjing, and has long since made himself into a Yuan Shikai. He has so many attendants and guards about him, on watch twenty-four hours a day, that an ordinary assassin could hardly get near him. So there's nothing for it but to seek out that Anhui man in Shanghai, Wang Yaqiao, who killed Zhao Tieqiao."

Tang Shengzhi sighed. "By the intelligence I've from Shanghai, Li Shaochuan has spoken with Wang Yaqiao a number of times, and the man still hasn't made his final decision. It looks as though Wang Yaqiao is waiting to see what attitude we in Guangzhou take before he decides whether to act."

Wang Jingwei, who had himself once gone to Beijing to assassinate the Prince Regent, spoke as one who knew the trade. "That Wang Yaqiao is slow to move is understandable. My own attempt in Beijing had twists enough. And how much more so now, when Chiang Kai-shek is guarded by whole companies and hard to approach? I think that since Wang Yaqiao has tacitly agreed to rid us of this evil, he must have the means to do it. The problem is that we can't go on promising him things by word of mouth; we must give him some support in fact. Once he has our deposit in hand, he won't go on holding back."

"Just so — if Wang Yaqiao is to remove this traitor for us, there must be a fund for the operation." Sun Ke woke to it suddenly. "Since we're asking him to kill Chiang for us, why do we not equip him properly at once?"

Tang Shengzhi said: "Our own funds are stretched thin as it's. How are we to pay out a costly assassination fund?" Wang Jingwei brought his fist down hard on the table. "If it's to cut off that head which is the sworn enemy of the revolution, what harm in paying the troops a few months less? And if we can't raise the sum at once, then I'll open my own purse rather than leave Wang Yaqiao short of what he needs!"

Sun Ke seconded him at once and resolved to give generously. "Good. Since killing Chiang is our first business, let us of this company open our purses. We'll not fail to scrape together the two hundred thousand!"

Before long Wang Jingwei, Sun Ke, Tang Shengzhi and other grandees had raised more than two hundred thousand for the operation. Sun Ke and Wang Jingwei conferred and decided to send two of their most trusted men, carrying a case of the money in secret, quietly out of Guangdong by train, then by ship through Hong Kong to Shanghai.

"Mr. Wang, this is the operating fund the Southwest commanders of Guangdong have raised with all their strength. It shows you what weight Mr. Wang Jingwei and Mr. Sun Ke place on you!" When the two envoys came secretly to the house on Rue Pottier in the French Concession and set the case of banknotes in front of Wang Yaqiao, he was at the tensest stage of his own planning. Seeing the great sum from Guangdong, he refused it on the spot. "Mr. Wang Jingwei and Mr. Sun Ke think too little of Wang Jiuguang. Do they suppose I've held back all this while waiting for their money? Not at all. It's a month to the day since Li Shaochuan first put this business in my hands. How would you know how many secret meetings we have held here already? Whatever I, Wang Yaqiao, mean to do, I would do without a single copper of funding. And conversely — if the man you asked me to kill were a righteous gentleman, you could put up a million and Wang wouldn't touch it."

The money-bearers pleaded hard. "It was always said that Wang Yaqiao was a man of honor, and now I've seen it with my own eyes and know the name is no empty one. But since Mr. Wang hates Chiang as we do, why refuse the operating fund? It's a thing your action can't do without. And if Mr. Wang refuses it, does that not chill the hearts of your supporters in Guangdong?"

"Very well." Seeing the earnestness of it, and hearing what hopes Guangdong placed in the Hatchet Gang, Wang Yaqiao took the money in the end.

One afternoon in late May, a fine drizzle came down over the old city of Nanjing.

Toward evening a passenger steamer up from Shanghai eased in to the Pukou wharf. The rain came steadily, and the whole old city lay under a thick black canopy of cloud. Among the passengers who came down the gangway was a young man in a yellow oilskin, a cultivated-looking man with a pair of shrewd eyes behind black-rimmed spectacles. This was Sun Fengming, who had fled the Northeast for the south and, during the Shanghai fighting, joined Wang Yaqiao's Iron and Blood Traitor-Purging Corps. Standing now on the wharf in the misting rain, gazing through a veil of water at the far bulk of Zhongshan, he heard again Wang Yaqiao's charge to him: "Fengming, we're doing a thing now that will one day stand in the histories. When you reach Nanjing, get Chiang's movements clear as fast as you can, and act as soon as you're able."

"Fengming — we have been waiting a good while for you." Sun Fengming turned quickly to see a small car coming up the muddy lane; out of it stepped a slender woman, his newly married wife Cui Zhengyao. And behind her a man: Yu Likui, the soldier Wang Yaqiao had posted to Nanjing to watch Chiang Kai-shek's movements.

Without wasting words, Sun Fengming took his wife's hand and got quickly into the car, and all the way in they talked quietly with Yu Likui about Wang Yaqiao's instructions. Sun Fengming told him that through May, Wang Yaqiao and the others had been pressing the planning in Shanghai, and that it had reached the final stage. He remembered how from early April he had gone with Hua Kezhi — who led the student-union struggle at Jinling University — secretly from Nanjing to Wang Yaqiao's little house on Rue Pottier, and joined the Iron and Blood Assassination Corps that Wang led. It was a civilian anti-Japanese body, and in it Sun Fengming had met many of like mind. Most were Hatchet Gang men under Wang Yaqiao; but Sun Fengming, an educated man himself, discovered that Wang Yaqiao was not the creature of bandit swagger the popular tales made him. He found Wang Yaqiao to be not the murder-king that made men blanch, but a man with a good deal of the scholar in him. Wang Yaqiao had made blood-cases that shook the country, in Anhui and Nanjing and Shanghai, and had killed with his own hands. Yet in Sun Fengming's dealings with him, what he found was a man full of human feeling. One day the two of them went to eat at the Lao Zhengxing restaurant, and over the table Wang Yaqiao talked of many things that had nothing to do with killing — above all the customs and country of Jiangnan, on which he was fluent and exact, reciting it like a man counting his family treasures. To Sun Fengming, who had been a soldier, Wang Yaqiao seemed a kindly elder.

Only after Hua Kezhi introduced them did Sun Fengming learn that Wang Yaqiao's assassination corps was in fact a body of patriots. Whenever a substantial sum came in, Wang Yaqiao never kept it to himself but shared it among the corps; at festivals he paid these young men who had given themselves to resisting Japan a living allowance besides. And so, with the nation in extremity, Sun Fengming had found a progressive assassination organization he was willing to die for. What thrilled him most was the important task Hua Kezhi set him this spring: "Fengming, Mr. Wang may be about to take major action against Chiang Kai-shek. Do you dare take part?"

"He truly means to kill the autocrat and thief of the people? Dare? Why should I not dare?"

"Mr. Wang has made up his mind."

"Splendid! If we can really rid the country of this traitor, I would spill my head and my blood for it and not begrudge them!"

Sun Fengming, a progressive young man who had longed for revolution since boyhood and was full of passion for Sun Yat-sen's Three Principles, had long hated Chiang Kai-shek's misrule; and learning that Wang Yaqiao was plotting to kill him, the blood came up boiling through him.

Through April, Sun Fengming and Hua Kezhi went almost daily to the house on Rue Pottier. Twenty-odd patriots gathered there, among them Communists such as Chen Wang. With the change of the times, Wang Yaqiao's assassination organization had taken in many who were not of the Hatchet Gang. Among the patriotic young men Sun Fengming met at Wang Yaqiao's house were Zheng Baozhen, Yu Likui, Gong Chunpu, He Guangpo, Zheng Shaocheng, Liu Wencheng, Zheng Hailong and Xie Wenda. All of them felt deeply for Sun Yat-sen's Three Principles, and burned with indignation at Chiang Kai-shek's slaughter of the workers in Shanghai.

"Now we must turn the plan into action at once, and the faster the better." Wang Yaqiao held his secret meetings at home every night.

"There are only two roads to killing Chiang. One is to kill him in Nanjing. The second is to blow up his special train on the road when he goes out to Nanchang or Lushan. Beyond those there's no other way. From my post in Nanjing, watching this past month, Chiang Kai-shek keeps close indoors and gives almost no opening at all. The only route he travels is between Nanjing and Lushan. We can only set our action inside that stretch." So spoke Yu Likui, the soldier posted to Nanjing on Wang Yaqiao's orders to watch Chiang's movements.

"But moving against Chiang in Nanjing is no easy business either." Hua Kezhi was another key man in the operation; years of student work in Nanjing had given him a settled, seasoned temper. "The plainest difficulty is that Chiang Kai-shek has doubled the guard strength of the Attendants' Office, and these men are almost never a step from him. The moment Chiang leaves the Huangpu Road residence, that long motorcade follows. What makes him so hard to strike is that his motorcade runs to twelve cars of various sizes, and you can't know which one Chiang is riding in on any given day. And the moment our killers came near the column, the attendants inside the cars would throw up a dense net of fire with their German pistols and rifles. We have no firepower of that kind, nothing that could beat down men so well trained."

Yu Likui added: "As for striking inside Chiang's residence, that's still more impossible. I've gone to look at the compound many times myself. Never mind entering by the gate — you can't even get near the wall. There's no way of knowing the layout inside; and even if we got in, we couldn't readily find where Chiang lodges, because we would certainly fall into the Attendants' Office's surveillance net. So for action in Nanjing, the only opening is at the Ming Palace airfield, when Chiang is about to leave the city on an inspection."

"Very well, Likui. Go to Nanjing now, and first rent a house near the Ming Palace airfield to use when the time comes." In the end Wang Yaqiao approved the plan they had all agreed on: to fire on Chiang's car at the Ming Palace airfield, with few attendants around him, as he left Nanjing.

And it was carrying this order of Wang Yaqiao's that Sun Fengming had now come from Shanghai to Nanjing. He and Yu Likui went to the rented house near the Ming Palace, and in that little gray Japanese-style building he found seven or eight killers already lying up — members of the special action group Yu Likui had assembled in Nanjing. Among them were early Hatchet Gang men such as Xuan Jimin, Wang Ganting and Niu Anru, and also the newer strength Wang Yaqiao had developed in Shanghai, such as Zheng Shaocheng and He Guangpo.

"Here are the funds and the weapons." Sun Fengming set the heavy leather case in front of Yu Likui and the others and passed on Wang Yaqiao's orders in a breath. "Guangdong is pressing hard now. Mr. Wang requires that we move against Chiang no later than early June. If we drag on, we may lose the best moment. So he wants us to find our opening at once."

The very day after Sun Fengming came to Nanjing with these orders, Yu Likui learned from his source inside the Attendants' Office a piece of intelligence of the first importance: Chiang Kai-shek had decided to go to Lushan in Jiangxi in early June to escape the heat.

For the action group waiting out their hours in the house near the Ming Palace, this was nothing short of splendid news. Yu Likui and the rest understood perfectly: if Chiang Kai-shek went from Nanjing to Lushan, he would take his private aircraft from the Ming Palace airfield — and then their special action group could slip in close to a field where few aircraft came or went at the best of times. Once Chiang Kai-shek stepped out of his car, they could fire on him at short range and kill him before he ever boarded.

"Now everything stands ready; we lack only the east wind." Hearing Yu Likui's plan of ambush, Sun Fengming was greatly stirred. That same night he carried Yu Likui's sealed report quietly back to Shanghai, where Wang Yaqiao and Hua Kezhi were waiting for the plan he brought from Nanjing.

### 17. Nine Jinhua Hams with Guns Inside Them

Lushan, at the southern end of Jiujiang by the shore of Poyang Lake, shows its full grandeur when high summer comes.

When Hua Kezhi brought his party of crack assassins up from Jiujiang, disguised as tourists, it was the cool season on the mountain, with the wind coming steadily over it. He and his eight men of the special action team were all in Western suits and elegantly turned out; they took mountain chairs through the Three Gorges Cave and past Guanyin Bridge and made straight for Hanpokou. All the way up, seated on that soft, swaying chair, he had the look of a man drinking in the scenery at his ease. In fact his nerves were drawn to breaking. He had come to Lushan to murder Chiang Kai-shek.

"Mr. Hua, I've decided to move against Chiang along two lines. Do you dare go to Lushan and carry out the killing there?" That unforgettable midnight two days before, in the dim-lit little house in the French Concession, the smoke had hung thick. A dozen of the core men were gathered around Wang Yaqiao. Sun Fengming had brought exact word from Nanjing that Chiang Kai-shek would go up Lushan on June 6, and Wang Yaqiao called a secret meeting that same night to work out the coming action. Hua Kezhi had not expected Wang Yaqiao to give him the mountain; from the gravity of Wang Yaqiao's face he saw that it was a mark of trust. He had long been confident of the work, and seeing Wang Yaqiao and the whole corps turn their eyes on him he felt his blood come up. "Set your mind at rest, Ninth Brother. Once Hua Kezhi is on Lushan, this traitor dies. And if I can't kill Chiang Kai-shek, I would as soon he killed me!"

Wang Yaqiao was delighted. "Good, Mr. Hua — that's what a man of mine should sound like. Only there are dangers on every side of this. We divide into two lines. The first is Yu Likui and Zheng Baozhen with the first group, acting in Nanjing; they wait at the Ming Palace airfield, and if Chiang Kai-shek is finished there, you come down off the mountain the moment you hear. The second step: if Zheng Baozhen and Yu Likui miss in Nanjing, then everything rests on your finding your chance on Lushan, because by then Chiang Kai-shek will be safely up. You must be in position before Chiang goes up. Once he is on the mountain with Soong Mei-ling, you may as well forget entering it at all. So you must leave for Jiujiang at once, get up Lushan as fast as you can, and decide whether to act on the word I send you from Shanghai."

Hua Kezhi said: "In that case the sooner the better. I go up Lushan now and hide, and act the moment Chiang arrives. Only — which of the brothers goes with me?"

"Mr. Hua, I'll go!" "I'll go too!" "Ninth Master, let me up the mountain — I've waited for this day!" The corps members who had sat so many days in the lamplight, aching for it, saw Wang Yaqiao name Hua Kezhi as his commander and pressed forward, rubbing their fists.

"Now that's what my brothers should be." Wang Yaqiao himself named Chen Cheng, Zhao Shifa, Long Lin, Tang Ming, Zhang Xianting and three others — eight men of the assassination team — to leave Shanghai with Hua Kezhi that night.

Having taken the charge gladly, Hua Kezhi added a worry. "For the sake of the work, Chen Cheng and I went quietly up Lushan a month ago. We found that around the Meilu villa, where Soong Mei-ling and Chiang Kai-shek pass the summer, there's almost nowhere to hide. Near those senior Nationalists' villas there are no inns for ordinary travelers. So we can only lie up around Taiyi Village; there are some middling and lower inns near Guling Street."

Chen Cheng was a quick and calculating assassin, and on that earlier trip with Hua Kezhi he had scouted the whole villa quarter. He had found the Meilu the least approachable of all — Nationalist operatives watching it secretly on every side, a stranger scarcely able to get near. Having reported the ground to Wang Yaqiao, he said: "As I see it, a strike at the Meilu itself is very nearly impossible. Even if we could get near it after going up, we couldn't break the three lines of defense the Attendants' Office has laid in front of it. So the best plan is to take Chiang and Soong on the road, once they have left the Meilu to go touring the mountain. The mountain paths are the best killing-ground."

Hua Kezhi agreed. "Chen Cheng's idea is a good one. Since Chiang Kai-shek is going up Lushan, he won't shut himself in the Meilu every day. And the moment he comes out, we'll find our chance to shoot. The mountain is nothing but winding paths. He may bring many attendants, but on a mountain path they have little room to work. That's when we can hit our target from cover. The problem now is how our weapons are to get up Lushan at all — that road is nothing but checkpoints."

Wang Yaqiao thought highly of the plan of ambush Hua Kezhi and Chen Cheng laid out. But when the talk came to getting guns and ammunition up the mountain, he too found himself without a move. Hua Kezhi, Chen Cheng and the rest — nine men — needed eight or nine pistols at the least. Yet Lushan had always been the summer preserve of Nationalist military and political dignitaries, so ordinary tourists with a special permit to go up were rare as phoenix feathers to begin with; and with checkpoints the whole way, carrying guns concealed was next to impossible. Wang Yaqiao had worked his Nanjing connections and had managed nine permits — but there was no conceivable way to carry weapons up. Ever since Chiang Kai-shek became chairman of the National Government and began taking Soong Mei-ling up Lushan every summer, layer upon layer of military-police checkpoints had been set along the road from Jiujiang. The agents made a specialty of searching tourists' luggage, and not even women were spared; still less would nine men together escape notice. How was their baggage to get past ten and more checkpoints?

"It's a hard problem — but never mind; you go up." Seeing Hua Kezhi and the others tied in knots over it, and knowing no answer would come at once, Wang Yaqiao said: "As for how the guns go up, leave that to me. In short: Mr. Hua pitches camp on Lushan tomorrow, and I guarantee that before the action, I'll have men deliver the weapons on time."

Now, riding his mountain chair into Guling Street, Hua Kezhi had it set down. He went into a wine shop by the road and settled himself, and before long Chen Cheng, Zhao Shifa, Long Lin, Tang Ming, Zhang Xianting and the rest of the eight came in as well. Hua Kezhi ordered a few plates of fresh fruit and delicacies, and the nine sat round a table drinking. As he ate he watched the street warily, and saw agents' figures passing along Guling Street from time to time. Quietly he told Chen Cheng, Zhao Shifa and Long Lin: "After the wine, we go up separately. I noticed on the road from Jiujiang that our party has already drawn the sentries' attention. We didn't think it through before we came — what nine men ever went up a mountain sightseeing together? And we're all carrying Hong Kong passports, which makes us more conspicuous still."

Chen Cheng and Long Lin, recalling the many suspicious glances on the way up, agreed that nine made too large a mark. Hua Kezhi set his cup down. "Presently Chen Cheng, Long Lin and I'll take chairs straight up to Taiyi Peak. The rest of you go into the mountain separately, and on foot if you can. That spreads the target and keeps the people up here from wondering. And the Taibai Hotel that Chen Cheng and I booked below Taiyi Peak last time can't be used again either. The best course is for us to lodge in three separate small inns. Otherwise a tall tree draws the wind, and the police up here make trouble on their night rounds."

"Right — break the whole into parts and move separately." Chen Cheng thought the arrangement thoroughly sound.

"Remember: once we're in our inns below Taiyi Peak, no open contact by day; watch for plainclothes." Hua Kezhi divided them into three parties under himself, Chen Cheng and Long Lin, chose three hilltop inns, and fixed the recognition signals and meeting places for the three groups. About half an hour later they took a hurried lunch and set off separately along Guling Street toward the mountain road to Taiyi Peak.

In Shanghai the rain went on and on.

After Hua Kezhi and the eight others left for Lushan, Wang Yaqiao spent whole days in the Concession house working at the problem of getting nine German pistols secretly up the mountain. And as he sat there without a plan, his wife's slender figure came in at the door again. She was as calm and clear as ever; her husband's day-and-night plotting of a murder had not altered her by a hair. Wang Yaqiao had always admired that unshakable steadiness in her. Now, seeing his brows knotted, she asked: "Jiuguang, everyone is up the mountain. Is there still something that won't go right?"

"Ai. This one is too hard." Wang Yaqiao told her the whole difficulty of the weapons. Wang Yaying listened, stood thinking a long while at the French window, and then her fine eyes flashed. "I've it! Yaqiao, this need not defeat you — why did you not tell me sooner?"

"Does my wife really have a way?"

"Naturally there's a way. Let me go up Lushan myself and be done with it."

"You go yourself? There are gates of hell on that mountain! It won't do — you must on no account go. Lushan is a place of trouble now. How could a woman run such a risk and carry weapons into the mountain?"

Seeing Wang Yaqiao waving his hands in agitation, Wang Yaying could not help laughing. "And what of a woman? Am I, Wang Yaying, not to be allowed to face a storm of bullets? Jiuguang, I've followed you all these years. Do you still doubt my nerve?"

"It isn't your nerve I doubt. But to carry eight or nine pistols up that mountain is no child's play. If they find a gun on you it costs your head! You should know that the men guarding that mountain for Chiang Kai-shek and the Nationalist dignitaries now search even a woman's underclothes. You can't work Sun Wukong's seventy-two transformations. How would you get those killing tools up there? It absolutely won't do!"

Wang Yaying laughed coldly. "Jiuguang, I laugh at you men. You go about shaking your arrogance out of every limb, and you have none of a woman's quickness. However hard the checkpoints search, there's always somewhere they won't look."

Seeing his wife in such earnest, and knowing Wang Yaying never talked emptily, Wang Yaqiao asked quickly: "What is your plan? Let me hear it. If it will really serve, I'll of course agree to your going. But if you have no extraordinary art, then don't play the hero for nothing — that would be gambling with your own head!"

Wang Yaying gave an unfathomable smile and struck her chest once. "Jiuguang, do as I tell you and nothing will go wrong. First send men to buy eight or nine Jinhua hams. I've a use for them."

"What do you want Jinhua hams for? Is this a joke?"

"Jinhua ham is a fine thing. Just get me the best there's; I'll have my use for it when the time comes." Knowing his wife's cleverness, Wang Yaqiao asked no more, and sent men off to Jinhua in Zhejiang, who bought overnight and brought back nine hams of tempting color and excellent grade.

Wang Yaying took the nine glossy hams in her hands, looked each one over, and sent the servants out. Then she found a sharp, quick knife and, working deftly, pared the meat out of each ham stroke by stroke. Next she quietly stripped down the nine German pistols Wang Yaqiao had sent in, until in a blink they were so many loose components; these she wrapped carefully in oiled cotton and packed, divided up, into the hollowed hams. Then she sewed every cut ham closed with fine, close stitches, and sealed the stitching over with oiled clay. When it was all done she set the nine hams out on the table and called Wang Yaqiao to look. Under the lamp the nine hams shone splendidly, mouth-watering, with not a flaw to be seen.

"Heaven — Yaying, you have been performing conjuring tricks!" Wang Yaqiao understood the whole of it at once, and clapped his hands and called it marvelous. "Marvelous! Only, carrying these hollowed hams up Lushan is no small thing. A woman with less nerve would simply never do it."

Wang Yaying clearly had it all drawn out already. "I've said it: I take all these weapons up Lushan myself. Only I'll need a quick-witted woman assistant besides."

"Ah — you mean to go up as a traveling woman scholar? Then of course you must have a maid with you."

"No. I'm not going as a scholar. I'm going up as the wife of a Nationalist dignitary."

By now Wang Yaqiao saw his wife's whole plan, brocade bag and all. A person came into his mind. "Yaying — let Yafeng go up Lushan with you. How would that be?"

Wang Yafeng was Wang Yaqiao's own younger sister, and she too had come from Anhui to Shanghai by then. The suggestion suited Wang Yaying exactly. Wang Yafeng had studied at the Hefei girls' high school; she knew books and manners, she was quick and shrewd, she understood what mattered, and she had long hated Chiang Kai-shek's misrule. So Wang Yaying nodded again and again. "Yafeng with me is best of all. Only — to make me a real official's wife in the eyes of those soldier-ruffians, I had better have two or three orderlies at my side as well. With that, I guarantee I can carry these hams up the mountain without a particle of trouble."

Wang Yaqiao too felt his wife's method wonderfully sound. He sent Sun Fengming and two other corps members to go up with Wang Yaying and Wang Yafeng. Two days later the party sailed from Shanghai for Jiujiang, and from the Jiujiang wharf went up Lushan.

All the way up, she and Yafeng rode in hired mountain chairs. Wang Yaying wore a close-fitting purple-red qipao and red high-heeled shoes, with a snow-white Panama sun hat on her head; and with her elegant, well-bred bearing, she looked every inch the lady of a high official. Wang Yafeng, beside her in plain dress and light makeup, was to all appearances a second concubine. Sun Fengming played the attending aide-de-camp, in a Western suit, cultivated and polished, running back and forth and busy as a man could be. The other two killers shouldered two cases of luggage and sweated through their clothes. Past every checkpoint the women held themselves perfectly at ease. In the eyes of those Nationalist soldiers, only great ladies and concubines such as Wang Yaying and Wang Yafeng had the standing to go up a Lushan thronged in summer with the carriages of the great and with women in clouds. And that their luggage held first-rate Jinhua hams would naturally rouse no sentry's interest, since carrying gifts up the mountain was perfectly reasonable. Who would imagine guns and ammunition inside the hams?

When the soldiers at one checkpoint made to search Wang Yaying's luggage, she said grandly from her chair: "Let them look. There's no opium and no pistols in my things — what have I to fear?"

Sun Fengming was tense at first. But when the sentries opened the two cases, what they found astonished them: letters in the hands of Chiang Kai-shek, Feng Yuxiang, Zhang Xueliang and Soong Mei-ling to the lady of the house; fine women's cosmetics; jade and jewelry of the kind the overseas Chinese of the South Seas carry — dazzling stuff. The soldiers did notice eight or nine glossy Jinhua hams, which caught the eye somewhat; but they took them for gifts the lady was carrying up to some dignitary, and who would dare rummage further? So the party passed the whole way unhindered.

Before long Wang Yaying's party had passed along the main street of the little town of Guling and were climbing the winding mountain road toward Taiyi Peak. It was not until they were nearly at the top that Sun Fengming let out his breath. And then he noticed that Wang Yaying's forehead too was beaded with sweat. As for Wang Yafeng, who had climbed with them, she was white with fright. But they both knew that the gate of hell had been passed at last.

### 18. Hua Kezhi Musters His Men on Lushan

Nanjing was murderously hot; since the start of June it had become a furnace where a man could hardly draw breath.

Yu Likui and his action group had been lying up all this while in a residential quarter not far from the Ming Palace airfield. On June 3 — the very day Wang Yaying and Sun Fengming took the assassination weapons up Lushan — Wang Yaqiao secretly sent Zheng Baozhen from Shanghai to Nanjing to inform Yu Likui that Hua Kezhi, Chen Cheng and the other nine were on the mountain awaiting orders, and that Wang Yaying, Wang Yafeng and Sun Fengming had gone up to support them.

"Of course, the Ninth Master still hopes you will succeed in Nanjing." Having passed on Wang Yaqiao's orders, Zheng Baozhen added: "Because Lushan carries the greater risk. In Nanjing, a few shots at long range as Chiang boards the plane will finish him, and everyone can scatter easily afterward; but after a killing on Lushan, getting down the mountain is dangerous in itself."

Yu Likui was much taken with the new weapons Zheng Baozhen had brought. The dumdum guns Wang Yaqiao had bought in Shanghai through a foreigner had a special efficacy at long range: once one of their bullets entered a man's body, the poison could kill him within half an hour.

On June 6, at the first light, Zheng Baozhen, Yu Likui and their group moved into the firing position they had chosen: a thick maple wood on the sunrise slope at the foot of Purple Mountain. They hid themselves deep among the trees, from where they could look down on the runway of the Ming Palace airfield. If the intelligence from the chairman's Attendants' Office was accurate, then at nine that morning Chiang Kai-shek and his wife Soong Mei-ling would take off from here for Lushan in Jiangxi.

As the light came up, Yu Likui and the others saw that an aircraft did indeed stand on the runway, and recognized it as Chiang Kai-shek's own plane, the Meiling. At the sight of it their blood rose, for now they could shoot at old Chiang from here with the dumdum guns. One had only to imagine that fearsome bullet flying out of the thick wood at a Chiang Kai-shek in the act of boarding, and the hero-villain of an age would be a ghost under the gun in a moment.

But the minutes and the seconds went by, and the aircraft showed no sign whatever of departing. No mechanics or ground crew appeared on the field, and there was none of the tight security of a great man's departure. Zheng Baozhen kept taking out his pocket watch; and when the hands stood at half past nine, he foresaw that all the enormous preparation of Yu Likui's Nanjing group had come to a stretch of perfectly meaningless waiting.

Yet Yu Likui and the others still believed their intelligence absolutely. And so the party waited in the dense woods of Purple Mountain a whole day and a whole night, and did not return to their lodgings until the small hours of the next morning. And then the news came that dashed them all: Chiang Kai-shek and Soong Mei-ling had left the night before, secretly, by warship from the Pukou wharf at Nanjing, bound for Jiujiang. So the killing-ground Yu Likui had laid at the Ming Palace airfield had come to nothing at all.

Word that the Nanjing chance had been lost reached Taiyi Peak on Lushan.

Hua Kezhi's feelings at the news were naturally mixed. He regretted the failure of the airfield ambush Wang Yaqiao had designed — and understood at the same time that his own responsibility had grown far heavier, since Wang Yaqiao's whole plan now rested on his group alone. Before long, word that Chiang Kai-shek and his wife were on the mountain was confirmed too. The sense of a sacred mission left Hua Kezhi and the killers in the three inns near Taiyi Peak both elated and tense: their target was within arm's reach, and the nine long-range German pistols had been delivered to their hands on time. In these circumstances Hua Kezhi resolved to put Wang Yaqiao's plan into effect at once.

Hua Kezhi went first to reconnoiter the Meilu near Guling Street — and found it a forbidden zone that could not be approached at all. Hidden in the grass a few hundred meters off, he looked across at the little European house standing in dense green shade by the Changchong River, and saw that it was indeed sited at a commanding point and heavily guarded. The Meilu had the crags of Dayue Mountain at its back, cliffs rising and falling to the north, and a deep ravine mouth to the south; no ordinary person could simply walk in.

Until then Hua Kezhi had known only that the Meilu was a mountain villa built in 1922 by a well-known Englishwoman, a doctor whose name the Chinese renders as Heli, and named at the time No. 13 Guling Street. In 1930, on the eve of the Englishwoman's return to London, she gave it to Soong Mei-ling as a Christmas present, and from then on it was where Chiang and Soong passed their summers. Because Soong Mei-ling considered the number 13 unlucky, it was renamed the Meilu. Now, finding sentries with loaded rifles posted on every side of it, Hua Kezhi felt that to strike at Chiang inside the Meilu would be harder than climbing to heaven.

Afterward he and Chen Cheng went to scout the "Meiling Bridge" not far off, and found that although a man could lie up there, the ground all around was running water, and if a shot were fired, escape became a serious problem. So Hua Kezhi abandoned at last the idea of a killing at Chiang Kai-shek's residence.

Wang Yaying had completed her delivery of the guns, but she did not go straight down. Lushan's beauty is a pleasure and its scenery all its own, yet she had no heart to look at it; her whole mind ran on how Chiang might be killed on the mountain, and quickly. In those days Wang Yaying lay low at the Mountain Village Inn below Taiyi Peak. She dared not show herself openly, and came out only at night to make contact with Hua Kezhi and Sun Fengming. She made the White Deer Grotto Academy below Wulao Peak their meeting point. Under the still moonlight, Wang Yaying would pace before the Hall of Preserved Books.

Hua Kezhi's report ran thus: in the last few days Chiang Kai-shek and Soong Mei-ling had gone out very little, and when they did it was only to Donglin Temple on the northern foot of the mountain, to see the Spring of Wisdom and the White Lotus Temple and other old sites, and once to Longshou Rock. Even on those two outings a large body of guards went with them, and no other tourist could get near along the road, still less fire on a Chiang and a Soong seated in mountain chairs.

"What makes it harder is that Chiang's movements are never fixed. Today he goes here, tomorrow there — always, it seems, decided on the spur of the moment. So the method of lying in wait beforehand is next to unworkable. And he has too many attendants: at a rough count, forty and more. Every one of them carries two guns and patrols the surroundings constantly. Let them see a suspicious passer-by and at best he is barked at, at worst arrested on the spot. Chiang Kai-shek travels under heavier guard now than an emperor of the old dynasties on progress. Still — these last days we have found one regularity: Chiang goes out mostly in the morning, and rarely in the afternoon, when the weather turns hot." "Mr. Hua, don't be impatient; that's already a real gain. Once you know Chiang's daily hours, you can find the pattern of his movements." Wang Yaying, staying on the mountain, had become Hua Kezhi's operational adviser.

"We have his pattern now," said Hua Kezhi excitedly one evening, meeting Wang Yaying again at the White Deer Grotto Academy. "For the first few days Chiang moved as he pleased. Then he and Soong became regular. Chen Cheng and I found that the pair of them leave almost every morning for the northeast slope of Lushan. They seem never to have gone to the south slope or the west. Because Donglin Temple is on the north slope. If we post men on the northeast slope, one day we'll meet Chiang going up to burn incense there."

"Very well — that's the fruit of your many days' shadowing." Wang Yaying thought, and then dissented. "But you must also guard against Chiang. He is a man who doubts everything. Go often enough to the north slope and he will begin to fear he is forming a pattern that outsiders may learn — and then the north slope becomes unsafe precisely because of it. I went up the north slope yesterday morning on the pretext of sightseeing, and found the ground there quite unsuited to an ambush: broad flagstone paths, with no line of retreat at all if we were spotted. So I would hope for nothing from the north slope. The ideal ambush ground, I think, is Lushan's southwest slope."

"The southwest slope?" Hua Kezhi was taken aback.

Wang Yaying laid it out in order. "I find the southwest of Lushan is nothing but goat-track paths, and most of them steep and hard going. And where the little paths run, the sides are mostly under tree cover, which makes concealment easy. That's why it's the best ground for us."

Hua Kezhi sighed. "A pity, then, that Chiang won't go in the southwest direction. Perhaps it's precisely because the road is hard and thoroughly unsafe that he refuses to go there."

But Wang Yaying shook her head. "I think he must go to the southwest slope one day. Chiang is a man of unfixed movements, and there are many antiquities on the southwest slope; sooner or later he will go. So I urge you to make your preparations there, scout the ground early, and lay your ambush. The war books say: appear where you aren't expected, attack where he isn't prepared. That's exactly the principle here."

Finding real sense in it, and knowing besides how dangerous an attack on the northeast slope would be, Hua Kezhi agreed. "Very well, madam. We'll keep men waiting on the southwest slope, and the day Chiang shows his head there, one shot finishes him!"

After the meeting with Wang Yaying, Hua Kezhi went back to the Taiyi Hotel and called a special action meeting that same night, redeployed his men, and drew up a plan to kill Chiang on the mountain paths of the southwest slope. This time Hua Kezhi's dispositions were thorough. He set assassins along every road Chiang Kai-shek might travel, in pairs, forming a mutually supporting net of ambush. Specifically: at every point on the necessary up-and-down routes of the southwest slope — Lulin Bridge, Hanpokou, Wulong Pool, the Imperial Stele Pavilion, Tianchi Temple, Taiyi Village, Lion Rock — killers were hidden, disguised as ordinary sightseers, waiting for Chiang Kai-shek to pass. Once Chiang appeared, whichever group he met was to fire without letting the moment slip.

Hua Kezhi and Sun Fengming formed one group, and to his men he swore this oath: "Whoever kills Chiang must get away fast. And if a man falls into Chiang's hands, he dies before he tells the truth. Die to accomplish benevolence; give up life to hold to the right!"

That night the assassins under Hua Kezhi drank cups of blood-wine in the suite of a small inn below Taiyi Peak and swore a common oath: until Chiang Kai-shek's head was taken, there would be no rest.

And on the second day after Hua Kezhi, following Wang Yaying's advice, had thrown his whole strength onto the southwest slope, the Chiang Kai-shek who had never once gone sightseeing there since coming up the mountain appeared on it at last.

Why did Chiang Kai-shek suddenly go to the southwest slope? For that, thanks are owed to Soong Mei-ling.

Inside the Meilu the setting was elegant and quiet, and the garden in particular gave Chiang Kai-shek and his wife a feeling of ease. Sitting on the little balcony, one could see the magnolia in the garden, twenty meters high. And the trumpet creeper the English doctor had planted years before had climbed the wall of the house to the third-floor balcony and made one green mass of it.

"Darling, how can we go on circling round the northeast slope every day like this? Kept up long enough, won't it form a pattern? And then, if anyone is watching our movements from the dark, it becomes far too dangerous!" It was Soong Mei-ling speaking. On the very afternoon of the day when Wang Yaying and Hua Kezhi met under the moon at the White Deer Grotto Academy to lay their plans, Soong Mei-ling suddenly raised a question that startled the guards considerably. She said to Chiang Kai-shek, who was sitting reading a telegram: "Before we ever came up, didn't Dai Yunong send urgent telegrams again and again, saying the Shanghai Hatchet Gang men are watching your movements from cover all the time? I wonder — could Wang Yaqiao be in league with those opposition people in the south?"

What Chiang Kai-shek was reading happened to be exactly a coded telegram from Dai Li in Shanghai.

It said that word had lately come through a thoroughly reliable channel that the southern government was doing everything possible to send men secretly to Shanghai, and that there was also a report of the Hatchet Gang being bought with a heavy sum. Suspicious indications had been found, though the details were not clear. It urged the Generalissimo to increase his precautions on the mountain and to beware of assassins coming up.

Chiang Kai-shek read Dai Li's telegram over several times. He had thought little of it at first; only after his wife's warning did he take it seriously. He knew Dai Li's telegrams were never wind out of an empty cave. If Wang Jingwei and Sun Ke were secretly in league with Wang Yaqiao, the consequences did not bear thinking about. Remembering the Anhui man who had dared attack him openly to his face in 1928, Chiang Kai-shek felt a spasm of tension. Nor could he forget the photograph of the scene at the gate of the China Merchants company in Shanghai, where that Zhao Tieqiao who had come over to him died so wretchedly. That bloody corpse still put a chill in Chiang Kai-shek whenever it came to mind.

"What was that, my dear?" Chiang Kai-shek wiped the cold sweat from his forehead with a handkerchief and turned to find several of his senior guards standing at attention behind him, listening respectfully to Soong Mei-ling. "I say there's no great harm in caution. Why is it that since coming up Lushan we go strolling on the northeast slope every single morning? Kept up long enough, the time and the place both become a pattern in other people's eyes." Soong Mei-ling was a woman of quick mind who had made her weather on the political stage. Ever since Chiang Kai-shek confined Hu Hanmin, and the Nationalist opposition led by Sun Ke and Wang Jingwei resigned their offices in numbers and flew off to Guangdong, Soong Mei-ling had been obliged to think privately about what might befall her husband. Even on these sightseeing outings she watched everything around her; anywhere she and Chiang passed, the least irregularity and she would order the attendants to run it to the ground. Today it had struck her all at once that there was a very serious error in going to the same place every day.

"Madam is over-suspicious." Yu Jishi, the Attendants' Office commander standing behind the pair, who had long kept silent, now came forward to answer. Yu Jishi was Chiang Kai-shek's fellow townsman from Fenghua in Zhejiang and was besides connected to the Chiang family by marriage; serving as their attendant officer, he was the man Chiang and Soong trusted most. Now, seeing Soong Mei-ling question the route he had chosen, he stepped up quickly to defend it. "It's like this, madam. Before Chairman Chiang came up Lushan we had already been over the ground here. We found that although the southwest offers more famous sights, the terrain is precipitous. Going up and coming down, the paths are as thin as goat tracks, and there's no protecting the Chairman and yourself. Whereas the northeast slope has level ground and broad roads. In such places, even if someone wished to hide himself, it would be simply impossible. For the safety of the Chairman and yourself, we had to choose the northeast slope."

"You meant well, of course. But if Mr. Chiang is always taken to the same place, then his growing bored of it's the lesser matter — what if it draws somebody's notice and something happens?" Soong Mei-ling's argument stood on its evidence, and Yu Jishi and the other attendants nodded and nodded; who would contradict her to her face?

"You may all withdraw. Since madam says the southwest slope, tomorrow we'll go sightseeing there!" Chiang Kai-shek waved Yu Jishi and the rest away, signaling that they were to do as Soong Mei-ling wished. But Soong Mei-ling called Yu Jishi back. She was plainly in earnest about Chiang's safety on Lushan, and charged him particularly: "But this by no means implies that we relax our guard because we go to the southwest slope. I mean that the pattern of Mr. Chiang's outings must on no account be detectable by the tourists on this mountain. That would be extremely dangerous. As for how we're to tour the southwest slope, Yu Jishi, you will still produce me a route map that can't fail. Otherwise, if anything happens, I'll hold you all to account!"

After the guard officers had withdrawn with their nerves showing, Chiang Kai-shek let Soong Mei-ling read Dai Li's telegram. Reading it, her heart rose in her. "By Dai Yunong's telegram, the southern opposition has already begun to move. That they're in league with Wang Yaqiao in particular shows how serious this is. Darling, now that we're on the mountain we ought to be more careful than ever. If Wang Yaqiao's men slip up here too, then even our Meilu isn't safe."

"No, no, my dear." Though the telegram had unsettled him as well, Chiang Kai-shek did not in the end believe Sun Ke and Wang Jingwei would dare buy assassins to move against him, and laughed. "Wang Jingwei and Sun Ke hate me in their hearts, but I judge neither has the nerve for that. As for that Anhui man named Wang, I've long been on guard against him; if he came up Lushan, Dai Yunong wouldn't fail to know it."

Still Soong Mei-ling was uneasy. "Darling, caution costs nothing. These days Lushan has been calm enough — and yet, for some reason, my heart is tight with fear all the time, as though a dreadful shadow keeps flickering in front of my eyes. I worry that Hu Hanmin's people may buy killers and send them up."

"They won't, my dear. Has Hu Hanmin such strength as that to set against me?" Seeing her so tense, Chiang Kai-shek laughed it off coldly. And that night, in the Meilu, Chiang Kai-shek had a nightmare. In it a big man with a heavy beard came at him and Soong Mei-ling with a pistol in each hand, roaring: "Chiang! You have killed so many innocent people — today Wang Yaqiao comes on behalf of all those wronged ghosts to collect the blood debt!" Chiang Kai-shek fled, but Wang Yaqiao came after him fast; and before Chiang could get out of the Meilu, shots broke out behind him. Chiang Kai-shek gave a great cry and woke with a start.

At first light next morning Chiang Kai-shek told Soong Mei-ling of the fearful dream, and she, uneasy already, said quickly: "Darling, since we both have this feeling of ill omen, let us simply not go out today."

But by afternoon Chiang Kai-shek, bored and restless in the Meilu, said to her: "My dear, the truth is that dreams at night come of hearing too much advice by day. Who would dare come up Lushan to trouble us? Why not go out for a walk — where's the harm?"

### 19. Clear Gunfire on Lushan

That afternoon the Chiangs and their party went out from the Meilu again.

Since this was the first time Yu Jishi and his large body of guards had gone toward the southwest of Lushan, the precautions along the way were redoubled. Chiang Kai-shek and Soong Mei-ling came to the Immortal's Cave, where great rocks stand jagged and an ancient pine rises among the crags behind a huge toad-shaped stone, dark green and straight. He led Soong by the hand inside and found a spring dripping within, clear water running between the stones, and on the wall four characters left by some ancient hand: JADE LIQUOR OF THE CAVERN HEAVEN. It was truly a fine resting place for immortals.

Chiang Kai-shek and Soong Mei-ling enjoyed themselves greatly in the Immortal's Cave. But never in their dreams could they have guessed what was coming: on the way back, Yu Jishi suddenly noticed a strange heap of something in the grass by the path, half buried in the earth. He called the guards over quickly and they scratched in the grass — and it proved to be eight or nine empty Jinhua ham casings! Yu Jishi lifted one on a stick: the ham, dried out by the summer wind, was an empty skin and nothing more.

Yu Jishi did not dare disturb Chiang Kai-shek and Soong Mei-ling. That night he called several trusted men together and they examined the ham skins from the grass over and over. In the end they concluded that these ordinary-looking ham casings had all been carefully pared open with a knife, and that sharp implements had been hidden inside. He said: "I dare state it: iron objects of some kind were concealed in these ham skins. Even if not weapons, they were contraband forbidden on the mountain — opium and the like. If opium, no great matter. What I fear is guns and ammunition; and if that's what it was, we're in serious trouble!"

Hearing Yu Jishi's analysis, several of the attendant officers felt the gravity of it. "Better urge Mr. Chiang to change his route. Otherwise, if something serious happens, we guards will hardly escape our share." "Just so, just so — the business of the ham skins ought to be reported to madam as well; seeing these things, she might change her mind." So the attendants in the discussion advised Yu Jishi, seven mouths and eight tongues at once. But Yu Jishi had his own view. He knew that inside the Meilu it was less Chiang Kai-shek's word that settled matters than Soong Mei-ling's, whose single word weighed as nine tripods; and since Soong Mei-ling had already decided on the southwest slope, to propose the contrary would certainly meet her opposition. He cut across them: "If there truly are men with evil designs on this mountain, then even if we avoid the southwest slope they will strike on the north. That being so, the only course is to strengthen the guard. There's no other."

That same night Yu Jishi settled a new scheme for protecting Chiang and Soong: one party open and one concealed, two guard details moving in parallel, to keep any suspicious person from approaching Chiang Kai-shek at any time.

Yu Jishi said: "We divide the guard regiment into two detachments. One goes as before, openly accompanying Mr. Chiang and madam wherever they tour. And I'll take the other detachment and follow quietly through the deep woods. That way, if there really is some assassin bold enough to plot against Mr. Chiang, then even if our open guards fail to spot him, the concealed party will."

"An excellent method!" The meeting approved Yu Jishi's arrangement with one voice.

On the morning of June 17, under a heavy overcast, a fine rain began to fall on Lushan, adding a thread of coolness to the sultry air. When Chiang Kai-shek and his wife left Lion Rock escorted by a detachment of armed attendants, Yu Jishi took a dozen and more men and slipped quietly into a dense stand of bamboo nearby. This was the first time since the two-party scheme began that Yu Jishi had personally led the concealed guard. His party spread into a skirmish line deep in the bamboo and followed, step for step, the column moving slowly along the crags under umbrellas toward the Meilu.

For several days Yu Jishi had not been able to sleep, fearing constantly that something unforeseen would happen on the mountain. Today, with the fine rain falling, his tension and anxiety were worse than ever, for reasons he could not name. About an hour on, the rain grew heavier. And then Yu Jishi suddenly saw, in the bamboo not far ahead, the back of a man in a Yunhu shirt. He started violently; the man flickered forward and was gone. Such dress was not rare on Lushan — the magnates and great merchants who came up from Southeast Asia to escape the heat mostly favored these Hong Kong-made shirts. But among Yu Jishi's own attendants very few wore so conspicuous a short-sleeved garment. And it was raining now; the only people walking quietly in that thick bamboo were the plainclothes men of the Attendants' Office under Yu Jishi, ordinary tourists having gone back to their inns out of the rain. Could it be an assassin? The moment the thought flashed into his head, Yu Jishi's heart began to hammer. He could see the stranger in the Yunhu shirt now, easing forward through the bamboo toward a huge bun-shaped boulder ahead. Yu Jishi quickened his pace; and when he came within a few yards of the boulder he suddenly saw the man's hand coming up from his waist — and knew there was a gun gripped in it. Through the layered green of the bamboo Yu Jishi could already see, on the high path across the way, a file of plainclothes men coming along under umbrellas, and among them two mountain chairs bearing Chiang Kai-shek and Soong Mei-ling in their rain capes. Heaven — this man was the assassin! In that instant Yu Jishi's heart nearly came out of his chest; he feared his own tension would startle the killer lying behind the boulder to take Chiang from ambush. And at that very moment the assassin in the Yunhu shirt spun round and thrust a cold, hard face into full view. In terror Yu Jishi shouted to the attendants on either hand and they rushed him together.

The man was none other than Chen Cheng, deputy commander of the special action team.

The night before, Chen Cheng and Hua Kezhi had sat plotting until three in the morning, and Chen Cheng had concluded that Chiang Kai-shek would very likely go from the Meilu to Lion Rock the following forenoon. For by his reckoning, of the sights on the western slope, Lion Rock was the only one Chiang had not yet visited in these last days; and Lion Rock is a place of fine scenery and many famous relics — the Hundred-Fathom Stair is there, and the Square Seal Stone. He said to Hua Kezhi: "If Chiang really does go there, it's a good place to kill him. I've looked over the ground around Lion Rock: it's mostly cliffs and precipices, deep caves and ravines. Even the paths among the crags are mostly wide enough for one man alone. Once they truly go to that place, I can put him to death by myself."

"All the same, we can't be careless." Hearing Chen Cheng's description of the ground at Lion Rock, Hua Kezhi at once agreed it was the ideal district for the killing. "If Chiang really goes there, we must send more men. One man lying in ambush there, even if he succeeds, has no way out."

On Hua Kezhi's strong insistence Chen Cheng agreed at last that he, Hua Kezhi and Sun Fengming should go together to lie in wait at Lion Rock. The next forenoon, not long after the three of them came out, the sky clouded over; soon a rain fine as ox-hair began drifting down and the air turned cool. Following Hua Kezhi's arrangement, Chen Cheng hid at the edge of the thick bamboo on the left of Lion Rock. From this place of concealment he could see, on the crags opposite, the road that must be taken from Lion Rock back to the Meilu — a goat track along the middle of the slope. If Chiang Kai-shek passed that way today, he could burst out and fire on him at short range as he made his difficult passage across the rock face.

Chen Cheng had not been in the bamboo long when the rain came down in earnest. In the hiss of it the grove was uncommonly still. And then Chen Cheng heard a confusion of footsteps ahead. He looked toward the sound, and his heart began to pound. On the path along the middle of the slope in front of him a weary column was moving slowly through the raking rain, dispirited. Most of the guards had nothing against the wet, with pistols stuffed into their belts. Only a few held umbrellas, closing tightly around two mountain chairs that moved along the streaming rock path toward the Meilu. Chen Cheng knew that the chance he had waited so many days for had come at last: he could see clearly that the man in the forward chair was the target of all his waking and sleeping thought — Chiang Kai-shek! With no thought for anything else, Chen Cheng broke from the deep green bamboo at a bound and hid himself behind the bun-shaped boulder nearer the crags.

From there he could see the guard column clearly through the rain, but for a shot at the Chiang Kai-shek in the chair the distance was still somewhat long. Chen Cheng had one thought only: to seize this chance that comes once in a thousand years and shoot dead the Chiang Kai-shek he had at last, and with such difficulty, come upon.

But just as he drew the pistol from his waist and made ready to fire on the approaching Chiang Kai-shek, he heard behind him, in the bamboo, the faint rustle of movement. He took it for Hua Kezhi and Sun Fengming, hidden in the other direction, coming up at the news. But when Chen Cheng looked back he saw a stranger springing out of the bamboo — and at the same moment saw seven or eight more agents flickering out of the grove nearby, in fedoras, with American Mauser pistols in their hands.

"Done for!" Chen Cheng understood at once that there was no escaping now. He had meant to wait until Chiang Kai-shek's column came a little nearer and then aim for a certain hit; but the armed agents were already on him, and with no time left he sprang up and, leveling at the file of people moving along the mountain path, squeezed the trigger — crack.

The clean report woke echoes through the silent gorge.

Hua Kezhi, hidden in the pines on the flank of Lion Rock, heard the shot in the bamboo opposite and knew Chen Cheng must have found his target. After that single cold shot, and just as Hua Kezhi was about to rush the crags to support him, gunfire like beans bursting in a pan broke out from the same direction. Hua Kezhi knew then that Chen Cheng was surrounded, and that this dense fire was Chiang Kai-shek's attendants swarming an assassin they had discovered. Hua Kezhi ran to the summit in one breath, and through the pine branches saw heads swarming in the green bamboo below Lion Rock. He wanted to go down and save Chen Cheng, but it was already too late.

In the mountain wind, in the fine rain, the choking smell of blood rose all at once. Chen Cheng was dead.
