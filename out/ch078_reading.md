### Brother Sheep

A man who is duped at every turn and put upon in everything is called, in the Shanghai saying, a yangpan, a "sucker." Besides the sucker there is another sort of man called "Brother Sheep," whose sense seems the same but is really not quite.

About the flower-and-willow haunts there are often to be seen fellows in rumpled short jackets, swarthy of face, caked with grime, and coarse of speech, loitering among the girls; and the women fuss over such a one and hang on his every word, trading flirtatious banter with him, in bliss without end. For however ugly the outside of this kind of man, the gaudy banknotes in his pocket are muchee-muchee—he spends them like so much mud and sand, and draw as he will they are never done. With the money in view the women cannot but fawn on him for dear life, counting nauseating flattery good fun, only behind his back they will name you "Brother Sheep." And it is no easy thing to qualify as a Brother Sheep of this stamp: first of all you must have money in plenty before ever you may earn the title!

There is yet another sort, short on experience and without an inkling of anything, who plays the man of parts before others—he too is called "Brother Sheep," and also "Wronged-to-Death."
