### The Weiyuan

Twenty years ago, of the gardens of Shanghai, the most famous and open to the public were reckoned the three of Zhang's Garden, Yu Garden, and Xu Garden — for at that time the Bansong Garden of Nanshi was not yet built, and neither the French Park of the French Concession nor the various parks of the International Settlement was yet open, so that Zhang's, Yu, and Xu were called the most famous gardens. By now only Xu Garden on Connaught Road (it stood earliest at Tang Family Lane on North Fujian Road, and later moved to Connaught Road) still remains; the Yu Garden on Edinburgh Road passed long ago into private hands, and the great Western mansion within Zhang's Garden, and its grounds, have long since been part torn down and part made over, so that one who passes through the place feels only an added measure of melancholy and of wandering loss.

Zhang's Garden, otherwise called the Weiyuan, lies at the western end of Bubbling Well Road; within the gate is an open square, planted right and left with trees, and beyond it a tall Western building named Arcadia Hall, with a billiard room behind. The garden's ornaments were not many, but its draw in those early days was very great: high officials and grand personages and the flowers of the courtesan houses all came often to stroll in it, and there was no need of a ticket at the gate — one entered Arcadia Hall to take tea and eat, paying a charge and a tip. Whenever some meeting was held or some notable welcomed, Arcadia Hall was borrowed for the assembly, for the garden was central for traffic and had a great, roomy building, upstairs and down holding several thousand, so that to borrow it for a meeting on the spur of the moment could not have suited better. One who once went to Arcadia Hall in Zhang's Garden, looking back on it now, cannot but feel the full weight of the world's changes.
