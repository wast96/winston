### Married with the Bud Unplucked

A man goes into the houses to amuse himself with a little master — a young courtesan, that is — and for the moment falls into so hot a love that he cannot let her go nor bear to be parted from her, and takes it into his head to have her leave the trade and keep her hidden in a golden house. The thing is not beyond reach; only you must lay in a great store of money before your wish can be answered. For the madams look on a little master as nothing short of a money-tree, and the moment a client would buy her off the register they seize their chance to soak him for all he is worth. Their pet name for it is "marrying her off with the bud unplucked."

What they mean is that this little master is a true maiden, the seal never yet broken, who has never known a man, and that now, since someone would take her to wife, she must be made over to you with her flower still folded in the bud — but then the price of her person (the fee, that is, to strike her from the register) had best be a handsome one, and they open the lion's mouth wide and name whatever wild figure they please. Should you grudge the money and refuse to fill the pit of their greed, this morsel of flesh you may give up all thought of tasting; they will balk you by a hundred wiles, and nothing comes of it in the end. Yet of these so-called little masters in the houses, the genuine article is rare; the most of them are "sharp masters" got up to pass, for the madams have their arts and can patch a melon already broken so cunningly that she seems as good as one who has never known a man. The besotted client bears his unplucked bud away, plumes himself that the jade has come back to him whole, and is hugely pleased — when in truth he has walked straight into the madams' trap and been fleeced once again, while the keeper of his hidden beauty sleeps on, snug and none the wiser, drum-tight in the dark.
