### The Mysterious Folding Fan

Last summer, walking along Fuzhou Road — Fourth Avenue, that is — I saw a man selling folding fans, one held out in his hand, crying, "No jar, no fan, and not an immortal could work the change!" I could make nothing of it, and went up to ask whether he had a good fan. He hastily drew one from his shoulder-bag; on its face was a picture, tinted in colors, of the meeting of the Herd-boy and Weaving-maid stars, the River of Heaven set crosswise between them and a wisp of cloud drawing the two together — a tender, lingering scene, the two stars gazing at each other askance — done as a lithograph, very fine and delicately wrought; and he asked a dollar for it. I thought the price too dear and turned to go, but the fan-seller spoke again: "Sir... look once more, closely now, and see whether it's worth it or no..." I turned it over for another glance — and the front of the picture had all at once become one of goblins at their scuffle, so that the whole secret stood revealed. I heard afterward that such a fan sells for eight jiao at the least, though without the mysterious touch two or three would be the most it could fetch. And so the general run of buyers, moved by curiosity, bought them up one after another, and a dozen-odd fans were gone in a twinkling. Ah, the mysterious folding fan!
