### The Iron Grille on the Counter

Ever since robbery began to swarm up, the shops through which much silver and cash pass have all, to guard against trouble before it comes and to spare themselves alarm, fitted iron grilles round the counter to keep off the visitations of the robber-gentry. Pawnshops, little pawnshops, opium-and-exchange shops and their like—of every ten, five will have their pairs of grilles up. Because in such shops a good deal of silver and cash moves in and out, if they did not thatch the roof before the rain came and defend themselves with iron grilles, they would be visited by robbers and bandits and suffer unlooked-for losses. As someone has put it: "A shop fitted with an iron grille is like a great birdcage, and the clerks within like a flock of birds shut up in the cage." The comparison has a certain aptness to it.

The banks and the native banks too all fit grilles, but their grilles carry a double meaning: first, they are likewise for defense against robbery; and second, they cannot but fit them, the better to keep their several sorts of business apart. Moreover, the grilles fitted by the banks and native banks—whether of brilliant, glittering brass or of a mellow, elegant antique bronze—are a thing quite different from the jet-black iron grilles of the opium-and-exchange shops and the rest.
