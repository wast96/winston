### Dancing

Eight or nine years ago the craze for dancing once flared up, and then, somehow, suddenly died down. But in these last two or three years the tide of dancing has surged up again, all the rage; and though the dance halls are not so many as the cinemas, they still run to over thirty. Those who go to the dance halls are not only modern women and callow young bloods, but ever so many white-haired old fellows in long gowns and riding-jackets besides. The commonest charge is a silver dollar for three dances, at three jiao three fen a dance (and there are newly opened little halls that, to draw custom, will sell six dance tickets for a dollar, six dances), and then you may fold in your arms a half-bare dance-girl, all powder and perfume and swaying grace. Those fond of dancing count it the cheapest of amusements — but once the champagne is opened and a few refreshments ordered, several dollars, or a few tens of dollars, will go, and that is reckoned nothing out of the way.

The dance-girls who make their living by it now come, at a rough count, to over two thousand, and by nationality they are of several kinds — Chinese, Russian, Japanese, Korean. As for where they spring from, there are women of respectable families and girls out of the brothels, and not a few so-called film stars besides (Liang Saizhen and her sort), who, envying how easily a dance-girl earns, have changed their calling for the dance floor. Once a dancer has made her name, she wins the honours of "dance queen" and "dance star," and may then gather both fame and fortune, her renown ringing through the dancing kingdom. As for the staffing of the halls, most work on a mixed Chinese-and-foreign system, though there are a few that employ foreign dance-girls only.

The halls' patrons, of course, are chiefly the native dance-going crowd, though uniformed foreign sailors come too. This dancing business, fairly judged, is no harm at all if a man plays along for the occasion — dancing in his eye and no lust in his heart — and does it now and then to pass an idle hour; but should he once enter the soul-bewitching maze, his heart's banner all a-flutter beyond his own command, drunk on the dance hall and unable to pull free, then he is bound for ruin of body and name, a fall into the bottomless pit, and past all cure!
