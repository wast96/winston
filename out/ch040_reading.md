### The Glory and Disgrace of Xiao Jiaotian

The reigning king of the bearded-male role, Xiao Jiaotian, was truly named Tan Xinpei; later he did away with the stage-name and trod the boards under his own. How many times in all he came to Shanghai to perform I can no longer clearly recall, and will leave the count aside. One year he came and performed at the Awakening Stage, managed by Huang Chujiu (that is, at the corner of Second Avenue and Zhejiang Road, now pulled down and rebuilt as the new Wing On building). One day, rehearsing "Stealing the Soul-Bell," he played against type as Zhu Wuneng the Pig, and was jeered by a spectator surnamed Li; at which Manager Huang flew into a rage, set his teahouse men on to drag the fellow into the counting-room, and dealt him two boxes on the ear. After this happened, Zheng Zhengqiu let fly at Huang in the Illustrated Theater News, which he then edited, saying: "Whether a spectator cheers or hisses is his absolute freedom; how can it be that for a single jeer a man deserves a beating?" And everyone said at the time: "King Tan was the one wronged; Zheng Zhengqiu, the man of honor."

The last time he came to Shanghai he performed at the New Stage at Jiumudi, singing ten nights in all, his vogue that season robust past all compare; by three or four in the afternoon a good many spectators had already come, content to go hungry and hurry over to seize the good seats. His beloved son-in-law, Boss Xia Yuerun the Eighth, was on the stage in person every night to see all went well. One night, in a performance of "The Empty Fort Strategy," Xia Yueshan and Qiu Zhiyun, master and pupil, the two of them shared the parts of the old soldiers; and the moment a scene was played out, before he could so much as reach the wings, the Xia brothers would be handing him back on — King Tan at this hour was glorious past all measure. After this trip, not long afterward, he died at Beiping.

Tan Xinpei had earlier served within the palace, and so bore the style of "Tan the Court Purveyor." After the Boxer troubles of the gengzi year he was living together with Sai Jinhua, and his fortune at that time too was at its reddest height; someone composed a bamboo-branch verse setting the matter down, whose last two lines ran: "The world cares nothing for the rise and fall of states, / yet the whole city vies to speak of Jiaotian." A true record, that.
