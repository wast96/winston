### Like a Dog's Fart: The Tianchou Collection

In the early Republic, when Mr. Dai Jitao was principal writer of the Minquan Bao, that paper carried a long editorial nearly every day, and it was Dai who wrote them; grand and cutting in their phrasing, they were much admired by readers. Yuan Shikai's every unlawful act Dai assailed with tongue and pen, sparing no effort, and so he was once arrested, though rescued early and soon set at liberty again.

Afterward the proprietor of the Minquan Bao gathered Dai's editorials and printed them as a volume of their own, which he titled the Tianchou Collection, "Tianchou" being Dai's pen name. The young students and party men of that day were mostly glad to buy and read it. Not long after it came out, the pictorial of the Dagonghe Bao ran a drawing of a dog, with the four characters "Tianchou Collection" inscribed behind the dog's tail: the last word, one may say, in vituperation and mockery.
