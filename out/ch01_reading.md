## Editorial Note

1. To carry forward the turn toward specialized, systematic work called for by the National Committee of the CPPCC's Research Committee on Cultural and Historical Materials and by the National Working Conference on Cultural and Historical Materials, we plan, alongside our continuing compilation of the Selected Historical and Cultural Materials of Shanghai, to edit and publish a number of special volumes from this year onward, keyed to the character of Shanghai's modern history. This volume, *The Gangs of Old Shanghai*, is a first attempt.

2. The pieces gathered here come from several sources. Some are older manuscripts our committee had held unpublished over the years; some are articles already printed in books and periodicals on the mainland, reproduced with the consent of their authors or of the parties concerned; and a good many are broad accounts newly solicited from comrades who knew the gangs at first hand or had themselves belonged to them, or are their own experiences and observations. All carry a certain documentary value. Zhu Xuefan, Vice-Chairman of the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress, wrote a special article for us amid the press of official business and brushed the book's title with his own hand; Professor Wei Jianyou of the History Department of Shanghai Normal University, who has long studied the history of the secret societies, wrote the preface while convalescing from illness and read through part of the manuscript. To both we offer our thanks.

3. The names Green Gang and Hong Gang have been given in more than one form; the several pieces in this volume follow, on the whole, each writer's own usage, and we have not forced them into agreement.

4. The gangs of Shanghai are a tangled subject of wide reach. Most of those who lived through these events are dead, and the circle of people we were able to canvass was neither broad nor deep; the contents of this volume are, in consequence, neither complete nor systematic. On some points the writers' recollections diverge and could not, for the present, be fully verified; we can only set the differing accounts side by side, or annotate them where fitting, in the hope that those who know will supply what is missing and set the record straight. And we hope readers will point out the faults and errors in our editing, so that it may be improved.

{d} The Committee on Cultural and Historical Materials, Shanghai Municipal Committee of the CPPCC &#183; April 1986
