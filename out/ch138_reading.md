### Buying Rotten Goods

"Buy your junk, buy your junk!"—the cry of the men who buy up old goods—is a sound to be heard the livelong day at every street-corner and lane-end.

They come shouldering a pair of basket-panniers and dart back and forth like weavers' shuttles, calling out to buy; broken glass, worn-out clothes, empty bottles, old newspaper, old furniture, ruined clocks and watches, scrap copper and rusty iron—so long as the price is cheap, they will take the lot. And buying at the one hand, they can sell it straight off at the other, each article to its own market. These petty brokers have the keenest of eyes and are shrewd past everything; buying in with a trifle of capital, they need only wait a little while to turn a profit several times over, and so people give them, one and all, the name of "old black-hearts."
