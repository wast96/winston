### Judgment Tea

Among the common folk of the lower orders, whenever the two sides to some petty quarrel fall out, they are sure to muster a crowd of friends and go off to a tea-house to "drink judgment tea." And what is meant by "drinking judgment tea"? It is that the rights and wrongs of the two parties shall be tried out, then and there, across a single table. If the upshot can be a peaceful settlement, a peacemaker mixes red tea and green together, pours them into a cup, and respectfully presents it to the two principals to drain at a draught, by way of a token of reconciliation. But it also happens often enough that the parley cannot settle the matter, and the end of it is a resort to force, the tea-house turned into a battlefield, with stools and tea-bowls for weapons. The commonest causes of these judgment-teas are secret affairs between men and women, and questions of money.

Every tea-house hangs up a little wooden board reading "By order: judgment-tea forbidden," this being the proprietor's way of guarding against trouble brewing on the premises. Yet let the judgment-tea friends once turn up, and the force of this "judgment-tea forbidden" comes to exactly zero.
