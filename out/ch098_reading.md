### The Su-Guang Ready-Made Clothiers

For the people who live in Shanghai, to have a garment made up—whether of cloth, of silk, or of fur—they must all go to a tailor to have it done, and so ready-made clothiers have sprung up until they meet the eye at every turn. Apart from the few who rent a shop to set up in, the rest rent a mere patch of ground at the mouth of a lane or under a gate-tower and turn it into a workshop. Their signboards mostly bear the words "Such-and-such Su-Guang Ready-Made Clothier": "Su" stands for Suzhou and "Guang" for Guangdong. Now the Suzhou people really do take pains over their dress—that much is true to fact; but the Cantonese care for their food and their lodging—those two things—and give no thought to their clothes, so what the "Guang" on the signboards is meant to signify, no one knows.

The tailors are, for the most part, of the Suzhou guild, the Wuxi guild, the Zhenjiang guild, the Jiangbei guild, the local guild, and the Ningbo guild, with few from any other. Besides their wages from the customer, they have an income from skimming: whatever stuff the customer hands over, be it cotton, silk, or fur, they must filch a pinch or two from it before they are satisfied, and so there is a saying current in Shanghai, "The tailor who does not filch his cloth will have to pawn his own wife" ("filch" here meaning to skim). Tailors are of two kinds, the master and the hired hand: the hired hand works for the master, earning a few dollars of wages a month; the master, over and above squeezing the hired hands' pickings, has his own skimmings too. But to become a master he must first lay by a certain capital before he can open a ready-made clothier's.

Their capital is not only for renting a place and buying gear; sometimes they must also advance the material for a customer. What is meant by "advancing the material"? There is a set of easygoing gentlemen who, in having a garment made, do not care to go and buy the cloth or silk themselves, but merely give the measurements and hand the job to the clothier; the shop then buys the material for you, and when the garment is done settles it all up together with the labour cost—and this is what is called "advancing the material."
