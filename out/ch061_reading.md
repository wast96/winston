### Confucius

The great ocean-going steamers all take their names from places — the Hanyang, the Nanjing, the Beijing, the Taiwan, the Anqing, the Shuntian, the Fengtian, the Jilin, and so on. I once asked a steamship company what meaning lay behind the choice. "There is no meaning in it," they said; "we wish only to have them easily told apart."

There was once a steamer of the ×× Company, plying the several ports of the Yangtze, that bore the name "Confucius" in three characters — to name a ship after the Sage of China was certainly to strike out a new line; but later, having sailed as far as Hubei, she was struck by lightning and sunk. One story has it that Master Kong the Second, unwilling to have his honoured title borrowed so, flew into a great rage and summoned there and then the gods of Heaven and their celestial troops to strike her down, by way of a chastening lesson. This, though, verges on the fabulous, and is not to be relied upon.
