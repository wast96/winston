### Pheasants

The "pheasant" spoken of here is not the lowest grade of streetwalker who solicits along the roads; it is a name applied to anything of a bogus character that stands outside its own trade. Take, for instance, a certain kind of hired carriage that does not belong to the man riding in it but has been rented or bought by the driver himself and put out on his own account: that is called a "pheasant carriage." Or again, there is a sort of broker (that is, a middleman in buying and selling) who has not joined the guild of his trade but goes out on his own to drum up business: everyone calls him a "pheasant broker." From these two examples the rest may be inferred.

There is also a kind of house-agent at the theaters who books a whole day's performance in advance, prints up patronage tickets, and peddles them round among the regular customers; this too is called "beating the pheasant" (also known as "beating the whirlwind"), the name coming, as its very sound suggests, from a certain likeness to the streetwalkers who tug at passers-by along the road.
