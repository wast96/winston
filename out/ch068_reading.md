### Ring Toss

In the amusement halls there is a kind of stall, like gambling and yet not gambling. The way of it is this: one or two long tables are set out crosswise and draped with a cloth, on which are ranged clocks and watches and toilet articles and toys of every kind, each with an iron peg a chi or so high stuck up beside it; and about five or six chi off from the tables a rope barrier is run round. A man outside the barrier sells wooden rings — one mao buys a number of them — and you stand outside and pitch them from a distance; if a ring drops over one of the pegs you win the prize — ring the peg beside a clock, say, and the clock is yours, and so on with the rest — only it is very hard indeed to land one. This game is called "ring toss," or else "hoop the peg."

In fact the thing is of long standing, and was already known as much as fifty years ago; only there were no amusement halls in those days, and a man commonly rented an empty room, fitted up inside just as they are now, where each ring sold for a bare ten cash. Later, as the trade throve and everyone rushed to set up in it, until there were several dozen of them, the authorities stepped in and put it down, and one after another they closed their doors.
