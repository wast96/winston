### New Drama, Such as It Is

The new drama (that is, spoken drama, popularly the "civilized play") has some thirty-odd years of history, and its first period ran from the Xuantong years of the fallen Qing down to the first and second years of the Republic. In those days the "Tianzhi school" of new drama was much in vogue — the Tianzhi school being the troupe led by Tianzhi, whose surname was Ren, a bannerman who had once taken Japanese nationality and so went also by the name Tōdō Chōbai; Gu Wuwei, Wang Youyou and their fellows were all his star pupils — and by the fifth or sixth year of the Republic it suddenly fell into decay.

In the early Republic the Chunliu Society, got up in Japan by Lu Jingruo, Ma Jiangshi, Wu Wozun, Ouyang Yuqian and others, afterward moved as a body to Shanghai to perform, taking the Moutrie & Co. premises on East Nanjing Road for their theater. The society's rules, in the manner of Peking opera, required that every play be done from a full script, with the actors studying their parts beforehand and every gesture held to a fixed standard — unlike the other troupes, which used only a scene-outline, the director explaining it briefly at curtain-time before they went on. The plays they staged were lofty and profound in their matter, too (pieces like Burugui, The Jeweled Bracelet, and Wang Xifeng Spills the Vinegar), which suited the palate of the upper sort but found no favor with the lower orders; and so, the tune being too high for the chorus, the house stood near empty, and in the end they stopped performing.

Then Mr. Zheng Zhengqiu, seeing the new drama in its decline, rose up and organized the Xinmin Society, taking the old site of the Tianxian Teahouse on Shi Road for a theater. After it opened, business was very good (the two plays A Record of Family Love and Hate and Sea of Regret were the best liked). On the strength of this a great many new-drama societies sprang up in turn — Su Shichi's Minxing Society, Sun Yusheng's Qimin Society, Zhang Shichuan's Minming Society. At the height there were six or seven such societies, and to draw together they organized a New Drama Guild besides, and put out a paper, the Xinju Ribao, to publicize themselves. One year, to raise funds for the guild, they mustered the players of all the societies for a joint performance (the place being the Minming Society at the corner of Third Avenue and Daxin Street). The new drama was then at its blazing height, all the rage, and old Mr. Zheng earned the name of the man who had brought it back to life. But after three or four years the fortunes of the new drama slid into decay once more; one by one the societies closed, and old Zheng's work of revival came to nothing. In his last years he set up the Xiao Wutai on Guangxi Road, which Zheng Zhengqiu and Shao Zuiweng managed by turns; but the day of the new drama was past, and struggle as they might they could not hold on, and in the end there was no keeping it up, and it closed.

In the flush times, the troupes had not only a guild and a paper to publicize them, but several of the new-drama magnates set up training schools besides (after the fashion of the apprentice-troupes of Peking opera) to recruit and teach pupils — Zheng Zhengqiu's Yaofeng Drama Society, Gu Wuwei's Wuwei Drama Academy, Su Shichi's Shichi Drama Academy — and the Yaofeng society at the same time put out a paper, the Yaofeng Ribao, to serve as its organ.

At the same time women's new drama arose to suit the hour. It first played at a foreign theater on Yuanmingyuan Road, three days running, to a great press of spectators; for the Shanghai public has ever been of a curious turn, and women's new drama was then an unheard-of thing, so that it made a great stir for a while. Lin Ruxin, Xie Tongying and their like were the pick of the company; but later, somehow, they never opened theaters of their own, only attaching themselves to the men's troupes, and every Monday through Friday, in the daytime, they took the Minming New Drama Society's premises on Daxin Street to play matinees only, and no evening shows. Su Shichi's Minxing Society, to draw custom, was the first to copy the Beiping Peking-opera practice of men and women acting together, and staged mixed-cast new drama; they kept it up some years, but in the end could not sustain it either, and stopped.

The new drama nowadays has long since become an appendage of the amusement halls, its standing on a level, this good while past, with the variety turns. To see new drama, one has only to go to the amusement halls. Of the stars who once made a splash, apart from those who have changed their trade (Zheng Zhengqiu, now a magnate of the film world; the variety champions Yi Fangshuo and Zhang Ye'er; the artist Ouyang Yuqian; the novelist Xu Zhuodai — all of them had trodden the new-drama boards), the rest could only stoop to the amusement halls to scrape a living. The rise and fall of this dozen years and more is like nothing so much as a spring dream, and leaves a man with a feeling he can hardly bear to look back on.
