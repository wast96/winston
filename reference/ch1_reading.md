# China's King of Assassins: Wang Yaqiao

## Chapter One: A First Assassination, Badly Begun

### 1. Chen Diaoyuan Within Arm's Reach, and the Moment Lost

The thirteenth of October, 1927 — to Western eyes, an unlucky date.

That morning, off the Pukou wharf at Nanjing, a warship came into view flying the Blue Sky, White Sun, and Wholly Red Earth. She had steamed down from Wuhu, and along both rails stood a dense black line of armed guards. The man they were guarding was the ship's master: Chen Diaoyuan, Superintendent of Military Affairs for Anhui Province.

"Son of a bitch. So the Chen bastard really came." Under a tree near the wharf crouched a man got up as a seller of Jinling dates, a battered straw hat on his head, holding a pair of field glasses in the shade and watching the river. The warship bore down, closer and closer, until he could pick out the adjutants scrambling on deck to bring her alongside. The escort was wound tighter still, drawn up as though an army waited on shore, every man with a Mauser in his fist, glaring at the bank as if ten thousand soldiers lay hidden on the wharf.

The watcher's name was Wu Hongtai. When the warship closed on Pukou he gathered up his satchel of dates and cut down a side path to a wineshop at the water's edge. At the door he ran into a heavily bearded man named Xuan Jimin, coat hanging open on a chest thick with hair despite the cold autumn wind. Xuan looked him over and asked in his gravelly voice, "What's the panic? Is Chen really here?"

"No mistake. If it weren't Chen Diaoyuan's ship, there wouldn't be that many rifles on her." Wu Hongtai's face was tight; all he wanted was to get what he'd seen to the head of the Hatchet Gang, Wang Yaqiao. Just then seven or eight men in plain clothes came out of the wineshop, working their knuckles, and Wu Hongtai knew them all for core men of the Gang: Yu Likui, Niu Anru, Wang Ganting, the rest. Every one of them had been waiting for a chance like this. Niu Anru pulled a snub-nosed pistol from his waistband, unable to hold himself in. "Let's go."

"No wild moves. Everyone acts on the Ninth Master's word, and whoever wrecks this won't be forgiven. We've all waited a long time to kill that dog." Xuan Jimin, keeping the door, watched his killers glaring and rolling up their sleeves, and worried that haste would spoil everything. He threw a glance toward the inner room, and Wu Hongtai took it and went straight in.

Only then did he see, behind a small table at the window over the river, a middle-aged man in a short jacket of white spun silk. Short, square-built, a squared-off face, lean and hard; and between the high brow and the shrewd eyes glinting behind black-rimmed spectacles, you knew at a glance this was a deep and calculating man. This was the Hefei notable they called the Ninth Master — Wang Yaqiao, chief of the Hatchet Gang.

The wineshop was strangely quiet. Wang Yaqiao sat at the window drinking slowly, a few plates of Jiangnan appetizers in front of him and a pot of aged nü'erhong, and though he sat perfectly still, his eyes never left the muddy sweep of the Yangtze below. When Wu Hongtai came in with Xuan Jimin, Yu Likui, Niu Anru and Wang Ganting pressing behind him, Wang turned just slightly. "So Chen came on schedule after all."

"Beyond any doubt." Wu Hongtai knew his chief ran hot inside and cold outside — nothing on the surface, and more impatient than any of them underneath. He held out the field glasses with both hands. "Ninth Master, see for yourself. Chen Diaoyuan's ship is already coming alongside."

Wang Yaqiao raised the glasses, and the warship sprang into the lens: armed sentries stem to stern, overtaking a merchantman putting in to shore, swaggering straight for the wharf. He knew who sat inside her — the corrupt official he had long wanted dead. Chairman of the Anhui Provincial Government Affairs Committee, Superintendent of Military Affairs, commander of the 37th Army of the National Revolutionary Army and commander-in-chief of the Northern Route Army besides: Chen Diaoyuan.

Watching the ship come in, Wang Yaqiao heard again the bitter words of the teacher he revered, Bo Wenwei, commander of the 33rd Army. "Jiuguang, my friend, this Chen Diaoyuan has a real gift for wriggling upward. He began as a Zhili-clique warlord — but the moment he saw Wu Peifu was no match for Chiang Kai-shek, he kicked his own patron aside and threw in with that political hoodlum, Chiang the Baldhead. He plays the tiger's jackal and gets promoted for it. Meanwhile we Anhui men, who bled through the Northern Expedition, hand the very ground we won over to Chen Diaoyuan. Where's the justice in it? A man who'd break with his own clique to get power — once he's loose in Anhui, what will he do but bleed the people white?"

Wang Yaqiao had asked him, when the old general finished: "Teacher, why does Chiang Kai-shek keep promoting him?"

Bo Wenwei sighed. "Because Chen Diaoyuan is a master of officialdom, and he knows how to give gifts — what else? But understand this, Jiuguang. What he gives Chiang Kai-shek isn't money. It's the flesh and marrow of the people of Anhui."

Wang Yaqiao swung the glasses onto the wharf itself. It was already thick with people, and when he looked closely they turned out to be senior officials and generals of the Nanjing government, gaudy women threaded in among them. The warmth of the reception lit a hatred in him — and called up another scene just like it.

On the rolling Yangtze, a luxury steamer approaching from far off: Chiang Kai-shek's official boat, running up from Nanjing toward Anhui. As she neared Wuhu, a bright noise of drums and brass came drifting over the water and drew Chiang out on deck from where he sat aboard the *Dongzheng*. Out on the wide river, several warships were bearing toward him in an A formation, the leader flying the national flag, a military band playing the Northern Expedition march. Chiang was delighted. "Good, good. Very good indeed." He knew who had brought those ships down from Wuhu in person to escort him to his review in Anhui: the Zhili warlord Chen Diaoyuan.

"Generalissimo — you honor us with your presence!" Chen Diaoyuan came up the ladder onto the *Dongzheng* and greeted Chiang first with a student's formal bow, deep from the waist: a gesture that declared him Chiang's pupil and hinted his submission in the same motion. Then, seeing the smile on Chiang's face, he turned and pointed back at his own warship. "If the Generalissimo would look — what do you make of that?"

Only now did Chiang Kai-shek notice that Chen Diaoyuan's deck was loaded with new artillery. The handsome gift this defecting warlord had brought was the most advanced ordnance of the day — British mortars and Krupp field guns, ranged in neat order on the deck and draped in red silk, impossible to miss.

"Good, good. Modern weapons are exactly what we lack." Chiang, delighted, took Chen Diaoyuan by the arm himself and walked him into the cabin. Inside, Chen did not dare sit. Chiang nodded at him with open approval. "General Chen. When you served under Wu Peifu, I heard you were a Westernized officer. Now I find you're not so Western after all — you understand Chinese courtesy very well. It seems the governance of Anhui belongs to no one but you."

"Thank you, Generalissimo, for your cultivation." Chen Diaoyuan had never dreamed that with elders like Bo Wenwei standing in Anhui, Chiang would hand the province to him. And that is how Chen Diaoyuan became the piece Chiang Kai-shek planted on the Anhui board.

"Hmph. Today is the day you die, Chen Diaoyuan." Wang Yaqiao kept the glasses trained on the warship. An official in a long gray gown emerged from within — Chen Diaoyuan himself. At first glance the man might have passed for a scholar: long lean frame, cloth shoes and a small cap, a certain refinement in the way he moved. In truth he was a warlord politician with a knife inside his smile. Wang Yaqiao got the face clear in his lenses and felt the hatred rise. He thought of what Bo Wenwei had pressed on him before he left Hefei for Nanjing, and he wanted to swallow Chen Diaoyuan whole and be done with it.

The old general had said it in fury: "Ever since Chen Diaoyuan became Chiang Kai-shek's favorite, he doesn't just drill troops at Wuhu and play every trick of office — his shadow reaches all the way to us in Hefei now. These days even I have to read Chen Diaoyuan's face before I act. If this goes on, there will simply be no living for us."

"Rest easy, General." Wang Yaqiao had drained a bowl of blood-wine that day and bowed to him, and sworn: "Let him carry on for now. So long as Wang Yaqiao draws breath, sooner or later I will rid us of this creature who ruins the country and blights its people. If I can't reach him at Wuhu, I'll chase him to the ends of the earth — I'll take Chen Diaoyuan's head off and offer it up to the elders of Anhui."

And now Chen Diaoyuan stood on the wharf, not far ahead at all. Through the glasses Wang Yaqiao followed his every movement: the full general, ringed by bodyguards with Mausers at their belts, picking his way down the gangplank and up onto the dock.

"Ninth Master — now. Do it now." Wu Hongtai, who had kept the watch on the wharf, saw Chen step onto the bank and had his pistol out of his waistband in the same breath, aching to rush out and put a bullet through Chen Diaoyuan's skull.

Xuan Jimin — the one they called the Great Killer — couldn't hold his fury down either. He shoved his sleeves up. "Ninth Master, at Wuhu he hid in his compound and there was no touching him. Now he's finally walked in front of our guns. One squeeze of the trigger and I promise you he's a dead man. The chance is here, and it won't come twice."

"He's right, Ninth Master. Give the word." Niu Anru, Yu Likui and Wang Ganting took it up together, all of them watching Wang Yaqiao, who had not moved.

They knew how long he had hunted this opening, in Hefei and Wuhu both. But Chen Diaoyuan knew what he had done and how deeply he was hated, and did not show himself lightly; his residence at Wuhu bristled with sentries. Now, at last, the moment had come. Wang Yaqiao had learned in advance that Chen would travel to Nanjing to report to Chiang Kai-shek, and ten days and more ago he had slipped into the capital from Hefei with a handful of the Hatchet Gang's best killers. The plan was simple: wait at the riverside for the official boat to reach Pukou, open fire without warning, and leave the man who owed Anhui a blood debt dead on the bank of the Yangtze.

But now Chen Diaoyuan had arrived — and Wang Yaqiao neither rose nor gave the order. The killers burned. They lost their patience and began talking over each other.

"Quiet. It is not time to shoot."

"Ninth Master — why not?" Xuan Jimin stared out at Chen Diaoyuan on the wharf, all smiles, cupping his hands to the assembled guests, and could not for the life of him guess what medicine his chief was selling from that gourd of his. Wu Hongtai, Yu Likui and Niu Anru stood rooted.

Wang Yaqiao might have been Mount Tai. He drew his gaze back in off the wharf and spoke evenly. "Panic at the first turn of events, and what great thing will you ever do? Look again. Why are there so many men in long gowns in front of Chen Diaoyuan and behind him? Those are his personal attendants. Why bring so many? Because Chen Diaoyuan came prepared. He may be in Nanjing, but he hasn't forgotten the fright we gave him at Wuhu. Which is to say — he expected assassins waiting on that wharf. So for the moment, we do not move."

"What?" The killers sagged.

Wang Yaqiao tipped his chin toward the window again. "Look once more. That wharf is full of the wives and womenfolk of Nationalist officials, and the only man we want is Chen Diaoyuan. Move now and we are certain to hurt the innocent. I, Wang Jiuguang, deal squarely with the world; I have never once done a thing that offends heaven. So we find another opening. Brothers — fall back."

He shook out his sleeve and rose from the table. At the door he paused, beckoned Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai to him, and said quietly: "This is only the beginning. Get on Chen's tail — quietly. Find out where he's lodging, then find out his schedule now that he's in the capital. What I want is that bastard shot dead on his way to call on Chiang Kai-shek. Done that way, it lands as a warning on Chiang the Baldhead himself. Do you follow me?"

Xuan Jimin, Wu Hongtai, Niu Anru and Yu Likui looked at one another, thrown by the sudden change of killing-ground. They watched their chief walk out of the wineshop, and only then did it come home to them: the show they had been preparing since dawn had closed before the curtain ever rose.

### 2. Just Who Is Wang Yaqiao?

Chen Diaoyuan rode toward the city of Nanjing in a bulletproof sedan.

"Chairman Chen, why so pale?" The man beside him was Zhang Wensheng, a former military governor of Anhui now living out a comfortable retirement in Nanjing, who had come to Pukou that morning with the rest of the welcoming party.

Chen Diaoyuan's face was ash-gray. On the wharf just now he had felt something like a hand on the back of his neck. "The moment I showed myself on that dock," he said, "I could feel someone watching me from cover. Once bitten by a snake, you shy at a well-rope for ten years. Could Wang Yaqiao's people have followed me to Nanjing?"

Zhang Wensheng started. "What — that Wang Yaqiao again? Chairman, you cannot be too careful. That man is not to be provoked. When I governed Anhui, it was he who drove me out. That Hatchet Gang of his is vicious. Watch yourself."

"So you too left Anqing in your day because of Wang Yaqiao?" The words settled cold in Chen Diaoyuan. Stepping onto the riverbank just now, he had seen figures flicker in and out of a wineshop near the wharf, and it had brought back the attempt at Wuhu. That had been shortly after he took office. One evening, returning by car from a merchants' banquet, his bodyguard suddenly reported: "Chairman, two suspicious carriages are following us."

"Nonsense. Who's eaten a leopard's gall, to dare follow my car?" Chen Diaoyuan had scoffed. Then he looked back, and there were the two carriages, keeping close, and he could not help but take care after that.

Since betraying Wu Peifu and Cao Kun and going over to Chiang Kai-shek, Chen Diaoyuan had cut a grand figure at Wuhu as the military and political head of Anhui. But before long he had noticed strangers of doubtful appearance drifting past his residence in Mijia Alley. He had thought little of it at first — merely doubled the guard around the compound. Now, hearing that two mysterious carriages were closing on his motorcade, he understood the situation at once. He had long known that the cashiered former military governor Bo Wenwei kept, under his wing, a Hatchet Gang set squarely against Chiang Kai-shek. Was Bo Wenwei nursing his grievance over the Anhui appointment — and had he sent killers? Chen Diaoyuan had spent half a lifetime in the army, and safety was his first study in any new place. He ordered the escort to chamber their rounds, and in the blink of an eye a skirmish line stood around his car.

The two glass-windowed carriages behind, seeing themselves discovered, spilled several dark-faced men who opened fire on the sedan. Chen Diaoyuan had no stomach for a standing fight in that hail; he had his dozen guards return fire and fall back by turns, and at last broke out of the net and fled for his residence.

So now, though he had reached Nanjing, the fear was still in his bones. Again and again he glanced through the rear window at the road behind. He knew the Wuhu ambush had been the work of Wang Yaqiao's Hatchet Gang. "Wensheng," he said unhappily, "I truly don't understand it. How does one hatchet gang turn all of Anhui upside down?"

Zhang Wensheng hated Wang Yaqiao to the marrow himself. "Perhaps the Chairman doesn't know the man's history. That rogue was born at Modian market-town in the northern countryside of Hefei, and he was a local tyrant from his youth. He followed Bo Wenwei first, then attached himself to Sun Yat-sen. After Sun Yat-sen died he led his rabble into greater Shanghai."

"Oh?" Chen Diaoyuan was half listening.

"That Hatchet Gang of his was made in Shanghai. And when Wang Yaqiao comes home to Hefei, even the officials in the provincial yamen walk small around him. Don't underestimate the rogue. When I governed Anhui, if he and his hatchet men hadn't made my life impossible, would I be idling in Nanjing today? So I tell you, Chairman: beware of this man. Killing, to him, is like treading on an ant."

The more Chen Diaoyuan heard, the colder he felt, but he said: "Wensheng, don't feed the other side's spirit and starve our own. I don't believe a little hatchet gang can amount to anything. One little Wang Yaqiao — is he going to turn Nanjing over too?"

"That is exactly what you don't know," said Zhang Wensheng. "Wang Yaqiao is truly not to be trifled with. Why do you think he fought his way from Anhui to Shanghai in the first place, if not because he commands a crew of desperadoes who fear neither heaven nor earth? Once upon a time Wang Yaqiao hoped to attach himself to Sun Yat-sen and become a political man. But all he understood was cutting and killing, and naturally Mr. Sun had no use for him. So off he went to Shanghai to be a chief of rogues."

Chen Diaoyuan gave a cold laugh. "I knew it. A wastrel like that comes to nothing."

"One year," Zhang Wensheng went on, "a few members of his Anhui laborers' union brawled with a factory owner over wages and beat him bloody. Afterward the owner meant to come down hard on those workers. When Wang Yaqiao heard, he resolved to fight the man to the blood. But he had no weapons — so what does the fellow do? He has an idea like a devil's: one order, and the blacksmiths' forges hammer out more than a hundred big axes overnight. At first light there is Wang Yaqiao, an axe in each hand, leading hundreds of Anhui laborers in a roaring flood into the owner's compound, hacking and smashing at everything in reach — until the man's nerve broke, and there was nothing for it but to pay out a great sum for the injured men's care and apologize to them before the crowd. Only then was the matter allowed to rest."

Chen Diaoyuan was quietly appalled. "Such a thing happened?"

"It did," said Zhang Wensheng. "And from that day Wang Yaqiao stood up a made man in Shanghai — a pauper magnate second only to Huang Jinrong and Du Yuesheng, and the name of the Hatchet Gang rang through the Southland. So I advise you, Chairman: do not provoke this Wang Yaqiao. He is a desperado with a cruel hand."

Chen Diaoyuan's heart was hollow but his mouth stayed hard. "I have never feared a local ruffian. On our own Anhui ground, are we to let a rogue like Wang Yaqiao raise wind and waves?"

Zhang Wensheng laughed coldly. "He doesn't only run riot in Anhui — he slips off to Shanghai and does as he pleases there too. Even Generalissimo Chiang closes one eye where Wang Yaqiao is concerned. What are the likes of us against him? If you want to keep the chairmanship of Anhui, keep your distance from Wang Yaqiao."

Chen Diaoyuan still would not bend. "You expect me to believe the Generalissimo fears a hooligan — are his generals all idiots? Wensheng, the truth is Wang Yaqiao was never any greenwood hero. He couldn't hold Anhui, and that's why he ran to Shanghai."

Zhang Wensheng pulled a long face. "Chairman, it was not that he couldn't hold it. It happened like this: at the time of the 1911 revolution, Wang Yaqiao meant to make something of himself in Anhui with Bo Wenwei's backing. He raised a local force, then set up a military government at Hefei. He was riding high then — you'd have thought he'd become a lord of the marches."

"That sort of scoundrel, forming a government?"

"Yes — but his good days were short. No sooner had he stood up his government and named himself provincial defense commander than the Shanghai militarist Sun Wancheng marched troops into Hefei, wrecked the government, and put several of his best commanders to the sword. A fine bloodletting. Wang Yaqiao happened to be away from Hefei, or he'd have gone under Sun Wancheng's knife with them. One black, windy night, he fled to Nanjing." Zhang Wensheng smiled with some satisfaction at his own telling.

Hearing this half-legendary history, Chen Diaoyuan felt for the first time how dangerous his enemy was. "But if Wang Yaqiao went to Nanjing, why Shanghai after that?"

Zhang Wensheng chuckled. "Chairman, those were Wang Yaqiao's Maicheng days. A bad seed like that can't sit still, and even in Nanjing he wouldn't keep quiet. He attached himself to Jiang Kanghu — you know, Jiang Kanghu was organizing his so-called Socialist Party then. Wang Yaqiao saw an opening and made himself a small chief of the party's Anhui men. He meant to ride Jiang Kanghu's coattails up and stage a comeback. But Yuan Shikai would not let him be. When Yuan made himself emperor, his first order dissolved Jiang Kanghu's 'Socialist Party' — and Wang Yaqiao's name went on Yuan Shikai's wanted list. So the ringleader fled once more, to Shanghai. And no one imagined that in Shanghai he would truly amount to something."

By now Chen Diaoyuan's face had gone white with anger. Zhang Wensheng talked on regardless. "Today Wang Yaqiao's Hatchet Gang is a power in Shanghai, a power in Hefei, and now it has come to be a power in Nanjing. What I fear most, of course, is that his ambition isn't dead — that he means to stage some terror on your watch, Chairman."

"Terror?" Chen Diaoyuan's heart quailed, but he was, after all, Chairman of Anhui, and before the retired Zhang Wensheng he kept his manner up. He gave a dry laugh. "You think I fear a gangster? I have commanded armies. Even Generalissimo Chiang holds me in some regard. Am I to fear one Wang Yaqiao?"

"It isn't fear. Bear no man malice, but guard against every man. And this is a man whose whole trade is setting himself against the great." Zhang Wensheng paused. "Call him a ruffian if you like. In truth, he understands politics through and through."

Chen Diaoyuan was afraid, and covered it with a contemptuous smile. "You call Wang Yaqiao a statesman?"

"He is certainly no statesman, and no general either."

"A bandit, then?"

"Not a bandit either. Strictly speaking, Wang Yaqiao must be counted a special case. Someone once hung a title on him: a social activist of no party and no faction, who comes and goes alone. Though if you ask me, he is simply an assassin of boundless daring."

Chen Diaoyuan spoke through his teeth. "Good. If Wang Yaqiao is an assassin, then sooner or later I will rid the world of him."

But Zhang Wensheng had one more thing to say. "Chairman, this is not the moment for you to be rid of Wang Yaqiao. What I hear is that his Hatchet Gang presently intends to be rid of you. He has lately slipped into Nanjing with his men, and if they have come to Nanjing they have come with a design. So I urge you: while you are here, be vigilant a hundred times over. They meant to take you at Wuhu; now that you are in Nanjing, stay deep indoors and off the public stage — against the one chance in ten thousand."

Chen Diaoyuan kept his surface calm, but underneath, the unease had begun its quiet work. As the two men talked, the long motorcade swept past Xuanwu Lake and turned in at a deep-walled compound bristling with guards. And behind Chen Diaoyuan's motorcade, all the way, had trailed a small English motorcar carrying two men in dark glasses: the Hatchet Gang killers Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai. They turned the car in at Xuanwu Lake, braked it under the green shade at the water's edge, and watched Chen Diaoyuan's temporary residence through field glasses as the motorcade rolled into the depths of the courtyard and the two great doors swung shut.

### 3. Less to Kill Chen Than to Shake Chiang

Under Purple Mountain the autumn rain fell without end.

Wang Yaqiao sat out the days in a quiet little courtyard, caged and restless. For the killing of Chen Diaoyuan he had rented a deep secluded compound near the Gate of China as the hiding place of the Hatchet Gang's assassination cell. But since the morning they had glimpsed Chen at the Pukou wharf, days had passed without a trace of him.

"Damn him — the old loach. Who'd have thought he'd reach Nanjing and go straight to the bottom to lie in the mud." The missed chance at the wharf sat badly with Wang Yaqiao, and no path to Chen had opened since. The man had gone to ground in the little villa at Xuanwu Lake and had not shown his face again. Wang Yaqiao had begun to regret the decision to hold fire.

"Ninth Master, it looks as if Chen Diaoyuan has picked up our scent. We've kept the watch on Xuanwu Lake day after day. Nanjing officials go in to call on him — but Chen never once comes out to see a guest to the gate." Xuan Jimin came and went through the quiet courtyard with the watchers' reports, and Wang Yaqiao's temper burned lower each day.

"Doesn't Chen Diaoyuan love his opera? Why hasn't he gone to the Confucius Temple?"

"He doesn't need to leave the house to hear singing. The guards have been ferrying in women by the carload."

"He may see no one else — but Chiang Kai-shek he must see," Wang Yaqiao reasoned. "He is Chiang's lackey. Now that he's in Nanjing, how would he dare not present himself at the Chiang residence on Huangpu Road? Take some men and lay up along the road from Xuanwu Lake to Huangpu Road. I promise you Chen Diaoyuan will go to see Chiang. This rain may be keeping him indoors — but the day he goes to the Chiang residence, you kill him on the way."

"Good. As the Ninth Master says." Xuan Jimin, seeing his chief read the fire like a chart and direct the hunt without stirring from the courtyard, hurried off to set it up.

Next day the rain passed and the autumn sun stood brilliant over Nanjing. But Xuan Jimin's report came back: Chen Diaoyuan had not moved; there was no sign of him paying any call at all. The fire in Wang Yaqiao climbed. He had not spent all this — bringing Xuan Jimin, Wu Hongtai, Yu Likui and a dozen of the Hatchet Gang's best in secret from Anhui to Nanjing — merely to repay one bullet. The bullet in question: after Chen Diaoyuan went over to Chiang, when Wang Yaqiao had sent men into Chen's garrison area to avenge the sidelining of Bo Wenwei, his Hatchet Gang lieutenant Kan Peilin had been taken and killed by Chen Diaoyuan. Revenge for Kan Peilin was the near cause. The true reason for striking at Chen Diaoyuan was less any hatred of the man himself than of the man behind him — the betrayer of Sun Yat-sen, Chiang Kai-shek.

"I am Mr. Sun's faithful disciple. And since I hold to Sun Yat-sen, I cannot suffer those shameless men who call themselves his faithful disciples with their mouths and betray his dying wishes with their hands." Wang Yaqiao remembered his first collision with Chiang Kai-shek clearly. It was at Wuhan, and it began with a speech. Chiang spoke at the ceremony for the launching of the Northern Expedition force, and what he said so angered Wang Yaqiao that he walked straight into the rest room behind the stage. Wang's blood ran hotter in those days than it does in this story. Chiang was talking with several senior Northern Expedition commanders when Wang Yaqiao burst in and put his question to Chiang's face, finger pointed at his nose:

"I, Wang Jiuguang, am a member of the Revolutionary Alliance, and a member does not forget his origins. Mr. Chiang — I remember when I first met Mr. Sun in Guangdong, he told me with his own mouth that unless the Nationalist Party carried out the three great policies — ally with Russia, ally with the Communists, assist the peasants and workers — it would sooner or later fail. And I never imagined that with Mr. Sun's bones not yet cold, men would leap out to oppose his three policies! It is beyond all decency!"

Chiang Kai-shek sat frozen. Sweat stood out on the bare crown of his head. Bo Wenwei was sitting at Chiang's side, and had never dreamed Wang Yaqiao would light into Chiang in this fashion; he shot him look after warning look. But Wang Yaqiao was not to be called off. He pressed on, louder: "And what was it you said on that platform just now? Every word of it an open violation of Mr. Sun's testament!"

Chiang Kai-shek sat in fury and could not find his tongue against the onslaught. Wang Yaqiao turned to the senior commanders around him and said, for the whole room: "The men I most despise in this life are those who shouted loudest for the Three Principles of the People while Mr. Sun lived, and put on a different face the day he died. Tell me, gentlemen — what is such a man, if not a traitor to the revolution?"

Bo Wenwei had watched Chiang's face go from red to white when Wang Yaqiao came through the door. Knowing the man as he did, he broke into a cold sweat on Wang's behalf, and when his glances went unheeded and Wang went on arraigning Chiang as though no one else were in the room, Bo Wenwei, fearing where it must end, stepped up and steered him bodily out the door.

"Jiuguang — exactly how many heads do you have?" Later, Bo Wenwei had Wang brought to his quarters in Wuhan. "How dare you say such things to old Chiang's face? Don't you know his eye can't take a grain of sand?"

Wang Yaqiao slapped his chest. "General Bo, what I said today was meant to tear the scab off Chiang the Baldhead. Everyone else fears his revenge; I don't put him in my eye at all. And what is so grand about Mr. Chiang? Didn't he win his trust by flattery in the first place? Let me see what he dares do to me."

"Jiuguang, you are young after all." Bo Wenwei, who had weathered many seas, saw that Wang would go his own way and still did not know what trouble he had made in Wuhan that day. "I grant you're a man. But you have courage without craft, and that is why you will never build anything great. Don't imagine that because you are an assassin who dares to kill, you can leave Mr. Chiang out of your reckoning. That would be your great mistake — because Mr. Chiang is the foremost killer in China today. Beside him, you are a small ghost calling on the great one. If you'll take my advice, leave Wuhan tonight. It is very dangerous for you here."

"You mean — because my words crossed Chiang Kai-shek today, he'd kill me?"

Bo Wenwei only patted his shoulder. "Go back to Hefei or Shanghai, now, tonight. The one place you cannot stay is Wuhan. Otherwise, this night brings your death."

"A joke!" Wang Yaqiao laughed it off. "General, how could that be? Everything I said was true — on what grounds would he kill me? You've been too many years in official life; a wretched politician like Chiang has frightened you. I am not like you — I'm a bare-stick proletarian. What can Mr. Chiang do to me?"

"Then be careful, for heaven's sake." Bo Wenwei watched him stiffen his neck, with no idea that the catastrophe was already at the door. "Jiuguang — do you think an ordinary soldier gets to speak his mind in front of Mr. Chiang? Chiang Kai-shek has a chicken's gut for grievances. He will never forgive you. If you trust me at all, pack now and get to Shanghai. Otherwise this will be a black night for you."

Wang Yaqiao heard the old man out as though it were a story, went back to his inn, and put the warning out of his mind. He never dreamed that at midnight, just as he slept, a clamor would break out beyond the walls. He rolled up at once. The shouting at the gate grew louder. He threw a coat over his shoulders, came out rubbing his eyes, and called: "Who's making that noise out there?"

"Ninth Master — it's bad." In came Niu Anru, one of the guards he had brought from Anhui for the launching ceremonies, his face tight. "The Wuhan expedition headquarters has the inn surrounded. There's a field officer at the head of them. He says he carries the order of Commander-in-chief Chiang — to arrest the traitor to the revolution, Wang Yaqiao."

"Damn him — say that again?" And in that instant the evening's warning, which he had taken for table talk, stood up as fact. Wang Yaqiao had never liked Chiang Kai-shek; but that the commander of the Northern Expedition could be so narrow in the chest, so base — that he could not swallow one angry speech from one ordinary soldier of his army — this Wang had not imagined. He tore the pistol from his waist and made for the gate in a fury, and was wrapped up bodily by Niu Anru, Wang Ganting and the rest. "Ninth Master, you can't gamble yourself — Wu Hongtai and Xuan Jimin are out front right now, tangling with the arrest party. Come out the back with me, now. A true man doesn't eat a loss he can see coming. If you fall into Chiang Kai-shek's hands, the Hatchet Gang is a dragon with no head."

For one breath Wang Yaqiao wanted the fight — wanted to shoot a few soldiers and be satisfied. But Wang Yaqiao was never a simple hothead. In the next breath reason took the wheel; he pushed the pistol back into his waistband, beckoned once, vaulted the inn's rear wall, and was out of Wuhan before morning. When the arresting troops broke into the room, Wang Yaqiao and his bodyguards had vanished without a shadow.

From the Wuhan business onward, Chiang Kai-shek stood in Wang Yaqiao's mind as a politician both despotic and treacherous. And before setting out in secret from Hefei for Nanjing with Xuan Jimin, Wu Hongtai, Niu Anru and Yu Likui, Wang Yaqiao had gone to see Bo Wenwei once more. When Bo heard the journey's purpose was the assassination of Chen Diaoyuan, he chose his words with particular care. "Jiuguang — killing a great officer of the frontier has carried the death penalty since ancient times. If you did the thing on Anhui ground, I might be able to look after you when it came out. But why must it be Nanjing? Isn't that stirring trouble under the Son of Heaven's eyelids? If it goes wrong past mending, Chiang will throw you in a death cell at the least — at the worst he'll wipe the Hatchet Gang from the earth. Think three times. Do not move rashly in Nanjing. Beware Chiang's hidden knife. Have you forgotten Wuhan already?"

Wang Yaqiao flicked his sleeve back, and his face set. "General Bo — do you not know by now that I am a man of hot blood? I could have found my chance at Chen Diaoyuan in Wuhu, or in Anqing. Why go the far way round and take him in Nanjing, at his military conference? Because the drunkard's interest is not in the wine. I am killing the chicken for the monkey to see. The point is to sound a bell in Chiang's ear. I want Chen Diaoyuan's head to shake Chiang's spirit — and if he goes on as he has, trampling Mr. Sun's testament, then my Hatchet Gang is not to be trifled with, and my next target may well be Chiang Kai-shek himself."

Only then did Bo Wenwei understand what Nanjing was for. He looked at the set of the man's face — cool, and ready to die — and knew persuasion was finished. At parting he filled a cup of strong wine and raised it to Wang Yaqiao in both hands. "Brother Jiuguang. Since you mean to be the Jing Ke of our history, I will not hold you back. You are a member of the Revolutionary Alliance, and I know that in this, too, you are carrying out Mr. Sun's unfinished will. But I charge you still: in Nanjing, move like a man on ice. If Chen Diaoyuan can be struck, strike; if he cannot, withdraw. A man with your ambition for the country will do his great work sooner or later."

Now, in the little courtyard by the Gate of China, Wang Yaqiao had waited bitterly for many days, and still Chen Diaoyuan showed no sign of stirring. Even the military conference he was to attend had been postponed again and again. Had something changed? Or had the plan leaked from the very start?

"Ninth Master — Chen Diaoyuan has been playing us." Wu Hongtai came in at a run, breathless, just as Wang Yaqiao's patience was burning through, with news no one had expected. Xuan Jimin's men had watched the front gate of the lake villa all this time. The villa had a second gate at the back. That was why no watcher had ever fixed Chen Diaoyuan's movements. On Wang Yaqiao's instruction, Xuan Jimin had then spent money on a servant in the Chen household — a Jiangsu girl, surnamed Chen herself, a distant relation brought up from the old home country to serve in the Nanjing residence. What the girl reported was this: on the very evening of his arrival, Chen Diaoyuan had gone out by the back gate to pay his call at Chiang Kai-shek's Huangpu Road residence. In the days since, he had attended Chiang's military conferences outside the city one after another. And shortly he would leave Nanjing and return to Wuhu.

"His mother's — he's had us in the drum all along!" Rage and shock together. Wang Yaqiao had never dreamed the net he had staked in Nanjing had a hole in it, and looking back now he could count the gaps himself. Perhaps Chen's people had scented the Hatchet Gang from the start. With Chen Diaoyuan leaving Nanjing within days, Wang Yaqiao cursed his men up and down for wine-sacks and rice-bags: "If we hadn't bought that one servant girl, Chen Diaoyuan could leave Nanjing and we'd still be squatting here like frogs at the bottom of a well! Tell Xuan Jimin to go back and dig. While there is one thread of hope, the plan stands."

"Ninth Master — good news!" Late that same night, as Wang Yaqiao paced and began to believe Chen Diaoyuan had truly slipped his death-trap, Xuan Jimin came into his room from the long watch at the lake. That afternoon the serving girl had used a market errand to pass him a message: the following evening, Chen Diaoyuan and one of his concubines would go to a banquet at Meixi Villa in the hills outside the city. And the man giving the farewell banquet was Zhang Qiubai — Commissioner of Construction for Anhui Province and concurrently a director of the Nanjing government's Construction Committee.

"Perfect. Heaven itself is helping me!" Days of cloud left Wang Yaqiao's face at a stroke. That same night he drove out to Meixi Villa himself and walked the ground; and on his return he sat till morning with Xuan Jimin, Wu Hongtai and Niu Anru, and a plan for killing Chen Diaoyuan at Meixi Villa took its shape.

### 4. Zhang Qiubai Dies in Chen Diaoyuan's Place

The next afternoon Nanjing lay under a stifling heat, the kind that promises a great rain.

Toward five o'clock the sky began to darken. Wu Hongtai, Niu Anru and seven others had been hidden since three in a stand of trees a dozen meters from Maple Grove Bridge, from which they could watch every vehicle that crossed. This was the first of Wang Yaqiao's two plans. The night before, Wang had ridden the road from Chen's villa to Meixi Villa several times over with Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai, studying every crossing Chen's car would pass on its way to the banquet, and had settled at last on the one point it could not avoid: Maple Grove Bridge, a wooden bridge from the first years of the Republic, a shallow noisy stream beneath it — room to hide men below, and bridge piers to take a charge of explosives. When Chen Diaoyuan's car came on, the fuse would go; the bridge would come down; and in the wreck, with his escort split before and behind, the guns would finish it. Without the bridge, Chen's cars simply moved too fast for a roadside attempt to carry.

Before first light, Wu Hongtai and Niu Anru had set charges enough to bring the bridge down. Then Wu Hongtai waited, from three o'clock until half past six, while the sky went dark, and no motorcade came.

"Damn it — has something changed? Chen Diaoyuan is a sly beast." Niu Anru lay in the brush nearest the bridge, glasses on the road, the fuse-line tight in his hand, ready to fire it the moment the column showed: one blast, the motorcade in pieces, head cut from tail, and then the killers up out of the stream-bed and firing into the wreck — a trap with no floor to it. But the two men grew more restless as the light failed, and when full dark came there was not so much as the shadow of Chen Diaoyuan's column. "It's past strange," said Wu Hongtai, who had held his patience longest; the bridge was the safest and shortest way out of the city, which was exactly why he had backed the plan. Nothing came — not the grand motorcade they had seen at Pukou, hardly a stray car at all. "The Ninth Master chose this bridge because it was the perfect ground," he said, "and the old fox simply didn't take the road." They held until about eight, when Niu Anru stamped his foot: "Mother of — the old fox has slipped us."

But Wang Yaqiao had allowed for exactly this — that Chen might shun Maple Grove Bridge and go round by the small roads. Hence the second plan: Xuan Jimin, Liu Decai and Wang Ganting, with six picked killers, lying up in the wooded hills around Meixi Villa itself. It was quiet, secluded country, with the Ming emperor's tomb near at hand among the folds of the hills.

Xuan Jimin had never favored the roadside ambush. If Chen Diaoyuan really went to Meixi Villa, then on the evidence of his ghost-walking through Nanjing these past days he would never travel in a straight line. From the day he arrived, Chen had run a tight defense against the Hatchet Gang — even his calls at Huangpu Road and his military conferences had gone by back streets and odd detours, arriving from nowhere and leaving without a trace. A man like that does not cross the one bridge everyone must cross.

And now Xuan Jimin felt his hillside ambush ripening toward a singular service. From the moment the sun dropped behind the ridge, he and Liu Decai and the rest — dressed as woodcutters, farmers, peddlers — had closed in on the crags above the villa from their separate directions. From up there they looked straight down on it: a compound set into the waist of Plum Blossom Hill among the plum thickets, a great black stone pagoda visible far off, a thread of cold spring-water sounding down from the crag above. The carved beams and painted rafters of Meixi Villa lay under their glasses. And at the gate of this establishment — where in ordinary times only Nanjing's high officials rated an invitation — cars had begun arriving in the early dusk, one after another up the winding hill road, every one of them clear in Xuan Jimin's lenses.

"Welcome, welcome! You honor us — forgive me for not coming out further to meet you — Zhang Qiubai at your service!" Through the glasses Xuan Jimin watched the host greeting his guests at the gate beside a fashionably dressed woman: the master of tonight's banquet, and a political enemy Wang Yaqiao hated to the bone — Zhang Qiubai. But Xuan Jimin had never in his life seen Chen Diaoyuan, and could not tell whether Chen was among the arrivals; nor did he know whether Wu Hongtai and Niu Anru had already sprung their trap at the bridge. He turned it over: even if the bridge party had already blown Chen Diaoyuan to pieces, his own column could not go home empty-handed. He settled it in his mind — at the first sound of an explosion from the city side, he would take his killers down the hill and put a bullet, personally, into the man the people of Anhui called Zhang the Flayer: Commissioner of Construction Zhang Qiubai.

"It's bad, elder brother — Chen Diaoyuan has fooled us again." In the pale moonlight Xuan Jimin saw Liu Decai hurrying up a hill path with two rattled men behind him — Wu Hongtai and Niu Anru, from the bridge. "We waited till now and never saw his shadow. Another empty net! If we'd known, we should have sat on Plum Blossom Hill from the start and let him walk into it."

"Brother Xuan," said Wu Hongtai, "did you see Chen Diaoyuan go into the villa?"

Xuan Jimin pointed at the cars below. "If he came, I couldn't be sure of it from the crag. But I watched Zhang Qiubai and his lady go in long ago — and would a host go in to his own hall before the guest of honor had arrived? Chen must be in there."

The others looked down at the parked cars and agreed: Chen must have come up by the small roads. And if he was already inside, why keep waiting? Liu Decai pulled the pistol from his waist: "Come on — we go in and see." It was Wang Ganting, the cool head, who blocked the eager ones. "No blind moves. Brother Xuan and I have watched this hill all evening. If Chen Diaoyuan came, he came with a wall of guards. So where is his motorcade?"

"The ancients said war never tires of deception," Wu Hongtai answered. "Chen Diaoyuan may be a coward, but he's a provincial chairman — you think he doesn't know empty-where-solid, solid-where-empty? To keep men like us off him, he may well have come up quietly with a light escort. Don't let him fool us out of it. The chance won't wait."

"That's sense." Xuan Jimin took out his pocket watch: eight-forty already. "If we don't go in now, in half an hour Zhang Qiubai's banquet may break up. Everyone into town clothes — we walk in through the front. Dressed as we are, the gate guard would never pass us."

Only then did Liu Decai and Wang Ganting remember they were still dressed as farmers and peddlers. The party changed into Western suits and came down off Plum Blossom Hill through the thin moonlight.

The villa grew grander as they climbed toward it, the sounds of toasts and drinking-games drifting out of the great courtyard. It was built as an old-style quadrangle; before the front gate stood a high spirit screen carved with two characters — Great Felicity — and at the ornamented inner gate, four or five guards with pistols at their belts stood looking out at the moonlit hills and the dark mass of the Ming tomb. When they caught sight of Xuan Jimin's party coming up the slope, the guards had their guns out together: "Who goes there — stand!"

The shadows did not so much as slow. Xuan Jimin swaggered to the front of them and greeted the gate with a bow and cupped hands. "We're guests of Commissioner Zhang — invited under his own hand, just up from the city. Our apologies for the hour."

The guards stood flat-footed. The sheer height of the man's manner defeated them; they could not tell true guest from false, and the suits and the bearing were unanswerable, so they gave way, unwilling. One of them, sharper than the rest, stepped across Xuan Jimin at the last moment. "Sir — forgive us, we have our orders. Whoever you are, we must see the invitation with Commissioner Zhang's own signature before you go in to the banquet. If we pass the wrong man, it's more than our lives are worth."

Xuan Jimin had not counted on a gate this strict. He had no invitation to show, and now several black muzzles had come up level with his face. He did not blink. He weighed it — force, then, and quickly — and threw one glance back at Wu Hongtai and Liu Decai. Quicker than the telling: Wu Hongtai turned, beckoned once, and Liu Decai, Niu Anru, Wang Ganting and the rest, eight or nine men, came up the steps in a single surge. Before the gate guards understood what was happening, the black muzzles were against their foreheads. These were men who played the tiger in Nanjing streets and had never once stood in front of a real gun; they folded, filthy with fear. Niu Anru and the others stripped their weapons in a tangle of hands and walked them into the tree-shadow off the gate. Then Xuan Jimin and Wu Hongtai led the rest down a winding covered walk, straight for the banquet hall in the deep of the compound.

Xuan Jimin went with his Mauser out and a killing light in his eyes. He knew what tonight was worth: whether Wang Yaqiao's first trial of strength in Nanjing opened with a victory hung entirely on whether they could kill Chen Diaoyuan in this villa. Succeed, and the Hatchet Gang's name would stand up over the whole country, and Wang Yaqiao with it — a man to be feared everywhere. The thought of what he and his brothers carried brought the blood up hot. He crossed the silent forecourt at speed, whipped round the gallery — and pulled up short before a dark pool, a few spent lotus swaying on it in the night wind. Ahead, all at once, a blaze of light: a great hall, splendid with it, red gauze lanterns burning at the eaves and brightening the whole court, and out of it the surge of toasts and women's abandoned laughter.

"Chen Diaoyuan is in there. With me!" Xuan Jimin was the arrow on the string. He took the steps in two or three strides and kicked; the shut doors flew open; and the whole banquet hall stood plain before him. Under the lanterns, four Eight Immortals tables in a row, gentlemen and ladies seated around them deep in their wine — and not one of them dreaming that through the door would come men with their eyes wide open for killing.

"Heaven!" The women started up first, shrieking. Then the men were on their feet, faces gone, the room boiling — some bolting for the doors, some standing stupid with shock, some diving under the tables, sure that bandits had come down on them.

"Nobody move!" Xuan Jimin, Wu Hongtai, Wang Ganting and Liu Decai stood in the doorway with knives and guns out, and the black muzzles pinned the room. Xuan Jimin's voice went over it like a whip: "An injustice has its author; a debt has its owner. The Hatchet Gang does its business in the open. We have not come tonight as your enemies. Speak up — where is Chen Diaoyuan?"

Terror, every man for himself. Xuan Jimin had never met Chen Diaoyuan, but from the earlier attempt at the Wuhu residence he had studied the photographs until the face had taken root: and now, sweeping the brilliant hall, to his and Wu Hongtai's sinking fury, the familiar long horse-face was nowhere in it. Chen Diaoyuan was not at Meixi Villa.

"A misunderstanding — gentlemen of honor, tonight is all a misunderstanding!" Out of the crowd's dead silence a man rose, trembling as he stood: middling height, scholarly in his movements, gold-rimmed spectacles. He bowed to the gunmen with a placating smile. "Your brother here did indeed send Chen Diaoyuan an invitation. But he has not come to the banquet tonight. I hear — I hear…"

"You hear what? Say it!" Disappointment and fury climbed together in Xuan Jimin. He put the muzzle on the sleek official. "And who are you?"

"Your brother is Zhang Qiubai." The man dared not give anything but his true name, and hurried on: "I hear Chairman Chen set out back to Wuhu this very evening — that is why he could not attend—"

"What did you say? Chen has run again?" The words went through Xuan Jimin like a torch through oil, and the pistol in his hand spoke its hatred for him — one, two, three shots into Zhang Qiubai's chest. Zhang Qiubai gave a single terrible cry and dropped into his own blood at the table's edge and did not move. For a moment the hall held a silence out of the grave; then, as the guests saw the killers vanish as suddenly as they had come, the screaming broke, and Meixi Villa dissolved: "Murder — the Hatchet Gang has killed a man!"

The cry went ringing out over the night hills of Plum Blossom Mountain.
